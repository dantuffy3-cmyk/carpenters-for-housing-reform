Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Pilot — Operational Brief

A working brief describing the proposed pilot design: purpose, participant requirements, tests, safeguards, evaluation approach and matters still requiring development.

This document is a working draft. It is not government policy and does not claim Victorian Government endorsement. Government, legal, regulatory and insurer development is required before implementation.

Pilot Purpose

The pilot is intended to test independently assessed Advanced Residential Carpentry capability in a controlled setting.

Registered Residential Carpenter remains the initiative's current proposed recognition model, but the pilot does not assume that RRC is the final statutory vehicle. Government would later determine whether the vehicle should be RRC, DB-L reform, an advanced credential or another proportionate mechanism.

The pilot tests capability, boundary recognition, documentation and escalation discipline. It does not grant new statutory authority. Participants must remain within their existing lawful authority throughout the pilot.

Policy Question Being Tested

  • Proposed: Does advanced residential renovation carpentry represent a distinct capability that government may wish to assess formally within Victoria's registration framework?
  • Proposed: If so, can that capability be tested safely before government determines the most proportionate implementation vehicle?
  • Requires government decision: Government would later determine whether the vehicle should be RRC, DB-L reform, an advanced credential or another proportionate mechanism.

Proposed Participant Cohort

  • qualified carpenters with verified practical renovation experience;
  • practitioners able to provide a portfolio of existing-building work;
  • practitioners willing to undergo independent assessment and technical questioning where appropriate; and
  • practitioners able to maintain records, communicate boundaries clearly and escalate promptly.

Entry Requirements

  • qualification verification;
  • verified practical experience;
  • evidence portfolio;
  • independent assessment;
  • technical questioning or oral defence where appropriate;
  • diagnostic capability in existing buildings;
  • concealed-condition recognition;
  • sequencing and temporary-support awareness;
  • scope-boundary recognition;
  • documentation capability; and
  • consumer communication capability.

Experience is evidence of exposure and eligibility. Experience alone must not confer advanced recognition.

What the Pilot Tests

  1. qualification verification;
  2. practical renovation experience;
  3. portfolio evidence;
  4. scenario-based technical assessment;
  5. concealed-condition recognition;
  6. sequencing and temporary-support judgment;
  7. scope-boundary recognition;
  8. stop-work and escalation decisions;
  9. documentation capability;
  10. consumer role communication;
  11. professional handoff decisions; and
  12. assessor consistency.

Additional Requirements

Consumer Role-Comprehension Test

The pilot should test whether a consumer can correctly identify what the assessed practitioner can do, what remains outside scope, when a builder is required, and when engineering, permits or licensed trades are required. Consumer comprehension should be tested at onboarding and at project completion rather than inferred from satisfaction alone.

Persistent Work and Evidence Record

Each pilot project should maintain a persistent record covering practitioner identity, assessed scope, pre-existing conditions, work undertaken, evidence captured, variations, concealed conditions, escalation events, professional referrals or handoffs, and completion status. This is a pilot governance record, not a statutory building manual.

Proportional Escalation Assessment

Assessment should test whether participants can distinguish between proceeding within scope, investigating further, obtaining specialist input, obtaining engineering input, involving a builder, or stopping work pending permit or regulatory clarification.

The preferred operational sequence remains: make the area safe, pause affected work, record the condition, notify the owner, obtain the appropriate input, revise scope and verify the response.

Practitioner Obligations

  • maintain complete pilot records;
  • use prescribed consumer disclosures;
  • avoid representing the pilot as government endorsement or new authority;
  • stop and escalate when the boundary is exceeded; and
  • cooperate with audit, complaints and evaluation processes.

Pilot Boundary

Included work should remain bounded to defined residential renovation carpentry functions that can be managed within the pilot's evidence and escalation model.

Excluded work should include any activity that would make the pilot operate as a substitute for domestic builder registration or for engineering, surveying or licensed-trade responsibilities.

Evaluation and Outcome

The pilot should generate evidence for government decision-making, not prove a predetermined outcome. Government may discontinue the concept, refine it, test it through another vehicle, or pursue no further action.

No participant should assume automatic transition to permanent registration, endorsement or expanded authority after the pilot.

Mature-Model Note

If Advanced Residential Carpentry recognition is later adopted as an ongoing formal pathway, continuing professional development should be included as a future design requirement. This brief does not design the CPD system.

Matters Still Requiring Development

  • lawful pilot architecture;
  • final implementation vehicle;
  • insurance settings;
  • audit and complaint authority;
  • scope wording and handoff drafting;
  • privacy and record-retention settings; and
  • any public claims about legal effect.
