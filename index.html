A practical, bounded reform proposal for Victoria: clearer public guidance, clearer boundaries, and clearer handoff points between residential carpentry work and domestic builder responsibility.

Industry-led reform proposal · Victoria

Clearer rules. Clearer roles. Better renovation outcomes.

Victoria’s current system does not clearly explain where practical renovation carpentry ends and where builder responsibility must begin.

We are proposing a clearer, bounded, consumer-facing Registered Residential Carpenter framework within Victoria’s existing domestic building system — with published scope, clear exclusions, and mandatory handoff points where domestic builder responsibility must apply.

Core Position

Not a backdoor builder licence

This proposal is about clarity, boundaries, accountability, and consumer protection. It is designed to work with Victoria’s current system — not cut across it.

  • Published scope
  • Clear exclusions
  • Builder handoff triggers
  • Consumer-facing guidance
  • Protection of builder responsibility

Alignment with Victoria’s 2026 Building Reform Framework

Victoria’s 2026 building reforms create a stronger and more intervention-focused regulatory environment through the Building and Plumbing Commission.

The proposed Registered Residential Carpenter pathway is designed to support that direction by making renovation responsibility clearer before problems occur.

A bounded RRC category would give consumers, builders, carpenters, insurers and regulators clearer guidance on:

  • what experienced renovation carpenters can do
  • what they cannot do
  • when a registered builder must take responsibility
  • when engineering, permits, insurance or specialist referral are required

This would support the broader reform objectives of improved consumer protection, clearer accountability, earlier intervention and better building outcomes.

Supports early intervention

Clear scope boundaries help regulators, consumers and practitioners identify responsibility before minor renovation issues become larger disputes.

Clarifies practitioner roles

The pathway would help define the practical boundary between renovation carpentry and domestic builder responsibility.

Keeps safeguards central

The model relies on insurance, documentation, written scope limits, permit triggers, engineering triggers and builder handoff rules.

Wider reform opportunity

Advanced Residential Trades Framework

Alongside the RRC pathway, we have published an Advanced Residential Trades Framework that sets out a staged approach to potential future reform. The RRC remains the first proposed pilot pathway.

Tiling, concreting and plastering are identified only as potential future investigations, while waterproofing is treated separately as a high-risk consideration requiring dedicated assurance settings.
