Industry-led reform proposal · Victoria

Evidence-led reform for Victoria’s carpenter registration, progression and accountability questions.

Victoria is consulting on carpenter registration and licensing reform. This initiative examines what the resulting baseline arrangements may recognise, require and authorise, how existing competence may transition into them, and whether any additional capability question remains once those arrangements are clearer.

The immediate regulatory question is what the proposed carpenter registration and licensing arrangements may recognise, require and authorise. Only once the baseline arrangements are clearer should government test whether any materially different additional carpentry capability remains and whether any further recognition would serve the public interest.

Registered Residential Carpenter remains a developed candidate implementation model, not the predetermined outcome. Possible later mechanisms could include RRC, DB-L reform, an endorsement, an advanced credential, another government-selected mechanism, or no additional formal recognition.

Core Position

Registration baseline first — not automatic builder authority

Technical capability, regulatory authority and contracting authority are separate questions. This initiative does not assume that demonstrated capability would expand builder powers, contracting permissions or statutory scope.

Certificate III remains the trade foundation. The current consultation on carpenter registration and licensing is the immediate baseline question, with any later recognition question to be tested only if a materially different capability remains once the baseline arrangements are clearer.

  • Emerging registration/licensing baseline

Understand this initiative in 60 seconds

The problem

Victoria's current consultation must clarify what the proposed carpenter registration and licensing arrangements may recognise and require, and how the arrangements ultimately adopted may interface with domestic builder responsibility.

The capability proposition

The evidence question is whether any materially different existing-building capability remains once the eventual baseline arrangements are clearer. Candidate capability areas include investigation, condition assessment, progressive scope determination and escalation judgement.

The boundaries

Demonstrated capability would not automatically confer builder authority, contracting authority, permission for new homes or unrestricted structural work. Those matters remain separate regulatory questions.

The government request

Clarify the proposed registration and licensing arrangements first. Then test whether any additional capability question remains, whether it can be assessed reliably, and whether any further recognition would be proportionate.

Occupational progression and existing-building capability

A qualified carpenter should not be the final recognised trade level

The evidence question is whether the baseline arrangements ultimately adopted will already address progression and existing-building capability, or whether a narrower additional recognition question remains while builder registration stays separate.

Alignment with Victoria's 2026 Building Reform Framework

Victoria's current building reforms provide the immediate policy context for considering practitioner capability, scope, accountability and consumer protection. The consultation has not yet settled the final registration or licensing model, scope, assessment settings or contracting permissions.

If the baseline arrangements ultimately adopted leave a materially different additional capability question, government could then compare proportionate mechanisms. A bounded RRC pathway would be one candidate model, not a predetermined result.

The current consultation is considering:

  • what the proposed carpenter registration and licensing arrangements may recognise
  • what the arrangements ultimately adopted may and may not authorise
  • how existing competence could transition into the baseline arrangements ultimately adopted
  • when builder, engineering, permit, insurance or specialist involvement is required

This would support the broader reform objectives of improved consumer protection, clearer accountability, earlier intervention and better building outcomes, regardless of whether any additional recognition is later justified.

Supports early intervention

Clear baseline scope boundaries help regulators, consumers and practitioners identify responsibility before minor renovation issues become larger disputes.

Clarifies practitioner roles

The reform process can clarify the practical boundary between carpenter work, builder responsibility and other regulated roles.

Keeps safeguards central

Any baseline or later recognition model should rely on documentation, written scope limits, permit triggers, engineering triggers and builder handoff rules.

Wider reform opportunity

Advanced Residential Trades Framework

Alongside the developed RRC candidate model, we have published an Advanced Residential Trades Framework that sets out a staged approach to potential future reform. Registered Residential Carpenter remains one possible recognition model if evidence later supports formal implementation.

Tiling, concreting and plastering are identified only as potential future investigations, while waterproofing is treated separately as a high-risk consideration requiring dedicated assurance settings.
