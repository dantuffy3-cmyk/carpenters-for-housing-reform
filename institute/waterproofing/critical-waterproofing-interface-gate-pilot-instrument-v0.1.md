# Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate v0.1 — Pilot Instrument

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria  
**Status:** Working Draft — Pilot Instrument v0.1  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Purpose:** Controlled testing of a narrow pre-waterproofing assurance gate. This document is not a statutory certificate, government-endorsed process or final regulatory proposal.

---

## 1. Pilot Purpose

The pilot tests one proposition:

> **Can a small set of observable or reasonably measurable interface conditions be checked immediately before waterproofing, so that unsuitable conditions are identified before membrane installation without transferring responsibility for upstream trades to the waterproofing practitioner?**

An independent third-party technical red-team review conducted during development found that some readiness controls appeared potentially useful, but that a broader readiness model risked:

- duplicating ordinary builder supervision;
- turning the waterproofing practitioner into an unofficial inspector of other trades;
- transferring or confusing responsibility;
- mixing statutory requirements with manufacturer requirements and normal trade practice;
- creating false confidence from a single pre-waterproofing check.

CWIG-01 is therefore deliberately narrower.

It does not attempt to certify the entire bathroom or the work of preceding practitioners.

Its purpose is to determine whether the **observable and reasonably measurable waterproofing interfaces presented to the waterproofing practitioner are suitable for installation of the identified waterproofing system**.

---

## 2. Pilot Scope

CWIG-01 v0.1 is deliberately narrow.

### Included

- internal residential bathrooms and shower areas;
- primarily Class 1 domestic work during initial pilot development;
- pre-membrane interface conditions;
- observable or reasonably measurable conditions;
- the identified waterproofing system;
- selected technical and manufacturer requirements relevant to installation;
- stop, referral, investigation and escalation decisions before membrane application.

### Excluded from v0.1

- balconies and external membranes;
- below-ground waterproofing;
- general building inspection;
- structural certification;
- plumbing certification;
- certification of concealed framing;
- certification of complete upstream trade compliance;
- certification of overall building compliance;
- final determination of regulatory or licensing architecture;
- novel or complex Performance Solutions unless separately reviewed.

External and balcony waterproofing should be developed as a separate technical module because the applicable technical pathways, risk profile, drainage conditions and consequences of failure differ materially from ordinary internal residential wet areas.

## 3. Applicable Technical Basis

Every CWIG-01 record must identify the technical basis relevant to the particular project before the gate is completed.

The record should identify, where applicable:

- applicable National Construction Code (NCC) edition;
- building classification;
- Deemed-to-Satisfy or other compliance pathway;
- relevant Housing Provisions;
- AS 3740 pathway where applicable;
- project drawings or specification;
- selected membrane system;
- manufacturer technical documentation;
- relevant substrate or lining system documentation;
- relevant drainage/interface details;
- any approved Performance Solution or project-specific technical direction.

The pilot must not assume that one construction detail applies universally to every bathroom, shower configuration, substrate or waterproofing product.

Where a requirement depends specifically on an Australian Standard that has not been directly verified from the lawful primary standard text during development, it must remain identified as requiring primary-standard confirmation before being presented as a final technical requirement.

## 5. Evidence Basis

Every material CWIG decision must identify its evidence basis.

### OBSERVED

The practitioner directly observed the condition at the time of assessment.

### MEASURED

A measurement was taken and recorded.

Examples may include:

- fall;
- height;
- distance;
- moisture condition where an appropriate method is required;
- location or dimension relevant to a technical detail.

### PHOTO_SUPPORTED

A photograph records the visible condition at that time.

A photograph does not automatically establish compliance.

### DOCUMENT_SUPPORTED

A drawing, specification, certificate, technical document or other controlled project record supports the entry.

### MANUFACTURER_SUPPORTED

The relevant manufacturer installation guide, technical data sheet or documented system detail supports the requirement or decision.

### INDEPENDENTLY_VERIFIED

A separate competent person has independently reviewed the relevant condition.

### UNKNOWN / UNABLE TO VERIFY

The available evidence is insufficient to make a reliable determination.

Unknown or unavailable evidence must not be treated as a positive condition.

# 6. CWIG-01 — Six Critical Waterproofing Interface Checks

CWIG-01 contains six core interface domains.

1. Substrate Acceptance
2. Water-Management Geometry
3. Drainage and Penetration Interface
4. Junction and Movement Treatability
5. Surface and Moisture Acceptance
6. Waterproofing System Compatibility

A practitioner is not required to certify the whole upstream construction system in order to complete these checks.

The relevant question is whether the **waterproofing interface presented at the time of assessment can responsibly receive the identified waterproofing system**.

---

# 7. CWI-01 — Substrate Acceptance

## Controlling Question

> **Is the exposed substrate presented to the waterproofing practitioner suitable for reliable installation of the identified waterproofing system?**

### Record

| Check | Pilot Response |
|---|---|
| Substrate identified | Yes / No / Unknown |
| Substrate type | Record |
| Selected system suitable for substrate | Acceptable / Not acceptable / Unable to verify |
| Visible substrate stable and sound | Acceptable / Not acceptable |
| Visible damage | None / Record |
| Loose material | None / Record |
| Significant voids or imperfections | None / Record |
| Protrusions affecting membrane installation | None / Record |
| Relevant substrate requirements resolved | Yes / No / Unknown |
| Evidence basis | Record |

### Practitioner May Assess

- substrate identity;
- visible stability;
- visible damage;
- loose material;
- obvious voids;
- protrusions;
- exposed surface condition;
- whether the identified substrate is compatible with the selected waterproofing system.

### Practitioner Does Not Certify

- hidden framing;
- complete structural adequacy;
- concealed fixings;
- hidden stud spacing;
- complete lining-trade compliance;
- work that cannot reasonably be observed;
- another practitioner's overall compliance.

### Possible Outcomes

**ACCEPTABLE**

**ACCEPTABLE WITH CONTROL**

**NOT ACCEPTABLE**

**UNABLE TO VERIFY**

### Example STOP Conditions

- substrate visibly unstable;
- damaged substrate materially affects waterproofing;
- selected membrane is not suitable for the identified substrate;
- substrate condition cannot support a documented waterproofing detail;
- information required to establish system suitability is unavailable.

# 8. CWI-02 — Water-Management Geometry

## Controlling Question

> **Is the visible and measurable geometry capable of providing the required water-management arrangement for this configuration?**

### Record

| Check | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Shower configuration identified | Record |
| Waste location identified | Record |
| Falls required | Yes / No / To verify |
| Falls measured | Measurement |
| Measurement method | Record |
| Threshold/hob/set-down configuration | Record |
| Waterstop arrangement applicable | Yes / No / To verify |
| Waterstop position | Measurement where applicable |
| Drainage path observable | Yes / No |
| Geometry consistent with identified technical pathway | Acceptable / Not acceptable / Unable to verify |
| Evidence basis | Record |

### Potential Critical Conditions

- reverse fall;
- required fall cannot be achieved;
- required water-management geometry is inconsistent with the applicable shower configuration;
- required waterstop or containment arrangement is absent;
- threshold, hob or set-down geometry prevents the selected waterproofing detail;
- drainage geometry is unresolved;
- configuration cannot be reliably identified.

### Pilot Principle

CWIG must not hard-code a single waterstop dimension, shower extent or fall requirement for every bathroom.

The applicable requirement must be derived from the actual:

**building class + compliance pathway + shower configuration + selected system + applicable technical detail.**

### Decision

**KEEP — CRITICAL PILOT CONDITION**

# 9. CWI-03 — Drainage and Penetration Interface

## Controlling Question

> **Can the selected waterproofing system form a reliable interface with the wastes and penetrations presented?**

### Record

| Check | Pilot Response |
|---|---|
| Waste/interface visible | Yes / No |
| Sufficient access for waterproofing connection | Yes / No |
| Waterproofing-to-drain connection identified | Yes / No / Unknown |
| Connection compatible with selected system | Yes / No / Refer |
| Plumbing penetrations identified | Yes / No |
| Penetration treatment identified | Yes / No / Refer |
| Penetration geometry treatable | Yes / No |
| Unresolved clash | None / Record |
| Evidence basis | Record |

### Practitioner May Assess

- location of visible wastes;
- accessible membrane-to-drain interfaces;
- visible plumbing penetrations;
- whether the selected membrane system can form its required detail;
- whether penetrations can be treated using a documented method;
- whether an unresolved interface prevents reliable waterproofing.

### Practitioner Does Not Certify

- plumbing-system design;
- pipe sizing;
- concealed plumbing;
- complete plumbing installation;
- plumbing compliance;
- matters outside the waterproofing interface.

### Example Referral Pathway

**DRAIN_INTERFACE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE**

↓

**STOP**

↓

**REFER TO APPROPRIATE PLUMBER / BUILDER / DESIGNER**

↓

**RECTIFY**

↓

**RE-PRESENT**

↓

**REASSESS**

The waterproofing practitioner is not required to determine whether the plumbing work is legally compliant.

The practitioner determines only whether the visible interface can responsibly receive the waterproofing system.

### Decision

**KEEP — CRITICAL PILOT CONDITION**

---

# 10. CWI-04 — Junction and Movement Treatability

## Controlling Question

> **Can all relevant visible junctions, material transitions and movement locations be treated using a documented detail for the selected waterproofing system?**

### Relevant Locations

Where applicable:

- wall/floor junctions;
- wall/wall junctions;
- hob/wall interfaces;
- material transitions;
- movement joints;
- niches;
- changes in substrate;
- penetrations adjacent to junctions;
- non-standard geometry.

### Classification

Each relevant condition may be classified as:

**TREATABLE**

**TREATABLE WITH CONTROL**

**UNRESOLVED**

**NOT APPLICABLE**

### Record

| Check | Pilot Response |
|---|---|
| Wall/floor junction resolved | Yes / No / N/A |
| Wall/wall junction resolved | Yes / No / N/A |
| Hob interface resolved | Yes / No / N/A |
| Material transitions resolved | Yes / No / N/A |
| Movement locations identified | Yes / No / N/A |
| Selected system contains documented treatment | Yes / No / Unknown |
| Non-standard detail present | Yes / No |
| Manufacturer/design advice required | Yes / No |
| Evidence basis | Record |

### Important Control

CWIG does not require one universal:

- bond breaker;
- tape;
- bandage;
- reinforcement fabric;
- sealant;
- proprietary junction product.

The required detail depends on the applicable construction pathway and selected waterproofing system.

### STOP / REFER Conditions

- no documented treatment exists for the observed interface;
- substrate movement or cracking remains unresolved;
- selected waterproofing system cannot accommodate the configuration;
- practitioner would need to improvise outside documented system requirements;
- technical direction is required before proceeding.

### Decision

**KEEP — HIGH VALUE**

---

# 11. CWI-05 — Surface and Moisture Acceptance

## Controlling Question

> **Is the exposed surface in a condition that permits reliable installation of the selected waterproofing system?**

### Record

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Surface clean | Acceptable / Not acceptable |
| Dust-free | Acceptable / Not acceptable |
| Loose material removed | Acceptable / Not acceptable |
| Significant contamination | None / Record |
| Protrusions affecting membrane | None / Record |
| Voids/imperfections affecting membrane | None / Record |
| Visible moisture concern | None / Record |
| Moisture measurement required by selected system | Yes / No |
| Measurement method | Record where applicable |
| Instrument | Record where applicable |
| Reading | Record where applicable |
| Manufacturer acceptance criterion | Record/reference |
| Result against system requirement | Acceptable / Not acceptable / Unable to verify |
| Evidence basis | Record |

### Important Control

CWIG must not create a universal numerical substrate-moisture limit.

Any numerical acceptance criterion must be derived from the relevant:

- membrane product;
- substrate;
- manufacturer;
- system specification;
- applicable technical requirement.

### Example

Plaster stopping and sanding has occurred before waterproofing.

Significant plaster dust remains on the shower substrate.

Result:

**CWI-05 — NOT ACCEPTABLE**

Decision:

**STOP**

Required action:

**CLEAN / PREPARE SURFACE**

Then:

**RE-PRESENT**

Then:

**REASSESS**

No finding of fault is necessary for the gate to prevent membrane installation onto an unsuitable surface.

### Decision

**KEEP — CRITICAL PILOT CONDITION**

---

# 12. CWI-06 — Waterproofing System Compatibility

## Controlling Question

> **Has the practitioner identified a documented waterproofing system that is compatible with the conditions and components actually presented?**

### Record

| Component | Record |
|---|---|
| Substrate | Identified |
| Membrane manufacturer | Record |
| Membrane product/system | Record |
| Primer | Required / not required / unresolved |
| Sealant | Identified where relevant |
| Junction treatment | Identified |
| Drainage-interface treatment | Identified |
| Penetration treatment | Identified |
| Overlay/tile compatibility | Resolved / unresolved |
| Required application method | Manufacturer reference |
| Required number of coats where applicable | Manufacturer reference |
| Required film/application thickness where applicable | Manufacturer reference |
| Recoat requirement | Manufacturer reference |
| Cure requirement | Manufacturer reference |
| Flood/water testing requirement if applicable | Manufacturer/project reference |
| Known deviation | None / Record |
| Evidence basis | Record |

### Critical Principle

The pilot must never create universal requirements such as:

- every system requires primer;
- every system requires bandage;
- every system requires two coats;
- every system requires three coats;
- every membrane has the same thickness;
- every system requires the same curing time;
- every system requires flood testing.

These requirements can differ materially between legitimate waterproofing systems.

### Decision Rule

If compatibility cannot be established:

**DO NOT IMPROVISE**

Use:

**INVESTIGATE**

**REFER**

or

**ESCALATE**

and obtain appropriate manufacturer, design or technical advice where required.

### Decision

**KEEP — CRITICAL PILOT CONDITION**

# 13. Gate Decision States

CWIG-01 uses the following decision states.

## PROCEED

All material CWIG conditions are acceptable.

No unresolved condition prevents reliable membrane installation.

## PROCEED WITH CONTROL

A condition exists but can be addressed through a specific, documented control without undermining reliable installation.

The control must be recorded.

This state must not become a general "near enough" pathway.

## INVESTIGATE

Additional evidence or investigation is required before a reliable decision can be made.

## REFER

Another appropriate practitioner or responsible party needs to resolve the condition.

## ESCALATE

The condition requires a higher-order technical, design, engineering, manufacturer, regulatory or other decision.

## STOP

The current condition prevents reliable waterproofing from proceeding.

---

# 14. Hard Gate Rule

CWIG-01 cannot reach **PROCEED** while any material:

**STOP**

**REFER**

**INVESTIGATE**

or

**ESCALATE**

condition remains unresolved.

A **PROCEED WITH CONTROL** decision must identify:

- the condition;
- the control;
- responsibility for implementing the control where known;
- evidence of implementation;
- whether reassessment is required.

---

# 15. Correction Ownership

CWIG identifies the **condition** first.

It does not automatically determine legal liability or blame.

Example:

`DRAIN_INTERFACE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE`

The pilot record may state:

**Originating interface:** Plumbing/waste interface  
**Waterproofing decision:** Do not proceed  
**Correction pathway:** Appropriate plumber/builder/project controller to determine  
**Re-presentation required:** Yes

This prevents CWIG from becoming a liability-allocation instrument.

Responsibility for rectification remains determined by the actual:

- work performed;
- contractual arrangements;
- statutory responsibilities;
- practitioner roles;
- project circumstances.

---

# 16. Practitioner Acceptance Statement

The following statement should accompany every completed CWIG-01 pilot record:

> **CWIG acceptance records only the condition of the observable and reasonably measurable waterproofing interfaces presented at the time of assessment and their suitability for installation of the identified waterproofing system. It does not certify concealed construction, structural adequacy, plumbing compliance, overall building compliance, design compliance or work outside the practitioner's competence and scope.**

This statement is a core pilot control.

It must not be weakened into language implying that the waterproofing practitioner has certified the complete bathroom construction system.

---

# 17. Deliberate Exclusions From CWIG-01

CWIG-01 does not certify:

- exact hidden stud layout;
- hidden framing configuration;
- general structural adequacy;
- complete concealed sheet fixing;
- complete lining-trade compliance;
- plumbing compliance;
- concealed plumbing;
- full bathroom design compliance;
- builder supervision;
- overall building compliance;
- future movement;
- future material deterioration;
- future trade behaviour;
- post-waterproofing penetrations;
- long-term waterproofing performance.

Where any excluded matter prevents the waterproofing practitioner from reliably determining interface suitability, the appropriate state is:

**INVESTIGATE**

**REFER**

**ESCALATE**

or

**STOP**

—not an unsupported assumption.

---

# 18. Upstream Wet-Area Construction Evidence

The narrowing of CWIG-01 does not mean upstream construction evidence lacks value.

Some information may be better captured before it becomes concealed.

A future **Upstream Wet-Area Construction Record** may include evidence relating to:

- framing;
- lining support;
- substrate installation;
- required backing or noggings;
- niches;
- shower-screen backing where specified;
- preformed shower-base interfaces;
- recesses and set-downs;
- concealed plumbing rough-in;
- representative sheet fixing;
- substrate identity.

The originating practitioner remains responsible for their work.

The waterproofing practitioner may use available upstream evidence to inform a decision but does not automatically become the certifier of that work.

# 19. Worked Pilot Scenario A — Dusty Shower Substrate

## Condition

A water-resistant wall lining is presented following stopping and sanding.

Significant dust remains across the waterproofing substrate.

## CWIG Assessment

**CWI-01 — Substrate identity:** Acceptable

**CWI-05 — Surface condition:** Not acceptable

## Gate Decision

**STOP**

## Action

Surface is cleaned and prepared.

## Evidence

- initial photograph;
- STOP record;
- rectification record;
- post-cleaning photograph;
- reassessment.

## Final Result

If all six domains are subsequently acceptable:

**PROCEED**

## Pilot Value Being Tested

Whether a low-cost intervention at the correct sequence point prevents waterproofing from being applied to a visibly unsuitable surface.

The pilot must not claim that a future waterproofing failure was definitely prevented.

---

# 20. Worked Pilot Scenario B — Drain Interface Cannot Receive Selected Detail

## Condition

Falls and substrate are acceptable.

The waste/interface arrangement cannot be reconciled with the selected membrane system's documented drainage detail.

## CWIG Assessment

**CWI-03 — Drainage Interface:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**CWI-06 — System Compatibility:** UNRESOLVED

## Gate Decision

**REFER / STOP**

## Practitioner Finding

> The presented waterproofing-to-drain interface is not currently suitable for installation using the identified waterproofing system.

The practitioner does not certify that the plumbing installation is non-compliant.

## Action

Appropriate plumber, builder, designer or system adviser resolves the interface.

The condition is then re-presented.

## Reassessment

CWI-03 and CWI-06 are reassessed before waterproofing proceeds.

---

# 21. Worked Pilot Scenario C — Hidden Framing Cannot Be Verified

## Condition

Wall lining is already installed.

No photographic or other upstream framing evidence is available.

The visible substrate appears stable.

## CWIG Boundary

The waterproofing practitioner does not become responsible for determining:

- hidden stud centres;
- hidden noggings;
- concealed fixing schedules;
- complete structural adequacy.

The record states:

`HIDDEN_UPSTREAM_FRAMING = NOT VERIFIED`

If suitability of the selected waterproofing system can still be reliably established from the exposed substrate, technical documentation and observable conditions, CWIG may proceed with the limitation recorded.

If system suitability cannot be established without the missing information:

**INVESTIGATE**

or

**REFER**

The absence of information must not be converted into assumed compliance.

---

# 22. Worked Pilot Scenario D — System Requires Different Preparation

## Condition

The substrate is generally clean and sound.

The waterproofing practitioner intends to apply a selected membrane.

The manufacturer's documented system requires a particular primer or preparation step for that substrate.

The preparation has not been completed.

## CWIG Assessment

**CWI-05 — Surface/Preparation:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**CWI-06 — System Compatibility:** CONDITION UNRESOLVED

## Decision

**STOP**

## Action

Complete the required system-specific preparation.

Record:

- selected system;
- manufacturer requirement;
- product used;
- completion evidence;
- reassessment.

## Pilot Principle

The finding is not:

> All bathrooms require primer.

The finding is:

> This selected system requires this preparation for the presented substrate.

---

# 23. Worked Pilot Scenario E — Unusual Junction

## Condition

A material transition or unusual junction exists for which the practitioner cannot identify a documented treatment within the selected waterproofing system.

## CWIG Assessment

**CWI-04 — Junction Treatability:** UNRESOLVED

**CWI-06 — System Compatibility:** UNRESOLVED

## Decision

**INVESTIGATE / ESCALATE**

The practitioner should not improvise an undocumented detail merely to keep the project moving.

Potential resolution may include:

- manufacturer technical advice;
- designer direction;
- alternative compatible system;
- alteration of the interface;
- other competent technical determination.

Once resolved:

**RE-PRESENT**

and reassess.

# 24. Minimum CWIG Evidence Pack

A completed CWIG-01 pilot record should ordinarily contain:

1. project identification;
2. applicable technical basis;
3. selected waterproofing system;
4. six CWIG domain decisions;
5. configuration-appropriate photographs;
6. required measurements;
7. manufacturer/system references where relevant;
8. identified unsuitable conditions;
9. STOP / REFER / INVESTIGATE / ESCALATE decisions;
10. rectification or control evidence;
11. reassessment evidence;
12. final gate decision;
13. practitioner acceptance statement.

The evidence record should preserve superseded findings rather than replacing them.

Example:

`09:13 — CWI-05 — NOT ACCEPTABLE — surface dust present`

should remain in the record.

After correction:

`10:02 — CWI-05 — RECTIFIED / ACCEPTABLE`

is added.

This produces an audit trail rather than a perfect-looking final checklist that hides the actual construction sequence.

# 25. Relationship to PCR-01

CWIG-01 occurs **before membrane installation**.

It cannot assess the quality of membrane work that does not yet exist.

Those matters belong primarily in:

# PCR-01 — Pre-Concealment Release

Potential PCR-01 matters include:

- membrane extent;
- waterstop termination;
- membrane-to-drainage connection;
- completed junction treatments;
- completed penetration treatments;
- visible pinholes;
- visible damage;
- manufacturer-required application evidence;
- film/application thickness evidence where relevant;
- recoat/cure condition;
- repairs;
- deviations;
- unresolved defects.

CWIG-01 must not absorb PCR-01 merely to create one large checklist.

---

# 26. Relationship to PMP-01

CWIG-01 and PCR-01 cannot prevent all later failures.

Waterproofing may subsequently be damaged by:

- shower-screen installation;
- accessory installation;
- plumbing changes;
- cabinetry;
- grab rails;
- mechanical fixings;
- drilling;
- later alterations;
- accidental site damage.

# 29. Evidence Interpretation Rule

A STOP decision does not prove that a future defect or leak was prevented.

It proves only that:

> **An identified condition considered unsuitable for reliable waterproofing installation was intercepted before membrane application.**

Claims about actual defect reduction require pilot evaluation.

Similarly:

- photographs do not automatically prove compliance;
- documentation does not automatically prove correct installation;
- a practitioner declaration is not independent verification;
- passing CWIG does not guarantee long-term waterproofing performance;
- missing evidence must not improve the assurance outcome.

---

# 30. Falsification Criteria

CWIG-01 should be narrowed, redesigned or rejected if pilot evidence shows that it:

- primarily duplicates ordinary builder supervision;
- adds little meaningful intervention value;
- routinely requires waterproofing practitioners to assess matters outside reasonable competence;
- creates responsibility transfer;
- produces high assessor disagreement;
- generates substantial administrative burden for few meaningful interventions;
- cannot adapt reliably across legitimate waterproofing systems;
- causes practitioners to treat a gate pass as proof of complete bathroom compliance;
- produces false confidence;
- increases disputes about responsibility;
- fails to identify material pre-waterproofing conditions;
- proves inferior to simpler alternative controls.

A failed pilot remains a legitimate research outcome.

---

# 31. Conditions Supporting Retention

CWIG-01 would warrant further development if pilot evidence demonstrates that:

- practitioners can complete the six checks consistently;
- the checks remain within reasonable waterproofing competence;
- meaningful unsuitable conditions are intercepted before membrane application;
- responsibility boundaries remain clear;
- administrative burden is proportionate;
- system-specific requirements can be accommodated without creating an unmanageable checklist;
- evidence records materially improve reconstruction of what was presented and what decisions were made;
- STOP and referral decisions can occur without inappropriate liability transfer;
- independent reviewers broadly reproduce practitioner decisions;
- later PCR-01 findings demonstrate that the staged architecture adds more assurance than a single inspection point.

---

# 32. Pilot Instrument Decision

The broad concept of a practitioner certifying complete "waterproofing readiness" is not retained.

The pilot instead tests a narrower proposition:

# CWIG-01 — Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate

CWIG-01 is limited to six pre-membrane interface domains:

1. substrate;
2. water-management geometry;
3. drainage and penetrations;
4. junction and movement treatability;
5. surface and moisture condition;
6. waterproofing-system compatibility.

The practitioner is assessing whether waterproofing can responsibly proceed.

The practitioner is not certifying the whole bathroom.

---

# 33. Provisional Position

**CWIG-01 warrants controlled pilot testing.**

This is not a recommendation for:

- a new practitioner licence;
- a new statutory registration category;
- universal mandatory inspection;
- automatic incorporation into the Registered Residential Carpenter proposal;
- automatic incorporation into any Domestic Builder — Limited class;
- a government-mandated digital system;
- a statutory certificate;
- compulsory adoption across all residential waterproofing work.

Those questions remain open.

The immediate objective is narrower:

> **Determine whether the Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate provides enough additional assurance, at proportionate burden, to justify further development.**

---

# 34. Core Pilot Principle

The controlling principle for CWIG-01 is:

> **CWIG does not ask whether the bathroom is compliant. It asks whether the waterproofing practitioner has been presented with an interface on which the identified waterproofing system can responsibly proceed.**

That distinction must remain intact throughout pilot development.
