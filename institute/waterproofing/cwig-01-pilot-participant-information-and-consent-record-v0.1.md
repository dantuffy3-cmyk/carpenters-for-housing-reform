## 2. Purpose of the Pilot

Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria is independently developing and testing a proposed:

**Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate — CWIG-01**

CWIG-01 is intended to test whether a narrow pre-waterproofing interface assessment can improve:

- decision clarity;
- evidence capture;
- responsibility boundaries;
- identification of material interface conditions before membrane installation;
- reassessment when material conditions change.

The pilot is experimental.

The pilot is not designed to prove in advance that CWIG works.

Evidence showing that CWIG is:

- unnecessary;
- impractical;
- duplicative;
- too burdensome;
- unreliable;
- responsibility-distorting;
- or unsuitable for further development

is a legitimate pilot outcome.

---

## 3. Canonical Scope & Limitation Statement

Participation in this pilot must be understood consistently with:

**CWIG-01 Scope & Limitation Statement v0.1**

The controlling meaning is:

> **CWIG-01 is a limited pre-waterproofing interface decision process. It records whether the observable and reasonably measurable interface presented at the time of assessment is suitable for the identified waterproofing system to proceed, subject to the evidence and technical basis available at that time.**

CWIG-01 does not certify the bathroom, building, waterproofing work, plumbing work, structural work, framing, lining or other upstream construction as compliant.

CWIG-01 is not:

- a statutory inspection;
- a building approval;
- a waterproofing compliance certificate;
- a plumbing certificate;
- certification of concealed construction;
- certification of upstream trade work;
- government approval;
- government endorsement;
- a guarantee against future leakage or waterproofing failure.

CWIG-01 has no statutory or regulatory status.

---

## 4. What the Pilot Assesses

CWIG-01 uses six existing assessment domains:

1. **CWI-01 — Substrate Acceptance**
2. **CWI-02 — Water-Management Geometry**
3. **CWI-03 — Drainage and Penetration Interface**
4. **CWI-04 — Junction and Movement Treatability**
5. **CWI-05 — Surface and Moisture Acceptance**
6. **CWI-06 — Waterproofing System Compatibility**

The waterproofing practitioner assesses the interface:

> **as presented for waterproofing**

within their reasonable role and competence.

They do not become the certifier of another practitioner's work merely because that work affects the waterproofing interface.

## 6. Who May Participate

Depending on the project, pilot participants may include:

- property owner/client;
- occupier where relevant;
- builder or project controller;
- waterproofing practitioner;
- another practitioner involved in the relevant interface;
- independent pilot reviewer.

Participation in the CWIG pilot does not create additional statutory authority for any participant.

## 7. Voluntary Pilot Participation

The pilot is intended to operate on a voluntary basis.

A person should not be represented as a voluntary participant unless they have been given sufficient information to understand:

- what CWIG is;
- what CWIG is not;
- what information may be collected;
- that adverse findings may occur;
- that a STOP, REFER, INVESTIGATE or ESCALATE decision may affect project sequencing;
- that pilot evidence may be independently reviewed;
- that the pilot may produce findings unfavourable to CWIG.

## 10. Adverse Findings

Participants must understand that the pilot is specifically designed to preserve adverse evidence.

This may include:

- NOT ACCEPTABLE conditions;
- STOP decisions;
- unresolved conditions;
- referrals;
- escalation;
- assessor disagreement;
- responsibility disputes;
- material change;
- commercial pressure;
- evidence gaps;
- missed conditions;
- practitioner criticism of CWIG;
- evidence that CWIG added little or no value.

An adverse pilot finding is not automatically:

- a statutory defect notice;
- a legal finding;
- a negligence finding;
- proof that a leak would have occurred;
- proof that another practitioner breached an obligation.

It records the condition and pilot decision made at that time.

# PART C — WHY INFORMATION IS COLLECTED

## 13. Proposed Pilot Uses

Information may be used to evaluate questions including:

- whether CWIG can be applied practically;
- whether different competent assessors reach substantially similar decisions;
- whether material interface conditions are identified before waterproofing;
- whether responsibility boundaries remain clear;
- whether reassessment controls work;
- whether the process is proportionate;
- whether CWIG creates unnecessary burden;
- whether CWIG should be retained, narrowed, changed, paused or abandoned.

---

## 14. Evidence Reconstruction

The pilot seeks to preserve enough evidence to reconstruct:

> **condition → evidence → decision → intervention → correction → reassessment**

The objective is not maximum data collection.

The objective is reliable decision reconstruction.

---

# PART D — ADVERSE-EVIDENCE PRESERVATION

## 15. Append-Only Principle

> **A material adverse finding that has entered the controlled pilot record should not be overwritten or deleted merely because the condition is later corrected.**

Instead:

**initial condition**

↓

**initial decision**

↓

**correction**

↓

**reassessment**

↓

**later current decision**

are linked.

This protects the integrity of the pilot evidence.

---

## 16. Why Earlier Findings Are Preserved

Example:

A project may initially receive:

**STOP**

because a material condition is unacceptable.

The condition may later be corrected and the project may eventually receive:

**PROCEED**

The pilot record should preserve both outcomes.

The final positive decision must not erase the fact that an earlier intervention occurred.

---

# PART E — POSSIBLE EFFECT ON THE PROJECT

## 17. Pilot Decisions May Affect Sequencing

Participants should understand that CWIG may produce:

- PROCEED;
- PROCEED WITH CONTROL;
- INVESTIGATE;
- REFER;
- ESCALATE;
- STOP.

A non-positive outcome may result in:

- further investigation;
- referral to another practitioner;
- technical clarification;
- rectification;
- reassessment;
- delay before waterproofing commences.

---

## 18. STOP Does Not Automatically Mean Defect Finding

A CWIG STOP means:

> **waterproofing should not proceed under the interface conditions presented at that time.**

It does not automatically mean:

- a law has been breached;
- another practitioner is negligent;
- the whole bathroom is defective;
- a regulator has made a finding.

---

## 19. Commercial Consequences

A pilot decision may affect project timing or coordination.

Possible consequences may include:

- following trades waiting;
- extra investigation;
- rectification;
- reattendance;
- reassessment.

Participation in CWIG does not change the hard non-PROCEED rule.

Project pressure cannot turn an unresolved material condition into a positive CWIG decision.

---

# PART F — MATERIAL CHANGE NOTIFICATION

## 20. Why Material Change Matters

A CWIG decision applies to the interface actually assessed.

If the interface changes before waterproofing, the earlier decision may no longer apply.

Potential changes include:

- waterproofing product/system substitution;
- substrate alteration;
- damage;
- changed geometry;
- changed threshold/waterstop;
- changed waste;
- new or altered penetration;
- moisture event;
- contamination;
- changed junction;
- new contradictory technical information.

---

## 21. Proposed Participant Change-Control Condition

For controlled pilot purposes, participating project parties should notify the waterproofing practitioner or pilot coordinator of a known material change occurring after CWIG assessment and before membrane application.

This is a proposed pilot participation control.

It is not represented as a new statutory obligation.

---

## 22. Pre-Application Status Reconfirmation

Immediately before membrane application, the pilot uses:

**CWIG-01 Pre-Application Status Reconfirmation Record v0.1**

If material change is identified, or a material change cannot reasonably be ruled out:

> **REASSESSMENT REQUIRED**

applies as appropriate before waterproofing proceeds under the earlier decision.

---

# PART G — INDEPENDENT REVIEW

## 23. Independent Comparison

Some or all pilot cases may receive independent comparison review in accordance with the controlled pilot methodology.

The reviewer may assess:

- evidence;
- technical basis;
- CWI findings;
- uncertainty classification;
- gate decision.

Where practical, the reviewer should complete their assessment before seeing the primary assessor's final decision.

---

## 24. Why Independent Review Is Used

Independent comparison is intended to test:

- reproducibility;
- calibration;
- technical-basis consistency;
- responsibility-boundary consistency;
- material decision disagreement.

It is not a statutory inspection.

It does not itself create regulatory approval.

---

## 25. Assessor Disagreement

Assessors may disagree.

A disagreement is not automatically evidence that one practitioner performed poorly.

The pilot distinguishes:

- FULL AGREEMENT;
- SUBSTANTIAL AGREEMENT;
- MATERIAL DISAGREEMENT;
- TECHNICAL-BASIS DISAGREEMENT.

Material disagreement is preserved as evidence.

## 40. Automatic Pilot HOLD Events

The wider governance architecture contains automatic HOLD controls for serious events including:

- waterproofing proceeding despite unresolved STOP;
- waterproofing proceeding despite material unresolved conditions;
- material PROCEED-versus-STOP reviewer disagreement;
- material privacy/evidence-integrity failure;
- intentional removal of adverse evidence;
- material responsibility-boundary failure.

Participants are not expected to administer pilot governance themselves.

These controls are managed through the pilot governance process.
