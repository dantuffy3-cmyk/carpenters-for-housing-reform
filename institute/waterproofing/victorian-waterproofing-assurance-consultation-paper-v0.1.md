# Victorian Residential Waterproofing Assurance Reform

## Consultation Paper — Alternative Pre-Concealment Assurance Model

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria
**Status:** Consultation Draft
**Date:** August 2026

## Purpose

Victoria is currently undertaking significant reform of its building regulatory system, including work relating to practitioner accountability, consumer protection, building records and trades registration.

Waterproofing sits within this reform environment because failures can involve concealed work, multiple practitioner interfaces and substantial rectification consequences once construction is completed.

In June 2026, proposed additional mandatory inspections at the pre-lining and waterproofing stages were removed from the Building Amendment (Building Manuals) Regulations 2026 to enable development of an **alternative approach**.

This consultation paper does not presume what that alternative approach should be.

It asks:

> **Could a proportionate pre-concealment assurance model improve residential waterproofing reliability and accountability without requiring another universal inspection stage?**

---

# 1. The problem being examined

Waterproofing should not be treated solely as membrane application.

Reliable wet-area performance can depend on conditions established before the membrane installer begins work, including:

**framing**

→ **substrate and sheet support**

→ **plumbing and drainage interfaces**

→ **falls and containment geometry**

→ **junctions and movement conditions**

→ **surface preparation**

→ **waterproofing installation**

→ **following trades**

→ **concealment**

Failures may therefore originate in work controlled by different practitioners at different points in the construction sequence.

Once waterproofing and its supporting construction are concealed by tiles, screeds, linings or finishes, important evidence may be difficult or impossible to reconstruct without destructive investigation.

The policy issue is consequently broader than:

> **Was waterproofing installed?**

The relevant assurance questions may instead be:

1. **Was the wet-area assembly ready to receive waterproofing?**
2. **Was the selected waterproofing system appropriately installed?**
3. **Was sufficient evidence available before the work was concealed?**
4. **Were higher-risk conditions identified and escalated appropriately?**
5. **Can responsibility later be reconstructed if failure occurs?**

---

# 2. Existing Victorian controls remain important

This paper does not assume that the current system is ineffective.

Existing controls include:

* NCC and referenced technical requirements;
* builder responsibilities;
* existing practitioner registration pathways;
* plumbing responsibilities;
* manufacturer installation requirements;
* building-surveyor functions where applicable;
* additional inspections where required;
* BPC inspection, audit and enforcement powers;
* contractual responsibilities;
* consumer-protection and defect-rectification mechanisms.

The reform question is whether these controls leave a material assurance gap around **concealed wet-area construction and the interfaces between practitioners**.

---

# 3. Candidate Waterproofing Readiness Hold Point

One option warranting evaluation is a limited **Waterproofing Readiness Hold Point** immediately before membrane installation.

Its purpose would be to establish whether the visible and measurable wet-area assembly is sufficiently ready for the selected waterproofing system.

Potential readiness considerations include:

* framing stability and support;
* suitability and fixing of substrates;
* plumbing penetrations and wastes;
* falls and drainage geometry;
* thresholds and water-containment details;
* junctions and movement conditions;
* surface cleanliness and moisture condition;
* compatibility with the specified waterproofing system.

The readiness decision could use defined states:

**PROCEED**

**PROCEED_WITH_CONTROL**

**INVESTIGATE**

**REFER**

**ESCALATE**

**STOP**

A competent practitioner refusing to waterproof an unsuitable substrate should be treated as exercising appropriate professional judgement rather than creating an unnecessary construction delay.

### Important boundary

A readiness acceptance must **not** become a broad compliance certificate.

The person accepting readiness should not automatically certify:

* concealed structural work;
* plumbing compliance outside their scope;
* design matters outside their competence;
* unseen construction;
* or the future performance of the complete building.

Responsibility should remain with the practitioner responsible for the underlying work.

---

# 4. Candidate evidence chain

A limited assurance record could preserve information at critical stages.

## Stage A — Wet-area readiness

Potential evidence:

* location and wet-area identification;
* framing/substrate condition;
* relevant measurements;
* penetrations and wastes;
* falls and containment;
* junction conditions;
* surface preparation;
* unresolved conditions.

## Stage B — Waterproofing installation

Potential evidence:

* responsible practitioner;
* waterproofing system and manufacturer;
* primer and compatible components;
* junction and penetration treatment;
* waterstops;
* membrane application stages;
* relevant measurements;
* deviations;
* cure requirements;
* manufacturer clarification where required.

## Stage C — Pre-concealment

Before tiling or permanent covering:

> **Is there sufficient evidence and verification to permit concealment?**

Photographs may form part of the record, but:

**photographic evidence does not prove compliance;**

**product documentation does not prove correct installation;**

**a practitioner declaration does not equal independent verification;**

and

**missing evidence must increase uncertainty rather than improve the assurance result.**

---

# 5. Risk-based verification

A further question is whether independent verification should be universal or targeted.

Potential risk triggers for stronger verification could include:

* balconies or external waterproofing;
* previous water-ingress failure;
* uncertain substrates;
* significant movement or cracking;
* complex junctions;
* unusual penetration density;
* occupied areas below;
* material system deviations;
* incomplete or contradictory evidence;
* adverse practitioner compliance history.

These are **research hypotheses only**.

They should not become regulatory triggers unless evidence demonstrates that they reliably identify higher-risk installations.

A central consultation question is:

> **Does universal evidence combined with targeted verification provide materially similar or better consumer protection than universal additional physical inspection, with lower unnecessary burden?**

---

# 6. Practitioner capability is a separate question

The assurance architecture should not predetermine who may undertake waterproofing.

Potential competent practitioners may come from different occupational backgrounds.

Capability should be determined through evidence of:

* technical application knowledge;
* substrate assessment;
* movement and junction understanding;
* drainage/interface understanding;
* system compatibility;
* sequencing;
* evidence creation;
* professional judgement;
* boundary recognition;
* ability to stop and escalate inappropriate work.

Experience alone should not establish competency.

Likewise, creating a new practitioner category should not be assumed to solve installation-evidence or interface problems.

The questions should remain separate:

> **What capability is required?**

and

> **What regulatory mechanism, if any, should recognise that capability?**

---

# 7. What this paper is not proposing

This consultation paper does **not** propose that Victoria has already established a need for:

* a new waterproofing licence;
* a new statutory practitioner category;
* universal independent waterproofing inspection;
* mandatory digital evidence for every project;
* automatic waterproofing authority for Registered Residential Carpenters;
* or a particular regulatory amendment.

The appropriate mechanism remains open.

Possible outcomes include:

* stronger use of existing controls;
* improved guidance and enforcement;
* standardised evidence;
* risk-triggered verification;
* reform of an existing practitioner pathway;
* competency recognition;
* a controlled pilot;
* another government-selected mechanism;
* or no additional intervention if evidence does not justify one.

---

# 8. Proposed next step

Before recommending regulatory change, Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria proposes further technical and policy validation of the assurance concept.

The immediate work should determine:

1. which defects originate before waterproofing begins;
2. which conditions can genuinely be observed or measured before concealment;
3. which conditions cannot reliably be verified;
4. which practitioner controls each condition;
5. where responsibility currently transfers between practitioners;
6. which evidence has genuine assurance value;
7. which risks justify independent verification;
8. the likely cost and construction impact of the process;
9. whether existing BPC powers could already support parts of the model; and
10. whether a controlled comparative pilot would provide useful government decision evidence.

---

# Consultation Questions

Stakeholders are invited to consider:

### 1.

Does Victoria currently have a material assurance gap between completion of preceding wet-area construction and commencement of waterproofing?

### 2.

Would a narrowly defined Waterproofing Readiness Hold Point improve defect prevention, or merely add another administrative step?

### 3.

Who should have authority to refuse commencement of waterproofing where preceding work is unsuitable?

### 4.

What evidence has genuine value before waterproofing and wet-area construction become concealed?

### 5.

Which waterproofing installations genuinely require independent physical verification?

### 6.

Can objective risk triggers reliably distinguish higher-risk work?

### 7.

Would standardised evidence improve later defect investigation and responsibility reconstruction?

### 8.

Could the proposed process distort or transfer existing builder, installer, designer, plumber or surveyor responsibilities?

### 9.

Could existing regulatory powers achieve the required outcome without new legislation?

### 10.

What evidence should government require before considering any broader regulatory intervention?

---

# Provisional Position

There is sufficient evidence to continue investigating whether a **Waterproofing Readiness → Installation Evidence → Pre-Concealment Assurance** architecture could provide a proportionate response to concealed wet-area risk.

There is **not yet sufficient evidence to prescribe the final regulatory mechanism**.

The objective should remain:

> **Make competent waterproofing work more reliable, critical concealed conditions more visible, responsibility more reconstructable, and independent intervention stronger only where evidence and risk justify it.**
