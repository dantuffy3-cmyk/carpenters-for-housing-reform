# CWIG-01 — Pre-Pilot Governance Corrections Sprint v0.1

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria  
**Status:** Working Draft — Controlled Pre-Pilot Governance Sprint v0.1  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Purpose:** Implement the bounded governance corrections identified through CWIG-IAR-001 before the CWIG-01 pilot can proceed to Participant Information & Consent development, specialist advice, Pilot Readiness Review and a formal GO / HOLD decision for CWIG-PILOT-001.

---

## 1. Purpose

CWIG-01 has progressed through:

- policy framing;
- technical verification;
- pilot instrument development;
- Pilot Record Form v0.1;
- adversarial simulation;
- Pilot Record Form v0.2;
- targeted adversarial simulation;
- assessor-calibration development;
- field-pilot protocol development;
- pilot evidence-register development;
- independent pre-pilot assurance review;
- independent-review reconciliation;
- CWIG-01 Scope & Limitation Statement;
- Pre-Application Status Reconfirmation Record.

The independent review did not identify a fundamental defect requiring redesign of the six-domain CWIG architecture.

It did identify operational and governance controls that should be resolved before a real field pilot begins.

This sprint implements those remaining bounded controls.

---

## 2. Controlling Question

> **Can the remaining governance risks identified through independent review be controlled without expanding CWIG beyond its six-domain waterproofing-interface function or creating unsupported technical, statutory or practitioner requirements?**

The objective is not to make CWIG appear ready.

The objective is to determine whether the governance architecture can become sufficiently controlled to justify a later Pilot Readiness Review.

---

This sprint may establish:

- hard non-PROCEED rules;
- PROCEED WITH CONTROL limitations;
- responsibility-boundary controls;
- uncertainty classification controls;
- referral and escalation governance;
- practitioner competence boundaries;
- material-change notification controls;

- independent-review rules;

This sprint must not:

- add a seventh CWI domain;

- invent universal technical tolerances;
- create a statutory inspection;
- create a certification scheme;

- imply government endorsement;
- pre-determine that CWIG should succeed.

# PART A — ARCHITECTURE FREEZE

## 4. Six-Domain Architecture Retained

The CWIG-01 domains remain:

1. **CWI-01 — Substrate Acceptance**
2. **CWI-02 — Water-Management Geometry**
3. **CWI-03 — Drainage and Penetration Interface**
4. **CWI-04 — Junction and Movement Treatability**
5. **CWI-05 — Surface and Moisture Acceptance**
6. **CWI-06 — Waterproofing System Compatibility**

No additional domain is created by this sprint.

---

## 5. Existing Gate Decisions Retained

CWIG gate decisions remain:

- **PROCEED**
- **PROCEED WITH CONTROL**
- **INVESTIGATE**
- **REFER**
- **ESCALATE**
- **STOP**

These remain separate from record-status controls:

- **VALID**
- **REASSESSMENT REQUIRED**
- **SUPERSEDED**

and separate again from pilot-level governance status:

- **GO**
- **HOLD**
- **PAUSE**
- **TERMINATE**

# PART B — HARD NON-PROCEED RULES

## 6. Purpose

The independent review identified a risk that commercial pressure or inconsistent judgement could convert a materially unresolved condition into a positive gate outcome.

The appropriate response is a deterministic governance boundary.

CWIG does not require a new universal technical STOP threshold table.

Instead:

> **Where a condition necessary to the waterproofing decision remains materially unresolved, a positive CWIG decision is prohibited.**

---

## 7. Hard Non-PROCEED Conditions

Neither:

**PROCEED**

nor:

**PROCEED WITH CONTROL**

may be recorded while any of the following remains material to the waterproofing decision:

- a CWI finding of **NOT ACCEPTABLE**;
- `UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`;
- an unresolved **INVESTIGATE** action;
- an unresolved **REFER** action;
- an unresolved **ESCALATE** action;
- a material waterproofing-system incompatibility;
- the waterproofing system has not been sufficiently identified;
- an applicable technical basis necessary to the decision cannot reasonably be established;
- contradictory technical information remains materially unresolved;
- CWIG record status is `REASSESSMENT REQUIRED`;
- the practitioner cannot make a defensible decision within their reasonable competence.

---

## 8. Commercial Pressure Override Prohibited

The hard non-PROCEED rule cannot be overridden because:

- the builder instructs work to proceed;
- the owner requests work to proceed;
- a tiler or following trade is booked;
- project delay will result;
- financial cost will result;
- the waterproofing practitioner is under program pressure;
- another participant disagrees with the CWIG finding.

Such pressure may become pilot evidence.

It does not change the current material condition.

---

## 9. Technical Basis Remains External to CWIG

The hard non-PROCEED rule does not create technical thresholds.

Whether a condition is acceptable must still be determined from the applicable:

- NCC pathway;
- Australian Standard where applicable;
- manufacturer system requirement;
- approved project documentation;
- Performance Solution where applicable;
- competent technical determination.

CWIG governs the decision process.

It does not create a replacement technical standard.

---

# PART C — PROCEED WITH CONTROL

## 10. Purpose

`PROCEED WITH CONTROL` must not become a general "near enough" category.

It is permitted only where the relevant technical issue is sufficiently resolved and the remaining control is specific, documented and capable of verification.

---

## 11. PROCEED WITH CONTROL Conditions

`PROCEED WITH CONTROL` may only be used where:

- the applicable technical basis is sufficiently established;
- no material uncertainty remains unresolved;
- the condition remains within reasonable practitioner competence;
- the required control is explicitly identified;
- the control is documented before waterproofing proceeds;
- responsibility for implementing the control is identified where relevant;
- the control can reasonably be verified;
- no material REFER remains unresolved;
- no material INVESTIGATE remains unresolved;
- no material ESCALATE remains unresolved;
- no known incompatibility remains unresolved;
- CWIG status is not `REASSESSMENT REQUIRED`.

---

## 12. Prohibited Use

`PROCEED WITH CONTROL` must not be used merely to avoid:

- STOP;
- REFER;
- INVESTIGATE;
- ESCALATE;
- reassessment;
- project delay.

---

## 13. No Numerical Control Limit

This sprint does not establish an arbitrary maximum number of concurrent controls.

The number of controls is not a reliable measure of materiality.

One unresolved material control may prohibit proceeding.

Several minor, resolved and verifiable controls may remain proportionate.

The pilot will record control frequency and burden so this proposition can later be tested against field evidence.

---

# PART D — RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARY

## 14. CWI-01 — Substrate as Presented

The controlling CWI-01 rule is:

> **The practitioner assesses whether the substrate as presented is suitable for installation of the identified waterproofing system.**

The practitioner does not certify:

- framing design;
- framing construction;
- structural adequacy;
- concealed fixings;
- lining installation compliance;
- plumbing compliance;
- work performed by another practitioner.

---

## 15. Originating Responsibility vs Decision Relevance

CWIG separates two questions:

### Question 1

Who is responsible for the work or condition?

### Question 2

Is that condition material to the waterproofing decision?

These questions are not interchangeable.

A condition originating in another practitioner's work can be material to CWIG without transferring responsibility for that work to the waterproofing practitioner.

---

# PART E — UNRESOLVED VS OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE

## 16. UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION

Use where:

> the practitioner cannot make a defensible waterproofing-interface decision without the condition being resolved or clarified.

The condition may relate to:

- another trade;
- design;
- structure;
- plumbing;
- manufacturer information;
- technical documentation.

Its origin does not determine whether it is material.

---

## 17. NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE

Use where:

- the matter is outside reasonable CWIG scope or competence;
- it does not need to be verified to make the present waterproofing-interface decision;
- no positive conclusion is inferred from the missing verification.

---

## 18. Conversion Rule

> **A matter cannot remain NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE where resolving that matter becomes necessary to determine whether waterproofing should proceed.**

Where that occurs, use:

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

or:

- INVESTIGATE;
- REFER;
- ESCALATE;

as appropriate.

---

## 19. Disagreement About Classification

Where a builder or other participant disputes an assessor's classification:

- preserve the assessor's original finding;
- record the disagreement;
- do not change the finding merely because disagreement exists;
- use REFER / ESCALATE where appropriate;
- reassess only where new evidence or competent technical information justifies change.

Disagreement itself is pilot evidence.

# PART G — ESCALATE

## 22. Escalation Governance

The pilot coordinator manages the escalation pathway.

The pilot coordinator is not automatically the technical decision-maker.

Depending on the issue, competent escalation may involve:

- manufacturer technical support;
- designer;
- structural engineer;
- relevant building surveyor;
- plumber;
- other appropriately competent practitioner;
- appropriate authority where genuinely necessary.

---

## 23. Escalation Outcome

Record:

- condition;
- why escalation was required;
- person/role consulted;
- evidence supplied;
- response;
- technical basis;
- effect on CWIG;
- whether reassessment is required.

Waterproofing does not proceed merely because an escalation has been initiated.

The material question must be sufficiently resolved.

---

# PART H — COMPETENCE CEILING

## 24. Practitioner Competence Boundary

CWIG does not require the participating practitioner to resolve matters beyond their reasonable competence.

Where the practitioner cannot identify and apply an appropriate technical basis within their reasonable competence:

> **do not invent the answer.**

Use:

- INVESTIGATE;
- REFER;
- ESCALATE;

as appropriate.

---

## 25. Examples of Potential Competence-Boundary Conditions

Examples may include:

- unusual geometry;
- unresolved Performance Solution;
- conflicting design documentation;
- structural determination;
- specialist plumbing determination;
- unclear product/system compatibility;
- manufacturer details that cannot be reconciled with the presented condition.

These examples do not automatically require referral in every case.

The controlling issue is whether the practitioner can make a defensible interface decision within their competence.

---

# PART I — MATERIAL CHANGE NOTIFICATION

## 26. Pilot Participation Control

Participants should be informed that known material changes occurring after CWIG and before membrane application should be disclosed.

Potential changes include:

- waterproofing system/component substitution;
- substrate alteration;
- changed geometry;
- changed waterstop;
- changed waste/drain;
- new penetration;
- changed penetration;
- damage;
- contamination;
- moisture event;
- changed junction;
- contradictory technical information.

---

## 27. Change Does Not Automatically Require Full Reassessment

The affected CWI domain or domains should be reassessed proportionately.

A complete six-domain reassessment is not automatically required unless the change materially affects the whole assessment.

---

## 28. Pre-Application Reconfirmation

The operational control is:

**CWIG-01 Pre-Application Status Reconfirmation Record v0.1**

Where material change or material inability to confirm exists:

> **REASSESSMENT REQUIRED**

before waterproofing proceeds under the earlier CWIG decision.

---

# PART J — ASSESSOR CALIBRATION AND DRIFT

## 29. Initial Calibration

Before participating independently in the controlled pilot, an assessor should be familiar with:

- the six CWI domains;
- evidence states;
- gate decisions;
- record-status controls;
- Scope & Limitation Statement;
- UNRESOLVED / OUTSIDE SCOPE distinction;
- responsibility boundaries;
- relevant field forms.

---

## 30. Drift Control

Calibration should not be assumed permanent.

Review calibration:

- before or at commencement of the pilot;
- after early workflow-debugging cases where practical;
- where repeated interpretation disagreement emerges;
- where an assessor appears consistently more lenient or strict than comparable reviewers;
- before expansion beyond the first cohort.

Calibration review is not intended to force artificial agreement.

It is intended to identify whether differences arise from:

- evidence;
- technical basis;
- terminology;
- competence;
- professional judgement;
- material decision disagreement.

---

# PART K — CASE SELECTION

## 31. Selection Principle

Cases must not be selected merely because they are expected to support CWIG.

The cohort should include reasonable variation within the approved initial scope.

---

## 32. Candidate Case Matrix

Seek reasonable variation across:

| Characteristic | Desired Variation |
|---|---|
| Project type | Bathroom / ensuite / shower area |
| Work type | Renovation / new work |
| Shower configuration | More than one legitimate configuration where available |
| Substrate | More than one legitimate substrate where available |
| Waterproofing system | More than one legitimate system where practical |
| Drainage interface | Reasonable variation |
| Complexity | Ordinary + moderately challenging in-scope conditions |
| Practitioner | More than one practitioner where practical |

---

## 33. No Predetermined Outcomes

Do not recruit cases because they are known in advance to produce:

- STOP;
- REFER;
- INVESTIGATE;
- PROCEED.

Pilot outcomes must remain evidence, not selection criteria.

---

## 34. Straightforward Cases Required

Ordinary, apparently straightforward projects are valuable.

CWIG must be tested on conditions where it may:

- add clear value;
- add little value;
- duplicate existing supervision.

That comparison is necessary to test proportionality.

---

# PART L — INDEPENDENT REVIEW

## 35. Review Purpose

Independent review tests:

- reproducibility;
- technical-basis selection;
- interpretation;
- responsibility-boundary consistency;
- material decision consistency.

It is not a statutory inspection.

---

## 36. Independent Completion

Where an independent comparison occurs, the reviewer should complete their assessment without first seeing the primary assessor's final gate decision where practical.

Record separately:

- evidence;
- technical basis;
- CWI findings;
- gate decision;
- uncertainty classification.

Only then compare results.

---

## 37. Agreement Categories

Use:

- **FULL AGREEMENT**
- **SUBSTANTIAL AGREEMENT**
- **MATERIAL DISAGREEMENT**
- **TECHNICAL-BASIS DISAGREEMENT**

---

## 38. Material PROCEED / STOP Disagreement

Where substantially the same evidence produces:

**PROCEED**

versus:

**STOP**

the case requires:

> **MANDATORY TECHNICAL / GOVERNANCE REVIEW**

before that case is relied upon as supporting evidence for CWIG.

---

## 39. Initial Review Sampling Rule

For the first 5–10 case cohort:

- independent review of all cases is preferred where genuinely practical;
- if full duplicate review is not practical, the minimum review sample must be fixed before Pilot 001 is selected;
- cases involving material disagreement, unusual technical basis or material adverse conditions should receive priority where additional review is possible.

The sampling method must not be changed after outcomes become known merely to improve results.

---

# PART M — ADVERSE EVIDENCE

## 40. Append-Only Rule

> **Once a material adverse finding is entered into the controlled pilot record, it must not be overwritten or deleted merely because the condition is later corrected.**

Later correction creates a linked later entry.

Preserve:

**condition**

↓

**evidence**

↓

**decision**

↓

**intervention**

↓

**correction**

↓

**reassessment**

↓

**final current decision**

---

## 41. Adverse Evidence Includes

- NOT ACCEPTABLE;
- STOP;
- REFER;
- ESCALATE;
- material INVESTIGATE;
- unresolved conditions;
- assessor disagreement;
- responsibility disputes;
- commercial pressure;
- material change;
- missed condition;
- participant criticism;
- excessive administrative burden;
- pilot governance event;
- evidence that CWIG added no observable value.

---

## 42. Field Record vs Evidence Register

The roles are:

### CWIG Pilot Record Form

Primary field source record.

### CWIG Pilot Evidence Register

Controlled governance/research summary and cross-case comparison record.

Where possible, the Evidence Register should reference source evidence rather than requiring unnecessary duplicate transcription.

# PART N — AUTOMATIC PILOT HOLD TRIGGERS

## 43. Purpose

Some events must stop progression to the next pilot case until review occurs.

The HOLD is a governance control.

It is not automatically evidence that CWIG has failed.

---

## 44. Automatic HOLD Events

The controlled pilot enters mandatory HOLD/review where:

### HOLD-01

Waterproofing proceeds despite a current unresolved **STOP** decision.

### HOLD-02

Waterproofing proceeds while:

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

remains unresolved.

### HOLD-03

An independent reviewer and primary assessor reach a material:

**PROCEED versus STOP**

difference on substantially the same evidence.

### HOLD-04

Repeated field evidence shows practitioners cannot reliably distinguish:

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

from:

`NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE`.

### HOLD-05

A material privacy or evidence-integrity failure occurs.

### HOLD-06

A material adverse finding is intentionally deleted, overwritten or omitted from the controlled pilot evidence.

### HOLD-07

CWIG materially causes the waterproofing practitioner to be treated as certifier of upstream work despite the stated responsibility boundary.

### HOLD-08

Material conditions that should reasonably have been identifiable within the CWIG interface are repeatedly missed.

### HOLD-09

A participant safety, governance or evidence-integrity event creates credible doubt that continuing the next case would produce trustworthy evidence.

---

## 45. Ordinary STOP Is Not Automatic Pilot Failure

A properly recorded STOP may indicate that CWIG is functioning.

Therefore:

**CASE STOP**

does not automatically equal:

**PILOT HOLD**

unless the case exposes one of the material governance conditions above.

---

## 46. HOLD Review

Before the next case begins after an automatic HOLD:

record:

- trigger;
- affected case;
- immediate control;
- technical review if required;
- governance review;
- document impact;
- whether correction is possible;
- decision:
  - RESUME;
  - MODIFY;
  - NARROW;
  - CONTINUE HOLD;
  - TERMINATE.

---

# PART O — PILOT TERMINATION FRAMEWORK

## 47. Termination Principle

A pilot that demonstrates CWIG is unreliable, disproportionate or responsibility-distorting must be capable of ending.

Continuation is not the default outcome.

---

## 48. Termination Should Be Considered Where

Evidence establishes that:

- practitioners cannot consistently operate within the CWIG responsibility boundary;
- material positive decisions remain unreliable despite reasonable calibration;
- waterproofing repeatedly proceeds despite unresolved material conditions;
- technical-basis selection cannot be made reproducibly;
- CWIG systematically creates responsibility confusion;
- adverse evidence cannot be reliably preserved;
- evidence integrity cannot be maintained;
- the administrative burden is clearly disproportionate to observable assurance value;
- material safety/governance risks cannot be corrected proportionately;
- a simpler assurance control appears capable of providing equivalent value.

---

## 49. Immediate Suspension

Where an event creates an immediate material safety or evidence-integrity concern, pilot activity may be suspended before a final termination decision is made.

---

# PART P — PREREGISTERED EVALUATION QUESTIONS

## 50. Purpose

The pilot must not decide what "success" means after results are known.

The following questions are frozen before Pilot 001 case selection.

---

## 51. Preregistered Evaluation Questions

1. Can the six-domain CWIG assessment be completed in a practical pre-waterproofing workflow by a competent participating practitioner?

2. Can practitioners distinguish:

   `UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

   from:

   `NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE`

   without case-by-case coaching from the pilot coordinator?

3. Do STOP, REFER, INVESTIGATE and ESCALATE decisions operate as intended?

4. Can PROCEED WITH CONTROL be used without becoming a general "near enough" pathway?

5. Can the technical basis for material decisions be reconstructed from the evidence record?

6. Does pre-application status reconfirmation identify material change after the original CWIG assessment?

7. Do independent assessors reach substantially similar practical outcomes from substantially similar evidence?

8. Where assessors disagree, can the cause be classified as:

   - evidence disagreement;
   - technical-basis disagreement;
   - terminology disagreement;
   - professional judgement difference;
   - material decision disagreement?

9. Does CWIG remain within its waterproofing-interface responsibility boundary?

10. Does CWIG identify material interface conditions that change project action before membrane installation?

11. Does CWIG impose proportionate administrative and time burden?

12. Does CWIG substantially duplicate existing supervision without providing additional observable assurance value?

13. Does CWIG improve or worsen responsibility clarity?

14. Are adverse findings preserved and reconstructable?

15. Are material conditions later identified that CWIG reasonably should have detected?

16. Is the Pilot Evidence Register sufficient to reconstruct:

   **condition → evidence → decision → intervention → correction → reassessment**?

17. Does the evidence support:

   - retaining CWIG;
   - narrowing CWIG;
   - modifying CWIG;
   - pausing CWIG;
   - abandoning CWIG?

---

## 52. No Predetermined Success Threshold

The first cohort is not statistically powered to establish industry-wide defect reduction.

Do not create unsupported claims such as:

> CWIG succeeds if it prevents X% of defects.

The pilot should report what was actually observed.

# PART W — FALSIFICATION CONTROL

## 64. Evidence Against CWIG Must Remain Actionable

The pilot must remain capable of demonstrating that:

- CWIG is unnecessary;
- CWIG is duplicative;
- CWIG is too burdensome;
- CWIG creates responsibility confusion;
- CWIG is not reproducible;
- CWIG misses material conditions;
- a simpler mechanism is preferable;
- the model should be narrowed or abandoned.

Suppressing such evidence would be.

---

## 65. Governance Success Does Not Equal Policy Success

Completion of these controls establishes only that:

> CWIG has a more governed basis for controlled pilot testing.

It does not establish that:

- CWIG works;
- CWIG reduces defects;
- CWIG should become mandatory;
- CWIG should become statutory;
- CWIG should be adopted by government;
- CWIG should become an industry standard.

Those propositions require later evidence.
