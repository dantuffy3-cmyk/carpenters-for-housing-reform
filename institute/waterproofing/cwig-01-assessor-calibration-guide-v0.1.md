# CWIG-01 — Assessor Calibration Guide v0.1

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria  
**Status:** Working Draft — Assessor Calibration Guide v0.1  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Purpose:** Support consistent application of the CWIG-01 Pilot Record Form v0.2 during controlled field-pilot preparation. This guide does not create new CWIG domains, technical requirements, statutory responsibilities or regulatory authority.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate (CWIG-01) is designed to test whether the observable and reasonably measurable waterproofing interfaces presented to a practitioner are suitable for installation of the identified waterproofing system.

The Pilot Record Form v0.2 establishes the six Critical Waterproofing Interface checks:

1. **CWI-01 — Substrate Acceptance**
2. **CWI-02 — Water-Management Geometry**
3. **CWI-03 — Drainage and Penetration Interface**
4. **CWI-04 — Junction and Movement Treatability**
5. **CWI-05 — Surface and Moisture Acceptance**
6. **CWI-06 — Waterproofing System Compatibility**

The targeted adversarial simulation of v0.2 found no further field-form redesign presently justified before controlled field-pilot preparation.

However, it identified an important remaining question:

> **Can different competent practitioners assess substantially similar conditions and reach substantially similar CWIG decisions?**

This guide is intended to support testing of that question.

It is a calibration document.

It is not a new compliance standard.

---

## 2. Calibration Objective

CWIG does not require every practitioner to use identical words or exercise identical professional judgement.

The objective is narrower:

> **Substantially similar material conditions should ordinarily lead competent practitioners to substantially similar gate outcomes when using the same technical basis and waterproofing system.**

Calibration should improve:

- interpretation of CWIG terminology;
- distinction between material uncertainty and matters outside CWIG scope;
- use of STOP, REFER, INVESTIGATE and ESCALATE;
- identification of the applicable technical/configuration basis;
- recognition of material change requiring reassessment;
- consistency in evidence recording;
- protection of practitioner responsibility boundaries.

Calibration must not remove legitimate professional judgement.

---

## 3. What This Guide Does Not Do

This guide does not:

- create additional CWI domains;
- create new statutory inspection stages;
- certify bathroom compliance;
- certify structural compliance;
- certify plumbing compliance;
- certify concealed framing;
- replace the National Construction Code;
- replace applicable Australian Standards;
- replace manufacturer installation requirements;
- replace project-specific technical documentation;
- replace design responsibility;
- replace appropriate building-surveyor functions;
- replace engineering or plumbing functions;
- establish government endorsement;
- establish a new practitioner registration or licensing category;
- prove that CWIG reduces waterproofing failures.

Where the applicable technical requirement cannot be reliably established, the assessor should not invent one.

---

## 4. Controlling CWIG Principle

The controlling field question remains:

> **Has the waterproofing practitioner been presented with an interface on which the identified waterproofing system can responsibly proceed?**

The practitioner is not being asked:

> Is the entire bathroom compliant?

The practitioner is not being asked:

> Is every preceding trade's work compliant?

The practitioner is being asked to assess the waterproofing interface that is actually presented.

---

# PART A — CORE CALIBRATION TERMS

## 5. ACCEPTABLE

Use **ACCEPTABLE** where:

- the condition is sufficiently observable or measurable;
- the relevant technical/system basis has been established where required;
- no material incompatibility is identified;
- no unresolved condition prevents reliable waterproofing;
- the condition falls within reasonable CWIG assessment scope.

An ACCEPTABLE finding does not mean:

- the complete bathroom is compliant;
- hidden work has been certified;
- future waterproofing performance is guaranteed.

### Example

The exposed substrate is identified, visibly stable, free from material defects affecting membrane installation, and supported by the selected waterproofing system documentation.

**Finding:** ACCEPTABLE

---

## 6. ACCEPTABLE WITH CONTROL

Use **ACCEPTABLE WITH CONTROL** where:

- the interface can remain suitable;
- a specific control is required;
- the control can be documented;
- the condition does not require another practitioner to resolve a material defect before waterproofing;
- the control does not rely on an unsupported assumption.

The required control must be recorded.

### Example

A minor surface-preparation requirement remains that can be completed and verified immediately before membrane application without redesign or external referral.

**Finding:** ACCEPTABLE WITH CONTROL

Potential gate outcome:

**PROCEED WITH CONTROL**

### Calibration Warning

Do not use ACCEPTABLE WITH CONTROL as a general pathway for:

- uncertainty;
- incomplete evidence;
- unresolved incompatibility;
- material geometry defects;
- unknown technical requirements;
- project-program pressure.

It is not a "close enough" category.

---

## 7. NOT ACCEPTABLE

Use **NOT ACCEPTABLE** where an observed or measured condition is presently unsuitable for reliable waterproofing installation.

Examples may include:

- materially contaminated substrate;
- reverse fall where the applicable detail requires drainage toward the waste;
- damaged substrate affecting membrane installation;
- a drain interface that cannot receive the identified waterproofing detail;
- an incompatible selected system;
- unresolved geometry preventing the documented waterproofing arrangement.

A NOT ACCEPTABLE finding will ordinarily lead to:

- STOP;
- REFER;
- or another non-positive gate decision.

### Domain-Specific Finding Language — CWI-04

CWI-04 (Junction and Movement Treatability) uses **TREATABLE** and **TREATABLE WITH CONTROL** in place of ACCEPTABLE and ACCEPTABLE WITH CONTROL. These are domain-specific labels for the same conceptual positions:

| CWI-04 finding | Core-term equivalent |
|---|---|
| TREATABLE | ACCEPTABLE |
| TREATABLE WITH CONTROL | ACCEPTABLE WITH CONTROL |

This terminology is intentional. The CWI-04 domain question concerns whether a junction *can be treated* using a documented detail; the word "treatable" directly reflects that question. The gateway logic and record-status rules are unchanged.

TREATABLE and TREATABLE WITH CONTROL are **domain finding states**, not gate decision states. The gate decision states remain PROCEED / PROCEED WITH CONTROL / INVESTIGATE / REFER / ESCALATE / STOP throughout all six CWI domains.

---

## 8. UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION

Use:

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

where information is necessary to determine whether waterproofing can responsibly proceed but has not been reliably established.

This state must not support final **PROCEED**.

### Example

The practitioner cannot establish whether the selected membrane system is compatible with the presented substrate.

That is not merely outside the practitioner's scope.

It is material to the waterproofing decision.

**Correct classification:**

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

Likely action:

**INVESTIGATE / REFER / ESCALATE / STOP**

depending on the issue.

---

## 9. NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE

Use:

`NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE`

where the matter:

- has not been certified;
- falls outside the defined CWIG practitioner acceptance boundary;
- is not necessary to determine suitability of the presented waterproofing interface.

### Example

Wall lining is installed.

The practitioner cannot verify every concealed stud, nogging or fixing.

The exposed substrate is stable and the selected waterproofing system can otherwise be reliably assessed.

The practitioner may record:

`HIDDEN FRAMING — NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE`

This does not mean the hidden framing complies.

It means CWIG has not certified it.

### Critical Calibration Rule

If the apparently outside-scope matter becomes necessary to determine waterproofing suitability, it must move to:

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

The category `NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE` must never be used to bypass a material unknown.

---

# PART B — GATE DECISION CALIBRATION

## 10. PROCEED

Use **PROCEED** only where:

- all material CWI conditions permit waterproofing to proceed;
- no STOP remains;
- no material REFER remains unresolved;
- no material INVESTIGATE remains unresolved;
- no material ESCALATE remains unresolved;
- no `UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION` remains;
- applicable controls and evidence are complete;
- the identified waterproofing system remains the system assessed.

### Calibration Rule

PROCEED means:

> Waterproofing may proceed on the interface assessed.

It does not mean:

> The bathroom is certified compliant.

---

## 11. PROCEED WITH CONTROL

Use **PROCEED WITH CONTROL** only where:

- a clearly identified control exists;
- the control is capable of implementation;
- the control is recorded;
- the condition does not require unresolved technical interpretation;
- the condition does not require another practitioner to correct material work before proceeding.

Record:

- condition;
- required control;
- evidence of control;
- responsibility pathway where relevant;
- whether reassessment is needed.

---

## 12. INVESTIGATE

Use **INVESTIGATE** where further evidence is required before a reliable CWIG decision can be reached.

Examples:

- substrate identity uncertain;
- manufacturer requirement not yet confirmed;
- technical/configuration basis unclear;
- moisture requirement is material but not established;
- evidence conflicts;
- a change may have occurred but has not been confirmed.

INVESTIGATE is an information state.

It is not automatically a finding that another practitioner has performed defective work.

---

## 13. REFER

Use **REFER** where the issue can be identified within CWIG but requires another appropriate practitioner or project party to resolve it.

Examples:

- plumbing interface requires alteration;
- project drawing requires clarification;
- substrate repair belongs with another trade;
- project controller must coordinate a correction.

### Example

The waste/interface cannot receive the selected waterproofing detail.

CWIG does not need to conclude:

> Plumbing is non-compliant.

It may conclude:

> The presented waterproofing-to-drain interface is not presently suitable.

Then:

**REFER**

to the appropriate pathway.

---

## 14. ESCALATE

Use **ESCALATE** where the issue requires higher-order technical or decision authority.

Potential pathways may include:

- manufacturer technical support;
- designer;
- engineer;
- relevant building surveyor;
- another appropriately competent technical practitioner;
- regulatory clarification where relevant.

Typical escalation conditions include:

- unusual geometry;
- conflicting technical sources;
- undocumented system deviation;
- Performance Solution issue;
- unclear design responsibility;
- condition beyond reasonable waterproofing competence.

---

## 15. STOP

Use **STOP** where waterproofing should not proceed in the present condition.

Examples:

- material surface contamination;
- incompatible system;
- required interface cannot be formed;
- unresolved condition materially affects waterproofing reliability;
- material geometry issue prevents the documented arrangement;
- reassessment is required after material change.

STOP does not necessarily mean:

> A legal breach has been proved.

STOP means:

> Waterproofing should not responsibly proceed on the interface presently presented.

---

# PART C — TECHNICAL BASIS CALIBRATION

## 16. Configuration Must Be Identified Before Geometry Is Judged

CWI-02 requires the reasoning chain:

> **configuration → applicable technical detail → measurement → decision**

Assessors must not begin with a remembered dimension and work backwards.

First identify the actual configuration.

Potential configurations may include, where applicable:

- enclosed shower;
- unenclosed shower;
- hob;
- step-down;
- level threshold;
- preformed shower base;
- other identified project configuration.

---

## 17. Technical Source Hierarchy for the Pilot

CWIG does not create a universal hierarchy replacing statutory interpretation.

For calibration purposes, the assessor should record the source actually relied upon.

Possible sources include:

- applicable NCC provision;
- applicable Australian Standard pathway;
- approved project drawing;
- approved Performance Solution;
- manufacturer/system documentation;
- other competent technical direction.

The field record must distinguish:

> **what the project drawing shows**

from:

> **what establishes the applicable technical requirement.**

A drawing should not automatically be treated as proof that the detail is technically correct.

---

## 18. Conflicting Sources

Where two relevant sources appear inconsistent:

### Do not:

- choose whichever is easier;
- average the requirements;
- invent a hybrid detail;
- proceed because the project program requires it.

### Instead:

Use:

**INVESTIGATE**

**REFER**

or

**ESCALATE**

depending on the issue.

Record:

- the conflicting sources;
- what cannot be resolved;
- who is asked to determine the correct pathway.

---

# PART D — CWI DOMAIN CALIBRATION

## 19. CWI-01 — Substrate Acceptance

### Core Question

> **Is the exposed substrate suitable for reliable installation of the identified waterproofing system?**

### Clearly Acceptable Example

- substrate identified;
- exposed surface stable;
- no material damage;
- no loose material;
- selected system supports the substrate;
- no material visible defect prevents membrane installation.

**Finding:** ACCEPTABLE

### Clearly Not Acceptable Example

The lining is visibly damaged, loose or deteriorated in a way that prevents reliable membrane application.

**Finding:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**Likely action:** STOP / REFER

### Material Uncertainty Example

The substrate cannot be reliably identified and the membrane system has different requirements depending on substrate type.

**Finding:**

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

**Likely action:** INVESTIGATE

### Outside-Scope Example

The practitioner cannot see all concealed framing after wall lining has been installed, but that information is not required to establish suitability of the exposed waterproofing interface.

**Record:**

`NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE`

Do not convert this into assumed framing compliance.

---

## 20. CWI-02 — Water-Management Geometry

### Core Question

> **Is the visible and measurable geometry capable of providing the required water-management arrangement for the identified configuration?**

### Clearly Acceptable Example

- shower configuration identified;
- applicable technical detail recorded;
- relevant measurements taken;
- geometry matches the identified detail;
- no material drainage conflict exists.

**Finding:** ACCEPTABLE

### Clearly Not Acceptable Example

A reverse fall exists where water must drain toward the waste under the identified arrangement.

**Finding:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**Likely action:** STOP

### Material Uncertainty Example

The assessor cannot reliably determine whether the shower is to be treated as the configuration selected in the record.

**Finding:**

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

**Likely action:** INVESTIGATE / REFER

### Calibration Warning

Accurate measurement does not correct an incorrect technical basis.

A perfectly measured dimension against the wrong shower configuration remains an unreliable decision.

---

## 21. CWI-03 — Drainage and Penetration Interface

### Core Question

> **Can the selected waterproofing system form a reliable interface with the wastes and penetrations presented?**

### Clearly Acceptable Example

- waste/interface visible;
- documented waterproofing-to-drain connection identified;
- sufficient access exists;
- penetrations can receive the selected system's documented treatment.

**Finding:** ACCEPTABLE

### Clearly Not Acceptable Example

The presented waste/interface prevents formation of the identified membrane connection.

**Finding:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**Gate action:** REFER / STOP

### Responsibility Calibration

Correct statement:

> The presented waterproofing interface cannot presently receive the identified waterproofing detail.

Avoid:

> The plumber has installed the waste illegally.

CWIG does not require that broader conclusion.

### Material Unknown Example

The practitioner cannot determine which drain-interface treatment is compatible with the selected waterproofing system.

**Finding:**

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

---

## 22. CWI-04 — Junction and Movement Treatability

### Core Question

> **Can the relevant visible junctions, transitions and movement locations be treated using a documented detail for the selected waterproofing system?**

### Clearly Treatable Example

Each relevant junction has an identified treatment within the selected system.

**Finding:** TREATABLE

### Treatable With Control Example

A specific documented preparation/control is required and can be completed before waterproofing.

**Finding:** TREATABLE WITH CONTROL

### Unresolved Example

An unusual substrate transition exists and no documented system detail can be identified.

**Finding:**

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

**Likely action:** INVESTIGATE / ESCALATE

### Calibration Warning

Do not improvise a junction treatment merely because a project is ready for waterproofing.

---

## 23. CWI-05 — Surface and Moisture Acceptance

### Core Question

> **Is the exposed surface in a condition that permits reliable installation of the selected waterproofing system?**

### Clearly Acceptable Example

- surface clean;
- no material dust;
- no material contamination;
- preparation requirements satisfied;
- moisture condition meets applicable system requirement where measurement is necessary.

**Finding:** ACCEPTABLE

### Clearly Not Acceptable Example

Significant plaster dust remains on the waterproofing substrate.

**Finding:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**Gate action:** STOP

Correct sequence:

**STOP**

↓

clean/prepare

↓

re-present

↓

reassess

### Material Uncertainty Example

The manufacturer requires a substrate-moisture condition to be established, but the relevant criterion or reliable measurement has not been obtained.

**Finding:**

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

Do not assume the substrate is acceptable because it appears dry.

---

## 24. CWI-06 — Waterproofing System Compatibility

### Core Question

> **Has a documented waterproofing system been identified that is compatible with the conditions and components actually presented?**

### Clearly Resolved Example

The assessor has identified:

- substrate;
- membrane;
- preparation/primer where required;
- junction treatment;
- drainage detail;
- penetration treatment;
- relevant overlay compatibility;
- application requirements.

No unresolved material incompatibility remains.

**Finding:** COMPATIBILITY RESOLVED

### Clearly Not Acceptable Example

The selected membrane is not supported for the identified substrate/system condition.

**Finding:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

### Material Uncertainty Example

Manufacturer information cannot establish whether the proposed components are compatible.

**Finding:**

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

**Likely action:** INVESTIGATE / ESCALATE

---

# PART E — RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARY CALIBRATION

## 25. Condition Versus Fault

CWIG should identify the condition before allocating blame.

Example:

`DRAIN_INTERFACE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE`

is preferable to:

`PLUMBER_ERROR`

unless a separate process with appropriate authority determines responsibility.

This keeps the instrument focused on:

- condition;
- consequence for waterproofing;
- required decision;
- correction pathway.

---

## 26. Upstream Work

The waterproofing practitioner may encounter conditions originating from:

- carpentry;
- plumbing;
- lining;
- screeding;
- design;
- builder coordination;
- another preceding activity.

CWIG may identify that the interface cannot proceed.

It does not automatically transfer responsibility for the originating work to the waterproofing practitioner.

---

## 27. Hidden Conditions

Assessors should not imply knowledge they do not have.

Where hidden work is outside the gate:

`NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE`

Where the hidden information is actually necessary to determine waterproofing suitability:

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

and the issue must be resolved before a positive gate outcome.

---

# PART F — RECORD STATUS CALIBRATION

## 28. VALID

`VALID` means:

> The assessment remains the current assessment of the materially unchanged interface and identified waterproofing system.

It does not mean:

> The gate decision is positive.

A record may correctly show:

**ASSESSMENT STATUS:** VALID

**GATE DECISION:** STOP

That means the STOP assessment remains current.

---

## 29. REASSESSMENT REQUIRED

Use:

`REASSESSMENT REQUIRED`

where a material condition upon which the assessment relied has changed.

Examples include:

- membrane product changed;
- substrate materially altered;
- waste changed;
- penetration moved;
- waterstop changed;
- geometry altered;
- significant damage;
- significant contamination;
- significant moisture event;
- relevant junction altered;
- new contradictory evidence.

The previous gate decision must not be relied upon until the affected domain has been reassessed.

---

## 30. SUPERSEDED

Use:

`SUPERSEDED`

where a later controlled CWIG record replaces the earlier assessment as the current assessment.

A superseded record must remain preserved.

Do not delete:

- original STOP findings;
- referrals;
- rectification history;
- previous evidence;
- prior assessment decisions.

---

## 31. Pre-Application Reconfirmation

The targeted v0.2 simulation identified a limitation that no form can independently solve:

> A practitioner may not know that another person changed the interface after the original assessment.

The Field Pilot Protocol should therefore include a pre-application reconfirmation.

Suggested pilot wording:

### Pre-Application Status Reconfirmation

Before membrane application:

- [ ] No material change is known to have occurred since the recorded CWIG assessment.
- [ ] A material change has been identified — reassessment required.

**Date:**  
**Time:**  
**Practitioner:**  
**Affected CWI domain if known:**  

This is an operational pilot control.

It is not an additional CWI domain.

# PART G — CALIBRATION SCENARIOS

## 32. Scenario A — Clean, Straightforward Shower

### Condition

All six CWI domains are adequately evidenced and acceptable.

No material unresolved condition remains.

### Expected Outcome

**CWI findings:** acceptable/resolved

**Gate decision:** PROCEED

### Calibration Objective

Practitioners should not create unnecessary STOP or referral decisions where the interface is genuinely suitable.

---

## 33. Scenario B — Plaster Dust

### Condition

Substantial sanding dust remains on the waterproofing substrate.

### Expected Outcome

**CWI-05:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**Gate decision:** STOP

### Incorrect Outcome

PROCEED WITH CONTROL while intending to membrane over the existing contaminated surface.

### Calibration Objective

Recognise a directly observable condition that should be corrected before waterproofing.

---

## 34. Scenario C — Hidden Framing

### Condition

Wall lining is installed.

Complete hidden framing cannot be inspected.

Visible substrate is stable.

System suitability can otherwise be established.

### Expected Outcome

Hidden framing:

`NOT VERIFIED — OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE`

CWIG may still reach PROCEED if all material interface conditions are resolved.

### Incorrect Outcome

Automatically STOP solely because hidden framing cannot be certified.

### Calibration Objective

Protect the practitioner boundary without treating missing upstream certification as positive evidence.

---

## 35. Scenario D — Hidden Information Becomes Material

### Condition

The practitioner cannot establish whether an apparently concealed upstream condition affects the suitability of the presented substrate.

### Expected Outcome

`UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL TO CWIG DECISION`

**Gate decision:** INVESTIGATE / REFER / ESCALATE as appropriate.

### Incorrect Outcome

Classify the matter as OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE solely because another trade originally performed the work.

### Calibration Objective

Distinguish trade responsibility from decision relevance.

---

## 36. Scenario E — Drain Interface Conflict

### Condition

The selected waterproofing system cannot form its documented connection to the waste/interface presented.

### Expected Outcome

**CWI-03:** NOT ACCEPTABLE

**Gate decision:** REFER / STOP

### Correct Practitioner Language

> The presented waterproofing-to-drain interface is not currently suitable for the identified waterproofing system.

### Calibration Objective

Avoid unnecessary plumbing-compliance conclusions.

---

## 37. Scenario F — Wrong Technical Configuration Selected

### Condition

Measurements are accurate, but the assessor selected the wrong shower configuration and therefore the wrong technical detail.

### Expected Review Finding

The CWI-02 decision is unreliable.

### Appropriate Action

INVESTIGATE / reassess technical basis.

### Calibration Objective

Demonstrate why:

**configuration → technical detail → measurement → decision**

must remain intact.

---

## 38. Scenario G — Drawing Conflicts With Technical Basis

### Condition

The project drawing shows one detail, but the assessor identifies a potential conflict with the applicable technical pathway.

### Expected Outcome

**INVESTIGATE / REFER / ESCALATE**

### Incorrect Outcome

Treat the drawing alone as conclusive evidence that the construction is technically acceptable.

### Calibration Objective

Distinguish project documentation from technical authority.

---

## 39. Scenario H — Product Substitution

### Condition

CWIG reaches PROCEED for Waterproofing System A.

Before membrane application, System B is substituted.

### Expected Outcome

**CWIG STATUS:** REASSESSMENT REQUIRED

Affected CWI domains are reassessed.

### Incorrect Outcome

Continue using the previous PROCEED decision.

---

## 40. Scenario I — Penetration Added After CWIG

### Condition

A new plumbing penetration is created after CWIG assessment but before waterproofing.

### Expected Outcome

**CWIG STATUS:** REASSESSMENT REQUIRED

CWI-03 and any other affected domain are reassessed.

### Calibration Objective

Recognise that a gate decision applies to the interface actually assessed.

---

## 41. Scenario J — Valid STOP Record

### Condition

A current CWIG assessment has not changed.

Its gate decision is STOP.

### Correct Record

**STATUS:** VALID  
**GATE DECISION:** STOP

### Calibration Objective

Ensure practitioners do not confuse record validity with technical approval.

# PART H — CALIBRATION EXERCISE METHOD

## 42. Independent Assessment Exercise

For calibration testing, two or more assessors should independently review the same controlled scenario without seeing each other's decision.

Each assessor records:

- CWI finding;
- evidence basis;
- gate action;
- uncertainty classification;
- technical source used;
- responsibility boundary;
- record status where applicable.

Only after independent completion should results be compared.

---

## 43. Agreement Categories

For pilot research, reviewer agreement may be classified:

### FULL AGREEMENT

Assessors reach materially the same:

- CWI finding;
- gate decision;
- responsibility boundary;
- uncertainty classification.

### SUBSTANTIAL AGREEMENT

Minor wording or evidence differences exist, but the practical outcome is the same.

Example:

Both assessors STOP waterproofing, although one selects REFER and the other INVESTIGATE before referral.

### MATERIAL DISAGREEMENT

Assessors reach materially different practical outcomes.

Example:

Assessor A: PROCEED

Assessor B: STOP

### TECHNICAL-BASIS DISAGREEMENT

Assessors disagree because they selected different applicable technical/configuration bases.

This should be recorded separately because it may indicate:

- training/calibration issue;
- unclear project documentation;
- genuine technical ambiguity.

## 45. Material Disagreement Review

Where assessors materially disagree, record:

1. what condition they interpreted differently;
2. whether they used the same evidence;
3. whether they used the same technical basis;
4. whether terminology caused the difference;
5. whether professional judgement reasonably explains the difference;
6. whether additional calibration guidance is justified;
7. whether the field form itself contains an ambiguity;
8. whether the issue should be referred for technical clarification.

Do not automatically rewrite CWIG after one disagreement.

Look for repeat patterns.

# PART I — PILOT CALIBRATION METRICS

## 46. Calibration Metrics

The controlled pilot should capture:

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Assessor agreement rate | Reproducibility |
| PROCEED vs STOP disagreement | High-consequence inconsistency |
| REFER/INVESTIGATE disagreement | Boundary interpretation |
| Technical-basis disagreement | Configuration/source calibration |
| Material vs outside-scope uncertainty disagreement | Evidence-governance calibration |
| Time to complete assessment | Burden |
| Number of escalations | Complexity |
| Number of calibration questions raised | Guide usability |
| Repeated ambiguous terms | Guide refinement |
| Repeated irrelevant guidance | Simplification opportunity |

---

## 47. No Premature Pass Threshold

Calibration v0.1 should not invent an arbitrary pass threshold such as:

> 90% agreement means CWIG is validated.

Early pilot evidence should first establish:

- where disagreement occurs;
- whether disagreement is material;
- whether the cause is terminology, technical basis, evidence quality or legitimate professional judgement.

Any future numerical threshold should require a separate reasoned decision.

---

# PART J — ESCALATION CALIBRATION

## 48. When an Assessor Should Not Decide Alone

An assessor should not force a CWIG determination where the matter:

- exceeds reasonable waterproofing competence;
- requires structural interpretation;
- requires plumbing certification;
- requires design change;
- involves conflicting technical requirements;
- involves an unusual Performance Solution;
- requires manufacturer interpretation beyond published documentation;
- requires regulatory or building-surveyor determination.

The purpose of ESCALATE is to preserve safe decision boundaries.

---

## 49. Escalation Is Not Failure

A competent practitioner who identifies that a matter exceeds their authority or expertise may be demonstrating stronger judgement than a practitioner who proceeds without resolving it.

Calibration should therefore not treat:

**ESCALATE**

as automatically worse than:

**PROCEED**

The quality of the decision depends on the condition presented.

---

# PART K — ASSURANCE AGAINST COMMERCIAL PRESSURE

## 50. Project Schedule Must Not Override the Gate

One recurring practical risk is pressure to continue because:

- tiler is booked;
- builder expects waterproofing today;
- project is delayed;
- client wants progress;
- materials are already on site.

Calibration should reinforce:

> **A project schedule is not evidence that the waterproofing interface is suitable.**

If a material CWIG condition is unresolved:

use the appropriate:

- INVESTIGATE;
- REFER;
- ESCALATE;
- STOP

state.

---

## 51. Appropriate Refusal as Competence Evidence

During the pilot, an appropriate decision not to proceed should be captured as meaningful practitioner evidence.

Examples:

- identifying an incompatible substrate;
- refusing undocumented improvisation;
- stopping because the drain interface cannot be waterproofed;
- escalating an unclear technical detail;
- requiring reassessment after material change.

The pilot should test whether CWIG supports appropriate refusal without creating unnecessary commercial conflict.

---

# PART L — CALIBRATION LIMITATIONS

## 52. What Calibration Cannot Prove

Successful assessor calibration does not prove that:

- CWIG prevents leaks;
- CWIG reduces defects;
- every assessor will always agree;
- a gate pass guarantees membrane performance;
- practitioner judgement can be fully standardised;
- the model should become mandatory;
- government should adopt CWIG;
- the proposed assurance architecture is superior to every alternative.

Calibration tests decision consistency.

Field evidence must test practical value.

---

## 53. Falsification Questions

The calibration exercise should actively look for evidence that:

- assessors cannot interpret the six CWI domains consistently;
- outside-scope classification is being abused;
- material uncertainty is frequently misclassified;
- PROCEED WITH CONTROL is being used as a workaround;
- STOP thresholds vary excessively;
- technical-basis selection is unreliable;
- the guide creates excessive burden;
- practitioner boundaries remain unclear;
- a simpler calibration approach would be sufficient.

Adverse findings must be preserved.

# PART M — CALIBRATION DECISION FRAMEWORK

## 54. Possible Calibration Outcomes

Following initial calibration testing, classify the guide:

### CALIBRATION EFFECTIVE

The guide materially improves consistency without excessive burden.

### CALIBRATION EFFECTIVE WITH REFINEMENT

The framework is workable but particular terms/examples require improvement.

### CALIBRATION INSUFFICIENT

Material disagreement remains too high or too poorly understood.

### FIELD FORM AMBIGUITY IDENTIFIED

The disagreement appears to arise from a defect in CWIG v0.2 rather than assessor application.

### TECHNICAL BASIS PROBLEM IDENTIFIED

The disagreement arises from uncertainty in technical/configuration interpretation.

### INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

Not enough assessor comparisons have been completed.

## 55. Change Control

This guide should not trigger automatic changes to CWIG-01 Pilot Record Form v0.2.

A form change should require evidence of:

- repeated material ambiguity;
- responsibility-boundary failure;
- recurrent unsafe interpretation;
- technical defect;
- or another material weakness.

Minor assessor preference should not expand the field form.

## 59. Current Position

CWIG-01 Pilot Record Form v0.2 has passed targeted adversarial simulation sufficiently to progress to controlled field-pilot preparation.

This proposition remains to be tested.
