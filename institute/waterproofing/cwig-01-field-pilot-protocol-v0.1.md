# CWIG-01 — Field Pilot Protocol v0.1

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria  
**Status:** Working Draft — Controlled Field Pilot Protocol v0.1  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Purpose:** Define the controlled real-world test environment for CWIG-01 Pilot Record Form v0.2. This protocol governs pilot eligibility, assessor roles, timing, evidence capture, change notification, reassessment, independent comparison, privacy, adverse findings and pilot evaluation. It does not establish a statutory inspection regime, government-endorsed process or final regulatory mechanism.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate (CWIG-01) has progressed through:

- policy framing;
- technical verification;
- adversarial simulation;
- Pilot Record Form v0.1;
- Pilot Record Form v0.2;
- targeted adversarial simulation;
- assessor-calibration development.

The next question is no longer primarily:

> **Can CWIG be designed coherently?**

The next question is:

> **Can competent practitioners use CWIG-01 on real residential bathroom projects in a way that is practical, reproducible, proportionate and capable of identifying material pre-waterproofing interface conditions without transferring responsibility for upstream work?**

This protocol defines the environment in which that question should be tested.

---

## 2. Pilot Status

The pilot is:

**CONTROLLED**

**VOLUNTARY**

**EVIDENCE-GATHERING**

**NON-STATUTORY**

**LIMITED IN SCOPE**

**SUBJECT TO REVISION OR TERMINATION**

The pilot is not:

- a statutory inspection stage;
- a building approval;
- a waterproofing certificate;
- a plumbing certificate;
- a structural inspection;
- a government-endorsed process;
- a mandatory industry requirement;
- evidence that CWIG reduces defects;
- evidence that CWIG should become law;
- a substitute for existing practitioner responsibilities.

A failed pilot is a legitimate outcome.

---

## 3. Controlling Pilot Question

The controlling pilot question is:

> **Does CWIG-01 add enough practical assurance at the pre-waterproofing interface, at proportionate burden and within reasonable practitioner competence, to justify further development?**

The pilot should test rather than assume:

- intervention value;
- assessor consistency;
- practical usability;
- responsibility clarity;
- administrative burden;
- evidence quality;
- reassessment effectiveness;
- technical-basis traceability.

---

# PART A — INITIAL PILOT POPULATION

## 4. Initial Pilot Scope

The first controlled pilot should be deliberately narrow.

### Included

Class 1 residential:

- bathrooms containing a shower;
- ensuites containing a shower;
- dedicated shower areas.

### Excluded From Initial Pilot

- laundries without showers;
- Class 2–9 wet areas;
- external balconies;
- terraces;
- podiums;
- below-ground waterproofing;
- swimming pools;
- highly unusual wet areas;
- work relying on complex Performance Solutions unless separately approved for research;
- projects where the technical basis cannot reasonably be established;
- projects where the waterproofing interface is already concealed.

Exclusion from the first pilot does not determine the ultimate possible scope of CWIG.

---

## 5. Pilot Cohort Size

The first field cohort should remain small enough to allow detailed review.

### Recommended Initial Cohort

**5–10 real bathroom/ensuite/shower projects**

The purpose is not statistical proof.

The purpose is to identify:

- practical failure points;
- assessor disagreement;
- workflow burden;
- terminology problems;
- responsibility conflict;
- missing or unnecessary fields;
- evidence-capture weaknesses.

Further expansion should depend on what the first cohort shows.

---

## 6. Project Selection Principles

Pilot projects should include reasonable variation where possible.

Examples:

- straightforward enclosed shower;
- level-threshold shower;
- hobbed shower;
- preformed shower-base system;
- renovation with existing-condition complexity;
- new wet-area construction;
- different legitimate substrates;
- different legitimate membrane systems;
- different plumbing/waste interfaces.

Do not deliberately create unsafe or defective work for pilot purposes.

The pilot should observe real project conditions as they arise.

---

# PART B — PARTICIPANT ROLES

## 7. Waterproofing Practitioner

The practitioner completing CWIG should be reasonably competent to assess the waterproofing interface within their existing professional scope.

The pilot should record:

- name;
- role;
- relevant qualifications/registration where applicable;
- relevant waterproofing experience;
- selected waterproofing system familiarity;
- whether the practitioner has completed CWIG calibration material.

Participation in the pilot does not give a practitioner broader statutory authority.

---

## 8. Project Controller / Builder

Where applicable, the builder or project controller should understand that CWIG may identify a condition requiring:

- investigation;
- referral;
- correction;
- reassessment;
- STOP.

The project controller is not required to accept that CWIG determines legal liability.

CWIG identifies the condition and its effect on the waterproofing decision.

Responsibility remains governed by actual:

- practitioner roles;
- contracts;
- statutory requirements;
- project circumstances.

---

## 9. Upstream Practitioners

The project may involve:

- carpenter;
- plumber;
- plasterer/liner;
- screeder;
- tiler;
- designer;
- engineer;
- other specialist.

CWIG may identify an interface condition associated with upstream work.

This does not make the waterproofing practitioner the certifier of the originating trade.

---

## 10. Independent Reviewer

Where practicable, selected pilot cases should receive an independent comparison assessment.

The independent reviewer should:

- not see the original assessor's final gate decision before completing their own assessment;
- use the same available evidence where practical;
- identify their own technical basis;
- record CWI findings independently;
- record uncertainty classification;
- record gate action;
- record responsibility boundary.

The reviewer is testing reproducibility.

They are not automatically acting as a statutory inspector.

---

## 11. Pilot Coordinator

The pilot should have a defined coordinator responsible for:

- allocating record IDs;
- confirming eligibility;
- controlling document versions;
- preserving evidence;
- separating original and review decisions;
- maintaining the Pilot Evidence Register;
- recording adverse events;
- preventing records from being retrospectively sanitised;
- coordinating pilot evaluation.

The coordinator should not alter practitioner findings merely to improve pilot results.

---

# PART C — PARTICIPANT INFORMATION AND CONSENT

## 12. Participant Information

Before participation, relevant participants should understand:

- CWIG is experimental;
- participation is voluntary;
- the pilot is not government-endorsed;
- CWIG does not replace statutory responsibilities;
- findings may identify work that should not proceed;
- STOP or referral may cause project delay;
- pilot evidence will be used for research/evaluation;
- adverse findings will be preserved;
- participation does not guarantee a positive outcome.

---

## 13. Owner / Client Awareness

Where project information or photographs concern a private residence, the owner/client should be informed that:

- evidence may be captured for the controlled pilot;
- personal information should be minimised;
- identifiable photographs should be avoided where unnecessary;
- pilot records may be de-identified for policy/research analysis;
- participation can be withdrawn from future research use subject to any legitimate record-retention requirements already agreed.

A separate consent template may be developed if pilot implementation requires it.

---

## 14. Practitioner Consent

Participating practitioners should acknowledge that:

- their CWIG decisions may be independently compared;
- disagreement is not automatically treated as poor practice;
- disagreement will be analysed for calibration purposes;
- adverse decisions are valuable pilot evidence;
- the purpose is to test the instrument, not rank practitioners.

---

# PART D — PILOT TIMING

## 15. When CWIG Occurs

CWIG-01 should occur:

> **after the relevant waterproofing interface has been presented sufficiently for assessment, but before waterproofing membrane installation begins.**

The interface must be sufficiently exposed to permit meaningful assessment.

CWIG should not be performed retrospectively after the membrane has concealed the interface.

---

## 16. Pre-CWIG Conditions

Before assessment, confirm where applicable:

- waterproofing system identified;
- relevant substrate visible;
- shower configuration identifiable;
- waste/interface presented;
- penetrations presented;
- relevant junctions visible;
- project technical basis available to the extent necessary;
- no membrane application has commenced.

If these conditions cannot be met:

**INVESTIGATE / REFER / OUTSIDE PILOT**

as appropriate.

---

## 17. Pilot Workflow

The standard pilot sequence is:

**PROJECT IDENTIFIED**

**ELIGIBILITY CONFIRMED**

↓

**PARTICIPANT INFORMATION / CONSENT**

↓

**CWIG RECORD ID ASSIGNED**

↓

**APPLICABLE TECHNICAL BASIS IDENTIFIED**

↓

**WATERPROOFING SYSTEM IDENTIFIED**

↓

**CWI-01 TO CWI-06 ASSESSED**

↓

**CWIG GATE DECISION**

↓

**STOP / INVESTIGATE / REFER / ESCALATE**

↓

**CORRECTION OR ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE**

↓

**RE-PRESENTATION**

↓

**REASSESSMENT**

↓

**PRE-APPLICATION STATUS RECONFIRMATION**

↓

**WATERPROOFING MAY PROCEED IF CURRENT GATE DECISION ALLOWS**

↓

**PILOT RECORD CLOSED OR CARRIED FORWARD**

# PART F — PRE-APPLICATION RECONFIRMATION

## 18. Purpose

The targeted adversarial simulation identified an operational limitation:

> A recorded CWIG decision cannot react to a material change that occurs after assessment unless that change is detected or disclosed.

Therefore a pre-application status reconfirmation is required for the pilot.

---

## 19. Pre-Application Status Reconfirmation

Immediately before membrane application, record:

### Condition Confirmation

- [ ] No material change is known to have occurred since the current CWIG assessment.
- [ ] A material change has occurred or may have occurred.
- [ ] Unable to confirm whether material change has occurred.

### If Material Change Is Identified or Suspected

**CWIG STATUS = REASSESSMENT REQUIRED**

until the relevant condition has been reassessed.

### Record

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| CWIG Record ID | |
| Date | |
| Time | |
| Practitioner | |
| Material change known? | Yes / No / Uncertain |
| Affected CWI domain(s) | |
| Reassessment required? | Yes / No |
| Notes | |

---

# PART G — CHANGE NOTIFICATION

## 20. Material Change

A material change includes a change that could affect the basis upon which CWIG was completed.

Examples include:

- membrane product substitution;
- substrate replacement;
- substrate repair;
- contamination;
- moisture event;
- change to falls;
- change to waterstop;
- change to threshold;
- change to waste;
- penetration added;
- penetration moved;
- junction altered;
- interface damaged;
- contradictory new technical information.

---

## 21. Change Notification Principle

Where a participant knows that a material interface condition has changed after CWIG assessment and before membrane application, the change should be brought to the attention of the waterproofing practitioner or pilot coordinator.

The pilot should test whether this is practical.

It should not assume perfect notification.

---

## 22. Unreported Change

If a material change is discovered only later:

- preserve the original CWIG record;
- identify when the change was discovered;
- record whether the change was known or reasonably detectable;
- assess whether the change process failed;
- record the event as pilot evidence.

Do not retrospectively alter the original assessment to make it appear the change had been known.

---

# PART H — REASSESSMENT

## 23. Reassessment Scope

A material change does not automatically require the entire CWIG record to be repeated.

Reassess the CWI domain or domains materially affected.

Example:

A new penetration is created.

Likely reassessment:

**CWI-03**

and any other domain actually affected.

This keeps the system proportionate.

---

## 24. Reassessment Record

Record:

- reason;
- affected domain;
- original finding;
- changed condition;
- new evidence;
- reassessment finding;
- gate decision;
- status;
- date/time.

Earlier evidence must remain preserved.

---

# PART I — INDEPENDENT ASSESSOR COMPARISON

## 25. Selection of Comparison Cases

Not every pilot project necessarily requires duplicate assessment.

Select enough cases to test:

- straightforward conditions;
- material uncertainty;
- STOP;
- REFER;
- technical-basis selection;
- unusual interface;
- out-of-scope classification.

---

## 26. Blind Comparison Where Practical

The independent reviewer should, where practical:

- not see the original assessor's final decision;
- assess the same evidence;
- record their own technical source;
- complete their own CWI findings.

After completion:

compare both records.

---

## 27. Agreement Classification

Use the calibration-guide classifications:

### FULL AGREEMENT

Materially the same result.

### SUBSTANTIAL AGREEMENT

Minor differences but same practical outcome.

### MATERIAL DISAGREEMENT

Different practical gate outcomes.

Example:

**PROCEED** versus **STOP**

### TECHNICAL-BASIS DISAGREEMENT

Different outcomes caused by different configuration or technical-source selection.

---

## 28. Material Disagreement Review

For each material disagreement ask:

- Did assessors have the same evidence?
- Did they identify the same configuration?
- Did they rely on the same technical basis?
- Did terminology cause the difference?
- Was one assessor outside reasonable competence?
- Was the issue genuinely open to professional judgement?
- Does the Calibration Guide require refinement?
- Does CWIG v0.2 contain a form ambiguity?
- Is further technical verification required?

Do not automatically change CWIG because two people disagree once.

Look for repeat patterns.

---

# PART J — EVIDENCE CAPTURE

## 29. Minimum Evidence

Each pilot case should ordinarily preserve:

- project ID;
- eligibility record;
- CWIG form;
- selected waterproofing system;
- relevant technical references;
- required measurements;
- relevant photographs;
- adverse findings;
- STOP/REFER/INVESTIGATE/ESCALATE records;
- rectification;
- reassessment;
- pre-application reconfirmation;
- final current status;
- practitioner feedback;
- independent-review result where undertaken.

---

## 30. Evidence Quality

Pilot evidence should be sufficient to reconstruct:

> **what condition was presented → what evidence was available → what decision was made → what happened next**

The goal is not to generate the maximum number of photographs.

The goal is decision reconstruction.

---

## 31. Adverse Evidence Preservation

Do not remove:

- failed checks;
- STOP decisions;
- referrals;
- incorrect assumptions discovered later;
- assessor disagreement;
- responsibility disputes;
- evidence gaps;
- pilot complaints;
- burdensome steps;
- examples where CWIG added no useful value.

A clean-looking pilot dataset is less valuable than an accurate one.

---

# PART K — PRIVACY AND INFORMATION CONTROL

## 32. Data Minimisation

Collect only information necessary for:

- pilot identification;
- technical assessment;
- evidence reconstruction;
- evaluation.

Avoid unnecessary:

- faces;
- personal photographs;
- unrelated rooms;
- financial information;
- private correspondence;
- owner personal information.

---

## 33. Pilot Identifiers

Where possible, research analysis should use controlled IDs such as:

`CWIG-PILOT-001`

rather than owner names.

Property addresses may be retained in controlled internal records where operationally necessary but should not automatically appear in public pilot reporting.

---

## 34. Public Evidence

No identifiable project evidence should be published merely because it formed part of the pilot.

Public reporting should ordinarily use:

- de-identified examples;
- aggregate pilot findings;
- controlled diagrams;
- anonymised scenarios.

---

# PART L — SAFETY AND EXISTING RESPONSIBILITIES

## 35. Existing Responsibilities Continue

Participation in CWIG does not suspend or replace:

- NCC obligations;
- building permit requirements;
- plumbing requirements;
- relevant Australian Standards;
- manufacturer instructions;
- builder responsibilities;
- practitioner registration requirements;
- building-surveyor functions;
- engineering requirements;
- workplace safety obligations.

---

## 36. Urgent or Serious Concern

If a practitioner encounters an issue requiring action outside the pilot:

CWIG should not prevent them from following their existing professional, contractual or statutory obligations.

The pilot is subordinate to real-world safety and compliance duties.

---

# PART M — COMMERCIAL PRESSURE

## 37. Schedule Pressure

The pilot should specifically record whether a practitioner experienced pressure to proceed because:

- tiler was booked;
- builder expected waterproofing that day;
- project was delayed;
- owner demanded progress;
- another trade was waiting;
- commercial cost would arise from STOP.

This is important pilot evidence.

---

## 38. Appropriate Refusal

Where CWIG legitimately results in:

**STOP**

**REFER**

**INVESTIGATE**

or

**ESCALATE**

the pilot should assess whether the framework:

- helped the practitioner explain the decision;
- created unnecessary conflict;
- clarified responsibility;
- increased delay;
- caused commercial pressure;
- supported appropriate refusal.

## 40. Qualitative Questions

For each practitioner ask:

- Was CWIG understandable?
- Was CWIG practical?
- Did any question duplicate normal supervision?
- Did any question fall outside reasonable competence?
- Did CWIG help explain a STOP or referral?
- Did CWIG create responsibility confusion?
- Was technical-detail traceability practical?
- Was evidence capture excessive?
- Was reassessment proportionate?
- What would you remove?
- What was missing?
- Would the form still be useful if shortened?

---

# PART O — PILOT SUCCESS AND FAILURE

## 41. No Predetermined Success Threshold

The first pilot should not invent an arbitrary rule such as:

> CWIG succeeds if it stops defects in 20% of bathrooms.

The pilot cannot know whether every intercepted condition would have become a defect.

Instead evaluate multiple dimensions.

---

## 42. Evidence Supporting Further Development

Further development may be justified if the pilot indicates that:

- practitioners can use CWIG consistently;
- meaningful interface conditions are identified before membrane;
- responsibility boundaries remain understandable;
- completion burden is proportionate;
- reassessment works;
- technical-detail traceability adds useful accountability;
- independent reviewers reach substantially similar outcomes;
- the form can be used without becoming a general bathroom inspection;
- adverse findings can be preserved without excessive conflict.

---

## 43. Evidence Supporting Narrowing or Rejection

CWIG should be narrowed, redesigned or rejected if:

- it primarily duplicates existing supervision;
- practitioners cannot use it consistently;
- STOP thresholds vary excessively;
- responsibility disputes materially increase;
- practitioners routinely assess matters outside competence;
- paperwork burden is excessive;
- technical-detail traceability is impractical;
- change/reassessment controls are unworkable;
- important interface conditions remain routinely missed;
- the gate creates false confidence;
- simpler controls provide equivalent assurance.

---

# PART P — PILOT PAUSE / STOP RULES

## 44. Pilot-Level Pause Triggers

The pilot itself should be paused for review if repeated evidence shows:

- serious responsibility ambiguity;
- unsafe interpretation;
- repeated misuse of OUTSIDE CWIG SCOPE;
- repeated PROCEED outcomes despite material uncertainty;
- consistent assessor disagreement on high-consequence decisions;
- evidence/privacy failures;
- participant complaints identifying a material governance issue;
- unexpected regulatory conflict.

---

## 45. Pilot Termination

The pilot may be terminated if evidence demonstrates that:

- CWIG cannot be applied within reasonable practitioner boundaries;
- burden is plainly disproportionate;
- the model systematically creates false confidence;
- material safety or governance risks cannot be corrected proportionately.

Termination is an acceptable research outcome.

---

# PART R — PILOT CASE CLOSURE

## 48. Case Closure

A pilot case may be closed when:

- CWIG assessment is complete;
- relevant reassessments are complete;
- pre-application status has been reconfirmed;
- evidence has been preserved;
- required feedback has been captured;
- independent comparison is complete where applicable.

Case closure does not mean the waterproofing work is guaranteed to perform.

---

## 49. Relationship to PCR-01

Where future PCR-01 testing occurs on the same project, link the records.

This will eventually allow research into:

> **Did a project that passed CWIG later reveal a material membrane-stage issue at PCR?**

That is important false-confidence evidence.

However, PCR-01 remains a separate instrument.

# PART S — FIRST COHORT DESIGN

## 50. Recommended First Cohort

For the first controlled cohort:

### Projects

**5–10 Class 1 bathroom/ensuite/shower projects**

### Practitioners

Prefer more than one waterproofing practitioner across the cohort where practical.

### Comparison

Select at least several cases for independent assessment.

### Variation

Seek reasonable variation in:

- shower configuration;
- substrate;
- membrane system;
- renovation/new work;
- simple/complex interface.

### Avoid

Do not deliberately select only known problem jobs.

The cohort should include ordinary straightforward work.

---

## 51. Suggested First Pilot Sequence

### Pilot 1–2

Use primarily for workflow debugging.

Do not immediately rewrite the architecture after minor usability problems.

### Pilot 3–5

Begin comparing:

- completion times;
- decisions;
- evidence quality;
- practitioner feedback.

### Pilot 6–10

If no material pause trigger emerges, expand comparison and calibration evidence.

After approximately 5–10 cases:

conduct a formal pilot review before expanding further.

---

# PART T — PILOT REVIEW

## 52. Pilot Review Questions

At the first formal review ask:

1. Did CWIG identify conditions that changed project action?
2. Were those conditions genuinely material to waterproofing?
3. Did the practitioner remain within reasonable competence?
4. Were upstream responsibilities preserved?
5. How long did CWIG take?
6. How often was reassessment required?
7. Did practitioners understand the uncertainty states?
8. Did reviewers reproduce decisions?
9. What caused disagreement?
10. Did CWIG create commercial conflict?
11. Did CWIG add meaningful assurance?
12. Could the same value be achieved more simply?
13. Should any CWI domain be narrowed?
14. Is any domain missing?
15. Is further pilot work justified?

---

## 53. Pilot Review Outcomes

Possible outcomes:

### CONTINUE UNCHANGED

The current pilot structure remains suitable.

### CONTINUE WITH MINOR CALIBRATION REFINEMENT

Operational/calibration changes only.

### MODIFY FIELD FORM

Evidence demonstrates a material form defect.

### NARROW PILOT

Some domains or project types should be removed.

### EXPAND PILOT

Evidence supports broader testing.

### PAUSE FOR TECHNICAL REVIEW

Technical uncertainty requires resolution.

### TERMINATE

Pilot evidence does not justify continuation.

---

# PART U — GOVERNANCE

## 54. Change Control

During the first cohort, avoid frequent uncontrolled form changes.

Any proposed change should record:

- identified problem;
- evidence;
- affected pilot cases;
- proposed correction;
- whether change affects comparability;
- whether previous records remain interpretable.

---

## 55. Version Control

All pilot instruments should retain explicit version numbers.

Example:

- CWIG Pilot Record Form v0.2
- Assessor Calibration Guide v0.1
- Field Pilot Protocol v0.1
- Pilot Evidence Register v0.1

Do not silently edit a field instrument while retaining the same version.

---

## 56. Independence of Adverse Findings

The pilot coordinator must not remove or reclassify adverse findings merely because they weaken the case for CWIG.

Evidence against the model is part of the pilot's value.

---

# PART V — RELATIONSHIP TO GOVERNMENT AND PUBLIC COMMUNICATION

## 57. No Government Attribution

The pilot must not be represented as:

- requested by government;
- approved by government;
- endorsed by the Building and Plumbing Commission;
- endorsed by the Department of Transport and Planning;

unless such status is explicitly and verifiably provided in the future.

---

## 58. Public Description

A safe public description would be:

> **Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria is independently developing and testing a proposed Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate for residential wet areas. The pilot examines whether a narrow pre-waterproofing interface check can improve evidence, sequencing and decision clarity without transferring responsibility for upstream trades. The model remains experimental and has no statutory status.**

---

# PART W — RELATIONSHIP TO THE ASSURANCE ARCHITECTURE

## 59. Architecture

The broader research architecture remains:

**UPSTREAM TRADE WORK**

↓

**UPSTREAM EVIDENCE WHERE JUSTIFIED**

↓

**CWIG-01 — CRITICAL WATERPROOFING INTERFACE GATE**

↓

**WATERPROOFING INSTALLATION**

↓

**PCR-01 — PRE-CONCEALMENT RELEASE**

↓

**TILING / FINISHES**

↓

**PMP-01 — POST-MEMBRANE PROTECTION CONTROL**

↓

**PERMANENT WATERPROOFING ASSURANCE RECORD**

The field pilot tests CWIG-01 only.

It does not validate the whole architecture.

---

## 60. Six-Domain Freeze During Initial Cohort

The six CWI domains remain:

1. CWI-01 — Substrate Acceptance
2. CWI-02 — Water-Management Geometry
3. CWI-03 — Drainage and Penetration Interface
4. CWI-04 — Junction and Movement Treatability
5. CWI-05 — Surface and Moisture Acceptance
6. CWI-06 — Waterproofing System Compatibility

No additional domain should be introduced during the first cohort without a documented material reason.
