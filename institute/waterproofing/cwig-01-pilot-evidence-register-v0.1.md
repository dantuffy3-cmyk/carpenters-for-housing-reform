# CWIG-01 — Pilot Evidence Register v0.1

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria  
**Status:** Working Draft — Controlled Pilot Evidence Register v0.1  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Purpose:** Define the structured evidence register for controlled CWIG-01 field-pilot cases. The register is designed to preserve comparable case-level findings, adverse evidence, intervention history, reassessment, assessor comparison, burden and pilot outcomes without implying statutory certification, government endorsement or proven defect reduction.

---

## 1. Purpose

The CWIG-01 field pilot requires more than individual completed forms.

A controlled register is needed so that evidence across multiple real bathroom projects can be compared consistently.

The register should allow the pilot to answer questions such as:

> **What conditions did CWIG identify?**

> **What decisions were made?**

> **Did those decisions change project action?**

> **Were adverse findings corrected before membrane installation?**

> **Did different assessors reach substantially similar conclusions?**

> **How much time and burden did CWIG add?**

> **Did the gate clarify or confuse practitioner responsibility?**

> **Were material conditions later found that CWIG had missed?**

The register must preserve evidence against CWIG as carefully as evidence supporting it.

---

## 2. Register Status

The register is:

**INTERNAL**

**CONTROLLED**

**PILOT-ONLY**

**NON-STATUTORY**

**EVIDENCE-GATHERING**

It is not:

- a government register;
- a building approval register;
- a waterproofing certification register;
- a practitioner disciplinary register;
- proof that individual work is compliant;
- proof that CWIG prevents defects;
- a public database of private residential properties.

---

## 3. Relationship to Pilot Documents

The register operates alongside:

- CWIG-01 Pilot Record Form v0.2;
- CWIG-01 Assessor Calibration Guide v0.1;
- CWIG-01 Field Pilot Protocol v0.1;
- Critical Waterproofing Interface Gate Pilot Instrument v0.1;
- CWIG-01 Worked Pilot Simulation Sprint v0.1;
- CWIG-01 Targeted Adversarial Simulation Sprint v0.2.

The register does not replace the full field record.

It records a controlled summary of each pilot case and links that summary to the underlying evidence.

---

# PART A — REGISTER PRINCIPLES

## 4. Evidence Before Advocacy

The register must not be designed to make CWIG appear successful.

It must capture:

- positive findings;
- adverse findings;
- neutral findings;
- assessor disagreement;
- participant criticism;
- excessive burden;
- missed conditions;
- responsibility disputes;
- cases where CWIG added little or no value;
- cases where the pilot should pause or stop.

A failed pilot remains a valid research result.

---

## 5. Preserve the Sequence

The register should preserve the actual decision sequence.

For example:

**09:13 — CWI-05 NOT ACCEPTABLE**

↓

**CWIG DECISION — STOP**

↓

**surface corrected**

↓

**10:02 — CWI-05 ACCEPTABLE**

↓

**CWIG DECISION — PROCEED**

The register must not reduce that history to:

> CWI-05 = PASS

That would remove the evidence of intervention.

---

## 6. No Retrospective Sanitisation

Original adverse findings must not be deleted merely because a later reassessment produces a positive result.

Earlier records may become:

`SUPERSEDED`

but must remain traceable.

The register should retain:

- original condition;
- original decision;
- correction;
- reassessment;
- current decision.

---

## 7. Minimum Personal Information

The central research register should use controlled pilot identifiers wherever possible.

Example:

`CWIG-PILOT-001`

rather than:

- homeowner name;
- full property address;
- unrelated personal details.

Where a property address is operationally necessary, it should remain in the controlled case file rather than automatically appearing in the research register.

### Gate Decision

- PROCEED
- PROCEED WITH CONTROL
- INVESTIGATE

**Gate Decision:** STOP

means the STOP decision is the current valid assessment.

## 18. Adverse Finding Interpretation

An adverse finding does not automatically mean:

- a statutory breach occurred;
- another practitioner was negligent;
- a leak would have occurred;
- CWIG prevented a defect.

The register should describe:

> **what condition was identified and what decision followed.**

## 40. Do Not Overinterpret Aggregate Numbers

Example:

> 4 STOP decisions occurred across 10 projects.

This does not prove:

> CWIG prevented four waterproofing failures.

The supported statement is narrower:

> CWIG recorded four cases in which practitioners determined that waterproofing should not proceed under the conditions presented at that time.

---

# PART U — PILOT INTERPRETATION

## 41. Evidence Supporting CWIG

Potentially supportive evidence may include:

- meaningful unsuitable conditions identified before membrane;
- consistent assessor decisions;
- proportionate completion time;
- effective reassessment;
- useful technical-detail traceability;
- clearer responsibility communication;
- practitioner support;
- successful adverse-evidence preservation.

No single metric proves the model.

---

## 42. Evidence Against CWIG

Potentially adverse evidence includes:

- frequent material assessor disagreement;
- excessive administrative burden;
- repeated responsibility confusion;
- misuse of outside-scope classification;
- missed material interface conditions;
- weak technical-basis selection;
- repeated unnecessary STOP decisions;
- minimal value beyond existing supervision;
- commercial conflict disproportionate to benefit;
- evidence process too complex to operate.

This evidence must remain visible.

---

# PART V — PILOT REVIEW STATUS

## 43. Case-Level Pilot Outcome

Each closed case may receive one research classification:

- CWIG added clear practical value;
- CWIG added some value;
- CWIG added little observable value;
- CWIG was duplicative;
- CWIG created material ambiguity;
- insufficient evidence to determine value.

This is a research classification only.

It is not a compliance rating for the bathroom.

---

## 44. Pilot-Level Outcome

Following the initial cohort, possible overall outcomes include:

- CONTINUE UNCHANGED;
- CONTINUE WITH CALIBRATION REFINEMENT;
- MODIFY FIELD FORM;
- NARROW PILOT;
- EXPAND PILOT;
- PAUSE FOR TECHNICAL REVIEW;
- TERMINATE.

The register should contain enough evidence to explain why that decision was reached.
