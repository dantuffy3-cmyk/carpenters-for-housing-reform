Our Purpose

Our Purpose

Many residential building problems become visible only after they have already caused homeowners substantial cost, delay and stress.

The Institute was established to help bridge the gap between construction practice, consumer outcomes and evidence-based policy.

Most policy research begins with systems and regulations. Our work also begins with what happens on real building sites: how work is scoped, coordinated, constructed, inspected, concealed and ultimately experienced by homeowners.

Our purpose is not simply to identify problems. It is to develop practical solutions worthy of consideration, discussion and further refinement with government, regulators, industry and consumers.

Our work begins where much policy research does not: on real building sites.

Every project begins with practical construction experience before being tested against legislation, technical standards, engineering principles and independent research.

Waterproofing Reform Model

A Practical Alternative for Waterproofing Assurance

Waterproofing failures are rarely caused by one isolated action. Performance depends on design, framing, substrates, sheeting, drainage interfaces, membrane installation, curing, tiling and protection of the completed system.

Once this work is concealed, responsibility can become difficult and expensive to establish.

The Institute is developing a trust-and-verify model built around four connected stages.

01

Wet Area Readiness

Verification that framing, substrates, sheeting, falls, wastes, thresholds and critical interfaces are ready to receive the specified waterproofing system.

02

Waterproofing Installation

Documented responsibility for primers, bond breakers, membrane selection, detailing, coverage, thickness, curing and manufacturer requirements.

03

Membrane Protection

A recorded handover confirming that following trades have not punctured, altered or compromised the completed membrane.

04

Final Wet Area Verification

A complete project record connecting construction readiness, waterproofing installation, following-trade activity and final builder responsibility.

The proposed model does not rely on government physically inspecting every bathroom. It combines accountable practitioners, mandatory digital records, targeted independent verification and risk-based regulatory auditing.

This framework remains under development and will be refined through further technical, regulatory and industry consultation.

About the Institute

About the Institute

The Residential Building Reform Institute is a research and policy initiative established by Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria.

It is grounded in practical residential construction and renovation experience and is being developed to contribute constructive, evidence-based ideas to Victoria’s building reform conversation.

The Institute does not claim to replace government, regulatory, engineering, legal, academic or industry expertise. Its role is to connect practical construction knowledge with rigorous research and policy development.

The Institute welcomes informed engagement from homeowners, builders, carpenters, waterproofing practitioners, tilers, plumbers, building surveyors, engineers, insurers, manufacturers, educators, regulators and government.
