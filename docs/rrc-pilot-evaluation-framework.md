# RRC Pilot Evaluation Framework
**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
Version: 1.1 — Working Draft
Date: August 2026
Status: WORKING DRAFT — Evaluation measures for the pilot. Specific thresholds remain to be determined during pilot design.

## Purpose

The pilot exists to test capability, not to grant new statutory authority and not to prove a predetermined legal vehicle.

Its function is to test whether independently assessed Advanced Residential Carpentry capability can improve consumer clarity, documentation, escalation discipline and boundary recognition within Victoria's existing framework.

## Primary Evaluation Questions

1. Does the pilot improve consumer understanding of what the practitioner can and cannot do?
2. Can consumers correctly identify when a builder, engineer, permit or licensed trade is required?
3. Do practitioners apply scope boundaries and escalation decisions consistently?
4. Can practitioners distinguish between proceeding within scope, investigating further, obtaining specialist input, obtaining engineering input, involving a builder, or stopping work pending permit or regulatory clarification?
5. Are work-and-evidence records complete, persistent and usable for audit?
6. Do assessors apply the capability framework consistently?
7. Does the pilot produce clearer and earlier professional handoff when concealed conditions change the risk profile?
8. Does the pilot remain within existing lawful authority and avoid implying new statutory powers?
9. Does the pilot generate evidence capable of informing a later decision about RRC, DB-L reform, an advanced credential or another mechanism?

## Escalation and Boundary Measures

Evaluation should record:

- number and type of concealed-condition events;
- number and type of stop-work decisions;
- number and type of referrals to builders, engineers, surveyors and licensed trades;
- whether escalation decisions were proportionate; and
- whether any project activity exceeded existing lawful authority.

## Success Criteria

A successful pilot would show that:

- the capability model can be assessed independently;
- consumers understand the practitioner role and its limits more clearly;
- practitioners escalate proportionately and on time;
- records are usable, consistent and auditable;
- assessors apply the model consistently; and
- the pilot does not imply or require new statutory authority in order to operate as a capability test.

## Failure Indicators

- recurring consumer misunderstanding about scope or handoff;
- repeated failure to stop and escalate when required;
- persistent gaps in the work-and-evidence record;
- inconsistent assessment outcomes without explanation;
- pilot activity drifting beyond existing lawful authority; or
- evidence showing that the model cannot be tested safely or intelligibly in its proposed form.

## Evidence Limitations

The pilot may support conclusions about operational feasibility, documentation behaviour, escalation patterns, consumer comprehension and assessor consistency within pilot conditions.

It cannot by itself establish that government should adopt RRC, and it does not by itself resolve every legal, insurance or registration-design question.
