# RRC Pilot Evaluation Framework
**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
Version: 1.0 — Working Draft
Date: July 2026
Status: WORKING DRAFT — Evaluation measures for the pilot. Specific thresholds to be determined during pilot design.

## Purpose

The pilot exists to generate evidence, not to prove a predetermined outcome. Its function is to test whether a bounded RRC model can improve consumer clarity, documentation, accountability and boundary management in residential renovation without weakening existing builder, engineer, surveyor or licensed-trade responsibilities.

## Evaluation Approach

The pilot should be evaluated through a controlled framework with clearly defined entry conditions, standardised participant obligations and pre-specified measures. Independent assessment is desirable so that findings are not based solely on participant self-reporting. A mid-pilot review should test whether the pilot settings remain safe, administratively workable and aligned with consumer protection objectives.

## Primary Evaluation Questions

1. Does the pilot improve consumer understanding of what an RRC practitioner can and cannot do?
2. Do practitioners apply scope boundaries and escalation triggers consistently?
3. Are Stage 1, Stage 2 and Stage 3 decisions documented in a usable and auditable way?
4. Does the pilot produce earlier and clearer handoff to builders, engineers, surveyors and specialists when progressive discovery changes the risk profile?
5. Are consumer complaints, defects and incidents reduced, unchanged or displaced relative to the baseline context available to the pilot?
6. Is the evidence schedule practical for participating practitioners to maintain without undermining project viability?
7. Do builders, engineers, surveyors, insurers and regulators regard the boundaries as clearer than the current informal environment?
8. Does the pilot create any unintended migration of work into categories that should remain with registered builders or other regulated professionals?
9. Is the category capable of being supervised, audited and enforced at a reasonable administrative cost?
10. Does the pilot generate enough credible evidence to justify continuation, redesign or discontinuation?

## Escalation and Handoff Measures

Evaluation should record:

- number and type of Stage 1 / Stage 2 / Stage 3 events;
- builder transfer rate;
- engineering or surveyor escalation rate;
- time from trigger to escalation completion; and
- disputes about escalation decisions.

These measures test both frequency and quality of handoff. A low escalation rate could mean the pilot is working well, or it could mean practitioners are under-escalating.

## Regulatory and Enforcement Measures

The evaluation should examine:

- complaint rate;
- defect or incident rate;
- insurance notifications;
- enforcement burden on regulator; and
- audit resource requirements.

These measures test whether the pilot is manageable from a public administration perspective. Insurance notifications should be tracked cautiously because they may indicate heightened transparency rather than heightened risk.

## Stakeholder Confidence Measures

Stakeholder confidence should be assessed through structured feedback from:

- builders on boundary clarity;
- insurers;
- building surveyors and engineers;
- regulators; and
- training organisations.

Confidence does not mean endorsement of the model as a permanent reform. It means assessing whether key participants consider the pilot intelligible, bounded and governable.

## Market Effect Measures

The evaluation should examine:

- whether informal work appears reduced, unchanged or displaced;
- whether scope creep occurs;
- whether the category is commercially workable; and
- whether participation is geographically equitable.

These measures should be interpreted with caution. A pilot will not produce a complete market map, but it may reveal whether documentation costs make participation impractical, whether the category expands beyond its original boundaries, or whether it creates a clearer lawful route for work otherwise carried out in ambiguity.

## Failure Indicators

- repeated failure to escalate when clear triggers arise;
- unresolved consumer confusion about practitioner scope;
- material increase in complaints, defects or safety incidents;
- persistent evidence gaps that prevent meaningful audit;
- scope creep into work that should remain with registered builders or other regulated professionals;
- insurer refusal patterns that make the model impractical;
- audit or enforcement burden that is disproportionate to pilot scale; and
- strong stakeholder evidence that the model is destabilising rather than clarifying professional boundaries.

## Stop, Pause and Modification Triggers

| Trigger | Response | Decision Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Serious safety incident linked to boundary failure | Immediate pause pending review | Pilot authority / regulator |
| Repeated material non-compliance by participant | Suspend participant; investigate broader settings | Pilot authority, with regulator input |
| Evidence schedule proves unworkable in practice | Modify documentation settings or narrow pilot | Pilot authority |
| Complaint or defect trend exceeds acceptable range | Pause expansion; review controls and thresholds | Pilot authority / regulator |
| Insurance or legal settings prove non-functional | Pause affected activity until resolved | Government / regulator / pilot authority |
| Clear scope creep into excluded work | Tighten scope, retrain or suspend pilot activity | Pilot authority / regulator |

## Evaluation Process

The evaluation process should run on a staged timeline:

**Baseline:** establish participant profile, pilot settings, evidence rules, disclosure model, audit method and comparison assumptions before commencement.

**Mid-pilot:** review compliance trends, escalation behaviour, complaint patterns, stakeholder concerns and any needed control changes.

**End-of-pilot:** complete file audit, stakeholder consultation, consumer outcome analysis and administrative burden assessment against the pre-specified measures.

**Recommendation:** provide a reasoned recommendation on whether the pilot should stop, continue in modified form, be repeated with better controls, or support consideration of a more formal policy option.

## Evidence Limitations

The pilot will generate limited, non-randomised evidence. It may support conclusions about operational feasibility, documentation behaviour, escalation patterns, consumer communication and stakeholder reaction within the pilot conditions.

It cannot, by itself, establish statewide market effects with certainty, prove long-term defect reduction across the full sector, or resolve every legal and insurance question that would arise in permanent implementation.
