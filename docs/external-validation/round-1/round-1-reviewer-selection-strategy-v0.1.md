## Purpose

Round 1 external review is deliberately small and challenge-focused.

It is not a broad consultation. It is not an endorsement exercise. It does not establish framework validity, regulatory need or government support.

Round 1 tests three specific and targeted questions by exposing the framework to three experienced, independent professional perspectives. The purpose is to surface credible objections, identify boundary failures and record any material weaknesses before any live recruitment or government engagement proceeds.

No outreach to reviewers, government or external parties occurs as part of this sprint.

---

## Selection Objectives

The objective of Round 1 reviewer selection is not to find people who support the Advanced Residential Carpentry framework.

The objective is to identify reviewers whose disagreement or well-reasoned criticism would materially improve the framework — or, if the criticism cannot be answered, would appropriately constrain or redirect the initiative.

A credible sceptic may be especially valuable.

Professional disagreement is not a ground for excluding a candidate. A person should not be excluded because they oppose:

- RRC or any formal recognition mechanism;
- advanced carpenter registration as a concept;
- the occupational progression proposition;
- the initiative's framing of the problem.

Reviewers are selected for the quality of their challenge perspective, their practical experience and their independence — not for their predicted conclusions.

## Three Round 1 Reviewer Perspectives

### Reviewer Perspective 1 — Experienced Residential Renovation Carpenter

**Primary question:**

Is advanced residential renovation carpentry genuinely distinguishable from ordinary qualified carpentry in real practice?

**Relevant experience:**

- Substantial residential renovation experience (existing-building work rather than primarily new-home framing)
- Direct experience with hidden conditions, concealed deterioration, alterations, structural repairs and sequencing decisions
- Experience identifying the limits of carpentry responsibility and when to refer or escalate
- Experience operating under or alongside registered builders in renovation projects

**Useful challenge perspective:**

This reviewer is positioned to challenge whether the capability framework describes a real practitioner distinction or an overstated occupational differentiation. They can test whether the seven capability domains (Technical, Diagnostic, Structural Understanding, Planning, Coordination, Professional Judgement, Boundary Recognition) reflect observable differences in practice or merely codify what any experienced carpenter should already be doing.

**Potential conflicts:**

- Current or prospective financial relationship with Sonsie Carpentry or Dan Sonsie
- Prior public endorsement of RRC or the initiative
- Commercial interest in a new recognition category (e.g., training product, assessment service)
- Role in an organisation that would administer the pathway

**Reasons a candidate may be unsuitable:**

- Experience primarily in new-home construction rather than existing-building renovation
- Independence genuinely compromised by personal or financial relationship with the proponent
- Unwilling or unable to provide independent critical assessment
- Prior public commitment to a predetermined outcome (either for or against) that cannot be declared and managed

---

### Reviewer Perspective 2 — Registered Domestic Builder

**Primary question:**

Does the framework improperly cross from advanced carpentry competence into domestic builder authority, responsibility or contracting obligations?

**Relevant experience:**

- Practical renovation management experience (existing-building projects preferred)
- Strong working understanding of domestic builder registration, authority and legal responsibility under Victorian legislation
- Experience coordinating trades, managing scope uncertainty and dealing with progressive discovery in renovation projects
- Ability to distinguish trade competence from builder responsibility — particularly in relation to structural decisions, contracting and project control

**Useful challenge perspective:**

This reviewer is positioned to test whether the capability framework inadvertently describes a builder function rather than a trade function. If the framework implies that an advanced carpenter may take on builder-like responsibilities — for scope management, structural decision-making or coordination — this reviewer should identify those boundary failures. They are not being asked to endorse the initiative. They are being asked to test whether builder registration authority remains properly protected.

**Potential conflicts:**

- Direct commercial competition with a proposed recognition category
- Membership of a builder association that has taken a position on the initiative
- Financial relationship with the proponent
- Prior public statement either supporting or opposing the RRC concept

**Reasons a candidate may be unsuitable:**

- Experience limited to new-home volume construction without renovation management
- Builder authority understanding is primarily theoretical rather than practical
- Independence compromised by relationship with proponent or competing commercial interest
- Unwilling to engage seriously with the framework's challenge question

---

### Reviewer Perspective 3 — Structural Engineer

**Primary question:**

Does the framework maintain a sufficiently strong boundary between advanced carpentry structural understanding and engineering design authority?

**Relevant experience:**

- Residential alterations and existing-building structural engineering experience (preferred over primarily new-construction)
- Practical understanding of load paths, temporary support, deterioration, structural uncertainty and referral
- Experience working alongside builders and trades in renovation environments where structural conditions are uncertain or concealed
- Understanding of when engineering authority must be invoked and what triggers that obligation

**Useful challenge perspective:**

This reviewer is not being asked to validate carpentry practice generally. They are being asked to test whether the framework's Structural Understanding capability domain and its boundary recognition requirements adequately protect engineering design authority. They should identify any language, assumption or claim in the framework that implies an advanced carpenter may substitute for an engineer, defer an engineering referral that should occur, or exercise structural design authority without authorisation.

**Potential conflicts:**

- Prior commercial relationship with the proponent or Sonsie Carpentry
- Financial interest in any training or assessment product related to the initiative
- Membership of an engineering body that has taken a position on trade scopes

**Reasons a candidate may be unsuitable:**

- Experience primarily in commercial or infrastructure engineering rather than residential
- Limited understanding of renovation environments (concealed conditions, progressive discovery)
- Independent position is compromised by relationship with proponent or by advocacy position
- Unwilling to engage with the boundary question seriously

## Reviewer Selection Qualities

All Round 1 reviewer candidates should be assessed against the following qualities:

**Relevant practical experience**
The reviewer should have direct practical experience in their professional domain as it applies to residential renovation or existing-building work. Theoretical knowledge alone is insufficient.

**Independence**
The reviewer should be free from financial, personal or advocacy relationships with Dan Sonsie, Sonsie Carpentry or Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria that would materially compromise their ability to provide independent assessment.

**Ability to challenge**
The reviewer should be capable of identifying weaknesses, boundary failures or unsupported claims in the framework and articulating those challenges clearly and specifically.

**Boundary knowledge**
The reviewer should understand the boundaries of their own profession and the boundaries of adjacent professions. Boundary recognition is a core capability domain; the reviewer should be able to test it.

**Reasoning ability**
The reviewer should be able to reason from evidence and practice rather than from general advocacy positions or predetermined conclusions.

**Evidence mindset**
The reviewer should understand the difference between observed practice and policy assertion. Reviewers who treat assertion as evidence are of limited value.

## Independence Principle

A reviewer should not be selected merely because they know Dan Sonsie, support the initiative or are likely to provide favourable feedback.

Selection based on predicted approval weakens the value of the review, creates a record that is more easily dismissed by government or regulatory bodies and may fail to surface genuine framework weaknesses before they become consequential.

At least one Round 1 reviewer should ideally be a person who is reasonably capable of disagreeing with the initiative — and who would be taken seriously if they did.

## Reviewer / Assessor Separation

**External reviewers test the framework.**

**Pilot assessors apply the framework to candidates.**

These roles should initially be treated as separate.

An external reviewer examines whether the framework is conceptually coherent, practically defensible and appropriately bounded. They are evaluating the design.

A pilot assessor evaluates individual candidates against the framework. They are applying the design.

The skills, experience and independence requirements for these two roles differ.

A reviewer may potentially become an assessor in a later phase, but only after explicit consideration of:

- any conflict of interest that arose during the review;
- the reviewer's independence position;
- whether their review role would compromise their assessor neutrality;
- whether the framework they reviewed has changed materially since their review.

This transition must not occur automatically. It requires a deliberate governance decision.

---

## Round 1 Limits

Round 1 external review does not establish any of the following:

- framework validity in a regulatory or legal sense;
- evidence of regulatory need;
- government support or interest;
- RRC adoption;
- legal feasibility of any recognition mechanism;
- statewide representativeness of practitioner views.

Round 1 establishes whether the framework survives credible independent challenge from three specific perspectives, and records the material objections that arise.

Its findings inform — but do not determine — the question of whether to proceed to subsequent validation rounds or to revise, narrow or abandon the framework.

## Candidates Generally Not Prioritised for Round 1

Round 1 should generally avoid prioritising the following categories as primary candidates:

- Close personal friends of Dan Sonsie whose independence may reasonably be questioned
- People financially dependent on Dan Sonsie or Sonsie Carpentry
- People selected primarily because they are expected to support the proposal
- People being sought mainly to provide testimonials or positive statements
- Politicians
- Journalists
- Government officials
- Department of Transport and Planning staff
- Building and Plumbing Commission staff
- Representatives of large industry associations in a representative capacity
- People whose experience is primarily unrelated new-home construction
- People with an unresolved material conflict that prevents meaningful independent challenge

These are Round 1 prioritisation controls. They are not permanent absolute exclusions. A person in one of these categories may still be considered for a later validation round if their conflict can be declared and managed, or if they bring a specific challenge perspective not otherwise available.

---

## Privacy and Record Control

Candidate screening records should use minimum necessary information.

If real individuals are entered into governance records, consideration must be given to whether the record belongs in:

- internal-only governance storage;
- a private repository;
- or a de-identified public register.

This is an unresolved implementation control. No privacy solution should be invented or assumed without explicit consideration of applicable obligations.

Do not place unnecessary personal contact details in the public repository.
