## A. Purpose of External Validation

External validation does not mean reviewers agree with the proposal.

External validation means the framework has been exposed to credible independent challenge and material objections have been recorded, investigated and responded to transparently.

Reviewers are being asked to identify problems, gaps and objections — not to provide endorsements.

Reviewer support is evidence of a reviewer's opinion. It is not proof that the practitioner function is distinct, that the framework is valid or that formal recognition is required.

Adverse findings, null findings and unresolved objections must be retained.

## B. The Primary Reform Question

> Does advanced residential renovation carpentry constitute a sufficiently distinct practitioner function that it warrants defined competence assessment, role boundaries and potentially formal recognition within Victoria's enhanced practitioner-registration framework?

This is an evaluation question. It is not an established conclusion.

```
Public-interest problem
→ Practitioner function
```

## D. Seven Capability Domains

### 1. Technical Capability
Knowledge and application of construction methods, materials, systems and specifications relevant to existing residential buildings. Includes understanding of deterioration, variation and concealed conditions.

### 2. Diagnostic Capability
Ability to observe existing conditions, identify defects, assess risk, recognise concealed-condition uncertainty and form a reasoned assessment of existing-building state before and during work.

### 3. Structural Understanding
Ability to identify structural elements, recognise load paths, identify temporary-support requirements and recognise when structural work requires engineering design input. This domain is bounded. It does not confer engineering design authority.

### 4. Planning Capability
Ability to sequence renovation work, assess scope, identify regulatory requirements, manage dependencies and communicate scope to owners and other practitioners.

### 5. Coordination Capability
Ability to coordinate with builders, engineers, building surveyors and licensed trades, understand professional boundaries and manage handoffs appropriately.

### 6. Professional Judgement
Integration of the other capability domains through the professional decision cycle:

> **Observe → Diagnose → Predict → Plan → Execute → Verify**

Professional Judgement is the governing capability domain. It integrates technical, diagnostic, structural, planning and coordination capabilities with boundary recognition. Stopping, investigating, referring or escalating may demonstrate advanced Professional Judgement, not a failure of it.

### 7. Boundary Recognition
Ability to recognise when work exceeds the practitioner's scope, to stop and escalate appropriately, to communicate limitations to owners and to engage the appropriate professional (builder, engineer, surveyor or licensed trade) before continuing.

## E. Assessment Architecture

### Eligibility ≠ Competence

Meeting the eligibility criteria demonstrates that a candidate has sufficient exposure to be assessed. It does not establish advanced competence.

### Experience as eligibility evidence only

Experience demonstrates exposure. It does not automatically demonstrate competence, judgement, documentation capability or boundary recognition. Time served alone is not a substitute for independent assessment.

### Assessment components

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Portfolio review | Review of documented project evidence, including photographs, scope records, structural documentation, referrals and outcome records |
| Structured technical interview | Probing of technical knowledge, diagnosis reasoning and prior decision-making |
| Scenario assessment | Presentation of realistic existing-building renovation scenarios requiring observation, diagnosis, prediction, planning and boundary decisions |
| Evidence interpretation | Assessment of candidate's ability to read and respond to ambiguous, incomplete or conflicting evidence |
| Consumer communication | Assessment of candidate's ability to communicate scope, limitations and escalation decisions to owners in plain language |
| Oral defence / clarification | Opportunity for assessors to probe ambiguous portfolio or scenario responses |

### Scoring

- Scores are recorded on a **0–4 rubric** per domain and sub-element.
- Critical failures in safety or boundary recognition cannot be averaged away by strong scores elsewhere.
- Stopping, investigating, referring or escalating may demonstrate advanced competence.
- Unknown information cannot improve an outcome. Candidates who claim certainty they cannot possess must not be scored as demonstrating advanced professional judgement.

## F. Assessor Calibration Architecture

Assessor calibration exists because **assessor agreement must itself be tested**. Without calibration, the assessment framework cannot produce reliable evidence.

Calibration uses practice scenarios to determine whether assessors independently reach consistent conclusions on:

- what constitutes a critical failure;
- where engineering, builder, surveyor and trade boundaries fall;
- how to score material uncertainty responses;
- how to assess consumer communication;
- how to treat missing or unverifiable evidence.

### Material disagreement categories requiring calibration

| Disagreement type | Example |
|---|---|
| Critical fail vs no critical fail | One assessor records a critical boundary failure; another scores the same response as a domain weakness only |
| Proceed vs stop/escalate | Disagreement on whether a candidate's decision to continue work was appropriate |
| Engineering boundary disagreement | Disagreement on whether a response crossed into engineering design authority |
| Builder boundary disagreement | Disagreement on whether a response assumed builder contracting or permit authority |
| Licensed-trade boundary disagreement | Disagreement on whether a response assumed a licensed-trade authority (electrical, plumbing etc.) |
| Material uncertainty disagreement | Disagreement on how to score a response that assumed certainty on an unknown condition |
| Consumer-risk communication disagreement | Disagreement on whether a consumer communication response was adequate, clear or appropriate |

Disagreement that cannot be resolved through calibration must be recorded. It may indicate that the assessment framework requires refinement before live candidate assessment.

**Dan Sonsie does not automatically assess candidates.** His role in assessment is subject to the conflict, independence and calibration controls in the governed documents.

## G. Pilot Governance Summary

| Control | Detail |
|---|---|
| Voluntary participation | No person is compelled to participate |
| Independent assessment | Assessors apply the framework independently |
| Dan Sonsie's role | Subject to conflict declaration and calibration controls; does not automatically assess candidates |
| Adverse and null findings | Retained; cannot be deleted |
| Recruitment independence | Cannot be limited to supporters or expected passers |
| Consent separation | Participation consent and publication consent are recorded separately |
| Evidence minimisation | Third-party evidence minimised and de-identified where appropriate |
| Pilot flexibility | The pilot may pause, narrow, redesign or stop |

## H. Master Operating Model (Summary)

The current operating flow is:

```
Governance readiness check
→ Assessor calibration and readiness
→ Recruitment (independent and varied)
→ Eligibility screening
→ Candidate onboarding and consent
→ Evidence preparation
→ Candidate assessment
→ Critical-fail and boundary review
→ Second review where required
→ Candidate outcome
→ Pilot evidence aggregation
→ Assessor variance review
→ Consumer comprehension review
→ Public-interest evaluation
→ Government decision on statutory vehicle, if any
```

Each stage is governed by a detailed controlled source document. The master operating model is an integration document; it does not replace the source documents.

Where any conflict exists between this pack and a detailed governed source document, the detailed source document controls.

## I. Pilot Limitations

The pilot cannot by itself prove:

- that a new registration class is required;
- that the Registered Residential Carpenter model should be adopted;
- that DB-L Carpentry reform is preferable;
- that residential defect rates will fall;
- that renovation costs will fall;
- that insurance viability will be achieved;
- that economic benefit will result;
- that workforce supply will improve;
- that the proposed framework is legally feasible;
- that pilot findings are representative of the statewide workforce.

Pilot success is evidence that the assessment framework can be administered and that it produces capability evidence. It is not by itself evidence that formal recognition is required or proportionate.

## J. Implementation Options

All six implementation pathways remain open. No option is predetermined.

| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| A | Distinct Registered Residential Carpenter class or category within Victoria's enhanced practitioner-registration framework |
| B | DB-L Carpentry renovation endorsement, stream or revised assessment |
| C | Advanced Residential Carpentry credential with no additional statutory authority |
| D | Another proportionate government-selected mechanism |
| E | **No formal recognition / status quo** — government may conclude that the public-interest case is insufficient to justify additional formal recognition |
| F | Further evidence before any decision |

**No formal recognition / status quo remains a legitimate and fully open outcome.**
