# External Reviewer Category Framework — Advanced Residential Carpentry v0.1

**Status:** Working Draft.
**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

This document defines the reviewer perspectives relevant to external validation of the Advanced Residential Carpentry reform framework.

These are reviewer perspectives, not mandatory appointments. Not every category needs to be filled for external validation to proceed, but gaps must be noted and their impact on the validation assessed.

No reviewer category should be selected primarily because they are expected to support the framework.

---

## Reviewer Category Definitions

---

### Category A — Experienced Renovation Carpenter

**Perspective sought:**
Practical assessment of whether the proposed practitioner function reflects genuine distinctions in residential renovation carpentry. Whether the capability domains capture real differences in renovation practice. Whether the assessment scenarios reflect realistic working conditions.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether advanced renovation carpentry is genuinely distinct from qualified carpentry practice, or whether the distinction reflects experience rather than a separate capability level. Whether the framework has been designed by someone whose experience is atypical. Whether the assessment model would disadvantage capable carpenters who have not documented their work.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May have a professional or financial interest in the initiative's success or failure. May hold prior views on the need for or opposition to formal recognition of renovation carpentry. May be a direct acquaintance or former associate of Dan Sonsie.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- Is the practitioner function genuinely distinct from ordinary qualified carpentry (Q1)?
- Are the assessment scenarios realistic (Q11)?
- Do scenarios test judgement rather than memorised answers (Q12)?
- What is the strongest argument against this initiative (Q29)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Yes. Without a practitioner perspective, the practical credibility of the practitioner-function claim cannot be challenged. This category gap would be a material limitation.

---

### Category B — Registered Domestic Builder

**Perspective sought:**
Assessment of whether the proposed practitioner function is genuinely distinguishable from domestic builder responsibility. Whether the framework risks blurring builder authority or contracting scope. Whether the assessment correctly reflects builder boundary obligations.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether an advanced carpenter performing diagnostic, planning and coordination functions is effectively acting as a builder without registration. Whether the framework creates regulatory confusion about who is responsible for contract, permit and warranty obligations. Whether the capability classification could be used to avoid domestic builder registration requirements.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May hold a commercial or professional interest in preventing recognition of a practitioner function that could compete with builders. May also hold a perspective that supports reform. Builder association membership may shape views.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- Is the function distinguishable from domestic builder responsibility (Q2)?
- Does the framework confuse carpentry competence with builder authority (Q6)?
- Could a technically strong but boundary-weak candidate pass (Q9)?
- Could consumers wrongly interpret the classification as builder registration (Q20)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Yes. Builder-boundary challenge is a core risk in the framework. Absence of this perspective would leave a material gap.

---

### Category C — Structural Engineer

**Perspective sought:**
Assessment of whether the Structural Understanding domain is correctly bounded from engineering design authority. Whether scenario responses that involve structural recognition could be interpreted as implying engineering competence. Whether the framework adequately distinguishes structural awareness from engineering design.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether the Structural Understanding domain, as defined, crosses into engineering design territory. Whether assessors are qualified to evaluate structural reasoning without engineering expertise. Whether the framework creates risk that a non-engineer will be assessed as having structural authority.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May hold a professional interest in preserving clear engineering authority. May be unfamiliar with the practical distinctions between structural awareness and engineering design in a renovation context.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- Are engineering boundaries sufficiently protected (Q7)?
- Could a candidate who understands structural principles pass in a way that implies engineering authority (Q7)?
- Are critical failures defined strongly enough (Q14)?
- What could make the pilot unsafe (Q21)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Yes. Engineering boundary protection is identified as a non-negotiable principle. Absence of engineering scrutiny would leave this protection unvalidated.

---

### Category D — Building Surveyor

**Perspective sought:**
Assessment of whether the framework correctly identifies permit, certification and surveyor responsibilities. Whether planning and documentation capability claims are consistent with surveyor and permit obligations. Whether the framework correctly positions surveyor authority as external to the practitioner function.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether the Planning Capability domain inadvertently implies permit or certification authority. Whether documentation capability claims could create confusion about the practitioner's role in permit or consent processes.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May hold a professional interest in preserving surveyor authority. May have limited familiarity with advanced residential renovation practice.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- Are professional boundaries clear (Q6, Q7, Q8)?
- Are critical failures defined strongly enough in relation to permit and certification obligations (Q14)?
- Could consumers wrongly interpret the classification as involving permit or surveyor authority (Q20)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Moderate. Surveyor-boundary challenge is less central than builder or engineering boundary challenge, but material. Absence should be noted.

---

### Category E — Competency / Assessment Specialist

**Perspective sought:**
Assessment of whether the assessment architecture is designed to produce reliable, valid and defensible evidence of capability. Whether the scoring rubric can be applied consistently. Whether critical failures are defined operationally. Whether the calibration model is adequate.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether the assessment model reflects recognised competency assessment principles. Whether portfolio evidence is a reliable basis for capability assessment. Whether assessor calibration is sufficient to produce inter-rater reliability. Whether the assessment is vulnerable to candidate preparation that substitutes for genuine competence.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May have a commercial interest in providing assessment or calibration services. May hold strong views on recognised assessment frameworks that do not map directly to the initiative's design.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- Can assessors reliably distinguish competent from advanced performance (Q13)?
- Are critical failures defined strongly enough (Q14)?
- Is the evidence model appropriate (Q16)?
- What could make the pilot methodologically invalid (Q22)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Yes. Without assessment-design expertise, the reliability of the assessment architecture cannot be credibly evaluated. This category gap would be a material limitation.

---

### Category F — Consumer Representative

**Perspective sought:**
Assessment of whether consumers can understand the capability classification, what it does and does not mean, and whether disclosure obligations are adequate. Whether the framework adequately protects residential owners from confusion, misrepresentation or harm.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether the capability classification, in practice, will be understood correctly by typical residential renovation clients. Whether consumers could be misled into believing the classification confers builder registration or contracting authority. Whether stop-work and escalation obligations are communicated clearly enough to protect owners.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May hold views shaped by particular consumer advocacy positions. May have limited knowledge of the building industry.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- Are consumer role disclosures understandable (Q19)?
- Could consumers wrongly interpret the classification as builder registration or licence authority (Q20)?
- What could make the pilot unsafe (Q21)?
- Should government retain the status quo (Q28)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Yes. Consumer protection is the stated primary objective of the initiative. Absence of consumer perspective would leave this objective unvalidated.

---

### Category G — Insurance / Risk Specialist

**Perspective sought:**
Assessment of whether the capability classification creates, transfers or affects professional indemnity, public liability or warranty risk. Whether the framework is consistent with known insurance market conditions. Whether the pilot design creates unmanaged risk for participants, consumers or the initiative.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether the capability classification could affect a practitioner's insurance obligations or coverage. Whether consumers could assume the classification implies insurance protection it does not provide. Whether the pilot creates unmanaged liability for the initiative.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May hold commercial interests in the insurance or risk advisory market. May have limited familiarity with the specifics of residential renovation practice.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- What could make the pilot unsafe (Q21)?
- Is the evidence model appropriate (Q16)?
- What evidence is currently missing (Q24)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Moderate. Insurance viability is listed as a matter the pilot cannot establish. Specialist input would strengthen confidence that the framework has not created unmanaged risk.

---

### Category H — Regulatory / Policy Reviewer

**Perspective sought:**
Assessment of whether the framework is consistent with Victoria's building regulatory architecture. Whether the proposed capability classification, if formally recognised, would fit within or require changes to existing legislative and regulatory structures. Whether the decision hierarchy is coherent from a regulatory design perspective.

**Likely area of challenge:**
Whether the framework assumes regulatory outcomes that are not legally or administratively achievable. Whether the six implementation options are genuinely open given Victoria's current statutory framework. Whether the initiative's positioning of the RRC model is consistent with the Building Act 1993, the Building Regulations 2018 and the enhanced practitioner-registration framework.

**Potential conflict / bias:**
May hold views shaped by a particular regulatory philosophy. May have a direct or indirect relationship with the Building and Plumbing Commission, DTP or related bodies.

**Questions particularly relevant to this reviewer:**
- Is the function distinguishable from domestic builder responsibility (Q2)?
- Are all six implementation options genuinely open (implicit in Q24–Q28)?
- What is the strongest argument against this initiative (Q29)?
- Is there anything requiring specialist legal or regulatory review (Q30)?

**Whether absence would materially limit validation:**
Yes. Without regulatory perspective, the feasibility and coherence of the framework within Victoria's statutory architecture cannot be credibly assessed.

---

*This framework identifies perspectives, not mandatory appointments. Gaps in reviewer coverage must be recorded in the validation closeout record.*
