# External Validation Process — Advanced Residential Carpentry v0.1

**Status:** Working Draft.
**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Important Notice

No outreach to reviewers occurs as part of this sprint.

This document describes the process that will apply when the initiative is authorised to approach reviewers. It is provided now so that the process can itself be reviewed and challenged before any contact with external parties commences.

---

## Process Overview

```
Reviewer selection
→ Conflict declaration
→ Reviewer onboarding
→ Reviewer pack issued
→ Independent review
→ Structured feedback
→ Clarification (if required)
→ Evidence classification
→ Initiative response
→ Change / no-change decision
→ Unresolved issue recording
→ Cross-reviewer comparison
→ Validation closeout
→ Decision whether to proceed toward pilot preparation
```

---

## Stage Descriptions

### Stage 1 — Reviewer Selection

Identify potential reviewers from the categories in the External Reviewer Category Framework.

Selection criteria:

- relevant professional perspective;
- sufficient knowledge to provide meaningful challenge;
- no undisclosed conflict that would materially compromise independence;
- willingness to participate as a challenger, not an endorser.

The initiative must not select reviewers primarily because they are expected to support the framework.

Reviewer categories from which no selection is available must be noted as a gap in the validation record.

---

### Stage 2 — Conflict Declaration

Before any review material is shared, each reviewer completes the External Reviewer Conflict Declaration.

Conflicts are assessed against the reviewer's assigned category.

A reviewer with a declared conflict is not automatically excluded. Their findings are interpreted in light of the declared conflict.

---

### Stage 3 — Reviewer Onboarding

Each reviewer receives:

- the External Validation One-Page Summary;
- the External Reviewer Pack;
- the Reviewer Independence Protocol (explaining what the reviewer is being asked to do and what protections apply);
- the Structured Reviewer Question Set;
- access to relevant governed source documents on request.

Onboarding must not direct reviewers toward a particular conclusion.

---

### Stage 4 — Reviewer Pack Issued

The External Reviewer Pack is provided to the reviewer.

Additional source documents may be provided on request, subject to any privacy or confidentiality considerations.

---

### Stage 5 — Independent Review

The reviewer conducts their review independently.

Dan Sonsie may clarify terminology and practitioner context.

Dan Sonsie must not direct, pressure or discourage any finding.

The reviewer may take as much time as they reasonably require.

---

### Stage 6 — Structured Feedback

The reviewer completes the Reviewer Question Set and any additional free-response comments.

Reviewers may submit findings in writing, verbally (with a written record made), or through a structured interview.

All material findings must be captured in writing and confirmed with the reviewer as an accurate record before classification proceeds.

---

### Stage 7 — Clarification (if required)

Where a reviewer's finding is unclear, the initiative may ask a clarifying question.

Clarification must seek to accurately understand the reviewer's intended meaning — not to revise or soften an adverse finding.

---

### Stage 8 — Evidence Classification

Each material finding is classified using the feedback classifications:

SUPPORTS / CHALLENGES / IDENTIFIES_GAP / REQUIRES_EVIDENCE / SUGGESTS_REFINEMENT / MATERIAL_OBJECTION / OUT_OF_SCOPE.

Classification is performed by the initiative and reviewed against the reviewer's intended meaning.

---

### Stage 9 — Initiative Response

The initiative prepares a response to each material finding.

Responses must:

- engage honestly with the substance of the finding;
- identify the relevant source document and control;
- acknowledge gaps where they exist;
- not mischaracterise an adverse finding as a minor clarification.

---

### Stage 10 — Change / No-Change Decision

For each material finding, the initiative records whether:

- a change to the framework is made;
- no change is made (with documented reasoning); or
- the matter is escalated, deferred or recorded as unresolved.

Changes must be traceable to the finding that prompted them.

---

### Stage 11 — Unresolved Issue Recording

Matters that cannot be resolved during validation are recorded in the Evidence Register with status UNRESOLVED.

Unresolved matters are carried into the validation closeout record.

---

### Stage 12 — Cross-Reviewer Comparison

After all reviewer feedback has been received and classified, the initiative reviews findings across reviewers to identify:

- consistent findings across multiple reviewers;
- contradictions between reviewers;
- reviewer findings that reflect a particular professional perspective;
- evidence gaps that no reviewer was positioned to address.

Cross-reviewer comparison does not resolve disagreements. It informs the validation closeout analysis.

---

### Stage 13 — Validation Closeout

The initiative prepares a validation closeout record that includes:

- a summary of reviewer perspectives engaged;
- reviewer categories not covered and why;
- a summary of material findings and their dispositions;
- a list of unresolved matters;
- a list of framework changes made;
- the initiative's overall assessment of the validation;
- the recommended validation outcome (from the External Validation Success Criteria document).

---

### Stage 14 — Decision Whether to Proceed

The validation closeout record informs the decision whether to:

- proceed toward governed pilot preparation;
- conduct further external validation;
- revise the framework;
- seek specialist review; or
- stop.

No formal recognition / status quo remains a legitimate outcome at this stage.
