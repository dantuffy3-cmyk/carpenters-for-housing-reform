# Consumer Role Comprehension Test — Pilot Version 0.1

**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
**Status:** DRAFT — pilot consumer comprehension testing for consultation and validation
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

This document defines a consumer comprehension test designed to measure whether consumers understand the assessed practitioner's role and the boundaries of that role.

The test is distinct from a consumer satisfaction measure. It does not ask whether the consumer is happy. It tests whether the consumer correctly understands what the practitioner is and is not able to do.

---

## Important

Consumer comprehension testing does not certify that the practitioner's disclosure was adequate in every case. It tests whether, following disclosure, the consumer demonstrates understanding of the practitioner's role.

---

## 1 — Test Design Principles

1. The test measures understanding, not satisfaction.
2. The test is administered twice — before disclosure and after disclosure — to measure the effect of the practitioner's communication.
3. The test uses scenario-based questions where possible, not yes/no questions about role definitions.
4. The test must be accessible — written in plain language appropriate for a general consumer audience.
5. The test must not prompt correct answers through its question structure.
6. The test must be consistent across consumers participating in the pilot.

---

## 2 — What the Test Measures

The test must assess consumer understanding of:

| Understanding Area | Why It Is Tested |
|---|---|
| What the practitioner is competent to assess or carry out | Consumers who misunderstand this may allow work they should not allow |
| What remains outside the practitioner's scope | Consumers must understand where the practitioner's authority ends |
| When builder involvement is required | Builder authority is a protected boundary — consumer understanding matters |
| When engineer input is required | Structural engineering is a protected boundary |
| When a permit or building surveyor's involvement may be required | Permit requirements affect the consumer's own position |
| When licensed trades are required | Electrical, plumbing and other licensed trades carry their own authority |
| What the pilot credential does and does not mean | Consumers must not believe the credential confers more authority than it does |

---

## 3 — Test Administration

### 3.1 Timing

- **Pre-disclosure test:** Administered before any disclosure by the practitioner.
- **Post-disclosure test:** Administered after the practitioner has explained their role and the scope of their assessment.

The pre-disclosure test establishes a baseline. The post-disclosure test measures the effect of the practitioner's communication.

### 3.2 Format

The test may be administered:

- in writing, using a structured question set;
- verbally, by a neutral observer using a structured question guide;
- digitally, using a consistent question set.

The format must be consistent across all pilot consumers.

### 3.3 Who Administers the Test

The test must be administered by a party independent of the practitioner being assessed. It must not be administered by the practitioner themselves.

---

## 4 — Sample Question Areas

The following areas are illustrative of the types of questions the test should include. Specific question wording must be developed and validated during pilot design.

**Pre-disclosure question areas:**
- What do you understand about what this type of practitioner can do?
- Who would you contact if you discovered a structural problem during a renovation?
- In what circumstances do you think a building permit would be required?

**Post-disclosure question areas (scenario-based):**
- Scenario: A carpenter has assessed your bathroom renovation and believes the wall may be load-bearing. What should happen next?
- Scenario: A carpenter has started your kitchen renovation and discovered what they believe is asbestos in the wall lining. Who should be involved?
- Scenario: The carpenter has told you they cannot proceed with a part of the work without engineer input. Does this mean the work cannot be done at all?
- Scenario: The carpenter has completed your renovation. Does their assessment of the work mean it has been certified or inspected under the Building Act?

---

## 5 — Outcome Categories

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `UNDERSTANDS_ROLE` | The consumer demonstrates accurate understanding of the practitioner's role and key boundaries after disclosure. |
| `PARTIAL_UNDERSTANDING` | The consumer demonstrates correct understanding of some aspects of the role but has identifiable gaps or misconceptions in others. |
| `MATERIAL_MISUNDERSTANDING` | The consumer holds a materially inaccurate belief about the practitioner's role or authority — for example, believing the practitioner can certify work, provide engineering sign-off or act with builder authority. |
| `UNABLE_TO_ASSESS` | The consumer comprehension test could not be completed or interpreted for this participant. |

---

## 6 — What the Results Are Used For

Consumer comprehension results are used for pilot evaluation only. They inform:

- whether practitioner disclosure methods are effective;
- whether disclosure wording or materials need to be revised;
- whether any patterns of material misunderstanding are present across the pilot cohort;
- whether the assessment framework adequately prepares practitioners to communicate their role clearly.

Consumer comprehension results are not used to pass or fail individual practitioners.

If a systematic pattern of `MATERIAL_MISUNDERSTANDING` is observed across multiple consumers, this constitutes a pilot stop condition — see Section 9 of `docs/advanced-residential-carpentry-assessment-framework-v0.1.md`.

---

## 7 — Privacy and Consent

Consumer participation in the comprehension test is voluntary.

Consumers must be informed:

- that the test is part of a pilot evaluation;
- that their responses are used for pilot evaluation only, not for individual practitioner assessment;
- that their identity will not be publicly disclosed;
- that they may withdraw at any time.
