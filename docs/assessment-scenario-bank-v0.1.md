# Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment — Scenario Bank Version 0.1

**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
**Status:** DRAFT — governed scenario set for pilot consultation and validation
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Important

These scenarios are assessment tools, not legal guidance.

They are designed to test decision-making under uncertainty. They do not prescribe a single correct answer where professional judgement legitimately allows more than one safe response.

Assessors must not score a scenario as failed simply because the candidate's safe response differs from the assessor's expected response, provided the candidate's reasoning is sound and their escalation decisions are appropriate.

Assessors must score a scenario as failing where the candidate:

- proceeds unsafely;
- ignores a mandatory escalation trigger;
- treats uncertainty as confirmation of safety;
- claims authority they do not hold; or
- fails to communicate material risk to the consumer.

---

## Scenario Design Principles

Each scenario:

- presents a realistic existing-building renovation context;
- includes information that is missing, ambiguous or potentially significant;
- includes at least one mandatory escalation trigger;
- requires the candidate to identify what is known and what is uncertain;
- does not encode a single perfect answer where professional judgement legitimately allows more than one safe response;
- includes a consumer communication requirement;
- includes prohibited conclusions — conclusions that no safe assessment of the scenario could support.

## Scenario 1 — Bathroom Renovation Revealing Framing Deterioration

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has engaged the candidate to renovate a bathroom in a 1960s weatherboard house. The scope as initially described involves removing existing tiles and wall lining, installing new waterproofing, tiling and fixtures, and replacing a deteriorated window.

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- The house is single-storey weatherboard on a timber subfloor.
- The existing bathroom is approximately 3m × 2m with a separate toilet adjoining.
- The consumer reports a history of a leaking shower for "several years, probably five or so."
- Photographs supplied show surface mould, discoloured grout and a soft area on the floor near the shower recess.
- No structural reports, no original drawings and no building permits for previous works are available.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

On removal of the tile and compressed fibre sheet wall lining, the candidate discovers:

- The bottom plate and base of multiple wall studs are soft and discoloured — consistent with timber deterioration from prolonged moisture exposure.
- One stud adjacent to the shower recess crumbles when probed.
- A horizontal nogging between two studs at approximately 900mm height is also soft.
- The floor framing immediately below the shower cannot be accessed from inside the bathroom without lifting the floor.

### Incomplete Evidence

- It is not known whether the deteriorating wall is load-bearing.
- Subfloor access is limited. The extent of floor-framing deterioration is unknown.
- No documentation exists for previous bathroom works or the original construction.
- It is not known whether any previous wall alterations have modified the load path.

### Assessment Questions

1. What is your assessment of what you have found?
2. What additional information do you need before proceeding?
3. What decision state applies at this point, and why?
4. Who needs to be involved in the next stage?
5. What do you tell the consumer?
6. What records do you create at this point?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate identifies that the structural significance of the deteriorated members is not yet determined.
- Candidate recognises that proceeding without further investigation risks inadvertently removing structural members without adequate assessment.
- Candidate identifies the need for subfloor access assessment before floor framing decisions are made.
- Candidate recognises that the extent of water damage may extend beyond the visible area.
- Candidate understands that temporary support or shoring may be required if the wall is load-bearing and members need replacement.
- Candidate identifies that the point at which structural member replacement or assessment is required may engage engineering input.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` applies immediately on discovery.
- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` applies if any load-bearing member is found to require replacement.
- `STOP_AND_MAKE_SAFE` applies if the structure becomes unstable or unsafe during investigation.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not conclude that they can replace structural members without determining load-bearing status and, if necessary, obtaining engineering input.
- The candidate must not proceed with tiling or waterproofing while structural integrity is unresolved.
- The candidate must not tell the consumer the work is safe to proceed without adequate investigation.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Subfloor inspection report from a qualified party.
- Structural assessment of the wall by an engineer.
- Original building documentation or permit records.
- Moisture mapping of the affected area.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

The candidate must explain to a simulated homeowner:

- what has been found;
- why work cannot proceed immediately;
- what further investigation is required and who will carry it out;
- what the possible implications are for scope and cost;
- that the consumer should not assume the original scope and budget remain fixed.

## Scenario 2 — Subfloor Access Revealing Moisture, Ventilation and Timber Deterioration

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has asked the candidate to investigate and remedy a musty smell and soft area in the floor of a 1950s brick-veneer house. Initial access reveals the subfloor is accessible via an external access hatch.

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- The house is single-storey brick-veneer on a timber subfloor frame.
- The floor area is approximately 120m².
- The consumer reports the problem has developed over the last two years and has progressively worsened.
- Photographs supplied by the consumer show discoloured boards in one room and evidence of condensation on interior walls adjacent to the area of complaint.
- One previous owner had concrete poured in part of the subfloor area for unknown reasons.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

On subfloor access, the candidate observes:

- Significant moisture on the underside of the floor boards in multiple areas, not only the area of original complaint.
- Floor joist timber in approximately a 15m² area at the rear of the building is soft, discoloured and penetrates with a probe — consistent with advanced fungal decay.
- Subfloor ventilation is blocked by the concrete pour in part of the space, creating stagnant air conditions.
- Evidence of recent termite activity is visible on two stumps and the underside of a bearer. Active tunnels are observed.

### Incomplete Evidence

- The extent of timber decay beyond the visibly affected area is unknown.
- Whether the termite activity is still active or historical is not yet established.
- The subfloor drainage situation is not fully mapped.
- The structural significance of the affected bearers and joists is not yet assessed.

### Assessment Questions

1. How many separate conditions have you identified?
2. What is the priority order for addressing them?
3. What decision state applies to each condition?
4. Who needs to be involved for each condition?
5. Can any remediation work begin now? If so, which?
6. What do you tell the consumer, and in what order?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate identifies multiple concurrent conditions, not a single problem.
- Candidate recognises that termite activity requires licensed pest specialist involvement before any structural timber decisions are made.
- Candidate recognises that bearer and joist replacement requires structural assessment — the extent of required replacement affects load-path implications.
- Candidate identifies that ventilation remediation may proceed within scope if independent of structural repairs.
- Candidate understands that the original scope (remedying a musty smell and soft floor area) has expanded materially and that the consumer must be informed.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` — licensed pest specialist must assess active termite activity before structural timber decisions.
- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — extent of bearer and joist replacement must be assessed with reference to structural adequacy.
- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — full extent of moisture and decay must be established before remediation scope is set.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not begin structural timber replacement until pest assessment is complete.
- The candidate must not represent the scope as fixed when the extent of decay is not yet fully determined.
- The candidate must not treat the absence of visible termites as confirmation that termite activity is historical.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Pest inspection report from a licensed specialist.
- Structural assessment of the affected bearing elements.
- Moisture mapping of the full subfloor area.
- Drainage survey of the subfloor drainage conditions.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

The candidate must explain:

- that multiple conditions have been found;
- that some conditions require specialist involvement before any remediation scope can be set;
- that the original scope is likely to change;
- that there is no single start date for remediation until the priority specialist inputs are obtained.

## Scenario 3 — Termite Damage Extending Beyond the Visible Repair Area

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has asked the candidate to repair a section of external timber cladding on a 1970s brick-and-timber house that was identified during a recent pest inspection. The pest report identified localised termite damage to external cladding in one corner of the building. The report did not include a structural assessment.

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- A copy of the pest inspection report is available. It identifies "localised termite damage to external cladding — rear south-western corner. Evidence of terminated activity (no live termites observed). Treatment completed."
- Photographs in the report show surface tunnelling on the external face of the cladding boards.
- The building is single-storey.
- No framing drawings are available.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

On removing the damaged cladding boards, the candidate observes:

- Wall framing studs immediately behind the damaged cladding are significantly hollowed. Two studs are substantially hollow for approximately 900mm of their height.
- Evidence of termite activity extends to the nogging and to the base plate in the affected corner.
- On removing the next adjacent cladding board, a further stud shows moderate gallery damage.
- The corner is a window/door intersection — the structural arrangement at this corner is not yet determined.

### Incomplete Evidence

- The full extent of framing damage beyond the immediately visible area is not yet known.
- The structural significance of the corner — whether it carries concentrated loads — is not established without further assessment.
- Whether the pest treatment has been effective and whether any activity remains is not confirmed by the report.

### Assessment Questions

1. Does the pest report adequately establish the scope of structural damage?
2. What is your assessment of the discovered condition?
3. What decision state applies?
4. Who needs to be involved next?
5. Can you proceed with cladding replacement while awaiting further assessment?
6. What do you record at this point?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate recognises that a pest inspection report is not a structural assessment.
- Candidate identifies that substantially hollowed studs at a corner junction require engineering assessment before replacement decisions are made.
- Candidate understands that some cladding replacement might proceed in unaffected areas while the structural assessment is underway, but that the structurally affected area must not be closed without resolution.
- Candidate identifies that the consumer must be informed that the original scope has changed.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — hollowed structural studs at a corner junction require engineering assessment.
- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — extent of damage must be established before framing replacement scope is set.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not replace structurally compromised studs without engineering assessment.
- The candidate must not close the wall without resolving the structural condition.
- The candidate must not rely on the pest report as a structural assessment.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Engineering assessment of the affected framing members.
- Confirmation from the pest specialist that treatment is complete and effective.
- Framing drawings or as-built documentation for the corner junction.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

The candidate must explain that the pest report identified surface damage and the structural implications of the found condition are a separate question that requires engineering input before the repair scope can be finalised.

## Scenario 4 — Deck Repair Revealing Concealed Framing and Footing Deterioration

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has engaged the candidate to replace the decking boards on a rear timber deck attached to a 1980s house. The deck is approximately 4m × 6m, elevated approximately 800mm above the ground at the highest point. The consumer reports that two decking boards are "rotten" and the rest appear sound.

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- Photographs show surface deterioration of two decking boards — consistent with end-grain exposure and moisture entry.
- The deck appears to have been built without a building permit or, if permitted, no documentation is available.
- The deck is attached to the house wall framing via a ledger board.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

On removing the deteriorated decking boards, the candidate observes:

- The bearer supporting the deck in the area below the removed boards is significantly deteriorated — the timber is soft and discoloured for approximately 1.5m of its length.
- Two posts in the affected area show base deterioration. One post base can be pushed by hand.
- The ledger board connection to the house wall framing appears to be coach-bolted, but the wall framing behind the ledger cannot be inspected without removing cladding.
- The footings below the deteriorated posts cannot be inspected without excavation.

### Incomplete Evidence

- The condition of the ledger-to-wall connection and the wall framing behind it is unknown.
- The footing condition and bearing capacity of the deteriorated posts is unknown.
- The full extent of bearer deterioration beyond the visible area is unknown.
- Whether the deck requires a building permit is not determined.

### Assessment Questions

1. Is it safe to allow the consumer to use the deck while investigation is underway?
2. What is your assessment of what you have found?
3. What is the appropriate decision state?
4. Who needs to be involved?
5. Does a building permit apply to this work?
6. What do you tell the consumer about the deck's usability?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate immediately identifies the deck as potentially unsafe for continued use while a post is unstable.
- `STOP_AND_MAKE_SAFE` applies immediately — consumer must be advised not to use the deck until structural integrity is established.
- Candidate identifies that bearer, post and footing assessment requires structural input.
- Candidate identifies that the permit question must be referred to a building surveyor.
- Candidate recognises that the ledger-to-wall connection may require investigation and engineering input.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `STOP_AND_MAKE_SAFE` — unstable post creates immediate safety risk.
- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — structural adequacy of bearing elements requires engineering assessment.
- `PERMIT_OR_SURVEYOR_CLARIFICATION_REQUIRED` — the permit status of the deck and of the proposed repairs must be clarified.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not advise the consumer that the deck is safe for use while a post is unstable.
- The candidate must not proceed with repair work until structural assessment is complete.
- The candidate must not determine on their own that no permit is required without building surveyor input.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Engineering assessment of the deck structure.
- Building surveyor advice on permit requirements.
- Excavation to inspect the footing condition.
- Inspection of the ledger-to-wall connection.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

The candidate must tell the consumer directly that the deck must not be used until the structural condition is assessed. This is a safety instruction, not a preference. The candidate must explain the reason clearly without causing unnecessary alarm.

## Scenario 5 — Cladding Removal Revealing Asbestos, Water Damage and Unsuitable Substrate

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has engaged the candidate to replace the external cladding on a 1965 fibrous cement-clad house. The consumer wants new fibre cement cladding installed. The original cladding is described by the consumer as "just old hardboard."

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- The house is a single-storey weatherboard-framed structure with what appears to be fibrous cement sheet cladding.
- No asbestos report is available.
- The consumer believes the cladding is "the same as what they sell now."
- Photographs supplied show the external face of the cladding — surface weathering and some cracking visible.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

On closer examination before any removal:

- The candidate identifies features consistent with asbestos-containing fibrous cement sheet — manufacture date indicators, sheet dimensions and composition appearance consistent with pre-1987 fibrous cement.
- The candidate has not disturbed any material.

If removal proceeded without identification:

- Water damage to the framing behind the cladding, including some mould on internal sheathing, would be found.
- Substrate is unsuitable for direct cladding installation without remediation.

### Incomplete Evidence

- No asbestos assessment report is available.
- The extent of water damage behind the cladding is not known.
- Whether the framing condition is structural or cosmetic is not determined.

### Assessment Questions

1. Can you begin removing the cladding?
2. What indicators led you to your assessment of the cladding material?
3. What decision state applies before any removal?
4. Who must be involved before work proceeds?
5. What do you tell the consumer about timing and cost?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate correctly identifies that suspected asbestos-containing material must not be disturbed without an asbestos assessment by a licensed asbestos assessor.
- `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` applies before any removal commences.
- Candidate understands the regulatory framework — licensed asbestos removalist requirements apply if asbestos is confirmed.
- Candidate recognises that the water damage and framing condition behind the cladding cannot be known until access is safely achieved.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` — licensed asbestos assessor must assess the material before any removal.
- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — the condition behind the cladding is unknown until safe access is established.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not begin removing the cladding before an asbestos assessment is completed.
- The candidate must not rely on the consumer's description as a material identification.
- The candidate must not proceed on the assumption that the material is not asbestos.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Asbestos assessment report from a licensed assessor.
- Original council records or documentation.
- Access to the wall cavity after safe preparation.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

The candidate must explain to the consumer why work cannot begin immediately, what an asbestos assessment involves, that a licensed assessor must be engaged, and that the project timeline and budget may change depending on the assessment outcome.

## Scenario 6 — Window and Door Replacement Revealing Structural and Water Management Issues

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has engaged the candidate to replace three timber-framed windows and one external door in a 1975 brick-veneer house. The existing windows are described as draughty, difficult to operate and "probably past their life."

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- Photographs show the exterior of the windows — some paint deterioration and weathering around the perimeter of two windows.
- The house is single-storey brick veneer.
- No building permits for previous window work are identified.
- No structural drawings are available.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

On removing the first window, the candidate observes:

- The lintel above the window opening appears to be a single brick course without visible steel. It is cracked along its length.
- The cavity tray drainage at the base of the window opening is absent — there is no weep hole or drainage provision visible in the brickwork below the removed window.
- The timber frame around the opening shows moisture staining and some softening consistent with long-term water ingress.
- The head flashing is absent.

### Incomplete Evidence

- Whether the lintel is structurally adequate for the window opening is not established.
- The condition of the framing at the other window openings is not yet known.
- Whether a building permit was required for the original installation is not determined.
- The extent of moisture damage to the framing around the opening is not fully assessed.

### Assessment Questions

1. Can you proceed with window replacement given the lintel condition?
2. What is your assessment of the structural question?
3. What water management issues have you identified?
4. What is the appropriate decision state for each discovered condition?
5. Who needs to be involved?
6. Can the other windows be removed and assessed while the first opening is temporarily protected?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate correctly identifies the cracked lintel as a condition requiring engineering assessment before the opening is modified.
- Candidate identifies the absence of a cavity tray and head flashing as a water management deficiency that must be remedied but notes this is within scope of remediation work.
- Candidate correctly understands that the opening must be temporarily secured while engineering assessment is obtained.
- Candidate demonstrates appropriate uncertainty about whether the other windows have similar conditions.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — cracked lintel of uncertain structural capacity must be assessed before the opening is modified.
- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — condition of remaining window openings unknown.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not proceed with window installation without resolving the lintel condition.
- The candidate must not assume the remaining openings are free from the same issues.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Engineering assessment of the lintel.
- Assessment of all window and door openings before committing to a fixed scope.
- Evidence of original lintel specification.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

The candidate must explain the lintel condition, why it must be assessed before proceeding, that the timeline is affected, and that similar conditions may exist in the other openings.

## Scenario 7 — Internal Alteration Revealing Load Path and Chimney Conditions

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has engaged the candidate to remove a wall between a kitchen and dining room in a 1955 brick house to create an open-plan living area. The consumer states "I was told it's not load-bearing."

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- The house is single-storey brick, approximately 140m² floor area.
- The wall to be removed runs north–south through the middle of the house.
- No structural drawings or building permits are available.
- The consumer's statement that the wall is "not load-bearing" is based on a verbal comment from an unidentified tradesperson.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

On inspection before any work:

- The candidate observes that the wall to be removed connects at its northern end to a brick chimney.
- The roof structure above is not visible without ceiling access, but the ridge line runs parallel to the wall.
- The candidate cannot determine from visual inspection alone whether the wall carries roof loads, acts as a mid-span support to the ceiling joists, or serves any other structural function.
- Ceiling access via the manhole reveals that ceiling joists terminate into a hanging beam that sits within the ceiling space — the hanging beam sits directly above and appears connected to the wall below.

### Incomplete Evidence

- Whether the hanging beam is structural and whether the wall supports it is not yet determined.
- The chimney connection and its effect on the wall's structural role is not yet assessed.
- No documentation supports the consumer's belief that the wall is non-structural.

### Assessment Questions

1. What is your assessment of the consumer's assurance?
2. What is the appropriate decision state before any work begins?
3. Who needs to be involved?
4. How do you communicate this to the consumer without discrediting the previous advice unfairly?
5. Can any preparatory work begin while engineering assessment is obtained?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate correctly identifies that a verbal assurance from an unidentified party is not a structural assessment.
- Candidate identifies multiple structural indicators that require investigation before any removal proceeds.
- Candidate understands that a consumer's belief, however sincerely held, is not a substitute for professional assessment.
- Candidate demonstrates appropriate sensitivity in consumer communication — noting the concern without being dismissive of the consumer.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — structural role of the wall must be assessed before any removal.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not proceed with wall removal on the basis of the consumer's verbal assurance.
- The candidate must not rely on visual inspection alone to determine that the wall is non-structural.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Engineering assessment of the wall and ceiling structure.
- Original building documentation or drawings.
- Building surveyor advice on permit requirements for the proposed alteration.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

The candidate must explain — carefully and clearly — that the condition found on inspection means that engineering input is needed before proceeding, and that this is not a reflection on the consumer's previous advice but a necessary step before a safe determination can be made.

## Scenario 8 — Progressive Scope Expansion Beyond Original Assumptions

### Initial Consumer Request

A homeowner has engaged the candidate for a general "renovation" of an older weatherboard house purchased approximately six months ago. The initial scope discussed includes: kitchen update, bathroom update, replace some flooring and "freshen up" the interior. The consumer has a fixed budget.

### Information Supplied to Candidate

- The house is circa 1940 weatherboard, single-storey, on stumps.
- The consumer has not obtained a building or pest inspection report.
- The consumer describes the house as "generally sound" based on their own inspection before purchase.
- No permits for previous works are identified.

### Concealed or Emerging Condition

As the candidate inspects the property before commencing work, they progressively identify:

- The kitchen floor is soft — subfloor condition under the kitchen is not confirmed.
- Bathroom wall lining is bulging — consistent with long-term moisture.
- Evidence of previous DIY electrical work is visible at the meter board.
- Ceiling in one bedroom shows water staining consistent with roof leak.
- A stump in the subfloor is visibly leaning when accessed briefly.

### Incomplete Evidence

- The extent of each individual condition is unknown.
- Whether any conditions are connected — for example, whether the subfloor moisture and soft floor are related — is unknown.
- The full extent of the renovation required to address all conditions cannot be set from initial inspection.
- The electrical condition is unknown beyond visible evidence.

### Assessment Questions

1. How do you manage a situation where discovered conditions progressively exceed the consumer's stated scope and budget?
2. How do you sequence further investigation?
3. Which conditions require specialist involvement before the renovation can proceed?
4. How do you communicate this to a consumer who has a fixed budget and a strong expectation that the renovation will proceed as planned?
5. At what point does the work become unmanageable without a builder?

### Expected Reasoning Themes

- Candidate demonstrates progressive-discovery management — recognising that each finding changes the risk and scope profile.
- Candidate identifies which conditions can be assessed by the candidate and which require specialist input.
- Candidate recognises that the electrical condition requires a licensed electrician to assess — this is not within the candidate's authority to assess or remedy.
- Candidate demonstrates structured consumer communication — not attempting to manage the consumer's expectations by minimising what has been found.
- Candidate identifies that the scale and complexity of the emerging scope may require builder involvement depending on what the full inspection reveals.

### Mandatory Escalation Triggers

- `LICENSED_TRADE_REQUIRED` — electrical condition must be assessed by a licensed electrician.
- `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` — structural stump condition may require structural or geotechnical input.
- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — full condition of the property must be assessed before any renovation scope can be fixed.

### Prohibited Conclusions

- The candidate must not commit to a fixed renovation scope until the full extent of discovered conditions is assessed.
- The candidate must not allow the consumer's budget to determine whether safety-related conditions are addressed.
- The candidate must not assess or advise on the electrical condition beyond noting that a licensed electrician must be engaged.

### Evidence That Would Improve Certainty

- Full building inspection report.
- Pest inspection report.
- Electrical inspection by a licensed electrician.
- Structural assessment of the subfloor system.
- Roof inspection report.

### Consumer Communication Requirement

This scenario tests whether the candidate can hold a difficult but honest conversation with a consumer whose expectations will not be met by the condition of the property. The candidate must:

- explain what has been found;
- be clear that the original scope cannot be confirmed until full investigation is completed;
- not make promises about cost or timing;
- not minimise conditions to preserve the client relationship;
- explain what specialist involvement is required and why.
