# Government Decision Paper
## Registered Residential Carpenter Pilot — Victoria
**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
Prepared by: Daniel Sonsie
Date: July 2026
Status: CONSULTATION DRAFT — Not government policy. For government, regulator and industry consideration.

**Pre-pivot status note:** This retained consultation draft predates the capability-first pivot. It is kept for record and legacy consultation context and should not be treated as the current canonical strategy.

## Decision Sought
This paper seeks an in-principle government decision to consider a structured next step on the Registered Residential Carpenter proposal, preferably through formal stakeholder consultation or a limited pilot design process, so that the renovation responsibility problem can be tested in a bounded and evidence-led way before any decision is made about wider reform.

## Why the Issue Matters
Victoria’s residential building framework contains a practical responsibility gap around certain renovation and alteration activities that sit near, between or across existing categories of regulated work. In many live projects, the difficulty is not only the physical work itself, but the decision-making required when concealed conditions, prior alterations, deterioration and incomplete documentation are discovered.

For consumers, this can make it difficult to understand who is responsible for diagnosis, planning, referral, coordination and verification when work changes in response to progressive discovery. For practitioners, the boundary between lawful trade activity, builder involvement, engineering input and surveying or certification responsibilities can be difficult to navigate consistently in real time.

This creates an enforcement challenge. Regulators may identify unsafe or non-compliant outcomes after the event, while the boundary decisions that shaped the work may have been informal, poorly documented or made under time pressure. Regulatory ambiguity is not the only driver of informal work, but it can enable it by leaving room for inconsistent interpretations, undocumented handoffs and low-visibility practices at the edges of the current framework.

## Proposed Response
The proposed response is a bounded Registered Residential Carpenter pilot concept designed for testing, not immediate system-wide implementation. The concept would use:

- a limited participant cohort;
- a defined and published work scope;
- mandatory safeguards and referral triggers;
- clear time and geographic boundaries;
- evidence, recordkeeping and audit requirements;
- an evaluation framework focused on consumer, compliance and implementation outcomes; and
- stop provisions allowing the pilot to be paused, narrowed or terminated if risks cannot be controlled.

The pilot concept is intended to test whether a clearly bounded capability-based model can improve clarity, discipline and evidence in a narrow part of the renovation environment without altering existing builder pathways or disturbing existing professional responsibilities.

## Why a Pilot Is Preferable to Immediate Statewide Reform
- It allows unresolved legal, insurance and operational issues to be tested before any permanent change is considered.
- It provides evidence about consumer understanding, practitioner behaviour and audit feasibility in live conditions rather than relying only on theory.
- It enables government and regulators to refine safeguards, boundaries and complaints pathways before broader implementation is contemplated.
- It reduces the risk of unintended consequences by using a small, reviewable and time-limited approach.

## Unresolved Matters
- Insurance availability, product suitability and evidence requirements
- Statutory triggers and how pilot scope would interact with current legal settings
- Enforcement powers, audit authority and administrative resourcing
- Treatment of discovered conditions that change project risk or regulatory status
- Interface rules between pilot participants, registered builders, engineers, surveyors and licensed trades
- Geographic scope and regional participation settings
- Data governance, privacy and evidence retention requirements
- Complaint jurisdiction, remedies and escalation routes
- Evaluation criteria for success, modification or termination

## Options Available to Government

### Option A — No Further Action
Government takes no further step on the proposal at this time. This would preserve the status quo, avoid immediate design effort and leave the identified renovation responsibility problem to existing arrangements, with their current strengths and limitations.

### Option B — Further Policy Investigation
Government undertakes or commissions additional internal policy work to better define the problem, map legal settings and test whether any reform pathway warrants continued attention. This option improves the evidence base but does not yet bring stakeholders into a structured co-design process.

### Option C — Structured Stakeholder Consultation
Government or the regulator convenes a structured consultation process with relevant industry, consumer, regulatory and professional stakeholders. This option is suitable where the issue warrants closer examination but the design remains too unresolved for operational commitment.

### Option D — Limited Pilot Design Process
Government agrees to develop a pilot design with the proponent and relevant stakeholders, without authorising live operation at this stage. This would focus on scope, safeguards, legal alignment, evaluation design, risk controls and implementation feasibility.

### Option E — Time-Limited Operational Pilot
Government authorises and sponsors a controlled operational pilot with defined participants, controls and evaluation settings. This would move beyond design into live testing.

*Option E is not immediately legally available without legislative or regulatory development. The recommended starting point is Option C or D.*

## Recommended Next Step
The practical immediate step is Option C or Option D. A structured consultation or limited pilot design process would allow government to test the quality of the problem definition, confirm whether the proposal can be made legally and operationally coherent, and determine whether any later pilot should proceed, be reframed or not proceed.
