# RRC Statutory Handoff Protocol
**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
Version: 1.0 — Working Draft
Date: July 2026
Status: WORKING DRAFT — Not government policy. Final triggers require statutory drafting and government consultation.

## Stage 1 — Remains Within Defined RRC Scope

Stage 1 describes work that may continue within the authority of an RRC practitioner, provided the work remains within the defined registered scope, no higher-risk trigger has arisen, and the practitioner can still make a reasonable professional judgement that the work can proceed safely and lawfully. This concept **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW** before any statutory use.

In Stage 1, the practitioner remains responsible for staying within scope, identifying uncertainty early and applying the professional decision cycle: observe, diagnose, predict, plan, execute and verify. Obligations include confirming the agreed scope before work begins, communicating exclusions to the owner, recording progressive discovery issues, maintaining site safety within the practitioner’s competence, and stopping any affected part of the work if a boundary issue becomes unclear.

Evidence maintained in Stage 1 should include a project identity record, practitioner identity and registration confirmation, homeowner acknowledgement of the defined scope, a statement of included and excluded work, before-work photographs, and written records of any scope change assessments.

Conditions that may move work out of Stage 1 include uncertainty about load paths, deterioration more extensive than first observed, prior alterations that change the risk profile, design questions, permit uncertainty, insurance boundary concerns, serious moisture or termite damage, unstable conditions, or work beyond the practitioner’s registered scope.

## Stage 2 — Builder Oversight or Formal Transfer Required

Stage 2 applies where the work no longer appears suitable to continue solely under the RRC practitioner’s authority and registered builder oversight, control or formal transfer becomes necessary.

Typical Stage 2 triggers may include substantial enlargement of project scope, major framing alteration, unresolved questions about whether the work remains within a trade-scale renovation category, or a discovered condition that changes the project from isolated remedial work into broader domestic building work. Some trigger settings may overlap with Stage 3 and **REQUIRE LEGAL REVIEW** in final drafting.

The handoff should be documented through a dated handoff decision record. At minimum, that record should identify the original scope, the discovered trigger, the portion of work paused, interim risk controls, the homeowner notification given, and whether the builder is assuming oversight, taking responsibility for a revised scope, or fully taking over the affected work.

Once engaged, the builder’s responsibilities would include assessing whether the revised work falls within domestic builder responsibility, determining whether permit or specialist involvement is required, deciding whether the existing practitioner may continue under the builder’s direction for defined components, revising scope and contractual arrangements where needed, and ensuring the work proceeds within the builder’s lawful authority. Final allocation of responsibility **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW**.

The practitioner retains residual obligations even after Stage 2 is triggered. Those obligations include making the affected area safe, preserving evidence of the discovered condition, not continuing the escalated component without authority, and cooperating with the builder’s assessment.

Evidence at Stage 2 should include the Stage 1 record set plus the handoff decision record, homeowner notice, builder engagement details, any revised scope documentation, instructions received from the builder, and a record of whether work paused pending transfer.

## Stage 3 — Engineer, Building Surveyor or Specialist Involvement Required

Stage 3 applies where a trigger requires engineer, building surveyor or specialist involvement before the affected work can safely or lawfully proceed.

Triggers may include unresolved load-path concerns, work requiring design, permit or statutory questions, unsafe or unstable conditions, serious structural deterioration, suspected illegal prior work with permit implications, regulated specialist trade work, or other circumstances where professional boundaries require a formal external assessment. Final trigger wording, authority pathways and threshold definitions **REQUIRE LEGAL REVIEW**.

Specialist involvement should be initiated by recording the trigger, notifying the owner, identifying the required specialist category, and arranging the appropriate referral pathway. Depending on final legal design, this may occur directly, through a registered builder, or through another prescribed mechanism. Whether an RRC practitioner may directly appoint every specialist **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW**.

Coordination requirements in Stage 3 include preserving the discovered condition as far as reasonably practicable for inspection, sharing relevant records with the engaged specialist, clearly distinguishing observations from conclusions, and integrating specialist directions into the revised work plan.

Documentation should include the trigger record, photographs and site notes, specialist referral details, any engineering advice or report, permit-related records, revised scope documents, records of work pause and recommencement, and final verification of the implemented response. In most Stage 3 cases, the affected work should pause pending specialist input. Limited temporary works may be undertaken only to make the area safe or preserve the site for assessment.

## Potential Trigger Categories

| Trigger Category | Likely Stage | Notes | Requires Development |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concealed structural damage | Stage 2 or 3 | Depends on extent, certainty and whether design input is required | Yes |
| Unresolved load-path concern | Stage 3 | Strong indicator for engineering review | Yes |
| Major framing alteration | Stage 2 or 3 | May require builder transfer and possibly engineering input | Yes |
| Substantial expansion of project scope | Stage 2 | Suggests formal builder oversight or transfer | Yes |
| Multiple dwellings | Stage 2 or 3 | Higher complexity and possible statutory implications | Yes |
| Work requiring design | Stage 3 | Design responsibility should not be assumed informally | Yes |
| Permit or statutory involvement | Stage 3 | Building surveyor or builder pathway likely required | Yes |
| Serious moisture, termite or decay damage | Stage 2 or 3 | Depends on severity and structural implications | Yes |
| Work outside the practitioner's registered scope | Stage 2 or 3 | Immediate escalation required | Yes |
| Unsafe or unstable conditions | Stage 3 | Work should normally pause except for making safe | Yes |
| Specialist trade work | Stage 3 | Licensed trade or other specialist involvement required | Yes |
| Insurance boundary exceeded | Stage 2 or 3 | Insurance settings require insurer input and legal definition | Yes |
| Progressive discovery of unknown previous alterations | Stage 2 or 3 | Escalation depends on effect on risk, design and legality | Yes |
| Suspected illegal prior work | Stage 3 | May require builder, surveyor or regulator pathway | Yes |

## Handoff Documentation Requirements

| Handoff Stage | Minimum Documents Required |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 continuing within scope | Project identity record; practitioner registration confirmation; homeowner scope acknowledgement; scope statement; before-work photographs; discovery record if relevant; scope-change assessment record |
| Stage 2 builder oversight or transfer | All Stage 1 records plus handoff decision record; homeowner notification; builder engagement details; revised scope or transfer record; pause/resume record; builder instructions where applicable |
| Stage 3 specialist involvement | All Stage 2 records plus specialist referral record; engineering, surveyor or specialist advice; permit-related records where applicable; revised responsibility allocation; final verification of implemented response |

## Limitations and Development Requirements

These proposed stages are not current legal triggers and should not be represented as operative law.

Final trigger definitions require statutory drafting and government consultation. Multiple elements in this document therefore **REQUIRE LEGAL REVIEW**, including trigger thresholds, authority pathways, documentation status, pause requirements and interaction with existing domestic building law.

Insurance and liability boundaries also require insurer and legal input. This document does not claim that a documented handoff automatically transfers liability or creates coverage.
