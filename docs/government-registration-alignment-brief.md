# Advanced Residential Carpentry
## Alignment with Victoria's Enhanced Practitioner Registration Reform

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria
**Audience:** Department of Transport and Planning — Building Policy Reform Team
**Date:** August 2026
**Status:** CURRENT — Government-facing alignment brief
**Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Purpose

Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria is not asking government to predetermine a new statutory class. We are asking that advanced residential renovation carpentry be assessed as a candidate practitioner function within Victoria's enhanced registration framework.

A controlled capability-assessment pilot could test whether the proposed framework:

- identifies advanced renovation competence reliably;
- establishes clear occupational boundaries;
- improves consumer understanding of practitioner scope;
- supports appropriate escalation of complex or concealed conditions;
- produces useful evidence and accountability records; and
- can be assessed consistently across participants.

Following a pilot, government would then determine whether the appropriate legal vehicle is:

- a distinct Registered Residential Carpenter (RRC) registration class;
- reform or modernisation of DB-L Carpentry (endorsement or stream);
- an advanced residential carpentry credential without additional statutory authority; or
- another mechanism that government considers more proportionate.

The initiative's preferred outcome is recognition of the capability problem and testing of the assessment architecture. The statutory vehicle should follow evidence.

## 2. Current Victorian Reform Direction

The Victorian Government's building reform program — described in the *Building Statement: Strengthening Victoria's Building System* (2025) and on the Building Reform web pages — is expanding and strengthening practitioner registration across the building industry.

Key reform directions relevant to this proposal include:

- expanded participant registration;
- clearer and more appropriate authorised scope;
- objective competence benchmarks;
- standardised practical experience requirements;
- assessment-based pathways for registration;
- improved consumer protection and role clarity;
- enhanced practitioner accountability; and
- ongoing practitioner capability requirements.

**Source basis:** Victorian Government Building Reform page (planning.vic.gov.au); Building Statement 2025. These are used as verified primary sources on current reform direction. No endorsement of this initiative is claimed or implied.

## 3. The Unresolved Carpentry Question

Victoria's current framework does not clearly assess or recognise the distinct capability required to manage advanced residential renovation work as a practitioner function.

Experienced renovation carpenters encounter existing-building conditions that create genuine decision complexity:

- concealed structural conditions discovered after access;
- previous alterations affecting load paths;
- deterioration that changes work scope mid-project;
- incomplete or absent documentation;
- progressive discovery requiring scope and responsibility revision; and
- genuine uncertainty about whether to proceed, investigate, refer or stop.

These are not construction activities alone. They require integrated diagnostic, structural, planning, coordination and professional judgement capability. That capability is not currently assessed as part of any Victorian practitioner registration pathway calibrated to renovation work.

The question this initiative asks government to examine is whether that capability gap is real, and if so, what — if anything — should be done about it.

## 4. Proposed Capability Model

The initiative has developed a capability framework describing seven domains of advanced residential renovation competence:

| Domain | Description |
|---|---|
| Technical Capability | Advanced renovation construction knowledge and execution |
| Diagnostic Capability | Assessment of existing conditions before and during work |
| Structural Understanding | Recognition of load paths, temporary support needs and structural implications |
| Planning Capability | Pre-work assessment, sequencing and risk identification |
| Coordination Capability | Management of trade interfaces, specialist inputs and escalation |
| Professional Judgement | Integration of all domains into sound decisions under uncertainty |
| Boundary Recognition | Clear identification of work requiring builder, engineer or licensed trade involvement |

**Professional Judgement** integrates the other capability domains. It is the capacity to observe conditions, diagnose risk, predict consequences, plan responses, execute appropriately, and verify outcomes — including the decision to stop.

The professional decision cycle is: **Observe → Diagnose → Predict → Plan → Execute → Verify.**

This framework is proposed as the assessment architecture. It has not been independently validated. Validation is a function of the proposed pilot.

## 5. Alignment with Registration Principles

The following table maps the initiative's proposed capability model against the Victorian Government's registration reform principles. Each row distinguishes between direct alignment, useful policy precedent, and our policy proposal.

| Government Reform Principle | Our Response | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Expanded participant registration** | Evaluate whether advanced residential renovation carpentry represents a distinct practitioner function requiring formal recognition or assessment. | Policy proposal — subject to government determination |
| **Clear and appropriate authorised scope** | Published bounded scope, defined exclusions and mandatory escalation triggers for each work category. | Policy proposal — bounded scope architecture developed; requires government review |
| **Objective competence benchmarks** | Independent assessment of advanced renovation-specific capability across seven domains. | Policy proposal — assessment architecture proposed; requires independent development |
| **Standardised practical experience requirements** | Verified project experience and evidence portfolio as eligibility evidence, confirming exposure — not as automatic conferral of advanced recognition. | Policy proposal — experience as eligibility, not competence |
| **Assessment for registration** | Scenario-based assessment, practical evidence review and technical questioning calibrated to renovation-specific conditions. | Policy proposal — assessment model proposed; requires independent assessor development |
| **Consumer protection and role clarity** | Mandatory consumer-facing scope statement and role-comprehension testing at onboarding and completion. | Policy proposal — consumer comprehension tested, not assumed from satisfaction alone |
| **Practitioner accountability** | Persistent Work and Evidence Record covering scope, conditions, variations, escalation events and completion status. | Policy proposal — pilot record design proposed; not yet a statutory requirement |
| **Ongoing practitioner capability** | Future CPD requirement if a formal ongoing pathway is adopted by government. | Policy proposal — conditional on government determination of implementation vehicle |

**Note on cladding reform (useful policy precedent only):** Victoria has used structured, risk-based intervention in other building contexts, including the cladding risk mitigation framework. The principle — that risk should be identified, evidenced, escalated and addressed proportionately — is a useful policy precedent for renovation escalation design. Current Victorian building-manual requirements for Class 1b, 2 and 3 buildings and cladding-specific rules are not represented as applying to Class 1a renovation carpentry and are not cited as direct legal authority.

## 6. Pilot Proposition

A controlled capability-assessment pilot is proposed as the next step before any authority change.

**The pilot grants no additional statutory authority.** Participants remain within their existing lawful authority throughout.

The pilot is designed to test:

1. practitioner eligibility verification;
2. independent assessment of seven capability domains;
3. concealed-condition recognition;
4. renovation sequencing and temporary-support awareness;
5. structural-boundary awareness;
6. lawful-scope recognition;
7. escalation judgement;
8. evidence and documentation discipline;
9. consumer-role communication and comprehension; and
10. assessor consistency across participants.

**Consumer comprehension** is tested at onboarding and at project completion — not inferred from satisfaction alone.

**Persistent Work and Evidence Record:** Each pilot project maintains a record covering practitioner identity, assessed scope, pre-existing conditions, work undertaken, evidence captured, variations, concealed conditions discovered, escalation events, professional referrals and completion status. This is a pilot governance record, not a statutory building manual.

**Government-facing evaluation question:** Does the pilot generate sufficient evidence to determine whether Advanced Residential Carpentry warrants formal recognition and, if so, through which regulatory vehicle?

**Stop provisions:** If evaluation identifies that consumer protection objectives are not being met, government can stop the pilot, modify its parameters or decline further implementation. The pilot does not lock government into any predetermined outcome.

For full pilot design, see the canonical pilot documents in the `/docs` directory.

## 7. Implementation Options

Four implementation vehicles have been identified. The initiative does not choose a winner.

### Option A — Distinct Registered Residential Carpenter Class

| | |
|---|---|
| **Advantages** | Clear occupational identity; direct consumer recognition; tailored scope for renovation work |
| **Risks** | Greater legislative and regulatory complexity; possible duplication with DB-L Carpentry class |

### Option B — DB-L Carpentry Renovation Endorsement or Stream

| | |
|---|---|
| **Advantages** | Uses existing registration architecture; potentially lower implementation friction |
| **Risks** | May blur advanced trade competence with builder responsibility; requires careful occupational distinction |

### Option C — Advanced Residential Carpentry Credential (no additional statutory authority)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Advantages** | Lowest-risk pilot vehicle; tests capability first; creates evidence before authority changes |
| **Risks** | Limited immediate market authority; requires clear consumer communication of credential meaning |

### Option D — Government-Selected Alternative

| | |
|---|---|
| **Advantages** | Preserves government flexibility; can fit emerging registration reform directions |
| **Risks** | Uncertain implementation shape at this stage |

## 8. Consumer Protection Position

The initiative does not frame this reform primarily as career progression for carpenters. The consumer rationale is:

- clearer practitioner role in renovation projects;
- better consumer understanding of authorised scope;
- earlier escalation of concealed or complex conditions;
- clearer responsibility when scope changes;
- traceable work and variation records; and
- reduced risk of consumers commissioning work under unclear responsibility assumptions.

The proposed model is intended to test whether clearer competence recognition, scope boundaries and escalation responsibilities can improve consumer understanding and accountability.

No claim is made that the proposal will reduce defects. That is an empirical question the pilot is designed to begin exploring, not an assumed outcome.

## 9. Work and Evidence Record

The initiative proposes a persistent **Advanced Residential Carpentry Work and Evidence Record** as a pilot governance document.

This is a pilot record — not a statutory building manual.

Its purpose in the pilot is to test whether persistent records improve:

- scope traceability across a project;
- concealed-condition discovery documentation;
- variation and scope-change history;
- escalation event history;
- practitioner responsibility records; and
- later understanding of the renovation work performed.

**Victorian building-manual reforms** for specified Class 1b, 2 and 3 buildings are noted as useful policy precedent — demonstrating that Victoria recognises the value of persistent building records in certain contexts. However, current Victorian building-manual requirements for Class 1b, 2 and 3 buildings are not represented as applying to Class 1a renovation work, and this record is not proposed as a statutory instrument at this stage.

## 10. Proportional Regulation

Victoria has used structured, risk-based intervention in other building contexts — including the cladding risk mitigation framework — demonstrating that proportionate, evidence-based responses to building risk are achievable within the existing regulatory framework.

The principle applied here is: risk should be identified, evidenced, escalated and addressed proportionately — rather than assuming every uncertainty requires the same regulatory response.

In the renovation context, this means:

- stop/proceed decisions based on discovered conditions;
- specialist referral when diagnostic uncertainty exceeds competence;
- engineering escalation for structural uncertainty;
- builder involvement when scope exceeds the practitioner function; and
- evidence capture regardless of the outcome chosen.

**Classification:** The cladding framework is cited as useful policy precedent only. Its specific legal rules — including Class 2 and 3 remediation requirements and cladding-specific regulatory powers — are not represented as applicable to Class 1a residential renovation carpentry.

## 11. Government Decision Requested

Five questions are offered for government consideration:

1. Does government consider advanced residential renovation carpentry a legitimate capability question within practitioner-registration reform?

2. Would the Department of Transport and Planning or the Building and Plumbing Commission consider reviewing the proposed capability domains and bounded scope architecture?

3. Would government consider a controlled capability-assessment pilot that grants no additional statutory authority?

4. Which statutory vehicle — if any — should be evaluated after pilot evidence is available?

5. Which DTP or BPC team should own or coordinate this assessment?

## 12. Important Limitations

The following limitations apply to all claims in this brief:

- No government endorsement of this initiative is claimed or implied. Government correspondence and engagement mean only that the proposal has been received and considered.
- Current Victorian building reform sources are used only as primary evidence of reform direction. They do not confirm that advanced residential carpentry has been or will be included in any reform stream.
- The capability framework, assessment model and pilot design are proposals. They have not been independently validated, legally reviewed or government-approved.
- The initiative does not claim that advanced carpenters are already included in any registration expansion.
- Cladding-specific legal rules are not applied to Class 1a renovation carpentry.
- Plumbing registration classes are not represented as legally applicable to carpentry.
- The pilot does not grant any statutory authority.
- Historical documents produced before the capability-first pivot may retain earlier RRC-first framing and should be read as legacy material unless separately updated.
