# RRC Operational Pilot Brief
**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
Version: 1.0 — Working Draft
Date: July 2026
Status: WORKING DRAFT — Not government policy. Requires government, legal, regulatory and insurer development before implementation.

## Pilot Purpose

- **PROPOSED:** The pilot is intended to test whether a bounded Registered Residential Carpenter (RRC) pathway can improve consumer protection, clearer responsibility allocation and better documentation practices in residential renovation work in Victoria.
- The pilot is a controlled test, not statewide implementation. It is designed to generate operational evidence before any broader policy or regulatory consideration.
- The pilot is framed around practical renovation conditions, including concealed defects, progressive discovery, changing scope and the need for timely escalation to registered builders, engineers, building surveyors and licensed trades.
- Existing builder pathways remain unchanged. Registered builders retain all existing authority. Engineers retain engineering responsibilities. Building surveyors retain surveying and certification responsibilities.

## Policy Question Being Tested

- **PROPOSED:** Can an experienced renovation carpenter operate within a clearly bounded, auditable and consumer-facing pathway without weakening existing builder, engineer, surveyor or licensed-trade responsibilities?
- The pilot tests whether defined entry screening, project registration, evidence capture, disclosure and escalation requirements can reduce ambiguity at the renovation scope boundary.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION:** Government would need to determine whether the policy objective is improved boundary clarity, a new recognition pathway, a pilot of administrative endorsement, or another limited mechanism.
- **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT:** Regulatory agencies would need to advise whether the proposed pilot produces useful evidence about compliance, enforcement and consumer outcomes.

## Proposed Pilot Authority or Sponsor

- **PROPOSED:** The pilot could be sponsored by the Victorian Government, an authorised regulator, or another formally approved public-interest body with a clear consumer protection mandate.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A pilot sponsor would appoint an administrative unit or delivery partner to receive applications, maintain pilot records and coordinate audit and evaluation functions.
- Daniel Sonsie is the founder and proponent of Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria. He is not a government official and should not be presented as holding public authority.
- Contact for proposal materials and practitioner-led background information: admin@sonsiecarpentry.com.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION** and **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW:** Any formal sponsor, delivery arrangement or delegated authority would need lawful design before implementation.

## Proposed Participant Cohort

- **PROPOSED:** The participant cohort should be limited to a small number of experienced Victorian residential renovation carpenters to control exposure and allow close monitoring.
- A pilot should prefer practitioners with substantial hands-on renovation experience across bathrooms, kitchens, structural repair interfaces, subfloor work, cladding rectification, window and door replacement, and concealed-condition work.
- Participants should be capable of working within professional boundaries, maintaining records and escalating promptly when work exceeds the defined pilot scope.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A small cohort supports higher-quality auditing and more reliable evaluation than a large intake in the first pilot round.

## Entry Requirements

- **PROPOSED:** Entry should require demonstrated renovation experience, identity verification, business registration status where relevant, and evidence of recent residential renovation practice in Victoria.
- Applicants should provide a portfolio showing technical capability, diagnostic capability, planning capability, coordination capability, professional judgement and boundary recognition.
- Applicants should declare any relevant disciplinary history, unresolved serious complaints or prior compliance actions affecting suitability for a pilot involving consumer-facing work.
- **REQUIRES INSURER INPUT:** Insurance or risk-transfer settings cannot be assumed. Any required cover, exclusions or conditions must be designed with insurers.
- **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW** and **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT:** Any formal eligibility settings must align with existing law, licensing settings and enforcement practice.

## Assessment and Selection Process

- **PROPOSED:** Assessment should involve document screening, portfolio review and structured panel assessment against a published capability framework.
- Assessment criteria should focus on demonstrated judgement in renovation conditions, especially decisions involving uncertainty, concealed conditions, staging, documentation and escalation.
- Selection should favour applicants who can show consistent evidence habits, clear homeowner communication and prior coordination with builders, engineers and licensed trades.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A capped intake allows borderline or unsuitable applicants to be excluded without pressure to fill places.

## Geographic Boundary

- **PROPOSED:** The pilot should operate within a clearly defined Victorian geographic boundary to support audit, oversight and manageable stakeholder coordination.
- The boundary could be metropolitan, regional, or mixed, provided that audit access, stakeholder awareness and escalation pathways are workable within that area.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION:** The geographic boundary should be set according to regulator capacity, participant availability and evaluation design rather than advocacy preference.
- The pilot should publish its geographic limit clearly so consumers, builders, councils and practitioners understand whether a project falls inside the test area.

## Time Boundary

- **PROPOSED:** The pilot should run for a defined period long enough to observe project commencement, progressive discovery events, completion outcomes, complaints and audit findings.
- The operating period should include a startup phase, an active project phase and a closing evaluation phase.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A time-limited design reduces policy risk and reinforces that the pilot is a controlled test rather than an implied permanent entitlement.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION:** Final pilot duration, commencement date and review points should be set by the sponsor after consultation with regulators and evaluation advisers.

## Included Work

- **PROPOSED:** Included work should be limited to defined residential renovation carpentry tasks within a bounded operating model and subject to project registration, disclosure, evidence capture and escalation rules.
- Included categories may involve renovation carpentry where existing conditions must be assessed and managed, provided the work remains within the pilot's published limits and does not displace existing builder, engineer, surveyor or licensed-trade responsibilities.
- Included work should be described by function and boundary conditions, not only by labels that risk oversimplifying renovation complexity.
- **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT** and **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW:** Included work descriptions must be tested against existing statutory categories, permit settings and enforcement realities.

## Excluded Work

- **PROPOSED:** Excluded work should expressly include any activity that would make the pilot operate as a substitute for a domestic builder pathway or a substitute for engineering, surveying or licensed-trade functions.
- Exclusions should cover new home construction, unrestricted structural decision-making, work beyond the pilot boundary, and any project that cannot be responsibly managed within the pilot's documentation and escalation model.
- Work should also be excluded where the homeowner refuses required disclosure, evidence capture, handoff or specialist involvement.
- **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW:** Exclusion language must avoid creating false permission, false immunity or conflict with existing legal responsibilities.

## Project Eligibility

- **PROPOSED:** Each project should be screened before commencement to confirm that it falls within the pilot boundary, is located within the pilot geography and can comply with pilot evidence and escalation rules.
- Eligibility should consider renovation context, foreseeable specialist interfaces, homeowner willingness to accept pilot disclosures, and whether a practical escalation pathway exists if progressive discovery occurs.
- Projects should not enter the pilot if the scope is already clearly outside the bounded model at the time of registration.
- **ASSUMPTION:** Early project screening is likely to prevent inappropriate pilot use more effectively than relying only on post-commencement audit.

## Practitioner Obligations

- **PROPOSED:** Participating practitioners should comply with all pilot conditions, maintain complete records, use the prescribed homeowner disclosures and follow escalation requirements without delay.
- Practitioners should not represent the pilot as government endorsement, statewide registration or authority beyond the published pilot scope.
- Practitioners should stop and escalate where the work exceeds the pilot boundary, where concealed conditions change the risk, or where required specialist input becomes necessary.
- Practitioners should cooperate with audits, complaint handling, data requests and evaluation interviews.

## Homeowner Disclosure

- **PROPOSED:** Before work begins, homeowners should receive a plain-language disclosure explaining the pilot, the bounded role of the practitioner, the limits of the pathway and the circumstances requiring escalation or handoff.
- Disclosure should state clearly that the pilot is not a domestic builder licence and does not remove the roles of registered builders, engineers, building surveyors or licensed trades.
- Homeowners should be told what records will be kept, how complaints may be made, and that the pilot is a working draft concept requiring formal government, legal, regulatory and insurer development.
- **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW** and **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT:** Consumer wording should be tested to ensure it informs without misleading.

## Builder and Engineer Escalation

- **PROPOSED:** A core pilot safeguard is mandatory escalation when project conditions indicate that builder responsibility, engineering judgement, surveying input or licensed-trade involvement is required.
- Escalation triggers should include progressive discovery, concealed damage, changed load paths, uncertainty about compliance pathway, specialist interfaces and any circumstance where continuing within the pilot would weaken consumer protection.
- The preferred operational sequence is to make the area safe, pause the affected work, record the condition, notify the homeowner, obtain appropriate specialist input, revise the scope and verify the response.
- Engineers retain engineering responsibilities. Registered builders retain existing authority over work outside the pilot boundary.

## Statutory Handoff Process

- **PROPOSED:** Where a project moves beyond the pilot boundary, there should be a documented handoff process to the appropriate registered builder, engineer, building surveyor or licensed trade.
- Handoff records should identify the trigger, the condition discovered, the work paused, the party notified and the evidence transferred to the receiving professional.
- The practitioner should not continue affected work until responsibility, scope and required approvals or arrangements have been clarified.
- **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW** and **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT:** Any handoff design must fit within existing statutory settings and avoid implying powers that do not currently exist.

## Evidence Requirements

- **PROPOSED:** Each pilot project should have a complete evidence file containing application and admission records, homeowner disclosures, site observations, scope descriptions, dated photographs, escalation notes, specialist communications, completion records and audit correspondence.
- Evidence should support later review of what was known, what changed, what was decided and when escalation occurred.
- Records should capture the professional decision cycle of observe, diagnose, predict, plan, execute and verify where relevant to the project.
- **ASSUMPTION:** Strong evidence requirements are necessary because residential renovation regularly involves progressive discovery and changed conditions.

## Record Retention

- **PROPOSED:** Pilot records should be retained for a defined period set by the sponsor to support audit, complaint review, evaluation and any lawful regulatory inquiry.
- Retention settings should apply to both practitioner-held and administrator-held records, with clear standards for completeness, accessibility and secure storage.
- Digital storage is preferable where it improves retrieval and auditability, but minimum format requirements should also consider small-practice capability.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION** and **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW:** Retention duration, privacy settings and access rights require formal design.

## Audit Process

- **PROPOSED:** Audit should include routine file reviews, targeted audits following complaints or anomalies, and close-out sampling after project completion.
- Audits should examine whether a project was eligible, whether required disclosures were used, whether evidence files are complete, and whether escalation occurred when required.
- Audit should test behaviour, not only paperwork. A technically complete file should not excuse failure to pause and escalate when conditions changed.
- **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT:** Audit powers, sanctions, remedial actions and referral pathways need regulator advice.

## Complaint and Incident Process

- **PROPOSED:** The pilot should include a clear process for receiving complaints from homeowners, builders, regulators, neighbours or other affected parties, and for recording safety incidents or serious scope breaches.
- Complaints should be acknowledged, triaged, documented and either handled within the pilot process or referred to the appropriate regulator or authority.
- Serious incidents should trigger immediate notification to the pilot administrator and, where appropriate, the relevant regulator or specialist authority.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION, REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT** and **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW:** The complaint pathway must align with existing consumer, safety and building oversight frameworks.

## Insurance Assumptions

- **ASSUMPTION:** A viable pilot is likely to require some form of insurer engagement, eligibility confirmation or risk treatment before commencement.
- The pilot should not assume that an existing product automatically applies, that coverage is available on acceptable terms, or that a new insurance product would be created.
- **REQUIRES INSURER INPUT:** Insurers would need to advise what information, controls, exclusions and claims-history data would be required to assess the pilot.
- **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW:** Any public description of insurance settings must avoid overstating cover, consumer recourse or market readiness.

## Regulator and Stakeholder Roles

- **PROPOSED:** Regulators, consumer representatives, builders, engineers, surveyors, licensed trades, training organisations and insurers should have defined roles in pilot design, oversight or evaluation.
- Regulators should advise on feasibility, audit design, complaint interfaces and data needs. Builders and engineers should advise on escalation triggers and boundary protection. Consumer bodies should review disclosure clarity and complaints accessibility.
- Insurers should advise on insurability assumptions. Training or assessment bodies may advise on evidence standards and capability assessment design.
- Daniel Sonsie may provide practitioner evidence and reform rationale but should not be represented as the approving authority.

## Evaluation Methodology

- **PROPOSED:** Evaluation should combine administrative data, audit results, project file review, participant interviews, homeowner feedback and stakeholder consultation.
- The methodology should distinguish verified evidence from practitioner evidence, policy analysis and scenario testing.
- Evaluation should test whether the pilot improved boundary clarity, documentation quality, escalation behaviour and consumer understanding relative to baseline expectations.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION:** Government should determine whether evaluation is internal, external or independently commissioned.

## Success Measures

- **PROPOSED:** Success should be assessed by whether the pilot strengthens consumer protection and responsibility clarity without destabilising the existing framework.
- Relevant measures may include project registration compliance, completeness of evidence files, appropriate use of escalation, homeowner understanding of practitioner limits, audit pass rates and stakeholder confidence in boundary protection.
- Additional measures may include whether complaints reveal confusion, whether inappropriate projects were screened out early, and whether handoff processes operated in a timely and documented way.
- The pilot should not be judged only by uptake. A small, disciplined pilot may be more successful than rapid expansion with weak controls.

## Failure Indicators

- Failure indicators should include repeated non-compliance with disclosure or evidence requirements, delayed escalation, inappropriate entry of out-of-scope projects, or patterns suggesting boundary drift into builder responsibility.
- Other indicators include recurring homeowner misunderstanding, material audit failures, unresolved insurer concerns, regulator concerns about enforceability or a pattern of complaints showing the model is not practically workable.
- **PROPOSED:** A key failure indicator is if the pilot creates more ambiguity, not less.
- Failure should be assessed conservatively because consumer protection is the primary objective.

## Stop, Pause or Modification Triggers

- **PROPOSED:** The sponsor should be able to stop, pause or modify the pilot if consumer harm risks increase, audit results deteriorate, escalation failures recur or stakeholder evidence shows the controls are inadequate.
- A pause may also be appropriate if insurer assumptions prove incorrect, if legal review identifies conflicts, or if regulatory capacity is insufficient to oversee the model safely.
- Modification triggers may include narrowing the scope, reducing the cohort, strengthening evidence requirements or increasing pre-project screening.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION:** Stop and pause powers must be formally designed and clearly communicated.

## Transition After Pilot

- **PROPOSED:** At the end of the pilot, government may discontinue the concept, refine it for further testing, or consider whether a more formal pathway deserves development.
- No participant should assume automatic transition to permanent registration, statutory endorsement or expanded authority after pilot completion.
- A closing process should address active projects, retained records, final complaints, evaluation publication and stakeholder briefing.
- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION, REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW** and **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT:** Any post-pilot transition path would require separate formal consideration.

## Matters Requiring Government, Legal, Regulatory or Insurance Development

- **REQUIRES GOVERNMENT DECISION:** pilot sponsor, pilot geography, cohort size, duration, governance model, evaluation method and stop/pause powers.
- **REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW:** lawful pilot architecture, consumer disclosures, excluded and included scope wording, handoff arrangements, privacy and record retention settings, and any public claims about authority or insurance.
- **REQUIRES REGULATOR INPUT:** feasibility, audit design, complaint referral pathways, project registration data fields, escalation triggers, enforcement interfaces and boundary protection.
- **REQUIRES INSURER INPUT:** market feasibility, eligibility conditions, evidence expectations, exclusions, claims handling assumptions and whether the proposed controls materially change insurability.
- **PROPOSED:** Until those matters are resolved, the RRC pilot should be treated as a structured implementation concept rather than an approved operational scheme.
