## Purpose Notice

This document constructs the strongest defensible BASE CASE against additional formal recognition of Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC) within Victoria's building regulatory framework.

This is not advocacy against ARC. It is a governed policy analysis exercise.

The governing questions are:

> Given Victoria's existing training, supervision, builder-registration, DB-L, statutory, consumer-protection and enforcement architecture, what residual public-interest problem — if any — remains that could justify additional intervention?

> If a residual problem remains, what is the lightest intervention capable of materially addressing it?

No regulatory outcome is predetermined. The no-action option (status quo) is retained as a legitimate outcome throughout.

## Part 1 — Third-Party Claim Audit

### 1.1 Purpose

A third-party challenge exercise identified approximately twenty regulator objections. That exercise is treated as a SEARCH and ANALYSIS LEAD only. Its factual claims are not inherited without independent verification against the project's governed evidence register.

### 1.2 Audit Classification Scale

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SUPPORTED | Claim is consistent with a directly verified source or governed finding |
| SUPPORTED_WITH_NARROWING | Claim is partially supported but requires qualification to reflect actual evidence |
| PROJECT_ALREADY_ADDRESSES | The project has already developed a treatment of this question |
| NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED | The claim is not established by a verified source accessible to this review |
| CONTRADICTED_BY_GOVERNED_FINDING | A current governed finding directly contradicts the claim |
| REQUIRES_GOVERNMENT_CLARIFICATION | The claim depends on information only government can provide |
| REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW | The claim raises a legal question not answerable from publicly available material |
| DO_NOT_USE | The claim is speculative, misleading or harmful to the analytical integrity of the project |

### 1.3 Audit of Material Third-Party Propositions

**Proposition 1: DB-L Carpentry already provides the relevant formal pathway.**

Classification: SUPPORTED_WITH_NARROWING

BPC public material confirms DB-L Carpentry expressly includes bathroom, kitchen and laundry renovations, external wall cladding, framing, and door and window replacement. This is directly verified. However, whether DB-L assessment tests the depth of judgement required to manage renovation-specific complexity is PUBLIC_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT_TO_DETERMINE (Finding D / 75%). The claim that DB-L is sufficient cannot be established from public evidence alone, nor can it be dismissed.

**Proposition 2: DB-L assessment tests particular judgement functions relevant to advanced renovation.**

Classification: NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED

Caution preserved. DB-L assessment architecture and depth are not publicly documented. This claim must not be treated as verified.

**Proposition 3: All higher-order renovation decisions are assigned to the builder or supervisor.**

Classification: NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED

Real-World Workflow B / 72% and Senior Autonomy B / 71% establish a plausible partial residual function in senior experienced carpenters. The claim of complete builder or supervisor assignment is not directly supported.

**Proposition 4: Minimum-effective-intervention analysis has not previously been conducted for ARC.**

Classification: PROJECT_ALREADY_ADDRESSES

The project's implementation options comparison matrix and DB-L implementation options analysis contain minimum-effective-intervention reasoning. This analysis extends that existing work.

**Proposition 5: Economic analysis, insurer participation and government data are mandatory preconditions for every intervention option.**

Classification: DO_NOT_USE

These are appropriate requirements at the regulatory impact statement stage. They are not mandatory for every option at this early analytical stage. Treating them as universal preconditions would prevent the project from reaching any governed position.

**Proposition 6: Advanced competence automatically creates regulatory need.**

Classification: DO_NOT_USE

Advanced competence ≠ regulatory need. A practitioner capability gap is a precondition for policy consideration, not a sufficient basis for statutory intervention. The project preserves this separation throughout.

**Proposition 7: The formal existence of a DB-L pathway proves functional sufficiency.**

Classification: CONTRADICTED_BY_GOVERNED_FINDING

Finding D / 75% preserves the position that functional sufficiency of DB-L assessment is not established from public evidence. Formal scope ≠ assessed depth.

**Proposition 8: Absence of statutory carpenter authority disproves practical renovation capability.**

Classification: DO_NOT_USE

Authority and capability are separate questions. Absence of statutory authority does not establish absence of practical capability.

## Part 2 — Strongest Base Case: No Additional ARC-Specific Intervention

### 2.1 Statement of the Base Case

> Victoria's current legal, qualification, registration, supervision, consumer-protection and enforcement architecture may already materially address the public-interest problem that ARC-specific intervention is proposed to solve.

This base case is tested honestly. It is not advocated.

### 2.2 Architecture Elements Tested

**CPC Trade Qualification Framework**

A Certificate III in Carpentry (CPC30220 or current equivalent) provides a nationally recognised qualification with prescribed competency units. Certificate IV and Diploma pathways exist for more complex work. This framework is accessible without new recognition mechanisms.

Limitation: Certificate III does not define advanced renovation capability in the terms used by the project's seven-domain capability model. Certificate IV and Diploma uptake in the renovation workforce is unknown.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — qualification framework provides a base; advanced renovation capability depth is not established by current publicly available VET unit analysis alone.

**Employer and Builder Supervision**

Under the Building Act 1993 (Vic) and associated regulations, a registered builder (DB-U or equivalent) carries the statutory obligation for building work performed under their registration. Supervisory arrangements are a normal feature of the building industry. Advanced renovation judgement functions may be discharged through builder-supervised workflows.

Limitation: The extent to which supervision is actually present and effective in renovation work is not established by direct field evidence. Senior Autonomy B / 71% indicates experienced carpenters may operate with reduced supervision in practice.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — supervision is a real mechanism; whether it reliably captures advanced renovation complexity in practice is unknown.

**Leading-Hand, Foreperson and Site-Supervisor Roles**

Industry practice includes leading-hand and foreperson roles that may discharge coordination and decision functions on renovation sites. These roles are not formally registered but operate within the builder-supervision chain.

Limitation: These roles are not standardised or assessed for the renovation-specific capability domains identified in the project's capability model.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — industry roles may absorb some function; coverage, reliability and assessment are unknown.

**DB-L Carpentry**

DB-L Carpentry is a registered building practitioner class with a defined scope that expressly includes residential renovation work. It is the most direct existing pathway mechanism.

Limitation: DB-L Assessment Depth Finding D / 75% preserves the position that assessment depth relative to the ARC capability model is not publicly determinable. The formal scope of DB-L does not establish the depth of assessed capability.

Assessment: CANDIDATE_EXISTING_MECHANISM — requires government clarification of assessment architecture to determine whether it already addresses the problem.

**DB-U Builder Responsibility**

A registered builder's statutory responsibility encompasses the quality and safety of building work performed under their registration. Consumer warranties, insurance requirements and enforcement mechanisms apply at this level.

Limitation: Builder responsibility attaches to contractual and statutory obligations. It does not directly address whether the practitioner executing renovation work has been assessed for the relevant capability domains.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — systemic accountability exists; practitioner capability assessment at the execution level is a separate question.

**Building Surveyor Role**

A building surveyor is responsible for issuing building permits, conducting inspections and issuing certificates of final inspection. The surveying function provides a checkpoint at defined stages of building work.

Limitation: Surveys occur at staged inspection points. They are not continuous supervision of renovation decision-making. Progressive discovery issues that arise between inspection stages may not be captured.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — regulatory checkpoint function exists; not a substitute for capability assessment at the practitioner level.

**Engineering Role**

Structural and civil engineers carry responsibility for engineering assessments and specifications. Where renovation work involves structural elements, engineering input is the appropriate mechanism.

Limitation: Not all renovation work triggers an engineering requirement. The boundary between carpentry work requiring engineering input and carpentry work that does not is the subject of the project's boundary recognition capability domain.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — engineering responsibility is clear where triggered; the question of when engineering is required is itself a capability question for the executing practitioner.

**Licensed-Trade Boundaries**

Plumbing, electrical, waterproofing and other licensed trades carry defined regulatory responsibilities. Their engagement in renovation projects is governed by statute.

Assessment: RELEVANT_BUT_SEPARATE — licensed-trade obligations define adjacent boundaries but do not address carpentry-specific renovation capability within those boundaries.

**WHS Obligations**

Work health and safety legislation imposes duties on persons conducting a business or undertaking and on workers. These obligations apply to renovation work.

Limitation: WHS obligations are process and duty obligations. They do not create a capability assessment mechanism for advanced renovation judgement.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — safety framework exists; capability assessment is a separate function.

**Permit and Inspection Architecture**

Victoria's building permit and inspection regime requires permits for defined categories of building work and staged inspections. This provides a structured oversight mechanism.

Limitation: Not all renovation work requires a permit. The scope of permit-exempt work is material to understanding where oversight gaps may exist.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — inspection architecture provides staged oversight; coverage of all renovation work is not complete.

**Consumer Protection Mechanisms**

The Australian Consumer Law (Cth), domestic building contracts regime, Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal jurisdiction and the Victorian Managed Insurance Authority framework provide consumer protection and dispute resolution mechanisms.

Limitation: These mechanisms are reactive. They address harm after it has occurred. They do not prevent capability-related errors during renovation work.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — remediation mechanisms exist; harm prevention through capability assessment is a separate function.

**Warranty and Assurance Arrangements**

Victoria's domestic building insurance requirements provide a form of consumer assurance for defined work above the prescribed value threshold.

Limitation: Insurance attaches to contractual and builder obligations. It does not guarantee practitioner capability at the renovation-judgement level.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — financial protection mechanism exists; capability assurance is a distinct function.

**Complaints and Enforcement**

The Building and Plumbing Commission and associated bodies provide complaint handling, disciplinary and enforcement mechanisms for registered practitioners.

Limitation: Enforcement operates after a complaint is made. It does not provide upstream capability assessment.

Assessment: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSES — accountability mechanism exists; upstream prevention through capability assessment is separate.

**Voluntary and Market Recognition**

Industry bodies, employer recognition, training provider credentials and procurement requirements may provide informal signals of advanced renovation capability.

Limitation: No verified evidence of a currently operating voluntary advanced renovation carpentry recognition mechanism in Victoria has been identified in this review.

Assessment: POTENTIAL_MECHANISM — not currently verified as operational.

### 2.3 Summary of Base-Case Assessment

The base case establishes that multiple existing mechanisms partially address elements of the identified problem. No single mechanism fully addresses it. The combined architecture may address the problem sufficiently, but this cannot be confirmed without further information about DB-L assessment depth, the actual coverage of supervision arrangements in renovation work, and the incidence of harm attributable to practitioner capability gaps.

The base case is PLAUSIBLE but NOT_ESTABLISHED as definitively sufficient.

## Part 7 — Supervision Falsification Test

### 7.1 Case: Supervision Explains the Higher-Order Function

Supervision as a complete explanation is tested by asking whether the leading-hand, foreperson, site-supervisor or builder role reliably captures all renovation-specific higher-order judgement functions identified in the ARC capability model.

The case for supervision as a complete explanation:

- Builder-registration obligation encompasses all building work performed under registration.
- Site supervisors and forepersons are present in many renovation contexts.
- Leading-hands may direct tradespeople on complex tasks.
- Builder statutory responsibility creates an incentive to ensure adequate supervision.
- Progressive discovery events would typically prompt escalation to the supervising builder.

This case is plausible in well-resourced, well-supervised renovation contexts.

### 7.2 Case: Supervision Does Not Fully Explain the Function

The case against supervision as a complete explanation:

- Senior Autonomy B / 71% documents a pattern of experienced carpenters operating without close supervision on renovation projects.
- Renovation projects frequently involve discovery events in real time that require immediate on-site judgment before a supervisor can be consulted.
- Small-scale and owner-builder adjacent renovation contexts may lack a consistently present supervising builder.
- The timing of renovation-specific decisions (during exposure of concealed conditions) may not allow for supervisor consultation.
- DB-L Carpentry scope does not itself establish that DB-L practitioners are functioning as supervising builders on all renovation sites where carpenters exercise these judgements.

### 7.3 Residual Function After Supervision Subtraction

After removing functions that can be attributed to leading-hand, foreperson, site-supervisor and builder supervision:

RESIDUAL: **PARTIAL_RESIDUAL_FUNCTION**

Preserved from governing findings. Not changed by reasoning alone. Direct field evidence is required to materially change this classification.

Specific residual dimensions identified:

- In-situ diagnostic assessment during progressive discovery before supervisor availability.
- Boundary recognition decisions about when to pause, escalate or continue in real time.
- Condition-specific documentation where the first responsible practitioner is the executing carpenter.

These functions are not established as material or systemic from current evidence. They remain PARTIAL and require field evidence for reclassification.
