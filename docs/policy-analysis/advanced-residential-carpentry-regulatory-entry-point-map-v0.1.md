## Purpose and Scope

This document maps the current Victorian regulatory and administrative entry points that could potentially be relevant to the recognition of Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC) capability.

The central question addressed is:

> **If the Advanced Residential Carpentry capability model is later shown to be useful and materially distinct, which parts could potentially be implemented administratively, which may require regulation change, which may require primary legislation, and which could remain non-statutory?**

This is not a legal opinion.  
This is not a recommendation to create the Registered Residential Carpenter (RRC) class.  
This is not a recommendation to reform DB-L Carpentry.  
This is not a government submission.

This document is a regulatory architecture map based on current publicly verifiable evidence.

# PART 1 — Current Victorian Regulatory Stack

## Overview

The Victorian building regulatory framework operates across four tiers relevant to practitioner recognition. Each tier carries different legal weight and requires different processes to amend.

---

## Tier A — Primary Legislation

### Building Act 1993

The *Building Act 1993* (Vic) is the principal statute governing building regulation in Victoria. It establishes the framework for registration of building practitioners, including:

- the categories of practitioners who must be registered;
- the powers of the registration authority;
- offence provisions for unregistered practice;
- the basis for prescribed classes of registration;
- the framework for insurance and warranty obligations in domestic building work;
- enforcement powers; and
- the general framework for the building permit system.

**Relevance to ARC:** The Act provides the head power under which practitioner classes are established and regulated. Any new statutory practitioner class or change to existing class architecture would trace back to the Act. The Act does not itself enumerate specific classes — that function is delegated to regulations.

**Confidence:** VERIFIED — *Building Act 1993* (Vic), publicly available on legislation.vic.gov.au.

### Building and Plumbing Administration and Enforcement Act 2026

The *Building and Plumbing Administration and Enforcement Act 2026* (Vic) is a significant 2026 reform instrument. It establishes or restructures the Building and Plumbing Commission as the unified regulator and sets out its administrative, enforcement and compliance powers.

**Current status:** The Act was passed in 2026. Its precise commencement provisions and the extent to which it modifies or replaces provisions in the *Building Act 1993* require verification against the current version on legislation.vic.gov.au.

`REQUIRES_LEGAL_OR_REGULATORY_REVIEW` — The full text and commencement status of the 2026 Act should be verified before any submission or government contact relying on its specific provisions.

**Relevance to ARC:** The 2026 Act may affect the administrative architecture within which BPC operates. It does not, on current public evidence, appear to change the substance of practitioner class prescriptions, which remain a regulatory function.

### Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995

The *Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995* (Vic) governs the terms and conditions of contracts for domestic building work. It prescribes mandatory contract terms, deposit limits, progress payment rules, and dispute processes.

**Relevance to ARC:** The Act is relevant to the contracting authority of registered practitioners. DB-L Carpentry registration confers authority to contract for work within scope. ARC capability classification (non-statutory) does not confer contracting authority under this Act.

**Confidence:** VERIFIED — *Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995* (Vic), publicly available on legislation.vic.gov.au.

## Tier B — Regulations and Statutory Rules

### Building Regulations 2018

The *Building Regulations 2018* (Vic) currently prescribe, among other matters:

- classes and categories of registered building practitioners;
- the scope of work for limited classes (including domestic builder limited classes);
- qualification and experience requirements for registration;
- fee structures; and
- various procedural and compliance matters.

**Relevance to ARC:** The Regulations are the primary instrument that defines existing domestic builder limited classes, including DB-L Carpentry. Any change to the statutory scope of DB-L Carpentry, or the creation of a new domestic builder limited class, would currently require amendment to the Building Regulations 2018 or their successor regulations.

**DB-L Carpentry in the Regulations:** The Building Regulations 2018 are the instrument under which the Domestic Builder (Limited) class is prescribed, with class-specific scopes described in or by reference to the Regulations. The specific schedule or provision prescribing DB-L Carpentry scope requires verification against the current text of the Regulations on legislation.vic.gov.au.

`REQUIRES_LEGAL_OR_REGULATORY_REVIEW` — The exact provision in the *Building Regulations 2018* prescribing DB-L Carpentry scope should be identified and cited before any submission relying on specific regulation text.

**Confidence basis:** BPC official material confirms DB-L Carpentry scope, class requirements and experience thresholds. The regulatory basis of these requirements is stated by BPC to derive from the Building Regulations 2018. Primary text verification is recommended before regulatory advice is given or relied upon.

## Tier C — BPC Administrative Mechanisms

The Building and Plumbing Commission administers the building practitioner registration system. Within the statutory framework, BPC exercises administrative authority over:

- application processes and forms;
- examination format, content and administration;
- evidence requirements for registration applications;
- Technical Referee forms and processes (including SD TRR 028 for DB-L Carpentry);
- assessment methodology and assessor conduct;
- class-specific exam design (within statutory scope);
- documentation standards;
- calibration of assessors and processes;
- risk-based verification; and
- ongoing registration administration.

**Critical distinction:** BPC's administrative authority is bounded by the statutory scope defined in the Building Regulations. BPC can reform assessment processes, update examination content, improve evidence requirements, and modernise Technical Referee forms — but it cannot change the statutory scope of a class, create a new registration class, or extend practitioner authority beyond what regulations prescribe, through administrative action alone.

**Assessment reform context (current):** BPC is currently rolling out online building examinations across builder registration classes. Public BPC material (bpc.vic.gov.au, "Building exams" page) states an online exam "may be required" for listed classes including DB-L Carpentry as part of a gradual rollout. Whether the online exam is currently implemented for DB-L Carpentry is `NOT_CONFIRMED_FOR_DBLC` on current public evidence.

**Confidence:** BPC administrative authority confirmed from bpc.vic.gov.au (Building practitioner registration pages, directly verified).

## Tier D — Non-Statutory and Industry Recognition

Non-statutory mechanisms exist outside the building registration system. They include:

- advanced credentials issued by industry or professional bodies;
- microcredentials issued by RTOs or assessment organisations;
- industry certifications;
- post-trade assessments;
- pilot classifications; and
- independently assessed capability classifications.

**Legal effect:** Non-statutory mechanisms do not confer registration, practitioner authority, contracting entitlement, or any statutory right. A practitioner holding a non-statutory ARC credential would retain only the statutory authority they already hold through existing registration.

**Relevance to ARC:** The ARC capability classification (independently assessed) currently operates as a non-statutory mechanism. It does not require legislative or regulatory change to implement. It does not, of itself, create statutory recognition.

# PART 2 — Legal Basis of Current DB-L Carpentry

## Registration Class

The Domestic Builder (Limited to Carpentry) [DB-L Carpentry] registration class is a domestic builder limited class within Victoria's building practitioner registration system.

**Statutory basis:** The domestic builder limited class structure is established under the *Building Act 1993* and prescribed by the *Building Regulations 2018*. BPC administers the class under delegated authority.

**Class scope (BPC-verified):** The BPC official class page (bpc.vic.gov.au — directly verified) describes DB-L Carpentry as covering carpentry work for homes and associated Class 10 structures involving construction, alteration, renovation and repair. The BPC expressly lists the following activities within scope:

- construction of non-habitable building structures;
- gates and fences;
- bathroom, kitchen or laundry renovations;
- framing;
- external wall cladding;
- door and window replacement and installation;
- cabinet making, joinery and stair construction; and
- attaching an external fixture.

**Qualification requirements (BPC-verified):** Certificate III in Carpentry (CPC30211 or CPC30220 or equivalent recognised prior learning) plus prescribed units: CPCCBC4026 (building applications and approvals), CPCCBC4005 (labour and material schedules), CPCCBC4003 (construction contract).

**Experience requirement (BPC-verified):** At least three years practical experience.

**Assessment inputs (currently publicly evidenced):** Online application form; Technical Referee Report / technical reference evidence (SD TRR 028); qualification evidence; experience evidence. Formal portfolio requirement is `NOT_VERIFIED` in the current publicly available DB-L Carpentry process description.

**Exam status:** BPC assessment reform is in progress. Online exam implementation for DB-L Carpentry is `NOT_CONFIRMED_FOR_DBLC` on current public evidence.

# PART 4 — Subordinate Legislation and RIS Process

## Framework

The *Subordinate Legislation Act 1994* (Vic) governs the making, review and expiry of Victorian statutory rules (regulations). The Better Regulation Victoria unit within the Department of Treasury and Finance administers the regulatory impact assessment (RIA) framework.

**Key process elements (publicly stated on betterregulation.vic.gov.au and in the Act):**

- Where regulations are being made or remade, a Regulatory Impact Statement (RIS) may be required if the regulations have a material impact on business, consumers or competition.
- Better Regulation Victoria assesses whether a RIS is required and, if so, reviews the proposed RIS for adequacy before the regulations are made.
- The responsible minister must be satisfied that consultation has been undertaken in accordance with the *Subordinate Legislation Act* requirements.
- The Governor in Council makes regulations on the recommendation of the responsible minister.
- The Victorian Parliament receives regulations by way of tabling and retains a disallowance right.

**RIS exemptions:** A RIS may not be required where the regulations are minor, technical, administrative in nature, or in specified circumstances described in the *Subordinate Legislation Act*. The relevant minister may certify that a RIS is not required in prescribed circumstances.

**Consultation:** Where a RIS is required, public consultation must be conducted. The *Subordinate Legislation Act* does not prescribe a fixed consultation period in all circumstances.

**Timing:** No fixed regulatory timeline can be stated without a published government timetable. The regulatory process for a major remake of the *Building Regulations 2018* could take 12–36 months from initiation to commencement, depending on the complexity of reform and the RIS process, but no government timeline is assumed or invented here.

**Confidence:** Better Regulation Victoria role confirmed from betterregulation.vic.gov.au. *Subordinate Legislation Act 1994* framework confirmed from legislation.vic.gov.au.

# PART 9 — Assessment Reform vs Statutory Recognition

## Assessment Reform

Assessment reform includes:

- examination format, questions and weighting;
- assessment criteria and capability domains;
- evidence submission requirements;
- Technical Referee process and form content;
- assessor calibration standards;
- digital or practical assessment delivery.

These matters are within BPC's administrative authority for existing classes. They do not require statutory change as long as the assessment remains within the existing class scope.

## Statutory Recognition

Statutory recognition includes:

- creation of a new registration class or category;
- prescription of class scope (what work may be contracted for);
- protected occupational title;
- registration obligations and compliance duties;
- enforcement provisions;
- insurance or warranty obligations.

These matters require regulatory amendment at minimum, and in some cases primary legislation.

## Why Evidence Supporting One Does Not Automatically Justify the Other

The evidence that an advanced residential carpenter exercises distinct capability beyond current DB-L Carpentry — if established — would support the case for revised or enhanced assessment within the existing class, or for a non-statutory credential. It would not, by itself, establish the regulatory case for a new statutory class.

The regulatory case for a new class requires additional demonstration that:

1. the public-interest benefit justifies the regulatory burden of a new class;
2. the scope of the proposed new class is clearly definable and enforceable;
3. the class does not unnecessarily duplicate or destabilise existing pathways;
4. the head power in the *Building Act 1993* (as amended) is sufficient; and
5. a Regulatory Impact Statement, if required, confirms net benefit.

Evidence of practitioner capability is necessary but not sufficient for statutory recognition. The regulatory design process is a distinct and additional step.

# PART 15 — Strategic Interpretation

## Assessment

Based on the current publicly verifiable evidence reviewed for this document, the following strategic interpretation is offered.

The ARC capability framework, independent assessment, assessor calibration, and non-statutory credential can all be implemented at Tier D (non-statutory) without any legislative or regulatory change. This is clear and well-supported.

The pathway to statutory recognition is less clear and depends on:

1. whether the *Building Act 1993* head power is sufficient for a new RRC class by regulation alone (unresolved: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_OR_REGULATORY_REVIEW`);
2. whether BPC can create an endorsement within DB-L Carpentry administratively (unresolved: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_OR_REGULATORY_REVIEW`);
3. whether a scope change to DB-L Carpentry would require a full RIS process and what evidence standard would be needed;
4. whether title protection requires primary legislation; and
5. the timing and scope of any Building Regulations remake process.

Assessment reform within the existing DB-L Carpentry class (Option B1) appears to be available as an administrative entry point without regulatory change, subject to the caveat that no new assessment criterion implies a statutory scope extension.
