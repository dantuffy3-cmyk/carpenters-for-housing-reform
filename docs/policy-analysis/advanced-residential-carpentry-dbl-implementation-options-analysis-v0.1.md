## 1. Purpose and Scope

This analysis examines implementation options for recognising advanced residential renovation carpentry capability within Victoria's building registration framework, with particular reference to the existing Domestic Builder (Limited to Carpentry) [DB-L Carpentry] registration class.

The analysis follows the canonical architecture adopted by Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria:

> **Public-interest problem → practitioner function → capability → assessment → assessor calibration → governed pilot → public-interest evaluation → government decision support.**

And the priority sequence:

> **Advanced Residential Carpentry capability first. Statutory vehicle second.**

This document does not predetermine the regulatory outcome. All six implementation options (A–F) remain open. The status quo remains a legitimate outcome. Further evidence remains a legitimate outcome.

## 2. Current DB-L Carpentry Registration — Source Verification

### 2.1 Scope of DB-L Carpentry

**Claim:** Domestic Builder (Limited to Carpentry) registration covers carpentry work involving construction, alteration, renovation, and repair of homes and associated structures, including framing, external wall cladding, and door and window replacement.

**Source confidence:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED.

The BPC resource hub lists a form titled *SD TRR 028 — Domestic Builder Limited to Carpentry Technical Referee Report* (URL: https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/forms/sd-trr-028-domestic-builder-limited-to-carpentry). Web sources citing BPC material describe the DB-L Carpentry class as covering construction, alteration, renovation, and repair of homes and associated Class 10 structures (garages, carports, decks, pergolas, verandas), with carpentry work including framing, timber floors, roofs, doors, windows, stairs, and timber flooring.

Direct access to the BPC website was not available in the verification environment. The scope description above reflects secondary sources that cite BPC material. It should be confirmed against current BPC registration class documentation before submission to government.

**Wording used in this document:** *Current BPC public material, as reported in secondary sources, indicates that DB-L Carpentry covers construction, alteration, renovation, and repair of homes and associated structures, including carpentry elements such as framing, doors, windows, and timber components.*

---

**Claim:** DB-L Carpentry includes bathroom, kitchen, and laundry renovation work.

**Source confidence:** NOT SUPPORTED.

Available evidence indicates that bathroom, kitchen, and laundry renovation is a distinct DB-L registration class — Domestic Builder (Limited) Bathroom, Kitchen and Laundry Renovation — supported by its own Technical Referee Report form (SD TRR 027). This work category is not part of the DB-L Carpentry class.

**Correction:** Any prior wording suggesting that DB-L Carpentry includes bathroom, kitchen, and laundry renovation as a general carpentry scope has been removed. A DB-L Carpentry practitioner may undertake carpentry elements within a wet-area renovation, but the class does not confer authority to manage wet-area renovation projects as a whole.

---

**Claim:** DB-L Carpentry includes external wall cladding.

**Source confidence:** NOT DIRECTLY VERIFIED from a carpentry-specific BPC source. External wall cladding is a carpentry-related activity, but the precise scope of DB-L Carpentry as it relates to cladding is not confirmed in the secondary sources reviewed. This claim requires further verification from official BPC class documentation.

**Wording used in this document:** *External wall cladding may fall within the scope of DB-L Carpentry where it constitutes carpentry work on domestic structures. This analysis does not establish that cladding work is confirmed within current DB-L Carpentry scope without further source verification.*

**Wording used in this document:** *The Certificate III in Carpentry training package may include units addressing approvals, scheduling, and contractual matters. This analysis does not establish that these specific units are assessed by BPC as part of the DB-L Carpentry registration process. Further verification is required.*

### 2.5 Registration Assessment Reform

**Claim:** BPC is reforming its registration assessment process toward more objective, more consistent, and class-specific examination.

**Source confidence:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED.

BPC publishes a licensing and registration reforms page (https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/builders/registration/licensing-and-registration-reforms). Secondary sources and industry publications report that BPC is implementing an online examination model, developed with the Australian Council for Educational Research (ACER), with class-specific question sets delivered through the Janison platform. The rollout began with Domestic Builder Unlimited (DB-U) in January 2026 and is reported to be extending to other registration classes including Domestic Builder Limited categories.

**Important limitation:** No secondary source confirms that a DB-L Carpentry-specific exam has been finalised or is currently available. The reform is used in this analysis as policy context only. It does not establish that BPC supports this initiative, its capability model, or any proposed assessment architecture.

**Wording used in this document:** *Current BPC public material, as reported in secondary sources, indicates that BPC is moving toward more objective, class-specific assessment for building registration, including online examination delivered with ACER. This reform provides policy context for the proposal that advanced residential carpentry capability assessment could align with an enhanced registration assessment architecture. This analysis does not claim that a DB-L Carpentry exam exists, or that BPC has endorsed any aspect of this initiative.*

### 2.6 Minimum Financial Requirements

**Claim:** Domestic builders registered in Victoria are subject to Minimum Financial Requirements.

**Source confidence:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED.

The Building Legislation Amendment (Buyer Protections) Act 2025 introduces a Minimum Financial Requirements (MFR) regime for domestic builders, replacing the previous insurer-administered financial eligibility system. The regime takes effect from 1 July 2026. Secondary sources describe a three-tier system based on Net Tangible Assets (NTA) and Maximum Construction Capacity (MCC).

**Use in this document:** The MFR regime is cited only to illustrate the regulatory and commercial responsibilities that attach to domestic builder registration — responsibilities that are distinct from the capability recognition proposed for Advanced Residential Carpenters. This document does not imply that an Advanced Residential Carpenter credential would inherit builder-level financial obligations. It does not make any insurance or financial performance claim beyond what secondary sources citing the Act and BPC guidance support.

**Wording used in this document:** *Victoria's building legislation introduces Minimum Financial Requirements for registered domestic builders from 1 July 2026. These requirements reflect the commercial and regulatory obligations associated with builder registration and are used here only to distinguish builder-level responsibility from the practitioner capability recognition proposed in this analysis.*

## 3. The Public-Interest Problem

Advanced residential renovation carpentry involves decisions that are materially different from new-construction carpentry. Existing buildings present:

- concealed structural conditions that may not be apparent until access is made;
- previous alterations that affect load paths in non-obvious ways;
- deterioration that changes the scope and risk of work mid-project;
- incomplete or absent documentation;
- variable original construction methods; and
- progressive discovery, where conditions found during work alter what can safely proceed.

These conditions require not only construction competence but integrated diagnostic, structural, planning, coordination and professional judgement capability. The current DB-L Carpentry registration does not assess this renovation-specific capability distinctly.

This analysis does not establish that the current framework is inadequate. It identifies a question for government evaluation: whether the capability gap is material enough to warrant formal recognition.

---

## 4. The Practitioner Function

Advanced Residential Carpentry is proposed as a practitioner function, not a title or credential.

The function involves:

1. assessing existing building conditions before work commences;
2. identifying concealed or uncertain conditions and their implications;
3. planning work that accommodates progressive discovery;
4. coordinating with engineers, building surveyors, and licensed trades at appropriate boundaries;
5. applying the decision cycle — observe, diagnose, predict, plan, execute, verify — to renovation conditions; and
6. documenting decisions, referrals, and outcomes in a form that supports accountability.

This function is distinct from new construction carpentry, from builder-level project management, and from engineering or surveying authority.

---

## 5. The Capability Framework

The initiative's capability framework comprises seven domains:

1. **Technical Capability** — construction and installation knowledge specific to existing buildings.
2. **Diagnostic Capability** — identification and interpretation of concealed or uncertain conditions.
3. **Structural Understanding** — recognition of load paths, structural elements, and boundary conditions requiring engineering input.
4. **Planning Capability** — work planning that accommodates uncertainty and progressive discovery.
5. **Coordination Capability** — management of professional boundaries, referrals, and licensed-trade scope.
6. **Professional Judgement** — integration of the above domains through the decision cycle.
7. **Boundary Recognition** — clear understanding of when to stop, refer, escalate, or seek specialist input.

Professional Judgement is the integrating domain. The decision cycle is: **observe → diagnose → predict → plan → execute → verify**.

## 6. Implementation Options

All six options are presented without predetermination. The purpose of this analysis is to support government evaluation, not to advocate for a specific outcome.

### Option A — Distinct Registered Residential Carpenter Registration Class

A new registration category within Victoria's building practitioner registration framework, purpose-designed for advanced residential renovation carpentry.

This option would require government decision-making, legislative change, or regulatory amendment. It is not within the initiative's power to create.

The initiative notes that government's current registration reform program provides a structural context in which such a class could in principle be considered.

**Conditions for viability:** Evidence that the practitioner function is sufficiently distinct, that the capability can be assessed reliably, and that formal recognition would materially improve consumer protection outcomes.

**Assessment under this option:** Purpose-designed assessment matched to the Advanced Residential Carpentry capability model; potentially aligned with BPC's emerging class-specific examination architecture.

**Wording standard:** This option is one of six. It is not the predetermined outcome.

---

### Option B — DB-L Carpentry Renovation Endorsement, Stream, or Revised Assessment

Advanced renovation capability incorporated into the existing DB-L Carpentry registration class, through:

- a renovation endorsement or stream within DB-L Carpentry;
- a revised or enhanced assessment component calibrated to renovation conditions; or
- administrative changes to existing BPC assessment practice for this class.

This option works within the existing legislative and regulatory framework and may require less structural change than Option A.

**Source note:** BPC's published registration reform materials indicate that assessment practices for existing registration classes are under review. Whether a DB-L Carpentry renovation stream or endorsement is feasible under current legislative authority requires legal and regulatory verification that this analysis does not provide.

**Conditions for viability:** Government or BPC determination that the existing DB-L Carpentry class can accommodate a renovation-specific endorsement without legislative amendment; or that legislative amendment is proportionate to the benefit.

---

### Option C — Advanced Residential Carpentry Credential without Additional Statutory Authority

Independent recognition of advanced renovation carpentry capability, not conferring additional statutory contracting or supervisory authority.

This option could be implemented without legislative change. It would provide reputational and market recognition but would not extend the practitioner's legal authority beyond what existing qualifications and DB-L registration confer.

**Conditions for viability:** Sufficient market and consumer recognition of the credential to create meaningful incentive for practitioners; a credible independent assessment body; and clear communication to consumers about what the credential does and does not confer.

---

### Option D — Another Proportionate Government-Selected Mechanism

Government may identify a mechanism not described in Options A–C that is more proportionate, more consistent with reform priorities, or more appropriate given evidence emerging from evaluation and consultation.

This option is included to ensure the analysis does not inadvertently constrain government decision-making.

---

### Option E — No Formal Recognition (Status Quo)

Government may determine that:

- the capability distinction is insufficient to justify formal recognition;
- the assessment architecture has not been validated to a sufficient standard;
- the public-interest benefit does not warrant the regulatory cost; or
- existing mechanisms adequately address the identified concern.

The status quo is a legitimate outcome. It is not treated as a failure of this initiative.

---

### Option F — Further Evidence Before Any Decision

Government may conclude that the evidence base is insufficient to support a decision at this time and that further evidence — from the pilot, from regulatory review, or from broader consultation — is required before any option is selected.

Further evidence is a legitimate and responsible outcome.

## 7. Professional Boundaries — Preserved

Regardless of which option is selected, the following professional boundaries are preserved:

- **Registered Builders** retain their existing registration authority.
- **Engineers** retain engineering judgement and certification responsibilities.
- **Building Surveyors** retain permit, inspection, and certification responsibilities.
- **Licensed Trades** retain their regulated responsibilities in their respective areas (plumbing, electrical, gas, waterproofing as applicable).
- Advanced Residential Carpentry recognition, under any option, does not confer builder, engineering, surveying or licensed-trade authority.

Physical-work overlap with another practitioner's scope is not treated as proof of functional equivalence.

---

## 8. Progressive Discovery Protocol

A practitioner recognising advanced residential renovation carpentry capability must understand and apply the progressive discovery protocol:

1. Make the area safe.
2. Pause the affected work.
3. Record the discovered condition.
4. Notify the owner.
5. Obtain appropriate builder, engineer, building surveyor, or licensed-trade input.
6. Revise scope, responsibility, and contractual arrangements before continuing.
7. Verify the completed response.

This protocol is not a legal immunity or liability transfer mechanism. Responsibility under contract law and consumer legislation remains subject to the applicable legal and insurance arrangements in each case. This analysis does not establish legal or insurance outcomes.

## 9. Remaining Evidence Gaps

The following evidence gaps remain open and should be addressed before any option is advanced to government submission:

1. **DB-L Carpentry scope** — The exact current BPC text defining DB-L Carpentry scope should be confirmed from the official BPC website or registration class instrument.

2. **Certificate III prescribed units** — The specific prescribed units claimed to cover approvals, scheduling, and contracts should be verified from training.gov.au or the current CPC training package.

3. **SD TRR 028** — The current version and content of the BPC carpentry-specific Technical Referee Report (SD TRR 028) should be confirmed by direct download from the BPC resource hub.

4. **DB-L Carpentry renovation endorsement feasibility** — Whether a renovation endorsement or stream is legally feasible within the existing DB-L Carpentry class requires legal and regulatory verification.

5. **BPC reform timeline for DB-L classes** — Whether and when the new class-specific examination architecture will extend to DB-L Carpentry requires confirmation from current BPC reform publications.

6. **MFR applicability** — Whether any form of Advanced Residential Carpentry recognition would trigger MFR obligations requires legal advice.
