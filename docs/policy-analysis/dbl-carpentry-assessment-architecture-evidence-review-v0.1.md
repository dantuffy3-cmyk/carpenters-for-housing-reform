## Source Verification Notice

This review is limited to current public evidence from primary Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC) material relevant to Domestic Builder (Limited to Carpentry) [DB-L Carpentry].

It applies the following control labels:

- **VERIFIED** — directly supported by current primary BPC material reviewed for this document.
- **NOT_VERIFIED** — not established by the current primary BPC material reviewed for this document.
- **NOT_CONFIRMED_FOR_DBLC** — public BPC reform material does not clearly confirm current implementation for DB-L Carpentry specifically.
- **NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED** — the public evidence reviewed does not clearly show whether a capability is or is not assessed.

This document does not infer capability absence from a lack of public evidence.

> **Critical control:** `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED ≠ capability absent from actual assessment`.

---

## 1. Purpose

This review examines whether current public BPC evidence is sufficient to determine the relationship between the current DB-L Carpentry assessment architecture and the initiative's seven-domain Advanced Residential Carpentry capability model.

The seven domains are:

1. Technical Capability
2. Diagnostic Capability
3. Structural Understanding
4. Planning Capability
5. Coordination Capability
6. Professional Judgement
7. Boundary Recognition

The question is evidentiary, not adversarial. The review tests what can presently be established from public BPC material. It does not claim that the current DB-L Carpentry assessment is inadequate, nor does it claim that public silence proves a domain is not assessed in practice.

## 3. Current Publicly Verified Assessment Inputs and Process Signals

The current public BPC material reviewed for this document clearly supports the following DB-L Carpentry assessment inputs or process signals:

1. **Online application form** — verified public application requirement.
2. **Technical reference / Technical Referee Report** — verified class-specific evidence artifact for DB-L Carpentry.
3. **Class qualification requirements** — including Certificate III in Carpentry and prescribed units, as publicly described by BPC.
4. **Practical experience threshold** — `at least 3 years practical experience` for DB-L Carpentry.
5. **Assessment reform context** — BPC is rolling out online building exams across builder classes, but current public evidence does not clearly confirm implemented DB-L Carpentry exam status.

For evidence-control purposes, a **formal portfolio requirement is not treated as publicly verified** in the current DB-L Carpentry process.

---

## 4. What the Public Evidence Does and Does Not Show

### 4.1 What is publicly evidenced

Current public BPC material reviewed for this document evidences:

- the registration class scope for DB-L Carpentry;
- the existence of class-specific qualification and experience thresholds;
- the requirement for technical referee evidence for carpentry applications;
- the existence of broader assessment reform toward online building exams; and
- builder-level regulatory obligations such as Home Warranty and Minimum Financial Requirements, where relevant to registration and consumer protection context.

### 4.2 What remains not clearly evidenced in public material

Current public BPC material reviewed for this document does **not clearly evidence**:

- a public domain-by-domain assessment map for DB-L Carpentry;
- the weighting, scoring or decision rules used in DB-L Carpentry assessment;
- whether diagnostic capability is assessed explicitly, implicitly, or not at all;
- whether structural understanding is tested beyond qualification and experience prerequisites;
- whether planning, coordination, professional judgement or boundary recognition are separately assessed in a public rubric;
- whether any current DB-L Carpentry exam, if implemented, is mapped to these capability domains; or
- whether assessor calibration or inter-rater consistency controls for DB-L Carpentry are publicly described.

The absence of public evidence on these points is an evidence limitation. It is not proof that the current assessment lacks these features.

### 5.2 Interpretation control

The current public evidence is sufficient to show that DB-L Carpentry registration is not based only on a qualification certificate. Public BPC material indicates an application process involving class requirements, experience requirements and technical referee evidence.

However, the same public material is not sufficiently detailed to determine whether the DB-L Carpentry assessment architecture presently tests the initiative's seven domains, partially tests them, or tests some of them under different terminology.

## 6. Related Regulatory Wording Controls

### 6.1 Home Warranty

For evidence-control purposes, the safe current formulation is:

> **Home Warranty generally applies to eligible domestic building work valued at more than $20,000 for applicable contracts from 1 July 2026, subject to exclusions, limits and eligibility requirements. Existing Domestic Building Insurance policies continue under the DBI scheme.**

This review does not state that every DB-L Carpentry project over $20,000 automatically requires Home Warranty.

### 6.2 Minimum Financial Requirements

For evidence-control purposes, the safe current formulation is:

> **From 1 July 2026, Minimum Financial Requirements apply to new applicants for domestic builder registration and to existing registered builders as an ongoing obligation and when seeking to increase their Maximum Construction Capacity.**

This is a builder financial-responsibility requirement. It is not used here as evidence of advanced residential carpentry capability.

---

## 7. Final Evidence Position

**Finding:** **D — Current public evidence is insufficient to determine the relationship between DB-L Carpentry assessment and the ARC seven-domain capability model.**

**Confidence:** **75%**

### Reason for this position

The public BPC material reviewed for this document provides enough evidence to identify some current DB-L Carpentry assessment inputs and surrounding regulatory context. It does **not** provide enough public detail to determine whether the seven ARC domains are fully assessed, partly assessed, differently assessed, or not assessed.

The controlling conclusion is therefore evidentiary insufficiency, not capability absence.

> `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED ≠ capability absent from actual assessment`
