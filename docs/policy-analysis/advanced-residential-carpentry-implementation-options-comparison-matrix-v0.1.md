## Purpose

This matrix compares the six implementation options (A–F) across key evaluation dimensions relevant to government decision-making.

The matrix is an analytical tool. It does not predetermine or recommend any option.

All options remain open. The status quo (Option E) and further evidence (Option F) are legitimate outcomes.

---

## Evaluation Dimensions

The matrix evaluates each option against the following dimensions:

1. **Consumer protection impact** — Does the option improve consumer understanding of practitioner scope and strengthen accountability?
2. **Regulatory proportionality** — Is the mechanism proportionate to the identified public-interest problem?
3. **Legislative change required** — Does the option require legislative or regulatory amendment?
4. **Assessment architecture** — Can the capability framework be applied under this option?
5. **Practitioner incentive** — Does the option provide meaningful incentive for practitioners to demonstrate advanced renovation capability?
6. **Boundary clarity** — Does the option maintain clear professional boundaries with builders, engineers, surveyors, and licensed trades?
7. **Evidence requirements** — What evidence standard must be met before the option could be implemented?
8. **Implementation complexity** — What is the estimated complexity of implementing this option?
9. **Status quo disruption** — Does the option disrupt existing registration classes, pathways, or professional arrangements?
10. **Option neutrality** — Does the option preserve government's ability to select an alternative outcome?

## Notes on Specific Options

### Option A — Registered Residential Carpenter

The RRC class, as one possible implementation mechanism, is not the predetermined outcome of this initiative. Its inclusion in this matrix reflects its status as a serious policy concept that has been developed through earlier initiative work.

This option would require the highest level of government engagement and regulatory investment. It should only be advanced if pilot evidence supports the case for a distinct practitioner function and formal recognition.

Selecting Option A does not close the initiative's other options at a capability model level. The assessment architecture is designed to remain transportable across vehicles.

### Option B — DB-L Carpentry Renovation Endorsement or Stream

This option is dependent on a legal determination about whether the existing DB-L Carpentry class can support a renovation endorsement without legislative amendment. That determination has not been made and is outside this initiative's scope to resolve.

**Updated context from verified BPC scope:** Official BPC material confirms that DB-L Carpentry already expressly includes bathroom, kitchen and laundry renovations, external wall cladding, framing, and door and window replacement. This verified scope makes Option B's design question more precise: an endorsement or stream would need to distinguish advanced renovation capability — in terms of depth, assessment method, or occupational responsibility — from what is already within the existing class scope. The question is not whether these activities can be done under DB-L Carpentry. The question is whether the current assessment tests the capability depth needed to manage renovation-specific complexity safely.

**Source limitation:** The feasibility of Option B within the current legislative framework is NOT DIRECTLY VERIFIED. It is treated as a policy option requiring legal advice.

### Option C — Credential without Statutory Authority

This option provides the most direct path to formalising the capability assessment model without requiring legislative change. Its limitation is that practitioner incentive depends on market and consumer recognition rather than statutory authority.

The initiative's assessment framework is capable of supporting this option. A credentialing body with appropriate governance, assessment integrity, and public communication would be required.

### Option D — Government-Selected Alternative

The initiative does not predict what form an alternative mechanism might take. Government reform processes may identify mechanisms not yet considered in this analysis.

### Option E — Status Quo

The status quo is included as a legitimate outcome and is not treated as a failure. If the evidence gathered through the pilot does not support the case for formal recognition, Option E is the appropriate outcome.

### Option F — Further Evidence

Further evidence gathering before any decision is a responsible and legitimate outcome. It is not a deferral of the problem — it is a commitment to evidence-based decision-making.

## Professional Boundaries — Preserved Across All Options

Regardless of which option is selected, the following professional boundaries remain protected:

- **Registered Builders** — existing registration authority unchanged.
- **Engineers** — engineering judgement and certification authority unchanged.
- **Building Surveyors** — permit, inspection, and certification authority unchanged.
- **Licensed Trades** — regulated responsibilities in each trade area unchanged.

Advanced Residential Carpentry recognition under any option does not confer builder, engineering, surveying, or licensed-trade authority. Physical work overlap between practitioner scopes does not establish functional equivalence.

## Remaining Evaluation Questions

The following questions remain open for government evaluation:

1. Is the Advanced Residential Carpentry practitioner function sufficiently distinct from existing registration classes to justify separate recognition?
2. Can the capability be assessed reliably and consistently across practitioners?
3. Would formal recognition materially improve consumer protection outcomes relative to the current framework?
4. Which option, if any, is proportionate to the identified public-interest problem?
5. What legislative, regulatory, or administrative steps are required for each viable option?

This initiative cannot resolve these questions. They are matters for government evaluation, informed by evidence from the pilot and further consultation.
