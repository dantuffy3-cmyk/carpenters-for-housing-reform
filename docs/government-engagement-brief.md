# Government Engagement Brief

**Registered Residential Carpenter Pathway — Victoria**
Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria
Last reviewed: July 2026

---

## Purpose

This brief is prepared for government officials, regulators, industry bodies and implementation partners who are considering the Registered Residential Carpenter initiative.

It provides a concise summary of the problem, the proposal, the safeguards, the pilot concept and the specific decisions the initiative is requesting the Victorian Government to examine.

---

## The Problem

Victoria's residential building framework does not clearly define where practical renovation carpentry ends and where a registered domestic builder must take responsibility.

This creates genuine difficulty:

**For consumers:** Homeowners undertaking renovations often do not know whether they need a carpenter, a builder, an engineer or a specialist. The boundary is not written in plain language accessible to ordinary consumers.

**For practitioners:** Experienced renovation carpenters regularly encounter conditions after demolition begins — hidden rot, termite damage, failed framing, out-of-level substrates, altered load paths — that change the nature and risk profile of the job. The current framework does not give them a clear, lawful response pathway.

**For regulators:** Informal work at the scope boundary is difficult to detect and enforce. When responsibility is unclear, the consumer protection framework is weakened.

**Contributing factors:** Regulatory ambiguity is not the only cause of informal renovation work. Affordability pressures, consumer behaviour, practitioner incentives, enforcement capacity, builder availability and project size all contribute. Regulation alone will not eliminate informality. But a clearer, lawful pathway may reduce it.

---

## The Proposal

The initiative proposes a bounded **Registered Residential Carpenter** pathway for experienced renovation carpenters undertaking defined residential renovation work in Victoria.

The proposed pathway would:

- define included and excluded renovation work categories
- set experience and assessment requirements for entry
- require insurance as a condition of registration
- require documentation and evidence records
- establish mandatory handoff triggers to registered builders, engineers, building surveyors and specialists
- provide consumers with a transparent, searchable record of the practitioner's scope
- establish a complaints pathway
- be subject to audit and regulatory oversight

**This is not:**
- a domestic builder licence
- permission to build new homes
- removal of builder or engineer responsibility
- unrestricted structural work
- deregulation of the building sector

**This is:**
- a bounded, consumer-facing recognition pathway
- a proposal to make the renovation responsibility boundary more visible, lawful and enforceable
- a pilot concept for government to test before any permanent regulatory change

---

## Safeguards

The proposed pathway is designed around consumer protection, not practitioner convenience. The safeguards include:

| Safeguard | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Published scope | Consumers and practitioners know what is and is not within the pathway |
| Mandatory insurance | Consumer protection and practitioner accountability |
| Documentation requirements | Evidence trail for audit, complaints and dispute resolution |
| Mandatory handoff triggers | Builder, engineer or specialist involvement required when scope is exceeded |
| Complaints pathway | Consumer recourse through an established regulatory body |
| Audit requirements | Ongoing compliance monitoring |
| Pilot evaluation | Evidence-based assessment before any expansion |
| Stop or modify provisions | Government can limit, pause or discontinue the pathway if problems emerge |

Registered builders retain full responsibility for domestic building work outside the RRC scope. Engineers retain engineering responsibilities. Building surveyors retain surveying and certification responsibilities. Licensed trades retain their existing regulated responsibilities.

## Pilot Concept

The initiative proposes a limited pilot before any statewide implementation. The pilot would:

**Participants:** A defined, limited number of experienced Victorian carpenters meeting entry requirements — experience threshold, portfolio assessment, insurance confirmation.

**Scope:** Defined residential renovation work categories (see Scope & Handoff Matrix at the project website).

**Geographic or time limits:** The pilot would operate within defined geographic or time boundaries to limit exposure during the evaluation period.

**Mandatory requirements:** Entry assessment, insurance, documentation framework and consumer disclosure obligations from day one.

**Escalation:** Clear builder and engineer handoff triggers, operative from the start of the pilot.

**Evidence:** Practitioners maintain prescribed records for each project.

**Audit:** Periodic audit of practitioner records during the pilot period.

**Complaints:** Consumer complaints handled through the established regulatory pathway.

**Evaluation:** Independent assessment of pilot outcomes against defined success measures.

**Pilot success measures include:**
- consumer understanding of practitioner scope
- number of scope escalations occurring and how they were handled
- completeness of practitioner evidence records
- complaints received and their nature
- defects identified
- practitioner compliance rate
- enforcement burden on the regulator
- builder and insurer feedback
- whether informal renovation work appears to be reduced or displaced

**Stop provisions:** The pilot includes defined criteria under which government may stop, modify or not proceed to further implementation.

---

## Public Benefit

A functioning RRC pathway would provide public benefit by:

- giving consumers a clear, searchable indication of what an RRC practitioner can and cannot do
- creating a lawful alternative for experienced renovation carpenters currently operating in an ambiguous boundary zone
- reducing the conditions that produce informal arrangements, liability confusion and weak evidence trails
- providing regulators with clearer accountability pathways in residential renovation
- supporting earlier intervention when scope changes require builder or engineer involvement
- contributing evidence for future renovation regulatory reform

## Evidence Base

This initiative is supported by:

- practitioner observation of the renovation responsibility boundary over approximately 20 years of Victorian residential renovation carpentry
- the Registered Residential Carpenter consultation documents and policy briefs (available at the project website)
- the Housing Supply Alignment Paper examining the relationship between the RRC proposal and Victoria's housing supply objectives
- the Advanced Residential Trades Framework examining broader trade pathway reform architecture
- the Reform Institute's ongoing research and evidence development

**Note on evidence status:** This is a practitioner-led reform proposal, not a government-commissioned research programme. The evidence base includes practitioner experience, policy analysis and publicly available regulatory information. Claims requiring additional authoritative evidence are identified in the project's Stakeholder Claims Register.

## Government Decision Requested

The initiative is requesting the Victorian Government to consider:

1. **Formally examine the renovation responsibility gap** — assess whether the current boundary is clear, enforceable and consumer-accessible.
2. **Consult experienced practitioners and stakeholders** — engage renovation carpenters, builders, insurers, surveyors, engineers and consumers.
3. **Assess a bounded RRC pathway** — evaluate whether a defined registration or endorsement category could address the identified gap.
4. **Define included and excluded work** — develop scope definitions with industry, regulators and consumer representatives.
5. **Develop statutory handoff rules** — establish enforceable triggers for when builder, engineer or specialist involvement is required.
6. **Test evidence and audit requirements** — examine what documentation should be required as a condition of the pathway.
7. **Examine insurance settings** — assess what insurance arrangements should apply.
8. **Establish a limited pilot** — test the pathway before any permanent regulatory change.
9. **Evaluate outcomes before expansion** — assess pilot results before considering statewide rollout.

The initiative does not claim the Victorian Government has agreed to examine, adopt or implement the proposed pathway.
