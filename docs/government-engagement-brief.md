# Government Engagement Brief

**Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Framework — Victoria**
Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria
Last reviewed: August 2026
Internal / government-facing working document

---

## Purpose

This brief is prepared for government officials, regulators and implementation stakeholders considering whether advanced residential renovation carpentry should be examined within Victoria's current building reform architecture.

It positions **Advanced Residential Carpentry capability** as the core reform object and **Registered Residential Carpenter (RRC)** as the initiative's current proposed implementation model rather than the predetermined legal outcome.

---

## Current initiative position

Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria is not asking government to predetermine a new statutory class.

The initiative is asking that advanced residential renovation carpentry be assessed as a candidate practitioner function within Victoria's enhanced registration framework, using principles of:

- defined authorised scope;
- objective competence benchmarks;
- practical-experience requirements;
- independent assessment;
- consumer role clarity; and
- proportionate accountability.

A controlled assessment pilot could test the capability model before government determines whether the appropriate vehicle is:

1. a distinct registration category such as Registered Residential Carpenter;
2. an endorsement, stream or revised assessment within an existing pathway such as DB-L Carpentry;
3. an independently assessed advanced credential; or
4. another proportionate mechanism identified through consultation or legislation.

---

## Why the issue matters

Residential renovation in existing homes regularly involves concealed conditions, incomplete documentation, progressive discovery and changing risk.

The practical difficulty is not only the physical work. It is also the decision-making required to diagnose conditions, sequence work, communicate with the owner, document discoveries, and escalate to the appropriate builder, engineer, building surveyor or licensed trade when the boundary is exceeded.

This creates a consumer-protection question, an accountability question and an occupational-recognition question.

---

## Reform alignment

Current Victorian building reform provides a relevant policy context because current official material refers to:

- expanded practitioner registration;
- clearer authorised scope of work;
- objective competence benchmarks;
- qualifications and experience settings linked to registration;
- practical-experience requirements and assessment;
- stronger practitioner accountability;
- consumer understanding of practitioner roles; and
- persistent building information for specified building classes.

**Important limitation:** current published reform material does **not** identify advanced carpenters as a proposed new registration category and should not be represented as government support for RRC.

---

## Capability model being proposed

The proposed core capability level is:

**Advanced Residential Carpenter — independently assessed**

This is a capability classification, not automatically a statutory registration class.

The capability model preserves:

- qualified carpenter entry;
- verified practical experience;
- evidence portfolio;
- independent assessment;
- technical questioning or oral defence where appropriate;
- diagnostic judgement in existing buildings;
- concealed-condition recognition;
- sequencing and temporary-support awareness;
- scope-boundary recognition and escalation;
- evidence and documentation capability;
- consumer communication; and
- understanding of builder, engineering, permit and licensed-trade boundaries.

Experience is evidence of exposure and eligibility. Experience alone must not confer advanced recognition.

---

## Current proposed implementation model

Registered Residential Carpenter remains the initiative's current proposed recognition model for this capability.

The final statutory vehicle remains open to government evaluation.

This preserves the existing RRC work while allowing government to assess whether the capability architecture is better delivered through RRC, reform of an existing pathway, an advanced credential, or another mechanism.

---

## Pilot position

A controlled pilot should test **capability**, not new statutory authority.

Participants must remain within their existing lawful authority during the pilot.

The pilot should test:

1. qualification verification;
2. practical renovation experience;
3. portfolio evidence;
4. scenario-based technical assessment;
5. concealed-condition recognition;
6. sequencing and temporary-support judgement;
7. scope-boundary recognition;
8. stop-work and escalation decisions;
9. documentation capability;
10. consumer role communication;
11. professional handoff decisions; and
12. assessor consistency.

It should also test consumer comprehension of practitioner role limits and the design of a persistent pilot work-and-evidence record.

---

## What is not being claimed

The initiative does not claim that:

- government has endorsed RRC;
- government has proposed registration of advanced carpenters;
- current building-manual requirements for new Class 1b, 2 or 3 buildings automatically apply to Class 1a residential renovation; or
- a pilot can grant participants new statutory authority.
