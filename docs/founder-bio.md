## Professional Profile

Daniel Sonsie is a Victorian residential renovation carpenter who has spent approximately 20 years working in residential renovation. His practice has focused on the kind of renovation work where scope, conditions and responsibility are regularly tested: bathrooms and kitchens requiring substrate and framing rectification; structural framing repairs involving bearers, joists, studs, lintels and subfloor systems; external cladding, weatherboard and building envelope work; window and door installations; subfloor remediation; and concealed-condition work — rot, termite damage, failed framing and altered load paths discovered after demolition begins.

This type of work regularly involves decisions about when to involve a registered builder, structural engineer, licensed plumber, waterproofer, tiler or other specialist. It involves progressive discovery — where conditions change after the job has started — and the practical challenge of explaining to a homeowner why the original scope no longer applies.

Daniel founded Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria to examine these conditions and develop an evidence-based reform proposal. The initiative's primary project is the proposed Registered Residential Carpenter pathway — a bounded recognition pathway for experienced renovation carpenters that would define where renovation carpentry ends, where builder responsibility begins, and what evidence practitioners should maintain.

He is director of Sonsie Carpentry Pty Ltd and has developed the initiative's policy materials, evidence frameworks, scope boundary model and government engagement documents.

The reform initiative is an industry-led proposal. It does not claim government endorsement, regulatory authority, or a mandate from the building or carpentry sector. It invites government, regulators, builders, insurers, surveyors, engineers, training organisations and consumer representatives to examine the renovation responsibility gap and evaluate the proposed pathway on its merits.
