## Important

This document is a working draft calibration instrument.

Calibration does not determine statutory competence standards.

It tests whether the proposed assessment framework can be administered consistently by independent assessors reviewing the same candidate evidence and scenario responses.

This document:

- **is not a licence**;
- **is not registration**;
- **does not confer statutory authority**;
- **does not alter capability domains**;
- **does not alter the 0–4 scoring rubric**;
- **does not alter critical-fail rules**;
- **has not been independently validated**.

---

## Core Question

> Can independent assessors apply the Advanced Residential Carpentry assessment framework consistently enough for a controlled pilot to generate reliable evidence?

If assessors apply the framework inconsistently, pilot outcomes will not be defensible as evidence of candidate capability.

This calibration pack exists to test and improve that consistency before live pilot assessment begins.

---

## 1 — Purpose

Assessor calibration serves the following purposes:

1. To confirm that independent assessors can reach materially consistent scoring decisions when reviewing the same benchmark candidate evidence and scenario responses.
2. To identify ambiguities in the current rubric, scenarios or expected-reasoning themes that lead to unintended scoring variance.
3. To confirm that critical-fail recognition is consistent across assessors.
4. To confirm that consumer communication scoring is consistent.
5. To generate documented calibration records that support the evidential basis for pilot delivery.
6. To identify whether rubric or scenario refinement is required before live pilot assessment begins.

---

## 2 — Scope

This calibration pack applies to:

- assessors proposed for engagement in any pilot assessment under the Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment Framework v0.1;
- calibration exercises conducted before live pilot assessment;
- review and re-calibration following material changes to the rubric, scenarios or critical-fail rules.

This pack does not apply to:

- live candidate assessments;
- eligibility determinations;
- appeals or review processes;
- statutory or regulatory proceedings.

Calibration results are internal governance records. They are not candidate outcomes.

## 4 — Assessor Preparation

Before participating in any calibration session, assessors must:

1. Read and confirm understanding of the Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment Framework v0.1, including all seven capability domains, the 0–4 rubric and the critical-fail rules.
2. Read the Assessment Scenario Bank v0.1, including the scenario design principles and the mandatory escalation triggers for each scenario used in calibration.
3. Read the Assessor Governance document v0.1, including conflict-of-interest declaration requirements.
4. Complete the assessor bias and conflict declaration set out in Section 15 of this document.
5. Confirm in writing that they understand the calibration principles in Section 5 of this document.
6. Confirm that they will score benchmark responses independently before any group discussion.

Assessors who have not completed these preparation steps must not participate in calibration exercises.

## 5 — Calibration Principles

The following principles govern all calibration exercises. Assessors must confirm understanding of these principles before participating.

**Principle 1 — Independent scoring before discussion**
Assessors must record their scores and rationale independently before any results are shared with or discussed among other assessors. Discussion of results before individual scoring is complete invalidates the calibration exercise.

**Principle 2 — Candidate identity minimisation**
Candidate identity in benchmark responses should be minimised or anonymised where practical. Calibration tests the rubric, not the candidate. Assessors must not allow assumed candidate identity to influence scoring.

**Principle 3 — Rationale is mandatory**
Assessors must record the reasoning behind each score, not the score alone. A score without a documented rationale is invalid. The rationale must identify the specific evidence or response on which the score is based.

**Principle 4 — Multiple safe responses may be acceptable**
Different professional responses to the same scenario may both be reasonable and safe. Assessors must not penalise a response simply because it differs from their preferred approach, provided the candidate's reasoning is sound and escalation decisions are appropriate.

**Principle 5 — Agreement on reasoning matters more than identical wording**
The calibration target is not word-for-word agreement. It is agreement on whether the candidate has demonstrated the required reasoning, identified the relevant unknowns and made appropriate escalation decisions. Assessors who reach the same substantive conclusion using different language are in agreement.

**Principle 6 — Critical-fail consistency is the highest priority**
The most important area of calibration agreement is the identification of critical failures. Assessors must reach consistent conclusions about whether a critical-fail rule has been triggered. A disagreement on critical-fail status is always a material disagreement requiring enhanced review.

**Principle 7 — Uncertainty must not be scored as competence**
A candidate response that treats uncertainty as confirmation of safety, or that proceeds without recognising known unknowns, must not receive a positive score. Assessors must not resolve candidate ambiguity in favour of the candidate.

**Principle 8 — Missing evidence must not be filled by assessor assumption**
Assessors must score what is present in the benchmark response. Evidence not provided by the candidate must not be inferred or assumed by the assessor.

**Principle 9 — Assessor seniority must not override the shared rubric**
The shared rubric is the governing instrument. An assessor's experience, seniority or personal professional preference does not override the rubric. Where assessors disagree, the dispute is resolved by reference to the rubric, not by deference to the most senior assessor.

**Principle 10 — Calibration disagreements must be documented**
All disagreements, including minor ones, must be recorded. Material disagreements must be reviewed using the process in Section 11 of this document. Disagreements must not be suppressed, papered over or resolved by assigning a compromise score without documented reasoning.

**Principle 11 — Benchmark answers are exemplars, not mandatory scripts**
Benchmark candidate responses illustrate capability levels. Assessors must not require candidates to reproduce the benchmark wording. Different language that demonstrates equivalent reasoning and boundary recognition should receive equivalent scores.

**Principle 12 — Calibration results must inform rubric refinement**
Where calibration reveals systematic assessor variance arising from rubric or scenario ambiguity, this must be recorded as a rubric or scenario defect and addressed before live pilot delivery. Calibration is a governance mechanism, not a rehearsal.

## 6 — Calibration Process

The calibration process is as follows:

1. Assessors complete preparation steps (Section 4).
2. Assessors complete bias and conflict declaration (Section 15).
3. Calibration session conducted in accordance with the session plan (`docs/assessor-calibration-session-plan-v0.1.md`).
4. For each benchmark exercise:
   a. Assessors read the benchmark scenario and candidate response independently.
   b. Assessors complete a score sheet (`docs/assessor-calibration-score-sheet-v0.1.md`) independently.
   c. Score sheets are collected and compared before discussion.
   d. Agreement and disagreement are recorded.
   e. Material disagreements are reviewed using the process in Section 11.
5. Consumer communication calibration exercise completed (Section 13).
6. Critical-fail calibration set completed (Section 14).
7. All results recorded in the calibration results template (`docs/assessor-calibration-results-template-v0.1.md`).
8. Readiness recommendation formed in accordance with Section 16.

## 7 — Calibration Scenarios

Three scenarios are selected from the Assessment Scenario Bank v0.1 to test different capability clusters.

### Selected Scenarios

**Calibration Scenario A — Scenario 2: Subfloor Access Revealing Moisture, Ventilation and Timber Deterioration**

Capability clusters tested:
- Diagnostic Capability (multiple concurrent conditions requiring prioritisation)
- Structural Understanding (bearer and joist assessment boundaries)
- Boundary Recognition (licensed pest specialist, engineer)
- Planning Capability (sequencing with incomplete information)
- Consumer Communication (progressive scope change)

Selected because this scenario requires assessors to evaluate multi-condition triage, specialist referral decisions and sequential escalation — areas where assessor variance is likely if the rubric is unclear.

**Calibration Scenario B — Scenario 1: Bathroom Renovation Revealing Framing Deterioration**

Capability clusters tested:
- Technical Capability (moisture-related timber deterioration)
- Diagnostic Capability (extent of damage with limited access)
- Structural Understanding (load-bearing status assessment)
- Professional Judgement (stop/escalate decision)
- Consumer Communication (scope and budget uncertainty)

Selected because this scenario tests the stop/escalate decision and the threshold for engineering referral — critical-fail territory that requires consistent assessor judgement.

**Calibration Scenario C — Scenario 7: Internal Alteration Revealing Load Path and Chimney Conditions**

Capability clusters tested:
- Structural Understanding (load path complexity, chimney conditions)
- Boundary Recognition (engineering boundary, heritage or planning considerations)
- Planning Capability (revised scope in response to discovery)
- Professional Judgement (integrating structural and non-structural findings)
- Consumer Communication (explaining discovered risk)

Selected because this scenario tests boundary recognition in a structurally complex context — an area where assessors from different trade backgrounds may diverge.

### Why This Combination

These three scenarios collectively test:
- progressive discovery and scope change;
- multi-specialist referral decisions;
- stop/escalate vs proceed decisions;
- engineering boundary recognition;
- licensed-trade boundary recognition;
- consumer communication under uncertainty.

They do not duplicate each other. Each tests a meaningfully different primary capability cluster while requiring integration across domains.

## 8 — Expected Reasoning Themes by Scenario

### Scenario A — Subfloor Moisture, Ventilation and Timber Deterioration

**Observations available to the candidate:**
- Significant moisture on underside of floor boards across multiple areas
- Soft, discoloured, probe-penetrable floor joist timber in approximately 15m² area
- Subfloor ventilation blocked by concrete pour
- Tunnels and surface galleries on two stumps and bearer underside consistent with termite activity

**Known facts:**
- The affected area is larger than the original complaint area
- Ventilation blockage from concrete pour is confirmed
- Pest indicators are visual only — they have not been professionally assessed

**Unknowns:**
- Whether termite activity is current or historical
- Full extent of structural timber decay
- Subfloor drainage conditions
- Structural adequacy of the affected bearing elements

**Acceptable interpretations:**
- The candidate identifies multiple separate conditions requiring different specialist inputs
- The candidate recognises that pest assessment must precede structural timber decisions
- The candidate identifies ventilation remediation as potentially separable from structural repair
- The candidate prioritises making the area safe before sequencing further work

**Prohibited unsupported conclusions:**
- Concluding that termite activity is historical without specialist assessment
- Treating the visually affected 15m² as the full extent of deterioration
- Recommending structural timber replacement before pest assessment is complete
- Representing the original scope as achievable before full investigation

**Evidence required to improve certainty:**
- Licensed pest specialist inspection report
- Structural engineer assessment of affected bearers and joists
- Moisture mapping of full subfloor area
- Subfloor drainage survey

**Likely escalation states:**
- `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` — pest specialist
- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — structural assessment of affected bearing elements
- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — full moisture and decay extent

**Acceptable alternative safe responses:**
- Stopping all work and making the area safe before any specialist is engaged
- Commencing ventilation assessment while pest and structural assessments are arranged
- Communicating a revised cost and time expectation to the consumer before recommending next steps

**Critical boundary failures:**
- Proceeding with structural timber replacement without pest specialist clearance
- Representing the pest indicators as confirmed historical without specialist assessment
- Failing to communicate scope change to the consumer

### Scenario B — Bathroom Renovation Revealing Framing Deterioration

**Observations available to the candidate:**
- Bottom plate and base of multiple studs are soft and discoloured
- One stud crumbles under probing
- A nogging is soft at approximately 900mm height
- Floor framing below the shower is inaccessible from within the bathroom

**Known facts:**
- A leaking shower has been active for approximately five years
- The visible deterioration affects multiple members
- No drawings, permits or documentation for previous works are available
- The bathroom is in a 1960s single-storey weatherboard house

**Unknowns:**
- Whether the deteriorating wall is load-bearing
- Extent of floor-framing deterioration
- Whether previous works altered the load path
- Whether deterioration extends beyond the visible area

**Acceptable interpretations:**
- The candidate identifies that the structural significance of the deteriorated members cannot be determined from visual inspection alone
- The candidate identifies the need to establish whether the wall is load-bearing before any member is removed or replaced
- The candidate identifies the need for subfloor access before floor framing decisions
- The candidate recognises that temporary support may be needed if the wall is load-bearing during investigation

**Prohibited unsupported conclusions:**
- Concluding that the wall is non-load-bearing without investigation
- Concluding that the deterioration is limited to the visible area
- Proceeding with tiling or waterproofing while structural integrity is unresolved
- Representing the work as safe to proceed without adequate investigation

**Evidence required to improve certainty:**
- Subfloor inspection report
- Structural engineer assessment of the affected wall
- Original building documentation or permit records
- Moisture mapping of the affected area

**Likely escalation states:**
- `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — applies immediately on discovery
- `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — if any load-bearing member is found to require replacement
- `STOP_AND_MAKE_SAFE` — if structure becomes unstable during investigation

**Acceptable alternative safe responses:**
- Stopping work, making the area safe and notifying the consumer before any investigation is completed
- Engaging the engineer before engaging the consumer, where immediate risk is present
- Communicating a preliminary concern to the consumer before the full investigation is complete, if delay would prejudice the consumer's position

**Critical boundary failures:**
- Removing a potentially load-bearing member without engineering input
- Proceeding with waterproofing while load-bearing status is unresolved
- Telling the consumer the structure is safe without adequate investigation

- Proceeding with the alteration while the load-path condition is unresolved
- Assuming engineering authority in assessing or designing the load-path fix
- Failing to notify the consumer of a material change in scope and risk

## 9 — Inter-Assessor Agreement Metrics

The following metrics are used to compare assessor results after independent scoring.

### 9.1 Agreement Categories

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Exact score agreement | Assessors assign identical numeric score (0–4) to the same domain for the same benchmark response |
| Score-band agreement | Assessors assign scores within one numeric point of each other (e.g., both assign 3 or 4) |
| Outcome agreement | Assessors reach the same candidate outcome category (e.g., both reach `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED`) |
| Critical-fail agreement | Assessors agree on whether a critical-fail rule is or is not triggered |
| Escalation-state agreement | Assessors agree on which escalation state(s) apply |
| Domain-level variance | The spread of scores across assessors for a single domain across all benchmarks |
| Written-rationale divergence | The degree to which assessors identify materially different evidence or reasoning to support the same or different scores |

### 9.2 Agreement Thresholds

Acceptable inter-assessor agreement thresholds remain to be validated through calibration data.

This pack does not prescribe a numeric agreement target as a validated standard. Calibration data collected during the pilot preparation phase will inform what level of agreement is achievable and what is required for pilot outcomes to be defensible.

The following provisional guidance applies:

- Critical-fail agreement should be treated as the highest-priority consistency requirement.
- Outcome agreement should be examined for systematic patterns of divergence.
- Domain-level variance should be reviewed for evidence of rubric ambiguity.
- Written-rationale divergence should be reviewed for evidence of scenario ambiguity.

Two unresolved questions are relevant. Q-013 addresses the recalibration threshold (what level of agreement is acceptable before pilot recalibration is required). Q-019 addresses the go/no-go readiness threshold (what level of agreement is sufficient before live pilot assessment begins). Both questions remain open. See `evidence/unresolved-questions.json`.

### 9.3 Recording Requirements

For each calibration exercise, the following must be recorded:

- each assessor's score per domain per benchmark
- each assessor's outcome determination per benchmark
- each assessor's critical-fail determination per benchmark
- exact-score agreement rate per domain
- score-band agreement rate per domain
- outcome agreement rate
- critical-fail agreement rate
- escalation-state agreement rate
- domains or criteria with the highest variance
- whether the variance appears to arise from rubric ambiguity, scenario ambiguity or genuine assessor disagreement about the candidate's performance

## 10 — Material and Non-Material Disagreement

### 10.1 Material Disagreement

A disagreement is classified as MATERIAL when assessors differ on any of the following:

- **Critical fail vs no critical fail** — one assessor determines a critical-fail rule is triggered, another does not.
- **Safe proceed vs stop/escalate** — assessors disagree about whether the candidate's escalation decision was appropriate.
- **Engineering boundary** — assessors disagree about whether the candidate correctly recognised or exceeded the engineering boundary.
- **Builder boundary** — assessors disagree about whether the candidate correctly recognised or exceeded the builder registration boundary.
- **Licensed-trade boundary** — assessors disagree about whether the candidate correctly recognised or exceeded a licensed-trade boundary.
- **ADVANCED outcome vs NOT DEMONSTRATED** — one assessor reaches `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED` and another reaches `CAPABILITY_NOT_DEMONSTRATED` or `REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`.
- **Whether material uncertainty exists** — one assessor considers the candidate adequately acknowledged uncertainty; another considers it unresolved.
- **Consumer communication suppression or misrepresentation** — one assessor considers the candidate's consumer communication suppressed, minimised or misrepresented a material risk or scope change; another does not. This includes disagreement on whether a candidate's characterisation of required specialist input (for example, describing engineering sign-off as a formality) misled the consumer about professional authority or risk.

Material disagreements require enhanced review in accordance with Section 11.

### 10.2 Non-Material Disagreement Examples

The following are examples of disagreements that are not automatically material:

- **Adjacent rubric scores with the same outcome**: One assessor scores a domain 3, another scores it 4, and both reach `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED`. The difference is noted and the rationale reviewed, but the outcome is not affected.
- **Wording differences in rationale**: Assessors reach the same conclusion using different language. The rationale is consistent in substance even if different in expression.
- **Different safe escalation routes where both are defensible**: One assessor expects the candidate to engage the engineer before the pest specialist; another accepts a different sequence. Both escalation routes are safe. Both assessors accept the candidate's decision as within the range of reasonable professional judgement.

Non-material disagreements must still be recorded. They may reveal patterns that require attention even when individual instances are acceptable.

## 11 — Disagreement Resolution Process

The following process applies when a material disagreement is identified:

1. **Independent scoring confirmed** — both assessors confirm that their scores were completed independently before the results were compared.
2. **Results compared** — scores, rationale and outcome determinations are compared systematically using the calibration results template.
3. **Material disagreement identified** — the specific criterion, domain or outcome on which the assessors differ is precisely identified.
4. **Evidence basis explained** — each assessor explains, in writing, the specific evidence or candidate response on which their score is based.
5. **Shared rubric applied** — both assessors review the relevant rubric criterion together. The question is: what does the rubric require, and does the candidate's response meet that requirement?
6. **Second reviewer or calibration chair involved** — if the disagreement cannot be resolved by rubric reference alone, a second reviewer or calibration chair is engaged. The second reviewer must review the evidence basis and rubric independently before any discussion.
7. **Final calibration position recorded** — the resolution, and the reasoning for it, must be recorded in the calibration results template. The final position must identify which rubric criterion applied and why.
8. **Rubric or scenario defect recorded separately** — if the disagreement arose because the rubric or scenario was ambiguous or unclear, that defect must be recorded separately and addressed before live pilot delivery. The fact that a calibration position was reached does not mean the ambiguity has been resolved.
9. **Benchmark response not rewritten to force agreement** — if assessors disagree, the resolution comes from the rubric, not from altering the benchmark response to eliminate the ambiguity. Benchmark responses may only be revised following a governed review process.
10. **Re-calibration occurs after material rubric change** — if the rubric is materially revised as a result of calibration, assessors must complete a new calibration exercise before live pilot assessment using the revised rubric.

### Principles of Resolution

- Hierarchy and seniority do not determine the result. A more experienced assessor does not automatically prevail.
- Resolution must be traceable to a rubric criterion, not to a general professional preference.
- Where the rubric is genuinely ambiguous, the correct resolution is to record the ambiguity, apply the more protective interpretation for the current calibration, and escalate the rubric defect for governance review.

## 12 — Recalibration Triggers

Recalibration is required in the following circumstances:

1. **Critical-fail rules change** — any change to the critical-fail rules in the assessment framework requires reassessment of assessor understanding and consistency.
2. **Scoring rubric changes materially** — a change to the meaning of any score level (0–4) for any domain requires recalibration.
3. **New mandatory domain thresholds introduced** — if new mandatory minimum scores are introduced for specific domains, assessors must recalibrate against those thresholds.
4. **Scenario architecture changes materially** — if the mandatory escalation triggers, prohibited conclusions or expected-reasoning themes for a scenario are materially revised, assessors must recalibrate using the revised scenario.
5. **Assessor composition changes significantly** — if a new assessor joins the panel, or if more than one assessor changes, recalibration is required for the changed panel composition.
6. **Material disagreement exceeds future validated tolerance** — once inter-assessor agreement thresholds are validated through calibration data, exceeding those thresholds in practice triggers recalibration.
7. **Pilot evidence shows systematic assessor divergence** — if pilot assessment data reveals systematic patterns of assessor divergence across candidates, recalibration is required.
8. **Legal review alters boundary definitions** — if a legal or regulatory review changes the definition of engineering, builder or licensed-trade boundaries used in the framework, recalibration is required before further assessments proceed.

## 13 — Consumer Communication Calibration

### 13.1 Architecture

Consumer communication is assessed as a substantive capability domain, not an administrative add-on. Calibration of consumer communication scoring uses the architecture established in `docs/consumer-role-comprehension-test-v0.1.md`.

Assessors must independently judge each benchmark consumer communication response against the following criteria:

- **Accuracy** — does the candidate accurately describe the discovered condition, its implications and the required response?
- **Completeness** — does the candidate cover the scope change, the unknowns, the next steps and the consumer's position?
- **Plain-language clarity** — would a non-specialist consumer understand the explanation without technical knowledge?
- **Boundary clarity** — does the candidate clearly explain who does what, and why the candidate cannot proceed unilaterally?
- **Uncertainty disclosure** — does the candidate acknowledge what is not yet known, and avoid false reassurance?
- **Risk of misunderstanding authority** — could a reasonable consumer misunderstand the candidate's explanation as implying authority the candidate does not hold?

### 13.2 Benchmark Consumer Communication Response — Advanced

**Scenario context:** Bathroom renovation, discovered structural deterioration. Candidate is speaking with the homeowner after discovery.

> "I've found something I need to explain to you before we go any further. When I removed the wall lining, I found that some of the structural timber in the wall is significantly deteriorated — it's soft, discoloured, and in one place crumbles when I probe it. This has probably happened because of the long-running shower leak.
>
> I can't tell you yet whether that wall is carrying load from above, or whether there's similar damage in the floor underneath the shower. I don't have access to check from inside the bathroom.
>
> What this means is: I have to stop work on that area until we know more. I can't proceed with tiling or waterproofing while the structure is in an uncertain condition.
>
> I'm going to need to arrange for a structural engineer to look at the wall and assess whether it's load-bearing and whether any members need replacing. I'll also need to get under the floor from outside if there's access, or we may need to lift the floor to check.
>
> I can't give you a fixed price for the repair at this stage, because I genuinely don't know yet what we're dealing with. What I can tell you is that we will not proceed until we have that information, and I'll give you a written summary of what I've found today.
>
> You should also let your insurer know what's been found, if you have building insurance. That's your decision, but it's information I'd want to have if I were in your position.
>
> Do you have any questions about what I've explained?"

**Expected calibration result:** This response should score positively on all six criteria. Assessors should reach consistent agreement that this meets the consumer communication requirement.

### 13.3 Benchmark Consumer Communication Response — Intentionally Ambiguous

**Scenario context:** Same bathroom scenario. A different candidate speaks with the homeowner.

> "Look, we've found some rot in the wall framing, which isn't unusual in old bathrooms. It happens a lot in these houses. We'll need to replace those members before we can finish, but it's pretty standard stuff. I'll need to get an engineer to sign off on it — that's just a formality these days, they normally just confirm what the carpenter has already worked out. It'll add a bit to the cost and maybe a week or two to the schedule. I'd say we're still in the same ballpark as the original quote."
>
> "The main thing is, don't worry too much about it. These things come up all the time and we deal with them."

**Assessor task:** Score this response independently against the six consumer communication criteria. Pay particular attention to:

- whether the reference to engineering sign-off as "a formality" mischaracterises the engineer's role;
- whether the statement that the engineer "normally just confirms what the carpenter has already worked out" implies a scope of authority the candidate does not hold;
- whether describing the discovery as "pretty standard stuff" and saying "don't worry too much about it" suppresses the consumer's ability to make an informed decision;
- whether the cost and schedule reassurance is supported by adequate investigation.

**Expected calibration result:** This response should trigger assessor concern on boundary clarity, uncertainty disclosure and risk of misunderstanding authority. Assessors should discuss whether the characterisation of engineering sign-off as "a formality" is a critical boundary failure or a significant weakness short of critical fail.

The purpose of this response is to test whether assessors independently identify the same concerns, and whether they apply the critical-fail rules consistently.

## 14 — Critical-Fail Calibration Set

The following candidate response fragments are provided for assessors to evaluate independently. For each fragment, assessors must:

- determine whether a critical-fail rule is triggered: **Yes** or **No**;
- identify the rule or rubric criterion relied upon;
- record brief rationale.

Results are compared before discussion.

### Fragment CF-1

**Context:** Subfloor inspection. Candidate response regarding timber deterioration and termite indicators.

> "I could see there was some damage to the joists and bearers, but I had a look and it seemed contained. I decided to proceed with scoping the joist replacement because the pest issue didn't look active to me — there were no live termites visible — so I treated that as a historical problem and got on with the structural work."

**Expected calibration result:** Critical fail triggered — `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` for pest assessment before any structural timber decisions are made. Treating the absence of visible live termites as confirmation of historical infestation is a prohibited conclusion. Assessors should reach consistent agreement on this result.

**Rule:** Critical-fail rule 2 or 4 — failure to recognise a clear specialist boundary, and treating uncertainty as confirmation of safety.

---

### Fragment CF-2

**Context:** Internal wall removal. Candidate response regarding load path.

> "I checked the wall and it seemed like a non-load-bearing partition based on how it was framed. The rafters above don't appear to bear on it, and there's a beam running parallel to the wall so I figured the load goes that way. I removed the wall and completed the alteration as scoped."

**Expected calibration result:** This is a calibration test of threshold. Assessors must decide whether the candidate has demonstrated genuine structural assessment capability, or whether "seemed like" and "I figured" without investigation crosses into unsupported structural conclusion. The absence of engineering input must be considered against the critical-fail rules.

**Expected assessor discussion:** Assessors may reach different initial conclusions. The question for discussion is whether the candidate's reasoning is traceable and proportionate, or whether the word choices indicate insufficient evidentiary basis. Neither automatic critical fail nor automatic pass is appropriate without examining the full context. This is the "weak-but-not-critical" response in this set — its purpose is to test assessor calibration on the threshold.

---

### Fragment CF-3

**Context:** Bathroom renovation. Candidate advising the homeowner.

> "I've checked the wall and there's some deterioration, but I'm comfortable we can press on. I've done plenty of these bathrooms and this is well within what I can handle. The engineer involvement would just slow things down — in my professional opinion, it's not needed here."

**Expected calibration result:** Critical fail — critical-fail rule 6 (recommending a course of action outside lawful authority as if it were within authority) and critical-fail rule 2 (failure to recognise a clear engineering boundary). The candidate is substituting personal comfort for the required specialist boundary recognition.

---

### Fragment CF-4

**Context:** Scenario progress report. Candidate email to homeowner.

> "Just to let you know, everything is progressing well. We found some moisture damage to the subfloor framing but it's all under control. We've removed the deteriorated sections and will have the new framing in by end of week. No need to worry — we've got it sorted."

**Expected calibration result:** Critical fail — failure to communicate material risk to the consumer, and proceeding with structural member replacement without adequate evidence that the required specialist assessments were completed. The message suppresses the consumer's ability to make an informed decision. This also likely engages critical-fail rule 7 (failure to communicate material risk in a consumer-facing component).

---

### Fragment CF-5

**Context:** Post-discovery scenario response. Candidate explaining their decision.

> "When I found the chimney condition, I knew the client wanted to keep the project moving, so I made a judgement call and kept going with the adjacent work. I figured we could deal with the chimney later. It didn't look like an immediate safety risk so I didn't want to alarm the client unnecessarily."

**Expected calibration result:** Critical fail — proceeding with adjacent work in the presence of an unresolved structural or heritage condition, and withholding material risk information from the consumer to manage their emotional response. Candidates may not delay consumer notification of material risk because they judge the consumer would prefer not to hear it. This is a critical-fail rule 7 trigger.

---

### Fragment CF-6

**Context:** Consumer communication. Candidate explaining scope change.

> "Look, I've found a few issues under the floor. There's some deteriorated timber and what might be termite activity, but I'm going to need a pest inspector before I can say for sure. I've paused the structural work until that's done. I'll need to revise the quote once we know the full extent. Can I get a pest inspector in this week?"

**Expected calibration result:** No critical fail. This response demonstrates appropriate stop/refer decision-making, honest uncertainty disclosure, appropriate sequencing (pest before structural), scope transparency and a practical next step. Assessors should reach consistent agreement that this is a competent response. The absence of more detailed consumer communication (insurance advice, written summary) may affect domain scoring but does not trigger a critical fail.

## 15 — Assessor Bias and Conflict Controls

### 15.1 Required Declaration

Before participating in any calibration exercise, every assessor must complete a written declaration addressing the following:

**Section A — Prior Relationship with Candidate**
- Do you have a prior professional or personal relationship with the candidate whose evidence you may be assessing in live pilot assessment?
- Have you engaged in any commercial relationship with the candidate?
- Have you provided training, coaching or assessment preparation to the candidate?

*Note: Calibration exercises use benchmark candidates, not real candidates. However, this declaration is required to establish assessor governance practice.*

**Section B — Trade or Professional Background**
- Describe your primary professional background as it relates to residential renovation work.
- Identify any trades or disciplines in which you have extensive experience.
- Identify any areas of residential renovation with which you have limited or no direct experience.

**Section C — Preference Awareness**
- Are you aware of any preference toward builder-led approaches over trade-led approaches, or vice versa?
- Are you aware of any tendency to prefer escalation over proceeding in ambiguous situations, or vice versa?
- Are you aware of any tendency to weight years of experience heavily when evaluating candidate capability?
- Are you aware of any tendency to weight polished communication positively in a way that might disadvantage candidates who are technically capable but less articulate?

**Section D — Familiarity with Candidate's Business or Reputation**
- Are you aware of any familiarity with the candidate's business, professional reputation or work history that could affect your assessment?

**Section E — Declaration**
> I confirm that I have read and understood the calibration principles in this document. I will score benchmark responses independently before any discussion. I will record my rationale for each score. I will apply the shared rubric and not my personal preferences. I declare that I am aware of the potential biases listed above and will actively monitor for them during calibration exercises.

### 15.2 Calibration Controls for Identified Biases

The following controls are built into the calibration process:

| Bias | Control |
|---|---|
| Prior relationship with candidate | Declare and withdraw if conflict identified; calibration chair confirms allocation |
| Assessor trade background | Multi-background assessor panels where possible; divergence by background recorded |
| Preference for builder-led vs trade-led work | Calibration includes scenarios testing both escalation and scope confidence; rationale reviewed for consistency |
| Tendency toward over-escalation | Calibration includes Fragment CF-6 (competent but not critical-fail) to test calibration of the threshold |
| Tendency toward under-escalation | Calibration includes Fragments CF-1, CF-3, CF-4, CF-5 to test critical-fail recognition |
| Over-weighting years of experience | Rationale must be based on the candidate's demonstrated response, not stated years of experience |
| Over-weighting polished communication | Consumer communication scoring criteria are functional (accuracy, boundary clarity, uncertainty disclosure) not stylistic |
| Familiarity with candidate's business or reputation | Benchmark responses are anonymised; any real-candidate-context declaration triggers review |

### 15.3 Post-Calibration Review

After calibration, the calibration chair should review:

- whether any assessor's scores cluster consistently higher or lower than the group;
- whether any assessor's critical-fail determinations are consistently more or less frequent than the group;
- whether any assessor's rationale consistently references factors not specified in the rubric;
- whether scores appear to correlate with the benchmark candidate profile in a way that suggests identity-related bias.

Patterns that raise concern must be recorded and reviewed with the assessor before live pilot assessment.

## 16 — Pilot Readiness Gate

Live pilot assessment should not begin merely because documents exist.

The following conditions must be satisfied before live candidate assessment commences:

1. **Calibration completed** — all assessors proposed for live pilot have completed calibration exercises using this pack.
2. **Material disagreements reviewed** — all material disagreements arising from calibration have been reviewed and resolved in accordance with Section 11, or escalated as rubric/scenario defects.
3. **Critical-fail consistency tested** — assessors have demonstrated consistent identification of critical-fail triggers in the calibration set in Section 14.
4. **Assessor governance confirmed** — all assessors have completed the conflict-of-interest declaration required by `docs/advanced-carpentry-assessor-governance-v0.1.md`.
5. **Evidence procedures tested** — the evidence submission and review process has been tested for practical operation.
6. **Consumer communication scoring tested** — assessors have demonstrated consistent scoring of the consumer communication calibration exercise in Section 13.
7. **Legal and boundary review completed** — any boundary definition questions arising from calibration that require legal or regulatory review have been addressed or documented as open risks.
8. **Unresolved high-risk issues documented** — any questions remaining open at the point of pilot commencement are recorded as open issues with a risk assessment. No open issue that constitutes an unacceptable risk to consumer safety or assessment integrity may remain unresolved at pilot commencement.

**Readiness is evidenced, not assumed.**

Calibration outcomes do not confer practitioner authority. Completing calibration does not confer any credential or recognition on assessors or candidates.
