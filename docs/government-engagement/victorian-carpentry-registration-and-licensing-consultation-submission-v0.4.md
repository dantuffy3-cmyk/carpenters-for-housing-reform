## 1. Executive Summary

This working draft provides post-consultation feedback to assist DTP’s consideration of carpenter registration and licensing design.

The submission supports examination of a clear baseline framework first, then progression questions only where evidence warrants further design work.

Core submission position:

1. Define baseline carpenter registration/licensing objectives and scope first.
2. Separate technical authority to perform prescribed work from contracting/business authority and related consumer obligations.
3. Design transition settings around demonstrated competence, practical assessment, fairness and workforce continuity.
4. Distinguish expected policy benefits from established evidence, particularly on defect causation and intervention effect.
5. Test whether material residual capabilities remain above baseline before selecting any advanced/specialist mechanism.
6. Keep mechanism selection open; no predetermined RRC or advanced-licensing outcome is proposed.

This draft does not claim DTP endorsement of any mechanism and does not claim consultation consensus.

## 4. Regulatory Problem and Objectives

### 4.1 What problem should regulation solve?

The consultation should distinguish between:

- minimum competence assurance;
- direct accountability for prescribed work;
- consumer protection and role clarity;
- regulatory visibility of who performed work;
- non-compliance and enforcement practicality;
- workforce qualification and participation settings;
- defects and rectification outcomes.

### 4.2 Defect-evidence discipline

The submission separates:

- DTP’s stated policy expectation that licensing may reduce defects/disputes/burdens;
- historical 2024 analysis used as background reference;
- participant criticism of causation assumptions;
- current evidence limitations;
- unresolved causation questions requiring further examination.

Consultation evidence identified multiple potential contributing factors requiring further examination. This draft does not present a quantified defect-reduction claim.

## 5. Baseline Carpenter Registration/Licensing

### 5.1 Foundational baseline sequence

Baseline settings should be defined from:

- foundational trade qualification and apprenticeship pathways;
- occupational competence and prescribed-work accountability settings;
- role boundaries across employee, subcontractor, contractor and builder contexts.

### 5.2 Qualification language controls

This submission does not state that Certificate III is insufficient.

The controlled qualification position remains:

- Certificate III pathways include competencies relevant to trade execution and existing-building contexts.
- For several advanced decision functions, outcomes are **not clearly established as mandatory qualification outcomes** in the evidence reviewed for this draft.
- This wording is not converted into “not taught” or “not covered.”

---

## 6. Technical Authority, Accountability and Contracting

### 6.1 Core distinction

The consultation should clearly separate:

1. competence/authorisation to carry out prescribed carpentry work; and
2. authority to contract for that work and assume associated business/consumer obligations.

### 6.2 Non-assumptions

This submission does not assume that worker registration/licensing automatically grants:

- builder authority;
- major domestic building contracting authority;
- permit/surveyor responsibilities;
- insurance eligibility;
- specialist-trade authority.

### 6.3 $10,000 threshold issue

The meeting record attributes to Dan a practical concern that ordinary carpentry work can intersect with threshold and contracting settings.

This submission treats that as a real practitioner issue for policy clarification, not as a definitive legal conclusion on the full current legal position.

The consultation should clarify:

- how technical competence, value/risk thresholds and contracting authority interact;
- how consumer obligations and builder responsibility apply at threshold boundaries;
- whether insurance implications differ across role/authority settings.

Current evidence reviewed for this draft does not establish all legal boundary outcomes.

## 7. Qualification and Assessment

### 7.1 Qualification currency position (CPC30220 / CPC30326)

The available project analysis relies heavily on CPC30220 and associated pathways for baseline discussion.

For CPC30326, this review did not identify equivalent controlled analysis in the consulted project documents establishing how it should be applied in this submission.

Accordingly, this draft uses cautious wording:

- CPC30220 remains highly relevant to existing workforce and earlier qualification architecture in the evidence reviewed.
- CPC30326 may be relevant as newer qualification context, but application settings for this submission require verification before final submission.
- This draft does not make claims about implementation dates, teach-out settings, mandatory applicability, or competency-change effects.

### 7.2 Comparator treatment of DB-L Carpentry

DB-L Carpentry is used as comparator evidence only.

The evidence reviewed supports that DB-L Carpentry is an active statutory registration class and that public material identifies qualification, experience and technical-referee inputs.

## 8. Existing Workforce and Transition

A transition model should be practical, fair and capacity-preserving.

The Department may wish to examine:

- existing qualification/apprenticeship evidence;
- demonstrated competence evidence (not years worked alone);
- practical and proportionate assessment pathways;
- targeted gap assessment/training where required;
- provisional arrangements with clear controls;
- regional accessibility and administrative feasibility;
- workforce continuity and transparent review pathways.

Participant support for a five-year provisional pathway is treated as consultation evidence, not adopted government policy.

---

## 9. General Carpentry and Risk-Based Scope

The structured meeting record indicates participant preference for a simple general carpentry baseline before multiple subclasses.

This is treated as a policy option for testing, not settled DTP policy.

The consultation should test:

- simplicity and mobility benefits;
- enforceability and fragmentation risk;
- whether specialist/high-risk domains require differentiated controls above baseline.

## 10. Existing-Building and Renovation Capability

### 10.1 Why this remains a separate evidence question

Existing-building work can involve concealed conditions, deterioration, incomplete records and progressive discovery that alter risk and responsibility during delivery.

### 10.2 Practitioner case identifiers and traceability

To avoid misattribution, this submission uses canonical practitioner-case IDs from the controlled case register.

| Thematic sequence used in this submission | Canonical case ID in controlled register |
|---|---|
| Case 1 — termite structural/floor repair theme | CASE-002 (Concealed Termite Escalation) |
| Case 2 — partial bathroom repair / concealed subfloor deterioration theme | CASE-003 (Bathroom Hidden Conditions) |
| Case 3 — piano-room floor/subfloor failure theme | CASE-001 (Piano Floor) |
| Case 4 — speech-pathology premises conversion / constructability conflict theme | CASE-004 (Speech Pathology Conversion) |

These cases are used as decision-function illustrations only, not statewide prevalence proof.

### 10.3 Progressive-discovery response

Where changed conditions materially alter risk or regulatory implications, preferred practice remains:

- make safe;
- pause affected work;
- record condition;
- notify owner;
- obtain relevant builder/engineer/surveyor/licensed-trade input;
- revise scope, responsibility and contractual settings;
- verify before continuation.

## 11. Advanced/Specialist Capability Progression

After baseline settings are defined, the consultation should test whether material residual capability remains above baseline.

Functions to test include:

- existing-building investigation;
- condition assessment;
- progressive scope determination;
- retention/replacement boundary judgement;
- constructability conflict recognition;
- changed-condition safety response;
- technical/regulatory escalation judgement;
- site leadership/supervision functions.

Assessment sequence remains:

**function → evidence → scope → assessment → controls → mechanism last**.

RRC is treated as one candidate mechanism only.

---

## 12. Defects, Evidence and Causation

Current material reviewed for this draft does not establish a single-cause defect model or a quantified licensing effect.

The consultation should continue to separate potential contributors, including:

- workmanship;
- design/engineering;
- supervision/coordination;
- sequencing/interface failure;
- material/product factors;
- existing-building uncertainty;
- accountability and organisational factors.

Further contemporary evidence is needed before finalising defect-related regulatory claims.

---

## 13. Wet-Area Interface / Readiness Proposal

The meeting record attributes to Dan concern about wet-area sequencing/accountability and a staged pre-waterproofing concept.

This submission presents that as a limited practitioner proposal for further testing only, framed as an **interface/readiness control before waterproofing/concealment**.

This draft does not treat the concept as:

- automatic statutory sign-off;
- liability transfer;
- replacement for statutory inspection;
- replacement for licensed waterproofing responsibilities;
- authority expansion for carpentry licensing.

---

## 14. Implementation and Regulatory Administration

Implementation design should address:

- operational role settings for employees, subcontractors, contractors and builders;
- administrative capacity and staged commencement;
- accountability records and public visibility of practitioner status;
- practical assessment access and regional delivery;
- review/appeal arrangements;
- clear consumer communication on role boundaries.

---

## 15. Recommendations for Government Consideration

1. Define baseline occupational scope and accountability settings first.  
2. Separate technical work authorisation from contracting/business authority.  
3. Design transition around demonstrated competence and proportionate gap assessment.  
4. Verify contemporary defect/problem evidence before final regulatory design.  
5. Test whether material residual capabilities remain above baseline after baseline is defined.  
6. Clarify threshold, insurance and authority interactions, including the $10,000 interface issue.  
7. Examine wet-area interface/readiness controls separately where evidence warrants.

These recommendations are evidence-led and mechanism-neutral.
