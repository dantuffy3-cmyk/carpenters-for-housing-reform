## 1. Executive Summary

This working draft provides post-meeting consultation feedback on Victorian carpenter registration and licensing, using the 29 September 2026 DTP consultation evidence record as the controlling meeting source.

The submission applies a baseline-first architecture:

Foundational qualification/apprenticeship  
→ baseline registration/licensing settings  
→ technical authority and accountability  
→ contracting/business authority distinction  
→ workforce transition  
→ residual capability test  
→ advanced/specialist mechanism testing only if justified.

Key controlled positions in this draft:

- The submission does not treat any single mechanism as predetermined.
- Structured meeting preferences (for example five-year provisional pathway and general licence simplicity) are treated as participant evidence, not adopted DTP policy.
- Certificate III claims are controlled: where capability is not clearly established as a mandatory qualification outcome, that limitation is stated directly.
- Defect-reduction claims are separated into policy expectation versus currently verified causation evidence.
- Technical authority to perform prescribed work is kept separate from authority to contract, assume builder obligations, or hold other regulated responsibilities.

## 4. Regulatory Problem and Objectives

### 4.1 What problem should regulation solve?

The consultation should distinguish between:

1. minimum competence assurance;
2. direct accountability for prescribed work;
3. consumer protection and role clarity;
4. regulatory visibility of who performed work;
5. non-compliance and enforcement practicality;
6. workforce qualification/participation settings;
7. defect and rectification outcomes.

### 4.2 Defect-causation discipline

The available material supports separation of:

- **A. DTP expectation:** licensing may reduce defects, disputes and associated burdens (meeting transcript-backed DTP framing);
- **B. historical 2024 RIS material:** historical reference point only;
- **C. participant critique:** causation assumptions and modelling were challenged in consultation;
- **D. current verified evidence:** contribution to defects may be multi-factor and actor-shared;
- **E. unresolved evidence questions:** magnitude, attribution and intervention effect remain open.

This draft does not present a quantified defect-reduction claim.

## 5. Baseline Carpenter Registration/Licensing

### 5.1 Foundational baseline

Baseline settings should be built from:

- Certificate III in Carpentry (CPC30220) as core trade qualification context;
- apprenticeship and supervised practical exposure;
- any required prescribed-work licensing/registration gate for persons physically carrying out covered carpentry work.

### 5.2 Qualification wording controls

This submission does **not** state that Certificate III is insufficient.

Controlled formulation:

- Certificate III includes competencies relevant to trade execution and some existing-building contexts.
- For several advanced decision functions, mandatory-outcome coverage is **not clearly established in currently reviewed public evidence**.
- “Not clearly established” is not converted into “not taught” or “not covered.”

### 5.3 Baseline policy questions for DTP examination

The Department may wish to test:

- what exact baseline capability outcomes must be demonstrated;
- what assessment approach is proportionate for employee and subcontractor settings;
- how direct accountability is recorded for prescribed work;
- what supervision expectations apply across role types.

## 6. Technical Authority, Accountability and Contracting

### 6.1 Core distinction

The consultation should clearly separate:

1. authority/competence to perform prescribed carpentry work; and
2. authority to contract for that work and carry corresponding business/consumer obligations.

### 6.2 Controlled non-assumptions

Trade registration/licensing should not be assumed to automatically confer:

- builder authority;
- major-contract authority;
- permit/surveyor responsibilities;
- insurance eligibility;
- specialist-trade authority.

### 6.3 Consultation evidence treatment

Contractor-focused regulation options raised in consultation are treated as stakeholder alternatives for analysis, not as adopted DTP policy and not as this submission’s predetermined answer.

### 6.4 $10,000 threshold interface question

Consultation records attribute to Dan a practical issue: competent carpenters can encounter ordinary carpentry work whose value/scope intersects with current contracting and registration settings.

The submission recommends that DTP examine, as a distinct legal/regulatory design issue:

- technical competence to perform work;
- value/risk thresholds;
- contracting responsibility and consumer obligations;
- insurance and builder-accountability implications.

This draft does not assume a threshold outcome and does not treat unresolved legal interpretation as settled.

## 8. Existing Workforce and Transition

A transition model should be practical, fair and capacity-preserving.

The submission recommends that DTP examine:

- recognition of existing qualifications and apprenticeship history;
- demonstrated competence evidence (not years worked alone);
- practical assessment pathways;
- targeted gap-assessment/training where needed;
- provisional arrangements and administrative feasibility;
- regional accessibility and workforce continuity;
- transparent review/appeal settings.

Participant support for a five-year provisional pathway is recorded consultation evidence only.

---

## 9. General Carpentry and Risk-Based Scope

Structured meeting evidence indicates a participant preference for a simple general carpentry baseline before multiple subclasses.

This submission treats that as a testable policy option, not as settled government policy.

The Department may wish to test:

- simplicity versus fragmentation;
- mobility and identity impacts;
- enforceability and compliance burden;
- whether specialist/high-risk domains require additional controls beyond a general baseline.

## 10. Existing-Building and Renovation Capability

### 10.1 Why this remains a separate evidence question

Existing-building work can involve concealed conditions, deterioration, incomplete records and progressive discovery that alter risk, scope and role boundaries during delivery.

### 10.2 Practitioner evidence use controls

Practitioner cases are used as decision-function illustrations only, not prevalence proof.

- CASE-001 Piano Floor
- CASE-002 Concealed Termite Escalation
- CASE-003 Bathroom Hidden Conditions
- CASE-004 Speech Pathology Conversion

Current repository status records these cases as practitioner-declared placeholders pending fuller controlled population.

### 10.3 Progressive-discovery response

Where changed conditions materially alter risk or regulatory implications, the preferred response remains:

- make safe;
- pause affected work;
- record condition;
- notify owner;
- obtain relevant builder/engineer/surveyor/licensed-trade input;
- revise scope/responsibility/contract settings;
- verify before continuation.

---

## 11. Advanced/Specialist Capability Progression

After baseline settings are defined, the consultation should test whether any material residual capability remains outside baseline.

Functions to test include:

- existing-building investigation;
- condition assessment;
- progressive scope determination;
- retention/replacement boundary judgement;
- constructability conflict recognition;
- changed-condition safety response;
- escalation and authority-boundary judgement;
- site carpentry leadership/supervision;
- specialist interfaces.

The submission recommends that DTP examine progression by sequence:

**function → evidence → scope → assessment → controls → mechanism last.**

RRC is treated as existing research and a candidate implementation pathway only.

---

## 12. Defects, Evidence and Causation

The available evidence does not yet establish a single-cause defect model.

Consultation and repository materials support continued differentiation between:

- workmanship issues;
- design/engineering deficiencies;
- supervision/coordination failure;
- sequencing/interface failure;
- material/product failure;
- existing-building unknowns;
- organisational/accountability breakdowns.

Further evidence should be obtained before attributing defect reduction to any specific licensing design.

---

## 13. Wet-Area Interface / Readiness Control Proposal

Consultation records attribute to Dan concerns about wet-area sequencing/accountability and a staged pre-waterproofing sign-off concept.

In this submission, this is framed as a **practitioner proposal for further testing** only.

Controlled boundaries:

- no liability-transfer claim;
- no claim that one sign-off proves complete upstream correctness;
- no replacement of statutory inspection or licensed-waterproofing competence;
- no implication that carpentry licensing itself grants waterproofing authority.

One option for testing is a limited interface/readiness control before concealment, with scope and authority boundaries explicitly defined.

---

## 14. Implementation and Regulatory Administration

Implementation design should address:

- regulator/admin capacity;
- role definitions for employees, subcontractors and contractors;
- digital register and accountability records;
- assessment access and regional delivery;
- staged commencement with review points;
- review/appeal mechanisms;
- communication so consumers understand role boundaries.

---

## 15. Recommended Further Work

The submission recommends that DTP examine:

1. finalised baseline role/scope definitions across worker-subcontractor-contractor-builder settings;
2. the contracting-authority versus technical-authority boundary;
3. transition pathways that recognise competence without automatic deeming;
4. current evidence quality on defect attribution and intervention effect;
5. whether any residual capability above baseline is material and assessable;
6. legal/regulatory implications of threshold settings and role permissions;
7. whether wet-area interface controls warrant separate scoped testing.
