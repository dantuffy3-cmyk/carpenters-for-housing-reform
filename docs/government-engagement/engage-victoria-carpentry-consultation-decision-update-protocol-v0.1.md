## 1. Purpose and Status

This document is the governance and change-control layer for how Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria will analyse the forthcoming Engage Victoria carpentry registration/licensing consultation and determine whether new government information changes any current evidence position, confidence level, policy option status, or next-step recommendation.

The companion capture instrument is:

> `docs/government-engagement/engage-victoria-carpentry-consultation-intake-v0.1.md`

That file captures information. This protocol determines what, if anything, that information changes.

This document is **not**:

- a consultation submission
- a government response
- an advocacy paper
- authority to begin outreach, interviews, pilot activity, FOI requests, field evidence collection or regulatory engagement

Consultation information may change the evidence picture. Consultation itself does not constitute endorsement of Advanced Residential Carpentry, the Registered Residential Carpenter concept, any pilot activity, or any statutory outcome.

The primary government question this project holds open is:

> **"Does advanced residential renovation carpentry constitute a sufficiently distinct practitioner function that it warrants defined competence assessment, role boundaries and potentially formal recognition within Victoria's enhanced practitioner-registration framework?"**

This is an evaluation question, not a conclusion.

---

## 2. Scope

This protocol governs:

- how consultation material is captured and archived
- how captured material is analysed against current project positions
- how findings, confidence levels and policy option statuses may be proposed for change
- what controls must be satisfied before any change is treated as canonical
- how contrary, null and adverse findings are handled
- what decisions, if any, flow from consultation review

This protocol does **not** govern:

- the content of any consultation submission (separate document required)
- regulatory engagement (requires separate authorisation)
- field evidence collection (remains inactive unless separately authorised)
- pilot activity (remains inactive unless separately authorised)

## 5. Consultation Source Hierarchy

When multiple government sources address the same question, apply the following hierarchy to assess policy authority:

| Tier | Source type |
|---|---|
| 1 | Enacted legislation or regulation |
| 2 | Official consultation paper published by the relevant Victorian government department or authority |
| 3 | Supporting technical material or regulatory impact statement released alongside the consultation paper |
| 4 | Minister or departmental media release or statement |
| 5 | Explanatory or background material on Engage Victoria |
| 6 | Transcript of official public briefing |
| 7 | Secondary media reporting of government position |

**Important:** Policy authority and evidentiary strength are separate assessments. A statement from Tier 1 may carry high policy authority while providing weak evidentiary support for ARC functional distinctiveness. Both must be recorded separately in the consultation update template (Section 25).

## 7. Two-Pass Analysis Method

Analysis of consultation material proceeds in exactly two passes, in order. Pass 2 must not begin until Pass 1 is complete.

### Pass 1 — Government Frame First

Extract only what the government says. Do not map material to the project's ARC framework during Pass 1.

Record government's stated position on each of the following, where addressed:

- stated problem
- policy objective
- legal scope
- practitioner classes being considered
- whether carpentry is being considered individually or as part of a broader framework
- registration versus licensing language used
- role of DB-L Carpentry, if mentioned
- assessment architecture described or proposed
- experience requirements referred to
- qualification requirements referred to
- scope boundaries proposed
- supervision expectations
- consumer protection rationale
- enforcement rationale
- insurance implications referred to
- transition arrangements
- implementation timing
- questions government is explicitly asking
- options government has placed on the table
- options government has not placed on the table

If the consultation does not address a field, record: **NOT ADDRESSED**.

### Pass 2 — Compare with Project Position

Only after Pass 1 is complete, compare the government material against each of the following project positions:

- existing pathways baseline
- functional distinctiveness
- recurrence
- actor attribution
- senior autonomy
- DB-L assessment architecture
- harm
- population
- assessability
- minimum intervention
- legal entry-point analysis

For each comparison, classify the relationship using one label only:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CONFIRMS | Government material is consistent with and supports the existing project finding |
| PARTIALLY_CONFIRMS | Government material is consistent in part but does not fully support the existing finding |
| CONTRADICTS | Government material is inconsistent with or undermines the existing finding |
| NARROWS | Government material reduces the scope or applicability of the existing finding |
| EXPANDS | Government material increases the scope or applicability of the existing finding |
| DOES_NOT_ADDRESS | Government material does not engage with this project position |

These labels describe the relationship between government material and the existing project position only. They are not new evidence-strength classifications.

---

## 8. Government Framing Extraction

During Pass 1, record the government's own language without interpretation.

Note in particular:

- whether government frames the problem as a consumer protection issue, a workforce issue, a regulatory modernisation issue, or some combination
- whether government distinguishes between registration and licensing in its framing
- whether advanced or specialist carpentry is treated as a distinct practitioner category or as part of a broader category
- whether government identifies a specific harm, risk or failure it is trying to address
- whether any regulatory mechanism is presented as settled or as a question for consultation
- whether DB-L Carpentry is mentioned, and if so in what context
- what the government explicitly says it does not yet know

Do not supply answers to these questions from project material. Record only what government says.

---

## 9. Project Comparison Method

During Pass 2, the comparison proceeds finding by finding.

For each finding listed in Section 4.2:

1. State the current finding and confidence level.
2. Identify the specific government statement or absence of statement that is relevant.
3. Apply the Pass 2 comparison label (Section 7).
4. Record whether the government material affects the finding itself or only the policy context surrounding it.
5. If a confidence change is proposed, follow the confidence-update rules in Section 11.
6. If no change is proposed, record: **NO CHANGE PROPOSED**.

A DOES_NOT_ADDRESS result does not reduce confidence. Silence is not adverse evidence unless the consultation specifically and directly addressed the topic and reached a contrary conclusion.

## 10. Evidence-Classification Rules

When recording government consultation material as evidence, use only the existing project evidence classifications:

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED | The government material directly and explicitly supports the claim |
| PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The government material provides some support but is incomplete or indirect |
| INFERENCE | The claim is a reasonable inference from the government material but is not explicitly stated |
| UNKNOWN | The government material does not allow the claim to be assessed |
| REQUIRES_GOVERNMENT_CLARIFICATION | The government material raises the question but does not resolve it; clarification is needed |
| REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW | The government material has legal implications that must be reviewed before the claim can be assessed |
| REQUIRES_PILOT_EVIDENCE | The claim cannot be assessed from the consultation alone; pilot evidence would be needed |

Do not create new Tiers, Levels, Ranks or evidence categories.

Policy authority and evidentiary strength must be recorded as separate fields. A high-authority source does not automatically produce high evidentiary support.

## 11. Confidence-Update Rules

Confidence changes must be non-mechanical.

### 11.1 What must not happen automatically

- Fixed percentages must not be added or subtracted because of government statements.
- Confidence must not increase automatically because a consultation mentions carpentry.
- Confidence must not decrease automatically because ARC or RRC is not named.
- Consultation questions must not be treated as government conclusions.
- Inclusion of an option in a consultation must not be treated as government endorsement.
- Exclusion of an option from a consultation must not be treated as permanent rejection without examining the context.

### 11.2 Required record for every proposed confidence change

Every proposed change to a governed finding must record all of the following:

1. Current finding (code and label)
2. Current confidence level
3. New government information (exact quotation or precise paraphrase with source reference)
4. Source authority (consultation hierarchy tier from Section 5)
5. Evidentiary relevance (evidence classification from Section 10)
6. Whether the new information affects the finding itself, or only the policy context
7. Proposed new finding, if any (use existing finding codes and labels only)
8. Proposed new confidence level, if any
9. Reasons for the change
10. Reasons against the change
11. Reviewer decision (to be completed at human approval gate)

### 11.3 Approval requirement

No confidence change becomes canonical without explicit human approval at the gate described in Section 20.

Until approved, proposed changes must be labelled: **PROPOSED — PENDING HUMAN APPROVAL**.

## 17. Contrary and Null Evidence Handling

Contrary, null and adverse evidence must be retained and recorded accurately.

Do not:

- suppress government information that weakens the ARC functional distinctiveness case
- reinterpret government silence as support for ARC
- reinterpret government disagreement or omission as misunderstanding
- discard options that reduce the need for formal recognition
- dismiss findings that support the status quo

If the consultation supports the conclusion that the current regulatory framework is adequate or sufficient, that outcome must remain fully available and must be accurately recorded.

If the consultation does not address ARC functional distinctiveness at all, that is a neutral result. It does not strengthen or weaken the current project position independently.

## 20. Human Approval Gate

No canonical change to a governed finding, confidence level, policy option status, pilot status, field evidence status or outreach status may be made without passing through this gate.

The gate requires:

- reviewer identity recorded
- date of review recorded
- decision recorded as one of: APPROVED / REJECTED / DEFERRED
- reasons for decision recorded
- any conditions attached to approval recorded

Until a proposed change clears this gate, it remains:

**PROPOSED — PENDING HUMAN APPROVAL**

## 21. Day-One Consultation Workflow

On the day consultation material is released and captured, the following sequence applies:

**Do:**

1. Capture authoritative source material (full document, URL, date, issuing authority, version)
2. Archive copies or stable references
3. Complete Pass 1 only — extract government framing without ARC mapping
4. Identify explicit government questions
5. Identify legal and regulatory references in the consultation material
6. Record all unknowns and gaps
7. Record the date and version of captured material

**Do not:**

- publish any public response
- send stakeholder outreach of any kind
- change canonical confidence levels
- launch field evidence activity
- modify pilot status
- send the DB-L Assessment Information Request
- claim that the consultation confirms project positions
- claim government support for ARC or RRC
- draft a final consultation submission before Pass 1 and Pass 2 are both complete

Pass 2 and all downstream decisions proceed in a separate, deliberate step after Pass 1 is reviewed.

## 23. Stop Rules

Analysis must stop and human review must be requested if any of the following conditions arise:

1. Government material directly contradicts two or more current confidence levels, and the direction of change is not clear
2. Government material introduces a new regulatory mechanism not contemplated in the current policy options
3. Government material suggests that DB-L Carpentry is being discontinued or substantially restructured
4. Government material names ARC, RRC or this project in a way that could affect the independence or credibility of the analysis
5. The consultation material contains legally privileged or commercially sensitive information that was not intended for public release
6. Proposed changes to canonical findings cannot be agreed without external expertise
7. The analyst is uncertain whether Pass 1 is complete before moving to Pass 2

In each case, the analyst must document the stop condition and the reason, and must not proceed without human instruction.
