# Advanced Residential Carpentry Pilot — Governance Plan v0.1

**Status:** WORKING DRAFT — operational governance architecture for consultation and validation.

**Prepared by:** Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Important Notice

This document describes governance architecture for a **future controlled pilot** of a capability-assessment framework. It does not commence a live pilot. It does not create statutory authority of any kind. Participation in the pilot does not confer registration, licence, builder authority, engineering authority, permit authority, surveyor authority, licensed-trade authority, contracting rights, or government endorsement.

---

## 1. Purpose

This governance plan defines the framework under which a controlled Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC) capability-assessment pilot may be conducted.

The pilot tests **capability-assessment architecture only**. Its purpose is to determine whether the assessment framework produces valid, reliable and useful evidence about the capability of residential renovation practitioners.

The pilot does not:

- grant or imply registration, licence or permit authority to any participant;
- create a new statutory category;
- establish a builder, engineer, surveyor or licensed-trade entitlement;
- constitute endorsement by the Victorian Government, the Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC), or the Department of Transport and Planning (DTP);
- recruit actual participants at this stage.

This document is suitable for government review and controlled pilot preparation.

---

## 2. Pilot Scope

### 2.1 What the Pilot Tests

The pilot assesses whether the ARC capability-assessment framework:

- can be administered reliably by trained assessors;
- produces consistent evidence across candidates with varying experience;
- captures the seven capability domains (Technical, Diagnostic, Structural Understanding, Planning, Coordination, Professional Judgement, Boundary Recognition);
- identifies evidence gaps, boundary weaknesses, and performance variation without bias;
- can be governed with appropriate independence, fairness and auditability.

### 2.2 What the Pilot Does Not Test

The pilot does not:

- determine whether any participant should receive statutory recognition;
- assess whether any statutory model is appropriate;
- create a register, pathway or entitlement;
- replace government or regulatory decision-making.

### 2.3 Pilot Phases (Provisional)

| Phase | Purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 — Dry Run | Calibration and assessor testing; no candidate outcomes recorded | Not yet commenced |
| Phase 1 — Bounded Candidate Pilot | Candidate capability assessment under governed conditions | Not yet commenced |
| Phase 2 — Validation (if required) | Expanded assessment for reliability testing | Not yet designed |

Cohort sizes for each phase remain unresolved. See Section 20 and Unresolved Question [Q-020].

## 3. Governance Principles

The following principles govern all pilot activity:

1. **Participation is voluntary.** No person is compelled or coerced to participate.
2. **Consent must be informed and recorded.** Participants must receive the Candidate Information Statement and sign the Participant Consent Form before any assessment activity.
3. **Participation does not imply competence.** Being selected does not mean the participant meets any particular capability standard.
4. **Assessment outcome does not imply statutory authority.** A pilot assessment result is evidence generated for the pilot only. It does not create or alter any statutory authority, registration, licence or contracting entitlement.
5. **Recruitment must not favour known supporters of the reform.** Selection controls must be designed to avoid participant bias.
6. **Candidate selection should support useful variation in experience and project exposure.** The pilot benefits from observing a range of performance, not only high performance.
7. **Assessors must be independent from candidates where practical.** Prior relationships must be declared.
8. **Conflicts must be declared before participation begins.** Undisclosed conflicts may result in exclusion.
9. **Evidence must be traceable.** All assessment records must identify source, date, assessor and participant ID.
10. **Unknown or unverifiable information must remain explicit.** Evidence gaps are findings, not failures of the process.
11. **Pilot governance decisions must be documented.** The Pilot Decision Register must be maintained throughout.
12. **Safety takes priority over completion of pilot activity.** Any safety concern is grounds for immediate pause.
13. **Participants may withdraw subject to agreed evidence-retention rules.** Withdrawal is not treated as failure. See Section 16.
14. **Pilot data must not be represented as statistically generalisable unless the methodology supports that claim.** The pilot is designed to generate assessment evidence, not population statistics.
15. **Adverse or null findings must be retained.** Selective reporting undermines pilot validity.
16. **Government or regulator observers do not become endorsers merely by observing.** See Section 21 and the Observer Protocol.
17. **Pilot findings must be reviewable.** Records must be maintained in a form that allows external review.
18. **The pilot may be paused, narrowed, redesigned or stopped.** See Section 18.

## 4. Participant Categories

### Category A — Candidate Carpenters

**Purpose:** Test the capability-assessment framework.

Candidates are carpenters with experience in residential renovation work who volunteer to undergo the pilot assessment.

Potential characteristics:

- recognised carpentry qualification (e.g. Certificate III in Carpentry) or recognised apprenticeship equivalent;
- verified residential renovation exposure across one or more project types;
- varied years of experience in carpentry and renovation;
- varied employment and business contexts (employee, contractor, sole trader);
- varied project categories (see Section 5 — Candidate Selection Principles).

No arbitrary minimum years of experience is required unless the pilot design evidence supports such a threshold. See Unresolved Question [Q-021].

### Category B — Assessors

**Purpose:** Apply the assessment framework to candidates.

Assessors must have sufficient expertise to evaluate candidate capability across the relevant domains. Potential assessor backgrounds include:

- experienced qualified carpenter with residential renovation expertise;
- registered domestic builder with renovation experience;
- suitably experienced vocational assessor with relevant industry background;
- relevant specialist reviewer for technical components.

Final assessor composition remains subject to governance review and is not pre-determined. See Unresolved Question [Q-022].

Assessors must:

- complete assessor calibration before conducting assessments;
- declare conflicts before each candidate assignment;
- not assess candidates with whom they have an undisclosed prior relationship.

### Category C — Technical and Governance Reviewers

**Purpose:** Provide specialist or independent review of assessment methodology, evidence or governance.

Reviewer roles are consultation or review roles only, unless formally appointed under a specific governance arrangement.

Potential reviewer backgrounds:

- structural engineer;
- building surveyor;
- regulatory or policy observer;
- consumer representative;
- insurance or risk reviewer;
- training or assessment specialist.

### Category D — Consumer Participants

**Purpose:** Where used, test role comprehension, communication clarity and disclosure adequacy.

Consumer participants are not exposed to live building work. Their involvement is limited to:

- role-comprehension testing of pilot explanatory materials;
- communication testing of candidate explanation capability;
- disclosure testing where applicable.

Consumer participant involvement requires separate consent and privacy controls.

### Category E — Observers

**Purpose:** Review and observe pilot governance and assessment processes.

Potential observers include representatives from DTP, BPC, industry bodies, training bodies, insurers and consumer bodies.

Observation is subject to the Observer Protocol (see Section 21 and `docs/pilot-observer-protocol-v0.1.md`).

**Observation does not constitute endorsement.** See Governance Principle 16.

## 5. Candidate Selection Principles

Candidate selection should aim to generate useful variation across renovation experience, including:

- bathroom renovation;
- internal alterations;
- subfloor work;
- decks and verandas;
- windows and doors;
- termite and timber deterioration;
- cladding;
- water ingress;
- occupied-home renovation;
- projects involving escalation to engineer, builder, building surveyor or licensed trade.

No candidate is required to have experience across every category. The pilot cohort as a whole should cover as wide a range as practical.

The pilot should be capable of observing:

- advanced performance;
- competent but not advanced performance;
- evidence gaps;
- boundary recognition weaknesses;
- unsafe or inadequate reasoning where present.

Selection must not bias toward candidates expected to pass. See Section 19 — Recruitment Bias Controls.

## 6. Decision Rights and Accountability

### 6.1 Pilot Sponsor / Initiative Lead

The pilot sponsor is responsible for:

- approving the governance plan;
- appointing the pilot coordinator;
- approving changes to pilot scope;
- approving pause or stop decisions;
- maintaining the Pilot Decision Register.

The pilot sponsor (Dan Sonsie as founder) is distinct from any independent assessor role. Dan Sonsie does not automatically hold assessor authority and should not assess candidates unless separately appointed through an independent governance process.

### 6.2 Pilot Coordinator

The pilot coordinator manages day-to-day pilot operations and is responsible for:

- recruitment logistics;
- eligibility screening;
- consent collection;
- conflict declaration management;
- evidence custodianship;
- incident reporting;
- record maintenance.

### 6.3 Assessors

Assessors apply the assessment framework and are responsible for:

- completing calibration before assessing;
- declaring conflicts;
- maintaining assessment records;
- escalating concerns about safety, evidence integrity or boundary issues.

Assessors may not unilaterally modify the assessment framework.

### 6.4 Calibration Chair

The calibration chair (where appointed) is responsible for:

- conducting assessor calibration sessions;
- recording calibration outcomes;
- identifying and managing assessor divergence.

### 6.5 Evidence Custodian

The evidence custodian is responsible for:

- maintaining secure storage of all pilot records;
- controlling access to identifiable participant data;
- implementing anonymisation and pseudonymisation protocols;
- handling withdrawal evidence in accordance with consent and governance rules.

## 7. Recordkeeping

All pilot governance decisions must be recorded in the Pilot Decision Register (`docs/pilot-decision-register-template-v0.1.md`).

Participant records must use participant IDs rather than full names where practical, in accordance with the Privacy and Data Handling Plan (`docs/pilot-privacy-and-data-handling-plan-v0.1.md`).

Records must be maintained in a form that:

- identifies the decision, date, decision-maker and evidence relied upon;
- records conflicts declared at the time;
- allows review by external parties where authorised;
- supports audit by government, regulatory or consumer bodies.

The following documents must be maintained:

## 8. Escalation

| Situation | Escalation Path |
|---|---|
| Safety concern during assessment | Stop activity immediately; notify pilot coordinator; record incident |
| Conflict identified mid-process | Pause affected activity; notify pilot coordinator; record in Decision Register |
| Evidence integrity concern | Pause affected assessment; escalate to pilot sponsor and calibration chair |
| Privacy incident | Notify evidence custodian and pilot sponsor; follow Privacy and Data Handling Plan |
| Legal or regulatory concern | Pause affected activity; seek legal or governance advice before continuing |
| Complaint | Follow Complaints and Review Process (`docs/pilot-complaints-review-process-v0.1.md`) |
| Assessor divergence beyond threshold | Escalate to calibration chair; do not finalise affected assessment |
| Participant distress | Pause activity; ensure participant wellbeing; record incident |

## 9. Pilot Pause and Stop Authority

The pilot may be paused, narrowed, redesigned or stopped if:

- any activity poses a safety risk;
- an unresolved legal boundary is identified that affects pilot validity;
- assessor divergence is unacceptable and cannot be resolved through calibration;
- a privacy or consent failure has occurred or is at risk;
- evidence integrity has been compromised;
- systematic consumer misunderstanding is identified;
- participant selection bias is identified that cannot be remedied;
- conflict management has failed in a material way;
- significant framework ambiguity is identified that affects reliability;
- a government or regulatory body formally requests a pause where it is involved;
- continuation would undermine pilot validity.

**Authority to pause or stop the pilot** rests with the pilot sponsor, or with the pilot coordinator where immediate action is required and the sponsor is not available. All pause and stop decisions must be recorded in the Pilot Decision Register.

A stopped or paused pilot may be resumed, redesigned or closed. Resumption requires documented review of the reason for pause, evidence that the issue has been addressed, and approval by the pilot sponsor.

## 10. Complaints and Review

Participants may raise complaints about:

- recruitment or eligibility decisions;
- assessor conduct;
- conflict management;
- assessment administration;
- privacy handling;
- communication;
- process fairness;
- record accuracy.

The Complaints and Review Process is defined in `docs/pilot-complaints-review-process-v0.1.md`.

A complaint does not automatically alter an assessment result. Process review and re-assessment are handled through separate processes.

## 11. Privacy and Consent

All participant personal information is handled in accordance with the Privacy and Data Handling Plan (`docs/pilot-privacy-and-data-handling-plan-v0.1.md`).

Consent is governed by the Participant Consent Form (`docs/pilot-participant-consent-form-v0.1.md`).

Consent to participate is separate from consent to publication. Publication consent must not be bundled with participation consent.

Privacy and legal obligations are not overstated in this document. Final privacy obligations and retention requirements require legal and governance review before live pilot commencement.

---

## 12. Conflict of Interest Management

All assessors, reviewers, governance participants and organisers must complete the Conflict of Interest Declaration (`docs/pilot-conflict-of-interest-declaration-v0.1.md`) before commencing their role.

Conflicts must be declared before candidate assignment. Undisclosed conflicts discovered during the pilot will be managed as an incident.

Possible outcomes following conflict declaration:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| NO_CONFLICT_IDENTIFIED | Participation proceeds as planned |
| DECLARED_MANAGEABLE_CONFLICT | Conflict noted; managed by agreed controls |
| RECUSAL_REQUIRED | Participant cannot assess or review the relevant candidate |
| UNABLE_TO_DETERMINE | Conflict status cannot be resolved; escalation required |

## 13. Legal and Regulatory Boundary Controls

The pilot operates within, and does not modify, the following boundaries:

- Registered Builders retain their existing authority under the Building Act 1993 and associated regulations.
- Engineers retain engineering responsibilities.
- Building Surveyors retain surveying and certification responsibilities.
- Licensed trades (plumbing, electrical, gas) retain their existing regulated responsibilities.
- The pilot does not grant any statutory right to undertake work outside a participant's existing legal authority.
- The pilot does not create a new registration class, licence or permit category.

Assessment scenarios must not require participants to perform work outside their lawful authority. Scenarios involving structural engineering decisions must be used to test boundary recognition, not to assign engineering responsibility to candidates.

The following statement applies to all participant communications and published outputs:

> "A pilot assessment outcome is evidence generated for the pilot only. It does not create or alter any statutory authority, registration, licence or contracting entitlement."

## 14. Recruitment Bias Controls

See `docs/pilot-candidate-recruitment-framework-v0.1.md` Section 8 for detailed bias controls.

In summary, the pilot governance plan explicitly requires that:

- recruitment is not limited to known supporters of the reform;
- recruitment does not rely solely on the founder's personal network;
- candidate selection does not bias toward applicants expected to succeed;
- highly experienced practitioners are not systematically over-represented;
- geographic, business-owner and trade-association bias is actively mitigated;
- self-selection bias is acknowledged in any reporting.

The pilot does not claim statistical representativeness unless the design and sample support that claim.

Final cohort sizes are not fixed in this document. Cohort size should depend on:

| Phase 1 | Bounded candidate pilot | Assess candidate capability under governed conditions | To be determined |

## 16. Withdrawal and Evidence Retention

A participant may withdraw from the pilot at any time. Withdrawal does not constitute failure and must not be characterised as such in any record or communication.

Following withdrawal:

- future participation stops immediately;
- identifiable material is handled according to the participant's consent record and the Privacy and Data Handling Plan;
- evidence already incorporated into anonymised aggregate analysis may not always be technically removable;
- the withdrawal is recorded in the Participant Register and the Decision Register.

Withdrawal does not make absolute legal claims. Legal obligations in relation to data retention require governance and legal review before the live pilot commences.

## 17. Safety Protocol

No assessment activity in v0.1 requires or involves unsafe practical structural work.

Assessment safety is governed by the Safety and Incident Protocol (`docs/pilot-safety-and-incident-protocol-v0.1.md`).

Safety takes priority over completion of any pilot activity. If any safety concern arises, the activity must stop. See Governance Principle 12.
