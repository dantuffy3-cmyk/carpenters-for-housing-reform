# Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Model — Version 1.0

**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**  
**Status:** Current governed policy document  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Policy status:** Initiative position. Not government policy.

## Purpose

This document defines the initiative's current core reform architecture.

The primary reform object is **Advanced Residential Carpentry capability**.

The statutory vehicle is a separate government design question.

Government-facing decision sequence:

1. Is there a sufficiently material public-interest problem to evaluate?
2. Is there a sufficiently distinct practitioner function?
3. Can that function be described through capability domains?
4. Can the capability be assessed reliably?
5. Can assessors apply the framework consistently?
6. Would formal recognition materially improve public-interest outcomes?
7. Which vehicle, if any, is proportionate?

## Core capability level

**Advanced Residential Carpenter — independently assessed**

This level represents demonstrated advanced residential renovation carpentry competence in existing-building work.

It is a **capability classification**, not automatically a statutory registration class.

## Capability basis

Advanced Residential Carpentry capability is assessed through the project's existing capability framework:

1. Technical Capability
2. Diagnostic Capability
3. Structural Understanding
4. Planning Capability
5. Coordination Capability
6. Professional Judgement
7. Boundary Recognition

Professional Judgement integrates the other capability domains through the decision cycle: **observe, diagnose, predict, plan, execute, verify**.

## Entry principles

Recognition at this level should preserve all of the following:

- qualified carpenter entry;
- verified practical experience;
- evidence portfolio;
- independent assessment;
- technical questioning or oral defence where appropriate;
- existing-building diagnostic capability;
- concealed-condition recognition;
- renovation sequencing;
- temporary-support and structural-boundary awareness;
- scope escalation;
- evidence and documentation capability;
- consumer communication; and
- understanding of builder, engineering, permit and licensed-trade boundaries.

## Experience rule

Experience is evidence of exposure and eligibility.

Experience alone must not confer advanced recognition.

Time served, by itself, is not a substitute for independent assessment of competence, judgement, documentation and boundary recognition.

## Assessment intent

The model is intended to recognise advanced existing-building renovation capability that can be demonstrated, evidenced and independently assessed.

It is not intended to create automatic new statutory authority.

## Implementation vehicles

If government concludes that this capability should be formally recognised, the capability model could be delivered through one of the following vehicles:

### A. Registered Residential Carpenter

A distinct bounded registration or recognition category.

### B. DB-L Carpentry renovation stream / endorsement / revised assessment

Advanced renovation capability incorporated into existing builder-registration architecture if government determines this is the more proportionate mechanism.

### C. Advanced trade credential

Independent recognition of advanced capability without automatic expansion of statutory contracting authority.

### D. Government-selected alternative

Another mechanism identified through registration reform, consultation or legislation.

### E. No additional formal recognition (status quo)

Government may conclude that capability distinction, assessment reliability or public-interest benefit is insufficient to justify additional formal recognition at this time.

## Architecture statement

The capability model is the reform architecture.

The statutory vehicle remains a government-design question.

Registered Residential Carpenter remains a serious proposed implementation model, but it is not the only possible delivery mechanism.

A no-reform outcome remains legitimate if evidence does not support further intervention.

## Non-negotiable principles across any vehicle

Regardless of vehicle, the initiative position remains that recognition should preserve:

- consumer protection as the primary objective;
- bounded scope;
- independent assessment;
- qualified carpenter entry;
- documented evidence and records;
- clear stop-work and escalation obligations;
- professional boundaries;
- builder, engineering, surveying and licensed-trade responsibilities; and
- accountability that is proportionate to the adopted mechanism.

## Mature-model principle

If Advanced Residential Carpentry recognition is adopted as an ongoing formal practitioner pathway, it should include continuing professional development.

Future CPD design should cover matters such as:

- technical knowledge;
- regulation changes;
- evidence and documentation;
- emerging materials and systems;
- consumer protection; and
- scope and escalation obligations.

This document does not design the full CPD system.
