## Please read this document before participating in the pilot.

This document explains what the Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC) capability-assessment pilot is, what participation involves, and what outcomes you can expect. Please read it carefully.

If you have questions after reading this statement, contact the pilot coordinator using the contact details provided to you.

---

## What is the pilot?

The ARC pilot is a controlled capability-assessment program. Its purpose is to test whether an assessment framework designed to evaluate advanced residential renovation capability can be administered reliably and fairly.

The pilot is organised by Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria, an initiative led by Dan Sonsie. It is not a government program.

---

## What is the pilot NOT?

**The pilot is not:**

- a government registration program;
- a licensing scheme;
- a certification process;
- a qualification;
- a government-endorsed assessment.

**Participating in the pilot does not:**

- grant you registration as a builder, carpenter or any other regulated category;
- authorise you to undertake work outside your current legal authority;
- constitute government endorsement of you or your work;
- guarantee employment, clients, or commercial advantage;
- affect your existing registration, licence or permits (if any).

> **"A pilot assessment outcome is evidence generated for the pilot only. It does not create or alter any statutory authority, registration, licence or contracting entitlement."**

---

## Why are you being invited?

You have been invited to express an interest in participating because your experience in residential renovation work appears to be relevant to the pilot's assessment objectives.

Invitation does not mean you will be selected. Selection depends on eligibility, evidence, cohort balance and available capacity. Selection decisions will be communicated to you by the pilot coordinator.

---

## What does participation involve?

Participation involves one or more of the following assessment components, depending on the pilot design:

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Portfolio evidence review | Submission of documented evidence of your renovation work (photos, project descriptions, records) |
| Interview | Structured conversation with an assessor about your renovation experience and decision-making |
| Scenario assessment | Presentation of realistic renovation scenarios; assessors observe your reasoning process |
| Written or recorded responses | Responses to questions or scenarios in written or recorded form |
| Follow-up questions | Assessors may ask follow-up questions about specific aspects of your evidence |

The specific components and their format will be confirmed before you are asked to sign a consent form.

No assessment component will require you to perform live structural or unsafe building work.

---

## Time commitment

The estimated time commitment for each assessment component will be provided before you are asked to consent. Times will vary depending on which components apply to you.

As an indication only, participation may involve:

- preparation and submission of portfolio evidence: variable (depending on your existing records);
- structured interview: estimated 1–3 hours;
- scenario assessment: estimated 1–3 hours;
- administration and follow-up: estimated 1–2 hours.

These are indicative only. Actual times will be confirmed before consent.

---

## Evidence requirements

You will be asked to provide evidence of your renovation work. Evidence may include:

- photographs of completed or in-progress work;
- written project descriptions;
- records of escalation to engineers, builders, surveyors or licensed trades;
- references or verifiable professional contacts (with your consent);
- other documentation of your work history.

You do not need to produce evidence of every possible project type. Gaps in evidence are noted as part of the assessment, not treated as automatic failures.

**Third-party information in your portfolio evidence**

Your portfolio evidence may include information about homeowners, clients or other parties involved in your projects. Before submitting evidence, you should consider whether:

- property addresses, client names or other identifying details can be de-identified or redacted without reducing the usefulness of the evidence;
- any material you intend to submit is subject to confidentiality obligations;
- you require permission from another party before sharing documents such as contracts, specialist reports, correspondence or invoices.

The pilot does not require you to submit identifiable third-party information where de-identification is practicable. Assessors will not request more third-party information than is necessary to assess your capability. The appropriate approach to third-party information in portfolio evidence requires legal review and has not yet been resolved. Guidance will be provided before live assessment commences.

---

## What are the possible assessment outcomes?

Assessment outcomes are generated for pilot purposes only. They describe what the pilot evidence indicates about capability, against the assessment framework criteria. They do not constitute a registration decision, a statutory finding, or a formal professional credential.

Possible outcomes include:

- evidence of advanced capability in one or more domains;
- evidence of competent but not advanced capability;
- evidence gaps in one or more domains;
- evidence of boundary-recognition weakness;
- insufficient evidence to reach a conclusion.

Outcomes are recorded and stored in accordance with the Privacy and Data Handling Plan. They are not published with your name unless you separately consent to publication.

---

## Privacy

Your personal information is collected and handled in accordance with the pilot Privacy and Data Handling Plan (`docs/pilot-privacy-and-data-handling-plan-v0.1.md`).

Identifiable information is stored securely and accessed only by authorised pilot personnel.

Pilot reports and aggregate findings use anonymised or pseudonymised data.

Your assessment outcome is not published with your name unless you separately provide written consent for publication.

Final privacy obligations and retention requirements require legal and governance review before live pilot commencement.

---

## Confidentiality

Assessment materials, including scenario content, are confidential. You are asked not to share assessment scenario details with other potential participants.

Assessment records are held confidentially by the pilot evidence custodian, in accordance with the privacy and consent arrangements.

---

## Withdrawal

You may withdraw from the pilot at any time, before or during assessment. Withdrawal is entirely voluntary and does not constitute failure.

To withdraw, contact the pilot coordinator. Your withdrawal will be recorded and your participation will cease.

Evidence already incorporated into anonymised aggregate analysis may not always be technically removable. Your identifiable records will be handled in accordance with your consent and the Privacy and Data Handling Plan.

---

## Complaints

If you have a concern about any aspect of the pilot — including recruitment, assessor conduct, privacy, or process fairness — you may raise a complaint with the pilot coordinator.

The Complaints and Review Process is available at `docs/pilot-complaints-review-process-v0.1.md`. Raising a complaint does not automatically change your assessment result.

---

## Independent review

Process complaints may be reviewed independently of the original decision-maker. The mechanism for independent review is described in the Complaints and Review Process.

---

## No statutory authority

The following statement applies to all participation in this pilot:

> "A pilot assessment outcome is evidence generated for the pilot only. It does not create or alter any statutory authority, registration, licence or contracting entitlement."

Your existing legal authority — including any builder licence, trade licence, or contractor registration — is not affected by your participation or any assessment outcome.

## No government endorsement

The pilot is organised by Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria. It is not a government program and has not been endorsed by the Victorian Government, the Building and Plumbing Commission, or the Department of Transport and Planning.

If government representatives observe any part of the pilot, their observation does not constitute endorsement of the pilot, the assessment framework, or any assessment outcome.

---

## No employment or registration guarantee

Participation in the pilot, or any assessment outcome, does not:

- guarantee employment;
- guarantee clients or commercial advantage;
- create an entitlement to registration or licensing;
- affect any existing application for registration.
