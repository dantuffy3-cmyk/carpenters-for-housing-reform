# RRC Pilot Operating Model
**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
Version: 1.1 — Working Draft
Date: August 2026
Status: WORKING DRAFT — Not government policy. Requires government, legal, regulatory and insurer development before implementation.

## Operating Premise

The pilot tests **Advanced Residential Carpentry capability** and not new statutory authority.

Registered Residential Carpenter remains the current proposed recognition model, but the pilot does not predetermine the final legal vehicle. Participants remain within their existing lawful authority.

## Stage 1 — Application and qualification verification

Confirm qualified-carpenter entry, identity and admission eligibility.

## Stage 2 — Experience and portfolio verification

Verify practical renovation experience and portfolio evidence. Experience is evidence of exposure and eligibility, not automatic recognition.

## Stage 3 — Independent assessment

Assess technical capability, diagnostic capability, sequencing judgement, boundary recognition, documentation capability and consumer communication. Technical questioning or oral defence may be used where appropriate.

## Stage 4 — Admission decision

Admit only participants who meet the assessed capability threshold. Admission does not grant new statutory authority.

## Stage 5 — Consumer disclosure onboarding

Before project activity, provide the consumer with a plain-language explanation of the participant's role, limits, escalation triggers and the pilot's non-statutory nature.

## Stage 6 — Consumer role-comprehension check

Record whether the consumer can correctly identify what the participant can do, what remains outside scope, when a builder is required, and when engineering, permits or licensed trades are required.

## Stage 7 — Project registration

Register only projects that can be tested within the pilot boundary and within the participant's existing lawful authority.

## Stage 8 — Baseline condition record

Capture pre-existing conditions, initial evidence and the assessed project scope before disturbance begins.

## Stage 9 — Work and evidence capture

Maintain the persistent work-and-evidence record covering work undertaken, evidence captured, variations and completion status.

## Stage 10 — Concealed-condition and variation assessment

When conditions change, record the discovery and assess whether the participant may proceed within scope, investigate further, seek specialist input, seek engineering input, involve a builder, or stop work pending permit or regulatory clarification.

## Stage 11 — Proportional escalation decision

Apply the preferred sequence: make the area safe, pause affected work, record the condition, notify the owner, obtain the appropriate input, revise scope and verify the response.

## Stage 12 — Professional referral or handoff

Where required, record the professional referral or handoff to the relevant builder, engineer, building surveyor or licensed trade.

## Stage 13 — Project close-out

Record completion status, work transferred, unresolved matters and final consumer communication.

## Stage 14 — Audit and consistency review

Audit project files for record completeness, escalation quality, consumer disclosure quality and assessor consistency.

## Stage 15 — Evaluation

Evaluate whether the pilot produced credible evidence about capability recognition, consumer clarity, documentation quality, proportional escalation and possible implementation vehicles.
