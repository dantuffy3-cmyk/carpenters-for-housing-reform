# Practitioner Incident Evidence Conversion Protocol v0.1

**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
**Status:** WORKING DRAFT — internal evidence-governance protocol
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** September 2026

> **Purpose and constraint notice.** This protocol creates methodology only. It does not add or authorise real practitioner incident records, public release of incident material, changes to public website files, or changes to controlled policy findings. Practitioner incident material is governed here as **function evidence**, not systemic or regulatory proof.

---

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines how practitioner incident material may be converted into controlled internal evidence records for future capability analysis, assessment-design work and limited illustrative policy use.

It is designed to preserve the boundary between:

- evidence that a capability, judgement point or professional boundary exists in practice; and
- claims about prevalence, public-interest scale, systemic regulatory failure or the need for any statutory reform.

---

## 2. Preserved Controlled Policy Findings

This protocol does not alter the current controlled findings below. Any use of practitioner incident material must remain consistent with them unless and until a separately governed evidence review changes them.

- **DB-L REGULATORY / SCOPE GAP:** NOT ESTABLISHED
- **DB-L GENERAL CAPABILITY GAP:** NOT ESTABLISHED
- **POTENTIAL BOUNDED ESCALATION-ASSESSMENT GAP:** PLAUSIBLE BUT UNCONFIRMED
- **PUBLIC-INTEREST CONNECTION:** NOT YET EMPIRICALLY ESTABLISHED
- **NULL HYPOTHESIS:** UNRESOLVED
- **NEW STATUTORY CATEGORY:** NOT JUSTIFIED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE

---

## 3. Governing Evidence Distinction

| Category | Meaning | May be used for | Must not be used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FUNCTION EVIDENCE** | Incident material showing that a capability, judgement point, escalation trigger, handoff boundary or decision sequence may arise in existing-building renovation work | capability analysis, assessment-design inputs, scenario design, bounded illustrative policy context | proving prevalence, frequency, systemic regulatory failure, DB-L inadequacy, public-interest scale, or the need for a new statutory category |
| **SYSTEMIC / REGULATORY EVIDENCE** | Evidence capable of supporting wider claims about scale, frequency, attribution, population patterns, system performance or regulatory outcomes | regulated-policy findings, prevalence claims, consumer-harm frequency claims, regulatory-gap findings | being substituted by anecdote or ungoverned practitioner recollection |

**Control:** Practitioner incidents are classified as **FUNCTION EVIDENCE** unless a separate evidence stream independently supplies systemic support.

---

## 4. What Practitioner Incidents Can and Cannot Establish

### 4.1 What practitioner incidents can establish

Practitioner incidents may establish, at most, that:

- a specific capability, decision point or boundary may arise in practice;
- progressive discovery can require observation, diagnosis, planning, escalation, pause/resume judgement or boundary recognition;
- a scenario is suitable for competency-assessment or assessor-calibration design;
- an incident can illustrate policy context if its limitations are disclosed.

### 4.2 Middle category: bounded contribution only

Practitioner incidents may contribute to:

- assessment-design development;
- scenario-bank development;
- illustrative policy context; and
- question formulation for later external validation.

They do so as bounded, de-identified, limitation-heavy inputs only.

### 4.3 What practitioner incidents cannot establish

Practitioner incidents cannot by themselves establish:

- prevalence;
- consumer-harm frequency;
- systemic regulatory failure;
- DB-L inadequacy;
- the need for RRC;
- the need for a new statutory registration category; or
- any resolved public-interest conclusion.

**Hard control:** No anecdote may be converted into a systemic or regulatory claim merely by repetition, aggregation of dramatic examples, or stronger wording.

---

## 5. Minimum Incident Record Structure

Every incident converted under this protocol must use the current controlled incident template and must separately record:

1. incident identification and broad project context;
2. what was known at the time;
3. what was unknown or unavailable at the time;
4. condition discovered and how it was discovered;
5. the decision point created by the discovery;
6. options available at that point;
7. action taken and whether work was stopped or controlled;
8. investigation, escalation and specialist engagement;
9. evidence required before work resumed;
10. final outcome;
11. outcome-independent assessment;
12. boundary test;
13. what the incident demonstrates;
14. what the incident does not prove;
15. capability coding;
16. evidence-quality level; and
17. external-use classification.

---

## 6. Evidence Labels and Source Discipline

Every evidence item must be labelled using one or more of the following controlled labels only:

- `CONTEMPORANEOUS DOCUMENT`
- `PHOTO`
- `PLAN / DRAWING`
- `INVOICE / QUOTE`
- `EMAIL / MESSAGE`
- `THIRD-PARTY RECORD`
- `PRACTITIONER RECOLLECTION`
- `INFERENCE`
- `UNKNOWN`

### 6.1 Hard controls for recollection

- `PRACTITIONER RECOLLECTION` is not documentary fact.
- Recollection must remain attributed to the practitioner unless independently supported by documentary or third-party material.
- A recollected sequence, diagnosis or authority claim must not be restated elsewhere as if it were proved by documents when it is not.
- `INFERENCE` must be explicitly marked as inference and must identify the evidence basis from which it is drawn.
- `UNKNOWN` must remain unknown until evidence is obtained. It must not be filled by assumption.

---

## 7. Capability Coding

Each incident may be coded against one or more capability domains using the controlled practitioner-incident capability set below:

The controlled practitioner-incident capability set is:

- Existing-building indicator recognition
- Controlled discovery
- Technical carpentry judgement
- Uncertainty recognition
- Investigation judgement
- Stop / Refer / Escalate judgement
- Specialist handoff
- Carpentry-scope sequencing
- Interface recognition
- Documentation / accountability
- Boundary recognition

Apply only the following coding values to those capability items:

- `DEMONSTRATED`
- `PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED`
- `NOT DEMONSTRATED`
- `INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE`

Coding must remain separate from legal authority, project success, commercial outcome and practitioner status.

### 7.1 Independent coding — mandatory

**Hard control:** Capability coding must be completed by someone other than the originating practitioner before the material advances beyond internal working status.

The independent coder must record:

- coder identity or role;
- date of coding;
- whether they had access only to the controlled record;
- any disagreement or uncertainty requiring review.

---

## 8. Boundary Test

Each incident record must include a boundary test addressing, at minimum:

1. What part of the event was within the practitioner’s observed or demonstrated capability?
2. What part required builder, engineer, surveyor, licensed-trade or other specialist input?
3. Was work paused, controlled or resequenced appropriately when the boundary was reached?
4. What information was missing at the point the boundary decision was made?
5. Does the incident show capability, referral judgement, or both?

**Control:** Do not treat participation in a project as proof of independent authority over all decisions within it.

---

## 9. Counterfactual and Hindsight-Bias Control

Each record must separate:

- what was known at the time;
- what was discovered later;
- what options reasonably appeared available at the time; and
- what is now easier to say with hindsight.

The record must include a counterfactual question:

> If the later outcome were unknown, what capability, judgement or boundary issue would still be evidenced at the moment of decision?

---

## 10. Outcome-Independent Assessment

Assessment must not collapse into success bias or failure bias.

- A correct escalation in a dramatic case does not prove broad advanced capability.
- A poor outcome does not prove that the underlying capability never existed.
- Routine or unnecessary-escalation cases are as important as dramatic cases.
- Assessment must focus on the quality of observation, judgement, escalation, documentation and boundary recognition at the time, not on whether the story sounds impressive.

---

## 11. Evidence Quality Levels

Each incident must receive one overall quality level:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Level 0** | No usable incident record; assertion only |
| **Level 1** | Recollection only, minimal detail, major uncertainty |
| **Level 2** | Structured recollection with some identifiable support but material gaps remain |
| **Level 3** | Structured record with multiple supporting items and reviewable chronology, though still incomplete or partly inferred |
| **Level 4** | Strong, reviewable record with contemporaneous and/or third-party support sufficient for robust external validation review |

**Control:** Higher quality does not convert function evidence into systemic evidence.

---

## 12. External-Use Classification

Each incident must carry one controlled external-use classification:

- `INTERNAL ONLY`
- `SUITABLE FOR DE-IDENTIFIED POLICY USE`
- `SUITABLE FOR EXTERNAL / REVIEWER VALIDATION`

Classification must be based on evidence quality, privacy risk, confidentiality risk, and whether the record can be understood without misstatement.

### 12.1 External-facing cross-reference — mandatory

**Hard control:** Any external-facing document using practitioner incident evidence must state or link to the current controlled policy findings so illustrative evidence cannot be mistaken for a resolved regulatory finding.

---

## 13. Claims-Control Block

Every incident-derived claim must be recorded in a claims-control block containing:

- `SUPPORTED CLAIM`
- `EVIDENCE BASIS`
- `CONFIDENCE`
- `LIMITATION`
- `WHAT WOULD FALSIFY OR WEAKEN THE CLAIM`

**Control:** The supported claim must be framed narrowly. It must describe only what the incident evidence supports, not what a broader policy argument wishes to prove.

---

## 14. Privacy and Confidentiality Controls

- Do not record client names, property addresses, contact details, project-identifying details, or commercially sensitive identifiers in working incident records intended for this repository.
- Use incident IDs and broad project-type descriptions only.
- Do not upload raw incident materials containing identifying information unless separately governed outside this repository.
- Use de-identification and minimum-necessary detail.
- Legal, insurance, liability and regulatory conclusions remain out of scope unless supported by verified authority.

---

## 15. Composition Log — Mandatory

A dated, reviewable composition log is mandatory for any practitioner incident register.

The composition log must track whether the register is becoming skewed toward:

- dramatic success cases;
- dramatic escalation cases;
- specialist-handoff cases;
- routine or simple-resolution cases;
- unnecessary-escalation cases; or
- records with weak documentation coverage.

**Hard control:** Incident material must not advance beyond ad hoc working use unless the current composition log has been updated and is reviewable.

---

## 16. Assessment-Scenario Conversion

An incident may be converted into an assessment scenario only after:

1. de-identification;
2. separation of observed fact, recollection, inference and unknowns;
3. independent capability coding;
4. completion of the boundary test;
5. completion of the claims-control block; and
6. confirmation that the scenario is being used to test capability, not to prove a regulatory conclusion.

Scenario conversion should preserve the decision problem, uncertainty, options and required boundary recognition while removing identifying detail and outcome glamour.

---

## 17. Prohibition on Systemic Claim Conversion

The following statements are prohibited unless independently established by other evidence streams:

- “These incidents show how common the problem is.”
- “These incidents prove a regulatory gap.”
- “These incidents show DB-L is inadequate.”
- “These incidents justify a new statutory category.”
- “These incidents prove consumer-harm frequency.”

Any attempt to move from illustrative incident evidence to systemic conclusion must be stopped and referred to a separate governed evidence review.

---

## 18. Working Status Gate

Until independently coded, composition-logged, and classified for use, practitioner incident material remains:

**INTERNAL WORKING MATERIAL ONLY**

It must not be cited externally as resolved policy evidence.
