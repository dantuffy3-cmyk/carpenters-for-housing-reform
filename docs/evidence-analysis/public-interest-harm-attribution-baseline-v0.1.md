## Purpose Notice

This is a bounded falsification exercise.

Its purpose is to determine whether publicly available Victorian evidence can establish:

1. material harm associated with residential renovation carpentry;
2. the scale or recurrence of that harm;
3. whether the harm can legitimately be attributed to carpenter capability;
4. whether any evidence supports a distinct Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC) policy question.

This is NOT a search for evidence to support ARC.

A legitimate finding is: **NO RELIABLE CARPENTER-SPECIFIC PUBLIC-HARM EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED.**

The canonical ARC finding preserved throughout this review is:

> **C — DISTINCT_CAPABILITY_QUESTION_REMAINS_PLAUSIBLE**
> Confidence: 45%

This finding is not increased merely because building defects exist.

The following logical separations are maintained throughout:

> General building defect ≠ carpenter defect.
> Carpenter defect ≠ advanced-capability gap.
> Advanced-capability gap ≠ need for statutory recognition.

## Evidence Classification

All claims are classified using the following evidence labels:

- **DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED** — established by the cited primary source
- **PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED** — the source supports some but not all aspects
- **PLAUSIBLE** — consistent with available evidence; not directly stated
- **NOT_YET_PROVEN** — possible but not established by sources reviewed
- **UNKNOWN** — not established by sources reviewed

---

## Part 1 — Decision Question

### Primary question

> Is there reliable Victorian evidence demonstrating recurrent public harm that can reasonably be linked to residential renovation carpentry capability, judgement or boundary recognition?

### Sub-questions

**A — Incidence:** Does evidence identify a recognisable incidence of harm associated with renovation carpentry work?

**B — Severity:** Where harm is identified, what is its reported severity — safety consequences, structural consequences, financial consequences?

**C — Attribution:** Can causation be attributed to carpenter capability, judgement or boundary recognition, rather than to builder management, design, engineering, supervision, product failure, or other factors?

**D — Recurrence:** Is there evidence that the harm is recurrent, not isolated?

**E — Regulatory relevance:** Does the pattern of harm, if established, give rise to a legitimate public-interest question that existing regulation does not adequately address?

## Part 5 — Source Review Findings

### 5.1 Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC)

**Dataset availability:** NOT_PUBLICLY_DISAGGREGATED (current BPC)

The BPC is the current Victorian building regulator following the abolition of the Victorian Building Authority (VBA) and associated machinery-of-government changes. As at August 2026, the BPC's publicly available material consists primarily of:

- corporate and operational information;
- general complaint and inquiry information;
- legislative and procedural guidance;
- published enforcement outcome summaries.

**Critical finding:** Current BPC public data does not disaggregate complaint or defect data by:

- practitioner class (builder sub-class, trade, carpenter);
- project type (renovation versus new construction);
- defect category (structural carpentry, framing, sequencing);
- capability attribution.

**Attribution level supported by BPC public data:** LEVEL 0 — GENERAL_BUILDING_HARM (at best).

**Evidence status:**

| Statewide scale | NOT_YET_PROVEN |

### 5.2 Historical VBA Data — HISTORICAL_VBA

**Dataset availability:** PARTIALLY_AVAILABLE (historical; pre-BPC transition)

The former VBA published periodic data and annual reports that included domestic building complaint volumes, practitioner disciplinary outcomes and some defect-related data.

**HISTORICAL_VBA — key limitations:**

- Published historical VBA data does not disaggregate defects or complaints by trade or by practitioner sub-class within the domestic building category.
- "Domestic building" complaints encompass the full range of builders and practitioners; carpentry-specific complaint lines are not separately reported in publicly available material.
- HISTORICAL_VBA data reflects the regulatory environment prior to the Building Statement (2022), the BPC establishment, and the enhanced registration reforms currently in development.
- These figures do not represent the current regulatory baseline.

**Attribution level supported by HISTORICAL_VBA:** LEVEL 0 — GENERAL_BUILDING_HARM.

---

### 5.3 VCAT Published Decisions

**Dataset availability:** PARTIALLY_AVAILABLE (published decisions; not representative sample)

VCAT's Domestic Building List and Building and Property List publish decisions in cases where parties proceed to hearing. The following reflects a review of publicly accessible VCAT decision summaries relevant to renovation carpentry.

**Search scope:** Renovation; structural alteration; framing; carpentry; rectification; domestic building defects.

**Critical limitation:** VCAT decisions represent contested disputes that proceeded to hearing. They are not a representative sample of defect prevalence. A small number of VCAT decisions cannot support a statewide prevalence estimate.

#### Summary of VCAT findings relevant to this review

The following generalised observations emerge from published VCAT renovation and building defect decisions reviewed for this analysis. Specific case names and citations would require formal legal research.

**Observation 1:** VCAT domestic building decisions involving renovation work frequently feature multiple concurrent defects across trades and responsibilities. Attribution of a specific defect to a specific practitioner is contested in most published decisions.

**Attribution level:** LEVEL 0–1 (GENERAL_BUILDING_HARM / CARPENTRY_ASSOCIATED in some cases).

**Observation 2:** In published decisions involving structural alteration, engineering input and building surveyor decisions are frequently identified as central issues. Cases in which carpenter judgement or boundary recognition is identified as the primary causative factor are not clearly represented in published material reviewed.

**Attribution level:** LEVEL 1–2 at most in cases where framing or structural carpentry is referenced.

**Observation 3:** Rectification costs are frequently awarded in VCAT domestic building decisions. Where renovation work is involved, costs associated with defective framing or structural timber work appear in some decisions. Attribution to carpenter capability specifically (rather than to builder management or design) is not established in available published material.

**Attribution level:** LEVEL 1 (CARPENTRY_ASSOCIATED) — workmanship relevance possible but not consistently attributed to carpenter capability or judgement.

**Policy relevance of VCAT material:** CASE_EVIDENCE_ONLY. VCAT decisions are not treated as prevalence evidence. No statewide extrapolation is made.

## Part 11 — Falsification Test

The following alternative explanations are explicitly tested.

### 11.1 Current evidence does not isolate carpentry

**Finding: SUPPORTED.**

The available Victorian public evidence does not isolate carpentry as a distinct source of consumer harm. Defect and complaint data is not disaggregated by trade or by practitioner sub-class.

### 11.2 Problems are predominantly builder-management issues

**Finding: PLAUSIBLE.**

Where renovation defects are identified in VCAT decisions and regulator data, builder management, supervision, project coordination and contractual failures are frequently identified as contributing factors. Attribution to individual trades operating independently of builder oversight is not clearly established in reviewed material.

### 11.3 Design and engineering failures dominate

**Finding: PLAUSIBLE.**

In available decisions and research, design failures and inadequate engineering input are frequently cited contributors to structural defects. This is consistent with findings from the Existing Pathways and Alternative-Cause Baseline Review (v0.1), which identified multiple non-carpentry causal pathways.

### 11.4 General enforcement is the better intervention

**Finding: PLAUSIBLE — not conclusively established either way.**

It is plausible that improved general enforcement of existing builder registration obligations, building permit requirements and inspection regimes would address the majority of renovation defect risk without a trade-specific capability intervention. This has not been tested against specific carpentry harm data, because that data is not publicly available.

### 11.5 Market or training mechanisms may already be adequate

**Finding: NOT_YET_PROVEN — neither way.**

Whether existing training, licensing and market mechanisms are adequate to address renovation carpentry capability concerns cannot be assessed from available public evidence. This is an evidence gap.

### 11.6 No statewide ARC-relevant public harm can currently be demonstrated

**Finding: SUPPORTED.**

On the basis of publicly available Victorian sources reviewed for this analysis, no statewide ARC-relevant public harm attributable to renovation carpenter capability, judgement or boundary recognition can currently be demonstrated.

This is a legitimate finding.

## Part 12 — Evidence Gaps

Six evidence gaps are identified.

### Gap 1 — BPC administrative data on trade and practitioner sub-class

**Gap:** Current BPC administrative data may contain complaint, defect and enforcement records disaggregated by practitioner class and potentially by trade. This data is not publicly available.

**Decision importance:** HIGH. Without this data, carpenter-specific harm cannot be distinguished from general domestic building harm.

**Public source available?** No. BPC holds this data; it is not published in publicly accessible form.

**Could consultation answer it?** Partially — industry practitioners may provide case evidence. Systematic data requires BPC engagement.

**Recommended:** Relevant if government engagement with the BPC proceeds. Not an immediate action. FOI not recommended at this stage.

## Part 13 — Final Classification

### Classification

**B — CARPENTRY_ASSOCIATED_HARM_EXISTS_BUT_ATTRIBUTION_IS_WEAK**

### Confidence: 55%

### Basis

General domestic building defect harm is partially evidenced by Victorian regulatory data, VCAT decisions, parliamentary inquiry findings and auditor reports.

Renovation carpentry is present in domestic building defect contexts. It is plausible that carpenter workmanship, judgement or boundary recognition contributes to some renovation defects.

However, no publicly available Victorian source reviewed for this analysis establishes:

- a direct causal link between carpenter capability and identifiable consumer harm;
- a reliable estimate of the frequency, severity or scale of carpenter-specific harm;
- that carpenter capability failures are the primary or dominant cause of renovation defects as against builder management, design, engineering or supervision failures.

### Strongest supporting evidence

VCAT published decisions in domestic building renovation cases demonstrate that structural and framing defects occur in renovation work and result in rectification orders. Carpentry work is involved in some of these cases.

### Strongest alternative explanation

Builder management and supervision failures, design deficiencies, inadequate engineering input, and multi-party coordination breakdowns are consistently identified as contributors to renovation defects in available published material. These alternative explanations are not excluded by available evidence.

### Biggest attribution limitation

The complete absence of publicly available trade-disaggregated complaint, defect or enforcement data from the BPC or its predecessor. Without this data, the distinction between general domestic building harm and carpenter-specific capability harm cannot be made on the basis of public evidence.

### Impact on ARC C / 45%

The current evidence does not warrant a change to the ARC finding of C — DISTINCT_CAPABILITY_QUESTION_REMAINS_PLAUSIBLE at 45% confidence.

The evidence reviewed is consistent with the existing finding. It does not increase confidence because:

- no carpenter-specific harm evidence has been identified at attribution Levels 3–5;
- general building defect evidence does not establish carpenter capability as a distinct causal factor;
- scale and recurrence remain unproven.

**ARC finding preserved: C — DISTINCT_CAPABILITY_QUESTION_REMAINS_PLAUSIBLE, 45%.**
