# Real-World Renovation Function Evidence Capture Protocol

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Status:** DESIGN PROTOCOL — NOT YET EXECUTED  
**Scope:** Design-only methodology for future field evidence capture concerning advanced residential renovation carpentry functions in existing-building work.  

---

## Purpose Notice

This protocol defines a governed method for future evidence capture only.

It does **not** authorise execution.  
It does **not** recruit participants.  
It does **not** collect evidence.  
It does **not** change any current governed ARC findings.

The protocol is designed to test, not prove, whether recurring carpenter-specific judgement or boundary-recognition functions are evidenced in real renovation workflows.

Permitted result states:

- **POSITIVE**
- **NEGATIVE**
- **MIXED**
- **INCONCLUSIVE**

## Part 1 — Research Questions

### Primary question

When a concealed, changed, uncertain or unexpected condition is encountered during residential renovation, which actor performs each material judgement function, on what evidence basis, and with what actual or formal authority?

### Secondary question

Does the observed pattern materially differ according to carpenter experience, role or renovation exposure?

Control: primary and secondary questions must be analysed separately.

---

## Part 2 — Incident Eligibility

### QUALIFYING_INCIDENT

A qualifying incident is an event in existing-building residential work where discovered conditions create a material point at which planned work cannot be assumed to continue unchanged without further judgement, boundary recognition, escalation, or authority check.

Candidate classes:

- TERMITE_DAMAGE
- TIMBER_DECAY
- CONCEALED_FRAMING_DAMAGE
- ALTERED_LOAD_PATH
- STRUCTURAL_UNCERTAINTY
- UNAUTHORISED_PRIOR_WORK
- PLAN_SITE_DISCREPANCY
- DEMOLITION_DISCOVERY
- SUBFLOOR_DETERIORATION
- WATER_DAMAGE
- CLADDING_OPEN_UP
- WINDOW_DOOR_OPEN_UP
- BATHROOM_KITCHEN_OPEN_UP
- UNKNOWN_SERVICES
- HAZARDOUS_MATERIAL_INTERFACE
- INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION
- OTHER_CHANGED_CONDITION

### NON_QUALIFYING_INCIDENT

Routine execution events where the planned method remained materially adequate and no boundary, escalation, or authority-sensitive judgement point arose.

### NULL_CASE

A candidate changed-condition event where, after reconstruction, the event either:

1. was not material; or
2. was material but cannot be attributed reliably to any actor/function stage due to insufficient evidence.

Control: include non-dramatic, ordinary, adverse, and unsuccessful events.

## Part 6 — Primary Method: CIT Interview Instrument (Design Only)

### Interview structure controls

- Incident-specific
- Sequential
- Neutral phrasing
- No prestige framing
- Capable of capturing deferral, mistakes, and ordinary execution
- Capable of recording builder/supervisor ownership and uncertainty

### Instrument

#### Section A — Incident framing

1. Please describe one specific project event where an unexpected or uncertain condition became visible.  
2. What was the original planned scope immediately before that event?  
3. What changed at the point of discovery?  
4. Why did this event matter to planned continuation, if at all?

#### Section B — Functional chain reconstruction

5. Who first observed the condition?  
6. What did they record or preserve at that time?  
7. Who first recognised it might be material?  
8. What interpretation was made first, by whom?  
9. What uncertainty was identified, and by whom?  
10. At that moment, was information considered sufficient to continue? By whom?  
11. Was work physically held or stopped? By whom and on what basis?  
12. Who was informed first, and how?  
13. Who sought clarification, from whom, and when?  
14. Was referral or escalation made? Who initiated it?  
15. Who recognised specialist input might be needed?  
16. Who decided specialist input was required?  
17. Who had authority to engage that specialist?  
18. Who recommended any scope change?  
19. Who had authority to approve or direct that scope change?  
20. Was resequencing required? Who decided and who authorised it?  
21. Who judged technical readiness to resume?  
22. Who had formal authority to authorise resumption?

#### Section C — Evidence and authority checks

23. What information did each decision-actor rely on at each stage?  
24. Which parts were based on direct observation, and which on interpretation?  
25. Which approvals were required before action could proceed?  
26. If a specialist was involved, what function did they perform?  
27. If no specialist was involved, why not?  
28. What documents or contemporaneous records exist for this incident?

#### Section D — Adverse, null, and deferral capture

29. Were any initial interpretations later found incorrect?  
30. Were there points where deferral was appropriate?  
31. Were there points where ordinary trade execution was sufficient without escalation?  
32. Did builder/supervisor authority fully explain the decision path in this case?  
33. What uncertainty remained unresolved?

#### Section E — Counterfactual and boundary probe

34. What would likely have happened if the identified decision-actor had not been available?  
35. Which actions were within your role, and which were outside your role boundary?  
36. Were any steps transferred to another actor due to authority limits?

#### Section F — Closing controls

37. Is there any part of this sequence you are unsure about?  
38. Is there any part that cannot be verified from records or other actors?  
39. Is this incident best treated as qualifying, non-qualifying, or null?

## Part 22 — Falsification Design

The method must be able to falsify whether:

1. L3 judgement is recurring
2. L4 boundary recognition is recurring
3. experienced carpenters materially differ from occupational carpenters
4. renovation creates a distinct function
5. supervision explains the observed function
6. DB-L explains the formal function
7. builder authority explains the decision layer
8. the function is suitable for objective assessment
9. the seven ARC domains reflect real workflows
