## Purpose Notice

This is a governed primary-source evidence review.

Its purpose is to determine whether publicly available occupational, qualification and regulatory evidence supports the existence of a recurring and materially distinct practitioner function in advanced residential renovation carpentry — one that can be separated from ordinary occupational carpentry, general site supervision, domestic builder responsibility, engineering and design authority, licensed-trade work, and permit or surveyor functions.

This is NOT advocacy for Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC).
This is NOT advocacy for the Registered Residential Carpenter (RRC) concept.
This is NOT a proposal for a new registration class.
This is NOT evidence that regulation is required.

The purpose is to determine what the evidence genuinely supports.

Legitimate null conclusions include:

> **NO_RELIABLE_FUNCTIONAL_DISTINCTIVENESS_EVIDENCE_IDENTIFIED**

or:

> **FUNCTIONAL_OVERLAP_EXISTS_BUT_DISTINCT_PRACTITIONER_FUNCTION_IS_NOT_ESTABLISHED**

The canonical ARC findings preserved at the commencement of this review are:

> **ARC / Existing Pathways: C — DISTINCT_CAPABILITY_QUESTION_REMAINS_PLAUSIBLE**
> Confidence: 45%

> **Public-Interest Harm: B — CARPENTRY_ASSOCIATED_HARM_EXISTS_BUT_ATTRIBUTION_IS_WEAK**
> Confidence: 55%

> **Practitioner Population: B — LARGE_GENERAL_CARPENTRY_WORKFORCE_BUT_RENOVATION_COHORT_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE**
> Confidence: 65%

These findings are not automatically altered by this review.

## Governing Controls

### Hierarchy

Public-interest problem → practitioner function → capability → assessment → assessor calibration → governed pilot → public-interest evaluation → government decision support.

### Sequencing principle

Practitioner function first. Capability evidence second. Regulatory vehicle last.

### Critical controls throughout

- `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED ≠ capability absent`.
- Renovation complexity alone ≠ distinct practitioner function.
- Experience ≠ functional distinctiveness.
- DB-L Carpentry is retained as the primary alternative explanation.
- Builder responsibility is kept structurally separate from carpentry capability.
- Engineering, surveyor and licensed-trade authority are excluded from any ARC hypothesis.
- Project ARC taxonomy is not used as proof of a distinct function.
- Case evidence is not extrapolated to a statewide finding.
- A null outcome is fully permitted and legitimate.

## Controlling Question

> Does advanced residential renovation carpentry involve a sufficiently recurring and distinguishable practitioner function — in capability, judgement, coordination and boundary recognition — that it can be objectively described and assessed separately from ordinary occupational carpentry and builder-level responsibility?

## Evidence Classification System

All claims are classified using the following labels:

- **DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED** — established by the cited primary source
- **PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED** — the source supports some but not all aspects
- **INFERENCE** — a reasonable analytical inference, not directly stated by the source
- **UNKNOWN** — not established by sources reviewed
- **REQUIRES_GOVERNMENT_CLARIFICATION** — depends on government-held material
- **REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW** — depends on authoritative legal interpretation
- **REQUIRES_PILOT_EVIDENCE** — depends on future evidence

Additional tags:

- `PROJECT_HYPOTHESIS` — project model, not independently verified
- `CASE_EVIDENCE_ONLY` — individual case, not statewide
- `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED` — public sources do not clearly confirm or refute
- `SHARED_FUNCTION` — present in more than one comparator group
- `OUT_OF_SCOPE` — beyond ARC hypothesis boundaries

The following comparator groups are maintained separately throughout this review.

| Code | Group | Description |
|---|---|---|

| C | SITE_SUPERVISION / FOREMAN FUNCTION | Oversight of trade activity on site, including sequencing and trade coordination, performed at a supervisory level without full builder responsibility |

| E | DB-U / BUILDER RESPONSIBILITY FUNCTION | The function of a registered domestic builder — full project responsibility including contracting, compliance, consumer protection, permit coordination and project risk |

| G | BUILDING_SURVEYOR / PERMIT FUNCTION | Permit authority, statutory inspection, certification, planning compliance |

## Part 2 — Ordinary Occupational Carpentry

### 2.1 Primary Source

**ANZSCO 3312 — Carpentry and Joinery Occupations** (Jobs and Skills Australia)

ANZSCO Unit Group 3312 describes carpenters as workers who:

- construct, erect, install, maintain and repair structures and fixtures made of wood and other materials;
- cut, shape and fit timber and other construction materials;
- read and interpret plans, specifications and drawings;
- measure, mark and arrange materials according to plans;
- use hand tools and power tools;
- install structural timber, framing, formwork, cladding, roofing, flooring, windows, doors and related fixtures;
- perform maintenance, repair and alteration work.

Evidence classification: **DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED** by Jobs and Skills Australia / ANZSCO.

### 2.2 CPC30220 Certificate III in Carpentry

CPC30220 is the nationally recognised trade qualification for carpenters. It is delivered under the CPC Construction, Plumbing and Services Training Package.

The qualification includes units of competency addressing:

- applying work health and safety requirements (CPCCWHS1001, CPCCOHS2001)
- reading and interpreting plans, specifications and drawings
- using construction tools and equipment
- carrying out measurements and calculations
- constructing and installing timber framing for floors, walls, ceilings and roofs
- installing windows, doors and associated hardware
- installing internal and external cladding
- carrying out renovation, maintenance and repair work — explicitly addressed in units such as CPCCCA3016 (Install timber wall and floor framing) and renovation-related units within the elective range
- working in and around existing structures
- managing site safety and sequencing work

Evidence classification for direct trade execution functions: **DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED** by CPC30220 training package, training.gov.au.

### 2.3 What is already clearly part of ordinary carpentry

The following are directly supported by primary qualification and occupational evidence as part of ordinary occupational carpentry:

| Function | Evidence classification |
|---|---|
| Construction, installation, alteration, renovation, repair | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — ANZSCO 3312; CPC30220 |
| Framing, cladding, windows, doors | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — ANZSCO 3312; CPC30220 |
| Measuring, marking, setting out | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — ANZSCO 3312; CPC30220 |
| Reading and interpreting plans and specifications | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — ANZSCO 3312; CPC30220 |
| Use of hand and power tools | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — ANZSCO 3312 |
| Workplace safety compliance | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — CPC30220 WHS units |
| Basic trade sequencing | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — CPC30220 |
| Working in and around existing structures | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — CPC30220 renovation/repair elective range |

### 2.4 Important qualification controls

CPC30220 is a trade qualification. It is not evidence of low competence.

A qualified carpenter has demonstrated competence across the above functions through nationally regulated training and assessment.

The question at issue in this review is not whether ordinary carpenters are competent. It is whether renovation work in existing buildings produces a recurring practitioner function that goes beyond what ordinary trade qualification and ordinary trade experience address — and, if so, whether that function is already addressed by builder registration, DB-L Carpentry, engineering, or other existing mechanisms.

## Part 3 — Advanced Renovation Function Hypothesis

The following hypothesised functions are tested against the comparator groups. For each, a comparator classification is assigned.

**Classification vocabulary:**

- `DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED_AS_CARPENTRY` — the function is part of ordinary carpentry as supported by ANZSCO and CPC30220
- `DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED_AS_BUILDER_FUNCTION` — the function is part of builder responsibility as supported by primary sources
- `DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED_AS_OTHER_PROFESSION_FUNCTION` — the function is assigned to engineering, surveying or a licensed trade
- `SHARED_FUNCTION` — the function appears in more than one comparator group
- `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` — the function is plausible as a distinct capability but is not formally defined in publicly available primary sources as either carpentry or builder responsibility
- `NOT_IDENTIFIED` — the function is not identified in primary sources reviewed
- `UNKNOWN` — status cannot be determined from sources reviewed

| Hypothesised Function | Classification | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis of concealed or uncertain existing conditions | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | CPC30220 renovation units imply working around existing conditions but do not clearly define a diagnosis function. Not formally defined as a distinct carpentry or builder competency in primary sources reviewed. |
| Interpretation of irregular existing structures | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Related to plan interpretation (carpentry) and site assessment (builder). Not separately defined at this level. |
| Determination of whether work can safely proceed | `SHARED_FUNCTION` | Touches both ordinary carpentry WHS obligations and builder compliance responsibility. Not clearly separated as a distinct carpentry judgement function in primary sources. |
| Recognition of structural uncertainty | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Structural awareness appears in CPC30220 framing units. Structural assessment and determination are engineering or DB-U functions. The boundary between structural awareness and structural assessment is not formally defined for carpentry. |
| Identification of when engineering input is required | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Not formally defined as a carpentry competency in primary sources. Not exclusively a builder function either. DB-L Carpentry scope implies some form of referral recognition but this is `REQUIRES_GOVERNMENT_CLARIFICATION`. |
| Sequencing around unknown existing conditions | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Trade sequencing is a Cert III competency. Sequencing specifically responding to unknown or discovered conditions is not formally defined as a distinct function at the carpentry level. |
| Integration of old and new construction | `DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED_AS_CARPENTRY` | CPC30220 renovation and repair units address working with existing structures. This function is within ordinary carpentry scope. |
| Coordination of interfaces between trades | `SHARED_FUNCTION` | Trade coordination is a site supervision, DB-L, and builder function. Not separately defined as a distinct advanced-carpentry function in primary sources. |
| Temporary works judgement | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Temporary support and propping appear in framing units. The judgement function around temporary works in uncertain conditions is not formally defined as a distinct carpentry competency. |
| Preservation / exposure decisions before work continues | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Not separately defined in primary sources as either carpentry or builder competency at the level of a formal function. |
| Scope clarification | `SHARED_FUNCTION` | Scope clarification occurs across builder contracting (DB-U), project management (Cert IV/Diploma) and practical trade work. Not defined as a distinct advanced-carpentry function. |
| Identification of conflicting evidence | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Not separately defined at any comparator level. |
| Recognition of limits of competence | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Professional-judgement competency frameworks include this. Not separately formally defined in primary carpentry qualifications in publicly reviewed sources. |
| Escalation / referral | `SHARED_FUNCTION` | Appears across multiple comparator groups — WHS obligations (carpentry), builder compliance and specialist-engagement responsibility (DB-U, DB-L). Not exclusively a distinct advanced-carpentry function. |
| Refusal to proceed under uncertainty | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED` | Not formally defined in primary sources. Consistent with WHS obligations at the ordinary carpentry level but not at the level of a distinct higher-order function. |
| Communication of unknowns to builder/client/other practitioner | `SHARED_FUNCTION` | Communication is a trade, supervision, and builder-responsibility competency. Not formally defined as a distinct advanced-carpentry function. |

### 3.1 Summary observation

Several hypothesised functions fall into `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_FORMALLY_DEFINED`. This does not mean those functions are absent from practice. It means that publicly available primary sources do not separately define them as a distinct carpentry function beyond ordinary Cert III scope, or assign them exclusively to a different comparator group.

This finding is consistent with the existing canonical finding:

> **C — DISTINCT_CAPABILITY_QUESTION_REMAINS_PLAUSIBLE** / 45%

It does not automatically strengthen that finding without further evidence.

## Part 4 — Renovation Versus New-Build Complexity

### 4.1 Whether authoritative sources recognise differences

Primary construction qualification and regulatory sources do recognise differences between new construction and alteration, renovation, repair or existing-building work. The following is directly supported:

| Difference | Source | Evidence classification |
|---|---|---|
| Renovation and repair work as a distinct competency category | CPC30220 elective unit range | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED |
| Existing-building work involves unknown and variable conditions — implied in scope of applicable units | CPC30220 training package; NCC Volume 1 and 2 existing-building provisions | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED |
| NCC Part H3 (existing buildings) distinguishes new construction from alteration, addition or change of use | NCC 2022 Volume 1, Section H | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED |
| Existing buildings may involve legacy materials, non-standard construction, prior alterations and incomplete documentation | INFERENCE from NCC existing-building provisions and CPC30220 renovation units | INFERENCE |

### 4.2 Critical control — complexity alone is not distinctiveness

The question is not whether renovation work is more variable than new construction.

The question is whether that variability produces a recurring practitioner function requiring a different kind of judgement — one that is not already captured by ordinary carpentry training, builder registration, or DB-L Carpentry assessment.

### 4.3 Finding on renovation/new-build distinction

Authoritative sources acknowledge renovation and existing-building work as a distinct competency category within carpentry. They do not separately define or name a distinct advanced practitioner function associated with that category at a level above Cert III and below builder registration.

Evidence classification: **PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED** that authoritative sources recognise meaningful differences. **NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED** that those differences produce a formally recognised distinct practitioner function at the hypothesised advanced-carpentry level.

**Classification vocabulary:**

- `DIRECTLY_EVIDENCED` — CPC30220 units clearly support this domain
- `PARTIALLY_EVIDENCED` — Some CPC30220 units touch this domain; full scope is not clearly evidenced
- `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED` — No clear public CPC30220 evidence for this domain at the hypothesised level
- `UNKNOWN` — Cannot be determined from sources reviewed

> **Critical control:** `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED ≠ capability absent`. Unit titles alone do not reveal the full content of assessment.

| ARC Domain | CPC30220 Crosswalk Finding | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Technical Capability | `DIRECTLY_EVIDENCED` | CPC30220 core and elective units directly address construction, installation, framing, finishing, renovation and repair. Technical execution is the core purpose of the qualification. |
| 2. Diagnostic Capability | `PARTIALLY_EVIDENCED` | CPC30220 renovation/repair units imply assessment of existing conditions. | 4. Planning Capability | `DIRECTLY_EVIDENCED` | CPC30220 includes plan reading, measuring, sequencing and preparation. Trade-level planning is directly evidenced. Higher-order project planning, scope management and contingency planning under unknown conditions are not separately defined at Cert III. |
| 5. Coordination Capability | `PARTIALLY_EVIDENCED` | CPC30220 addresses worksite communication and co-working. Formal trade coordination and interface management are not separately defined as Cert III competencies. |
| 6. Professional Judgement | `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED` | Professional judgement integrating multiple domains under uncertainty is not separately and formally defined as a Cert III competency in publicly reviewed sources. Boundary Recognition | `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED` | Recognition of competence limits, formal referral protocols and stopping decisions are not separately defined as Cert III competencies in publicly reviewed sources. ### 5.1 Crosswalk observation

Domains 1 (Technical Capability) and 4 (Planning Capability) are directly evidenced in CPC30220. Domains 2, 3 and 5 are partially evidenced. Domains 6 (Professional Judgement) and 7 (Boundary Recognition), as formally structured at the hypothesised advanced level, are not clearly evidenced in CPC30220 public sources.

This is consistent with the plausibility finding. It does not prove that a distinct advanced function exists, but it does identify that the hypothesised function is not already fully addressed at the Cert III level.

## Part 6 — Cert IV / Diploma Crosswalk

### 6.1 CPC40120 — Certificate IV in Building and Construction

CPC40120 addresses building supervision, project planning, contracts, compliance, estimating, coordination and site management. It is the primary qualification for building supervisors and site managers.

Functions within CPC40120 that overlap with hypothesised advanced-carpentry functions:

| Function | Comparator group | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Scope management and project planning | C — Site Supervision / E — Builder | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED_AS_BUILDER_FUNCTION or SHARED_FUNCTION |
| Trade coordination and sequencing | C — Site Supervision | SHARED_FUNCTION |
| Compliance and regulatory awareness | E — Builder | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED_AS_BUILDER_FUNCTION |
| Estimation and cost control | E — Builder | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED_AS_BUILDER_FUNCTION |
| Quality and safety management | C — Site Supervision / E — Builder | SHARED_FUNCTION |

### 6.2 CPC50220 — Diploma of Building and Construction

CPC50220 addresses full building management: project management, financial management, contracts, occupational health and safety management, project administration and team management.

These functions are clearly builder-management and project-management capabilities. They are not trade-specific judgement functions. The Diploma does not separately define an advanced-carpentry function at the hypothesised level.

### 6.3 Critical separation

Builder-management capability (Cert IV / Diploma) must be separated from advanced trade-specific judgement in existing-building renovation contexts.

The Cert IV and Diploma capture coordination, compliance and management functions. They do not capture the hypothesised function of a carpenter-level practitioner exercising judgement about concealed conditions, structural uncertainty and sequencing decisions within their trade scope before escalating to builder or engineering authority.

Evidence classification: **DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED** that Cert IV and Diploma functions are builder-management rather than advanced trade-specific. **INFERENCE** that this leaves a gap at the advanced trade level. The gap inference is plausible but not independently proven.

## Part 9 — Engineering, Surveyor and Licensed-Trade Boundaries

The following functions are explicitly identified as outside any ARC or advanced-carpentry hypothesis.

### Engineering design authority

Structural design, structural certification, design documentation, engineering calculations and engineering professional responsibility are engineering functions. Any hypothesised ARC function that requires structural determination must trigger escalation to engineering authority.

Classification: `OUT_OF_SCOPE`

### Building surveyor / permit authority

Permit issue, statutory inspection, certification of occupancy and statutory building approvals are building surveyor functions. Any hypothesised ARC function that involves certification or permit determination is `OUT_OF_SCOPE`.

Classification: `OUT_OF_SCOPE`

### Plumbing authority

Plumbing work — sanitary, stormwater, hot water, drainage — is licensed-trade work in Victoria. Any carpentry work that interfaces with plumbing must stop at the plumbing boundary.

Classification: `OUT_OF_SCOPE` for ARC

### Electrical authority

Electrical work is licensed in Victoria. Any carpentry work that encounters or affects electrical installations must stop at the electrical boundary.

Classification: `OUT_OF_SCOPE` for ARC

### Waterproofing / licensed trade

Where waterproofing is a licensed trade function in Victoria, it is `OUT_OF_SCOPE` for ARC.

### Structural certification

Certification of structural adequacy or structural compliance is an engineering function.

Classification: `OUT_OF_SCOPE`

### Statutory inspection authority

Mandatory and statutory inspection is a building surveyor function.

Classification: `OUT_OF_SCOPE`

### Boundary principle

ARC must not be made broader to create distinctiveness. Recognition of these boundaries — and the associated referral and stopping obligations — is itself one of the hypothesised competency dimensions.

Assessment of professional judgement in existing-building renovation contexts is not described in CPC30220 or CPC40120 public assessment guidelines as a distinct formal method.

### 11.3 Potential assessment methods

- Boundary recognition — candidate identifies when to stop and refer

The project's existing scenario bank and assessment framework are the project's proposed answer to this question, not independent evidence that the answer is correct. This is noted without prejudice to their quality.

## Part 16 — Residual Function Test

> After accounting for ordinary carpentry (A), DB-L Carpentry (D), builder responsibility (E), supervision (C), engineering (F), surveying (G) and licensed-trade functions (H), what practitioner function — if any — remains?

### 16.1 Analysis

Ordinary carpentry (A) directly addresses technical execution, plan reading, renovation and repair work.

DB-L Carpentry (D) addresses the same technical base, adding builder-registration framework, experience and technical referee evidence — but its assessment of professional judgement and boundary recognition at the hypothesised level is not publicly established.

Builder responsibility (E) addresses project-wide compliance, contracting, consumer protection, permit coordination and specialist engagement.

Site supervision (C) addresses coordination and sequencing oversight.

Engineering (F), surveying (G) and licensed trades (H) are excluded from the hypothesised function.

### 16.2 Residual function

After accounting for these comparator groups, a plausible residual function remains in the space between:

- ordinary trade execution (A); and
- full builder responsibility or DB-L registration (D/E).

The residual function — if it exists — is in the domain of:

- diagnostic judgement in existing-building renovation contexts (recognition of concealed conditions, determination of unknowns, identification of what cannot yet be determined);
- professional judgement applied to sequencing, stopping and referral decisions at the trade level;
- structured boundary recognition — formal recognition of the point at which engineering, builder or licensed-trade authority must be engaged.

This residual function is not engineering. It is not builder management. It is not fully captured by Cert III primary sources. Whether it is already captured by DB-L Carpentry assessment is `NOT_CLEARLY_EVIDENCED`.

### 16.3 Residual function test result

> **PARTIAL_RESIDUAL_FUNCTION**

A partial residual function is plausible at the intersection of diagnostic judgement, professional judgement and structured boundary recognition in existing-building renovation contexts. This function is not fully established. Its distinctiveness from DB-L Carpentry cannot be confirmed from public evidence.

## Part 19 — Falsification

The following falsification tests are applied.

### Claim 1 — Advanced renovation carpentry is functionally distinct

**Falsification attempt:** Ordinary Cert III carpentry explicitly includes renovation and repair. DB-L Carpentry provides a builder-registration framework specifically for carpentry renovation. Builder oversight covers scope, compliance and specialist engagement. Experience within ordinary carpentry may develop all hypothesised judgement functions over time.

**Falsification result:** Not falsified, but not proven. The hypothesis survives as plausible. The evidence does not confirm the function as established and distinct.

### Claim 2 — The distinction is not merely experience

**Falsification attempt:** No primary source separately defines the hypothesised functions at a formal level above Cert III. The most parsimonious explanation is that experienced renovation carpenters develop these functions through ordinary practice.

**Falsification result:** Not resolved. Whether experience alone is sufficient, or whether a formal distinct function exists, is not determinable from public primary sources alone.

### Claim 3 — The distinction is not merely builder responsibility

**Falsification attempt:** Builder responsibility — through DB-L Carpentry specifically — covers renovation work within a builder-registration framework including technical evidence requirements.

**Falsification result:** Partially falsified. Several hypothesised functions (scope management, compliance coordination, consumer communication) are substantially captured by builder responsibility. The residual function — diagnostic judgement and structured boundary recognition at the trade level — is not fully falsified.

### Claim 4 — The distinction is not already DB-L Carpentry

**Falsification attempt:** DB-L Carpentry requires Cert III, practical experience and technical referee evidence in a registration framework. If DB-L Carpentry already assesses the hypothesised professional judgement and boundary recognition domains, no distinct function remains.

**Falsification result:** `REQUIRES_GOVERNMENT_CLARIFICATION`. The current public evidence does not allow this claim to be falsified or confirmed. This is the most important unresolved falsification question.

### Claim 5 — The function can be objectively assessed

**Falsification attempt:** No independent validation of the project's assessment architecture exists. The scenario bank and framework are project hypotheses.

**Falsification result:** Not falsified in principle — scenario and portfolio assessment methods are standard in VET and professional contexts. Not confirmed as reliable and consistent without pilot evidence.

### Claim 6 — Boundary recognition is a genuine competence dimension

**Falsification attempt:** Boundary recognition obligations exist at the ordinary WHS level (Cert III) and builder compliance level. There may be no separate distinct dimension between these.

**Falsification result:** Partially falsified — at least some boundary recognition functions are already covered. Not fully falsified — the hypothesised structured referral and stopping competency in renovation contexts is not fully captured by either ordinary WHS obligations or builder compliance descriptions in public sources.

### Claim 7 — The function is recurring rather than anecdotal

**Falsification attempt:** Five cases are case evidence only. No population data establishes recurrence. ANZSCO and CPC30220 do not separately describe a recurring advanced-renovation function as a distinct occupational category.

**Falsification result:** Not established. Recurrence is plausible but not evidenced at population scale.

### Claim 8 — The function has public-interest relevance

**Falsification attempt:** Harm attribution to carpenter capability specifically is weak (Finding B / 55%). Population of advanced-renovation practitioners is not identifiable (Finding B / 65%). Without established harm attribution and identifiable population, public-interest relevance is not established.

**Falsification result:** Not established at the level required for a formal policy recommendation. Public-interest relevance is plausible but dependent on resolving harm attribution and population identification.

## Part 20 — Final Classification

### Finding

> **C — DISTINCT_FUNCTION_IS_PLAUSIBLE_BUT_NOT_SUFFICIENTLY_EVIDENCED**

**Confidence: 50%**

### Reasoning

Primary occupational and qualification sources confirm ordinary carpentry includes renovation and repair work. They partially evidence several of the hypothesised advanced-function domains. They do not clearly evidence professional judgement and boundary recognition as formally defined distinct competencies above the Cert III level.

A partial residual function is plausible at the intersection of diagnostic judgement, professional judgement and structured boundary recognition in existing-building renovation. This function cannot be fully explained by either ordinary Cert III training or builder registration from public primary sources alone. Whether it is already captured by DB-L Carpentry assessment is the most important unresolved question.

The function is not falsified. It is not established.

### Supporting analysis

**Strongest evidence for distinctiveness:**

Domains 6 (Professional Judgement) and 7 (Boundary Recognition) are not clearly evidenced in CPC30220 public material as formally defined competencies. The hypothesised functions in these domains — structured diagnostic decision-making under uncertainty, formal identification of when to stop and refer — are not fully captured by ordinary WHS obligations or builder compliance descriptions. The five practitioner cases consistently illustrate functions consistent with this residual hypothesis.

**Strongest evidence against distinctiveness:**

Experience within ordinary carpentry is a credible full explanation. DB-L Carpentry is a credible institutional explanation — its assessment content is not publicly established as excluding the hypothesised domains. Builder oversight substantially captures several hypothesised functions. No primary source separately defines or names the hypothesised advanced-renovation practitioner function.

**Strongest alternative explanation:**

DB-L Carpentry — as a builder-registration mechanism specifically scoped to renovation carpentry, with Cert III and practical-experience requirements — is the strongest institutional alternative explanation. Its assessment architecture is not publicly established, which means it cannot be confirmed as capturing or not capturing the residual function.

**Clearest residual function:**

Structured diagnostic judgement and formal boundary recognition in existing-building renovation contexts — specifically the function of identifying the point at which ordinary carpentry authority must stop and engineering, builder or licensed-trade authority must be engaged, and doing so as a formal competency rather than an incidental WHS obligation.

**Strongest overlap with DB-L Carpentry:**

Technical capability, practical experience requirements and renovation scope. The overlap in professional judgement and boundary recognition domains is possible but not publicly established.

**Strongest overlap with builder responsibility:**

Scope management, compliance coordination, consumer communication, specialist engagement and project-wide risk. These functions are substantially builder functions.

**Biggest evidence gap:**

The content of DB-L Carpentry assessment — specifically whether it tests professional judgement and boundary recognition in the hypothesised sense — is the single most important unresolved question. Government clarification of DB-L Carpentry assessment architecture would materially alter this review's conclusions.

**What evidence would change the conclusion:**

- Published DB-L Carpentry assessment specifications showing the domains tested → would either confirm DB-L already covers the residual function (shifting to B or A) or confirm it does not (shifting toward D or E)
- A governed pilot producing independent evidence that the function can be reliably assessed → would shift assessability from `REQUIRES_PILOT_EVIDENCE` to established
- A larger independently documented case series showing recurrence across diverse renovation contexts → would shift the recurrence finding
- Victorian Government Engage Victoria consultation material clarifying how renovation carpentry is treated within the registration reform framework → material clarification
