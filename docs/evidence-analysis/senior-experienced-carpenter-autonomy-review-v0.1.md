# Senior / Experienced Carpenter Autonomy Evidence Review

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Status:** WORKING DRAFT — for Dan and Atlas review. Not approved for external distribution.  
**Scope:** Governed evidence review testing whether experienced, senior, leading-hand, specialist-renovation or otherwise advanced carpenters are formally or practically distinguished from ordinary occupational carpenters by higher autonomy, judgement, diagnostic responsibility, sequencing responsibility, uncertainty recognition, escalation/referral responsibility, technical stop/withhold decisions, supervision, coordination, or responsibility for interpreting existing-building conditions.

---

## Purpose Notice

This is a bounded evidence review.

It is **not** advocacy for ARC.  
It is **not** advocacy for RRC.  
It is **not** proof that experience itself creates advanced competence.

Its purpose is to determine whether higher carpenter autonomy exists as a recurring and evidentially identifiable function.

Legitimate null outcomes are preserved.

Experienced carpenters are plausibly allocated more complex work in practice, but no strong direct public source reviewed clearly distinguishes a senior carpenter complexity tier. The closest formal context is that carpentry as an occupation includes repair, alteration and renovation work.

### Strongest S3 evidence

### Strongest S4 evidence

### Strongest evidence against distinct senior-carpenter autonomy

The strongest evidence against a distinct senior carpenter function is that the clearest autonomy indicators are already better explained by **leading-hand**, **foreman**, **site-supervisor**, **builder** or **DB-L** role structures.

### Leading-hand finding

Leading hand is the clearest directly supported higher-autonomy role, but it is a **supervisory** distinction rather than a clearly defined advanced carpenter autonomy class.

### Foreman / supervisor overlap finding

Foreman and supervisor evidence substantially overlaps the autonomy functions under review and better explains sequencing, work checking, coordination, part-site/site supervision and crew-level decision-making.

### DB-L overlap finding

DB-L remains a plausible formal pathway for bounded carpentry scope within the Domestic Builder (Limited) framework, but this second pass did not verify any public claim about assessment depth; the governed DB-L **D / 75%** finding remains unchanged.

### Self-employed / subcontractor finding

Self-employed and subcontract carpenter status more clearly evidences **business autonomy** and, for self-employed persons, **duty-holder autonomy** than distinct **technical judgement autonomy**.

### Renovation-specialist finding

Renovation-specialist practice is plausible, but no formal public autonomy distinction for specialist renovation carpenters was identified.

### Market-recognition finding

Market recognition is more clearly evidenced than formal competence recognition or regulatory recognition.
