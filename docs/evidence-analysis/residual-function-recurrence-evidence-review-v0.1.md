## Part 1 — Define the Residual Function

### RESIDUAL_FUNCTION_HYPOTHESIS

A recurring trade-level function in existing-building renovation involving:

1. recognising incomplete, concealed or conflicting evidence;
2. diagnosing whether observed conditions permit safe continuation;
3. identifying limits of carpentry authority or competence;
4. determining when investigation, referral or escalation is required;
5. deciding when work should stop or scope should change; and
6. communicating uncertainty and the next required action.

### Boundary control

This definition is intentionally narrow. It does **not** broaden the function into builder, engineering, surveying or licensed-trade authority.

### Part 2 finding

Independent sources do show recurring **function types** involving hidden conditions, reassessment, withholding action and specialist input in existing-building contexts.

However:

- recurrence is more clearly evidenced at a **system/process** level than at a **carpenter-specific occupational** level;
- many sources describe **shared** or **builder/supervisor/competent-person** functions rather than a distinct carpentry function; and
- much of the clearest stop-work evidence is **generic WHS** rather than technical carpentry judgement.

## Part 3 — Renovation / Existing-Building Conditions

### Recurring contexts identified

The following renovation or existing-building conditions recur across independent sources reviewed:

- concealed structural deterioration;
- termite or timber-pest damage;
- water ingress and rot;
- hidden services;
- undocumented or changed site conditions affecting permits or compliance;
- structural instability during demolition or alteration;
- plan/site inconsistency requiring clarification;
- hazardous-material interfaces, especially asbestos; and
- altered work requiring revised evidence of suitability or expert input.

### Actor expectation by context

| Context | Who is expected to recognise it? | Who is expected to stop/refer/escalate? | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden services / asbestos / instability | Worker and supervisor | Competent person, licensed trade, PCBU, specialist | Not carpenter-specific |
| Permit-affecting changed conditions | Builder / permit applicant / surveyor | Relevant building surveyor, engineer, authorities | Strong builder/surveyor overlap |
| Termite or concealed deterioration | Practitioner, owner, inspector mix | Pest inspector, engineer, builder, surveyor as needed | Mixed attribution |
| Plan/site mismatch in carpentry work | Carpenter | Clarification through relevant personnel | Carpentry-relevant but limited |
| Compliance solution for altered work | Builder / surveyor / expert mix | Expert judgement / revised documentation | Strong non-carpenter overlap |

### Part 3 finding

Renovation uncertainty is independently evidenced.  
Carpenter-specific ownership of the full residual function is not independently evidenced.

## Part 9 — Recurrence Threshold

### Assessment

**Recurrence status:** **REPEATED**

### Basis

The repeated rating is based on multiple independent source classes assuming that changed conditions, hidden hazards or site/document mismatch recur often enough to require:

- formal permit-amendment pathways;
- repeated WHS stop/reassess controls;
- repeated training coverage; and
- repeated compliance reassessment mechanisms.

### Boundary

This **REPEATED** finding does **not** mean:

- materially recurring as a carpenter-specific occupational function;
- prevalence across Victoria has been quantified; or
- isolated case repetition has been treated as system-wide proof.

## Part 11 — Positive vs Negative Evidence

### A. POSITIVE RECURRENCE EVIDENCE

- Independent official sources repeatedly assume hidden-condition uncertainty in existing-building work.
- WorkSafe and Safe Work material repeatedly require withholding action and obtaining competent input when uncertainty remains.
- BPC and NCC material repeatedly assume altered conditions can require formal reassessment, revised documents and expert involvement.
- Carpentry training material supports recurrent plan/site comparison and immediate pre-start review in renovation/refurbishment/maintenance contexts.

### B. EVIDENCE GAPS

- No strong independent source in this run clearly assigns the full residual function to carpenters as distinct occupational actors.
- No public source in this run quantifies the prevalence of these functions in residential renovation carpentry specifically.
- No authoritative source in this run clearly separates experienced carpentry from builder/supervisor responsibility at the required resolution.

### C. ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS

- The function may be largely captured by generic WHS duties.
- The function may be largely a builder, supervisor, surveyor or engineer coordination issue.
- The function may be shared across multiple actors rather than residual to carpentry.

### D. EVIDENCE AGAINST DISTINCT RECURRENCE

- Official occupation profiles do not expressly define diagnosis, referrals, authority limits or stop-work decisions as core carpenter functions.
- The clearest stop-work evidence is hazard-led, not carpenter-led.
- Permit amendment, redesign and compliance reassessment are strongly tied to builder/surveyor/engineer pathways.

## Part 17 — Final Classification

### Classification selected

**C — REPEATED_RECURRENCE_IS_PLAUSIBLE_BUT_ACTOR_OR_FUNCTION_DISTINCTION_REMAINS_UNCLEAR**

**Confidence:** **60%**

### Reason

Multiple independent sources support repeated recurrence of hidden-condition uncertainty, stop/reassess pathways, specialist-input triggers and site/document clarification in existing-building work.

The evidence is not strong enough to support **D** or **E** because:

- actor attribution to carpenters remains unclear;
