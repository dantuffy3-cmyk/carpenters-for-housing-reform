## Purpose Notice

This is a governed primary-source evidence review.

Its purpose is to determine whether publicly available Victorian and national evidence can establish that a distinct, identifiable and materially sized population of renovation-focused or advanced residential carpentry practitioners exists in Victoria.

This is NOT advocacy for Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC), the Registered Residential Carpenter (RRC) concept, or any regulatory change.

A legitimate finding of this review is:

> **NO RELIABLE EVIDENCE OF A DISTINCT OR MATERIAL ADVANCED-RESIDENTIAL-CARPENTRY POPULATION WAS IDENTIFIED.**

The canonical ARC findings preserved throughout this review are:

> **C — DISTINCT_CAPABILITY_QUESTION_REMAINS_PLAUSIBLE**
> Confidence: 45%

> **B — CARPENTRY_ASSOCIATED_HARM_EXISTS_BUT_ATTRIBUTION_IS_WEAK**
> Confidence: 55%

Population size alone must not strengthen either finding.

## Controlling Question

> How large is the Victorian carpenter population relevant to residential renovation, what evidence exists of an advanced-capability cohort, and is the population sufficiently identifiable and material to warrant further policy consideration?

## Part 1 — Occupational Definition

### ANZSCO 3312 — Carpenter

The Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification of Occupations (ANZSCO) classifies Carpenters under unit group 3312.

The ANZSCO description for Carpenters and Joiners (3312) includes the construction, erection, installation, maintenance, repair, and renovation of structures and fixtures made from timber and related materials.

**Relevant activities within ANZSCO 3312 description (where publicly supported):**

- constructing and erecting frameworks for buildings
- installing and fitting floor structures, ceilings, wall framing, roofing
- fitting and installing doors, windows and staircases
- renovating, repairing and altering existing structures

**Evidence status:**

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| ANZSCO 3312 description includes renovation activities | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED — from official ANZSCO documentation |
| ANZSCO 3312 description identifies a distinct renovation-specialist sub-group | NOT SUPPORTED — ANZSCO classifies by occupation, not specialisation |
| ANZSCO 3312 definition implies a measurable advanced-capability cohort | NOT SUPPORTED — occupational classification does not measure capability level |

**Control:** The presence of renovation activities within an occupational description does not establish that a measurable renovation-specialist population exists or is separately identifiable in workforce data.

## Part 3 — Residential Construction Workforce (Population B)

### 3.1 Occupation × industry cross-tabulation

To identify carpenters working specifically in residential construction, an occupation × industry cross-tabulation is required: ANZSCO 3312 within ANZSIC residential building construction or equivalent.

**Current ABS LFS position:**

The ABS Labour Force Survey does not publicly publish occupation × industry cross-tabulations at the level of ANZSCO 3312 within residential construction for Victoria on a standard basis. Detailed occupation × industry matrices are available from Census data but require specific data requests or table builder access.

**Classification risk:**

Carpenters engaged in residential construction may be classified under multiple industry codes, including:

- Residential Building Construction (ANZSIC)
- Construction Services
- Carpentry Services
- Other Construction Industries
- Self-employment / unincorporated business classifications

A carpenter operating as a sole trader on residential renovation contracts may be classified under Carpentry Services or similar, not under Residential Building Construction. This means that a residential carpentry workforce count derived from industry classification alone is likely to understate the actual residential workforce.

## Part 4 — Renovation, Alteration and Repair Workforce (Population C)

### 4.1 Whether public data distinguishes new-build from renovation carpentry

The ABS and Jobs and Skills Australia occupation profiles for ANZSCO 3312 do not disaggregate the workforce into new-build and renovation subcategories. The ANZSCO occupational description spans both activities, but the statistical collections do not separately identify practitioners who work primarily or exclusively on renovation, alteration and repair.

**Finding:**

> **RENOVATION_COHORT_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE**

No primary statistical source reviewed identifies a population of carpenters whose principal activity is renovation, alteration or repair of existing residential buildings. This is not evidence that such practitioners do not exist. It reflects that the statistical classification system does not measure this dimension.

### 4.2 Proxy analysis — activity data

The following proxies may indicate the scale of renovation-related construction activity in Victoria:

**Building approvals data (ABS 8731.0 / successor series):**

ABS building approvals data distinguishes alterations and additions (A&A) from new residential construction in terms of approved project counts and values. This provides evidence about the *volume of renovation activity* but does not translate directly into a workforce count.

| Proxy | Available | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Victorian A&A approvals (count) | Publicly available (ABS) | Activity count ≠ worker population |
| Victorian A&A approvals (value) | Publicly available (ABS) | Expenditure ≠ worker population |
| Proportion of approvals that are renovation | Calculable from ABS data | Does not identify carpentry-specific workers |
| A&A worker-hours or FTE equivalent | Not publicly available at trade level | Would require industry modelling |

**Control:** Activity volume data does not convert into a practitioner population count without a defensible labour-input model. No such model has been constructed in this review. Activity ≠ worker population.

### 9.2 Policy test — formal recognition of advanced carpentry capability

**Test question:**
> Does Victoria provide a formal mechanism recognising advanced carpentry competence while retaining a carpenter occupational identity?

**Finding:**

The current framework does not include a formal credentialled mechanism that:

1. recognises advanced residential renovation carpentry capability, AND
2. retains a carpentry (rather than builder) occupational identity, AND
3. is distinct from builder registration.

DB-L Carpentry is the closest existing mechanism, but it operates as a builder registration sub-class (not a carpentry credential) and its scope and requirements may not fully address the advanced capability domains identified in the project's capability framework.

## Part 20 — Final Classification

### Selection

Reviewing all primary evidence:

- The total Victorian carpenter workforce (Population A) is large on a SYSTEM_DERIVED estimate.
- A residential carpenter subpopulation (Population B) is plausible but not directly identifiable from standard public statistical sources.
- A renovation-specific cohort (Population C) is not identifiable from public statistical sources.
- DB-L Carpentry exists but its population is not publicly disaggregated.
- The advanced capability cohort (Population H) is not measured.
- All third-party numerical estimates remain unverified.

**Final classification:**

> **B — LARGE_GENERAL_CARPENTRY_WORKFORCE_BUT_RENOVATION_COHORT_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE**

**Confidence: 65%**

This classification is reached independently from the third-party reconnaissance finding of the same classification at 74% confidence. The lower confidence in this governed baseline reflects:

- the SYSTEM_DERIVED (not direct) basis for the Victorian workforce estimate;
- the absence of confirmed Victorian Census or LFS primary figures;
- the unverified status of all third-party estimates.

### Supporting and alternative analysis

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strongest evidence supporting conclusion | SYSTEM_DERIVED Victorian carpenter estimate (~44,700) is consistent with a large workforce. No primary Victorian renovation-specific cohort count is identifiable from public sources. |
| Strongest alternative explanation | A renovation-focused cohort may be identifiable from ABS Census occupation × industry cross-tabulation or from NCVER qualification data, but this has not been confirmed in this review. The classification may understate the available evidence if Census cross-tabulation supports a defensible residential or renovation workforce estimate. |
| Largest data gap — A (total workforce precision) | Absence of a directly published Victorian ANZSCO 3312 count (LFS or Census). This gap affects precision of Population A only. Closing it would not identify the renovation or advanced-capability cohort. |
| Largest data gap — B (cohort identification — the more important policy gap) | No public dataset identifies renovation-focused carpenters (Population C) or advanced-capability practitioners (Population H) as distinct, measurable sub-populations. A directly published Victorian carpenter count does not resolve this gap. DB-L Carpentry population data does not resolve this gap. Both gaps remain open and require different evidence to address. |
| What evidence would change conclusion | A primary-source Victorian renovation carpenter count (from ABS Census cross-tabulation or authoritative government data) would allow reclassification to C or D. DB-L Carpentry population data would allow a more specific assessment of Population D. |
