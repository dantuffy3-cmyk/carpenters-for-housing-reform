## Purpose

This review tests whether the capabilities that constitute the hypothesised Advanced Residential Carpentry (ARC) residual function can be assessed **reliably, consistently and fairly**.

The residual function currently holds the status: **PARTIAL_RESIDUAL_FUNCTION**

It is centred on:

- structured diagnostic judgement;
- uncertainty recognition;
- boundary recognition;
- deciding when work should stop or be withheld;
- referral and escalation decisions;
- sequencing and scope decisions under uncertain existing-building conditions.

This review determines whether capabilities of this type can be objectively assessed in a repeatable way.

**This review is not advocacy for ARC.**  
**This review is not advocacy for RRC.**  
**This review does not constitute proof that a new regulatory assessment is required.**

## PART 1 — Defining Assessability

Assessability is not a single property. The following dimensions are assessed separately.

| Dimension | Definition |
|---|---|
| A. DESCRIBABLE | The capability can be articulated in precise terms |
| B. OBSERVABLE | Practitioner behaviour or reasoning can be observed during assessment |
| C. SCORABLE | Performance can be classified using specified criteria |
| D. REPEATABLE | The same assessor reaches a similar outcome on re-assessment |
| E. INTER-RATER_RELIABLE | Different assessors reach sufficiently similar outcomes independently |
| F. VALID | The assessment actually measures the intended capability rather than a proxy |
| G. FAIR | Candidates are judged consistently and without irrelevant bias |
| H. CONSEQUENTIAL | Serious boundary failure has the ability to affect outcome decisively |

These dimensions are not equivalent. A capability may be describable but not reliably scorable. A method may produce consistent scores without measuring the intended capability.

## PART 4 — Judgement Assessment

### Can professional judgement under uncertainty be assessed reliably?

#### What authoritative evidence establishes

**ASQA and AQF:** ASQA's published assessment guidance and the AQF Second Edition recognise that competency-based assessment must evaluate the application of skills and knowledge in varied conditions, including problem-solving and decision-making in context. Assessment of reasoning and decision-making is a recognised expectation in higher AQF levels (AQF Levels 4–6 involve application of knowledge and skills in a range of contexts with some complexity). This establishes that judgement-type assessment is **DESCRIBABLE** and that criteria-based scoring is expected at these AQF levels.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 1 — PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED

**Limitation:** ASQA guidance establishes that judgement should be assessed but does not provide construction-specific evidence of achieved inter-rater reliability.

**Medical OSCE analogy:** The Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) system, used in Australian and international medical registration, assesses clinical judgement, problem-solving and patient management through standardised scenarios. Published assessment literature documents that well-calibrated OSCE systems can achieve acceptable inter-rater agreement when detailed marking rubrics and examiner training are applied. However, medical OSCE operates with extensive examiner preparation, moderated bench-marking, and large validity study programmes that are not replicated in any publicly identified construction-trade assessment.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE  
**Transferability:** Partial. The analogy supports feasibility of judgement assessment under controlled conditions but does not establish construction-trade reliability.

**Aviation and engineering analogy:** Aviation initial and recurrent assessment includes scenario-based evaluations of decision-making and risk management (e.g., multi-crew pilot licence assessment, Civil Aviation Safety Authority competency standards). Engineering Australia competency assessment for chartered status assesses professional judgement. These systems use structured criteria and assessor training.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE  
**Transferability:** Partial. Different risk domains and regulatory contexts. Supports plausibility not validation.

#### Critical distinction

A candidate producing a plausible oral or written response does not in itself establish that the assessment method can distinguish:

- COMPETENT  
- BORDERLINE  
- UNSAFE / CRITICAL_FAILURE

This distinction requires validated scoring criteria, trained assessors, and moderation. No published construction-trade evidence of validated scoring criteria at this distinction level was identified.

**Evidence gap:** DIRECT_CONSTRUCTION_EVIDENCE for reliable judgement-assessment scoring is an EVIDENCE_GAP.

## PART 5 — Boundary Recognition Assessment

### Can boundary recognition be objectively assessed through scenarios?

#### Scope of boundary recognition

Boundary recognition assessment in the ARC context includes recognising when:

- engineering referral is required;
- builder responsibility is triggered;
- building surveyor or permit input is required;
- licensed-trade input is required;
- work should stop;
- evidence is insufficient to continue;
- referral is required.

A sound assessment must distinguish between:

| Response | Description |
|---|---|
| appropriate continuation | Evidence supports continued work without referral |
| appropriate controlled continuation | Work can continue with identified safeguards |
| investigate | Further information required before a sound decision can be made |
| refer | Responsibility should be transferred to a more appropriate practitioner |
| escalate | Urgency or risk requires immediate action |
| stop | Work must not continue under current conditions |

The correct answer is **not** always "refer." Over-referral represents a separate but equally important assessability risk.

#### What authoritative evidence establishes

**training.gov.au and CPC30220:** The CPC Training Package includes units with stop-work and safety obligations. These are assessed as part of Certificate III and IV programmes. However, the public unit and assessment requirements documentation at training.gov.au does not identify specific rubrics, calibration protocols or reliability evidence for boundary-recognition scoring. The DB-L Carpentry licence holder assessment is classified:

**DB-L finding (preserved):** D — PUBLIC_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT_TO_DETERMINE_ASSESSMENT_DEPTH / 75%

No change is made to this finding.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 1 — EVIDENCE_GAP for boundary-specific scoring rubrics and calibration.

**Surveying and engineering analogies:** Professional competency assessment for surveyors and engineers in Australia includes boundary-of-competence assessment, particularly for chartered or registered status. The Engineers Australia Competency Standard (2019) includes a dimension of "professional and ethical responsibilities" that encompasses recognising the limits of competence. This establishes that boundary recognition is a recognised assessable dimension in analogous professional systems.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE  
**Transferability:** Partial. Demonstrates plausibility of boundary-recognition assessment in professional contexts. Does not establish that trade-level renovation boundary recognition has been assessed reliably.

**New Zealand Building Practitioners (NZBP):** The Licensed Building Practitioners (LBP) system in New Zealand is a relevant comparator. LBP licensing involves written assessment of building-code knowledge and practitioner obligations. Scope-of-licence boundaries and referral obligations form part of the LBP competency framework. However, no published independent inter-rater reliability study of LBP boundary-recognition assessment was identified.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE  
**Limitation:** New Zealand context; public assessment detail limited; no published inter-rater reliability evidence identified.

## PART 6 — Critical Failure Logic

### Do high-stakes competency systems use mandatory-fail criteria?

#### Authoritative evidence

**Medical and clinical assessment:** The use of critical fail (mandatory fail) criteria in medical OSCE and clinical competency assessment is well-documented in the published medical education literature. Systems that include patient-safety critical-fail criteria prevent a serious boundary failure from being averaged away by otherwise strong performance. This is a standard design feature in safety-critical clinical assessment.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE

**Aviation:** Aviation competency standards (e.g., CASA competency-based assessments) include mandatory fail criteria for safety-critical actions or omissions. This is consistent with critical-fail logic.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE

**VET and AQF:** Competency-based assessment under the AQF does not use traditional averaging. Assessment is competent or not yet competent. Units designated as mandatory or safety-critical may require demonstration. This is structurally analogous to critical-fail logic, though not identical to a scored mandatory-fail threshold.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 1 — PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED

**Construction-specific validation:** No published evidence of a validated critical-fail scoring protocol in a construction or trade-level competency assessment in Australia was identified.

**Evidence classification:** EVIDENCE_GAP — DIRECT_CONSTRUCTION_EVIDENCE

#### Project ARC critical-fail logic

The existing ARC assessment framework includes critical-fail design features. This is treated as **PROJECT_HYPOTHESIS** only.

The existence of critical-fail design features in the project framework does not constitute validation that those features are reliable, calibrated or defensible in a construction-trade context.

## PART 7 — Assessor Calibration

### What evidence exists for assessor calibration reducing variability?

#### Authoritative evidence

**ASQA guidance:** ASQA's published guidance on assessment validation (consistent with the Standards for Registered Training Organisations 2015 and successor frameworks) requires RTOs to validate assessment tools and judgements. Validation includes review by panels including industry representatives and other assessors. Moderation of assessor decisions is a compliance requirement. This establishes that assessor calibration and moderation are **DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED** as required practice in Australian VET.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 1 — DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED

**Limitation:** ASQA compliance requirements establish that calibration and moderation must occur. They do not independently quantify the reliability improvement achieved in any specific assessment context, including renovation carpentry.

**Medical examiner calibration:** Published medical education literature documents that examiner calibration, benchmark responses, anchor examples and structured rubrics reduce inter-rater variance in OSCE and other scenario-based assessments. Effects are documented but context-specific.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE

**CITB (UK Construction Industry Training Board):** CITB administers trade competency assessment including health, safety and environmental (HS&E) testing. Assessor accreditation and quality assurance procedures are published. These include standardisation exercises. However, no published independent inter-rater reliability study specific to CITB assessor calibration for judgement-based assessment was identified.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE  
**Limitation:** UK context; limited to published public material; judgement-assessment specificity not confirmed.

#### Summary

| Feature | Classification |
|---|---|
| Assessor calibration as required practice | DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED (ASQA / AQF) |
| Calibration reduces inter-rater variance | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED (analogous professional evidence) |
| Calibration eliminates subjectivity | NOT_ESTABLISHED — no evidence reviewed supports elimination of subjectivity |
| Calibration quantified for construction-trade judgement | EVIDENCE_GAP |

## PART 8 — Inter-Rater Reliability

### Can scenario-based judgement assessment achieve acceptable inter-rater reliability?

This is the highest-value evidence gap identified and is addressed directly.

#### What authoritative evidence establishes

**General assessment theory (TIER 2/3):** Psychometric and assessment-design literature documents that inter-rater reliability in judgement-based assessment is sensitive to:

- rubric clarity and granularity;
- assessor training and calibration;
- scenario design (closed versus open scenarios);
- the nature and complexity of the capability being assessed.

These are well-established findings in VET and professional competency assessment literature. They do not establish achieved reliability for any specific construction-trade context.

**AQF-level reliability expectations:** The AQF does not prescribe quantitative inter-rater reliability thresholds. ASQA requires validation and moderation but does not publish achieved kappa or ICC statistics for specific trade assessments.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 1 — EVIDENCE_GAP for published construction-trade reliability statistics

**Medical and clinical assessment:** Medical OSCE reliability studies (widely published) document that inter-rater reliability at acceptable levels is achievable with well-designed rubrics, trained examiners and adequate pilot investment. These studies inform understanding of what calibration investment is required.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 2 — ANALOGOUS_PROFESSIONAL_EVIDENCE  
**Transferability:** Partial. Medical OSCE operates under conditions of substantially greater resource investment and standardisation than any currently described carpentry assessment. Analogy supports plausibility not equivalence.

#### Construction-specific reliability evidence

No published evidence of validated inter-rater reliability data for scenario-based or oral judgement assessment in Australian construction trades was identified.

This is recorded as: **NOT_IDENTIFIED_IN_REVIEW**

This does not mean such evidence does not exist elsewhere. It means it was not identified in the sources reviewed.

#### Null finding

Inter-rater reliability for the specific capability combination under review — diagnostic judgement and boundary recognition in residential renovation — cannot be confirmed from currently identified sources.

**Finding:** INTER_RATER_RELIABILITY — NOT_ESTABLISHED (construction-specific)

This finding does not preclude development of a controlled pilot. See Part 15.

## PART 9 — Validity

### Do proposed assessment methods actually measure the intended capability?

The central validity risk for ARC assessability is that scenario-based or oral assessment measures:

- verbal communication ability;
- test-taking familiarity;
- general literacy;
- builder-management knowledge;
- theoretical engineering recall;

rather than:

- trade-level diagnostic judgement under realistic uncertainty;
- boundary recognition under actual existing-building conditions;
- capacity to make a safe stop/refer/continue decision in real time.

#### Validity considerations by method

| Method | Validity consideration | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Written response | May test literacy and recall rather than real-time diagnostic judgement | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED at best |
| Oral/structured assessment | May measure verbal confidence and communication style. High assessor variability risk. | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED — fairness risk significant |
| Portfolio | Can support validity if cases demonstrate real diagnostic decisions and outcomes, not just descriptions | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED |
| Scenario-based assessment | Validity depends on scenario realism, authenticity of presented uncertainty, and rubric alignment with the specific capability | PLAUSIBLE |
| Observed workplace assessment | Highest construct validity for real conditions. Standardisation and safety constraints are significant. | PLAUSIBLE |

#### Construct validity evidence

No published validity study of a construction-trade diagnostic-judgement assessment was identified. The claim that scenario-based methods have adequate construct validity for trade-level boundary recognition is:

**PLAUSIBLE — NOT_ESTABLISHED**

## PART 10 — Fairness and Accessibility

### Does judgement-based assessment risk disadvantaging candidates for non-capability reasons?

#### Identified fairness risks

| Risk | Description | Mitigation evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Literacy | Written assessments may disadvantage candidates with lower literacy unrelated to renovation capability | Adjustments required under AQF/ASQA guidelines. Evidence of effectiveness in trade contexts: PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED |
| English proficiency | Oral assessments may disadvantage NESB candidates unrelated to judgement capability | Interpreter provisions; rubric design. Construction workforce NESB proportion: significant. |
| Communication style | Oral assessments may reward articulate candidates rather than sound decision-makers | Rubric design mitigates; assessor calibration required; residual risk acknowledged |
| Assessor bias | Assessor demographic assumptions may influence scoring of tradesperson responses | Calibration, diversity, moderation reduce risk; not eliminable |
| Neurodiversity | Standard written/oral formats may disadvantage neurodiverse candidates | Reasonable adjustment policies apply under relevant legislation |
| Regional access | In-person assessment may not be accessible to regional and rural practitioners | Online and recorded alternatives available with design investment |
| Digital access | Online or digital assessment requires device and connectivity access | Regional and digital equity remains a live concern in Australian VET |
| Assessment anxiety | Unfamiliar formal testing may affect performance independently of capability | Recognised in VET literature; mitigated by clear information and practice |

**Critical differentiation:**

Communication of reasoning ≠ verbal polish.

An assessment should be capable of crediting a candidate who clearly articulates a sound diagnostic process using plain language, without requiring formal expression or theoretical vocabulary.

Rubric design must explicitly distinguish between the quality of the decision and the quality of the presentation.

#### ASQA fairness requirements

ASQA guidelines require RTOs to ensure access and equity in assessment, including reasonable adjustment. This is a regulatory requirement, not an optional design feature.

**Evidence classification:** TIER 1 — DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED as a regulatory requirement

#### Conclusion

Fairness risks are real and identifiable. They do not make assessment impossible, but they require deliberate design, rubric clarity, assessor training, and access provisions. They represent a non-trivial design burden that should inform any pilot design.

**Finding:** FAIRNESS_RISKS_IDENTIFIED — MITIGABLE_WITH_DESIGN_INVESTMENT

## PART 15 — Pilot Validation Metrics

A controlled pilot would need to collect the following to begin validating assessability. This section identifies candidate metrics only. It does not prescribe sample size, scenario count, assessor count or psychometric thresholds, as no such prescriptions are independently justified by the evidence reviewed.

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Assessor agreement rate | Proportion of cases where two or more assessors reach the same overall outcome |
| Score variance | Distribution of scores across candidates and across assessors for the same candidate |
| Inter-rater reliability statistic | Quantitative measure of agreement above chance (appropriate statistic to be determined by assessment design) |
| Critical-fail agreement rate | Agreement rate specifically on cases where one or more assessors apply critical-fail criteria |
| Second-review reversal rate | Proportion of second-review decisions that reverse first-assessor outcome |
| Rubric interpretation consistency | Whether assessors interpret rubric criteria consistently across cases |
| Candidate comprehension | Whether candidates understand the assessment instructions and task without technical communication assistance |
| False-positive risk | Likelihood that unsafe boundary decisions pass assessment |
| False-negative risk | Likelihood that sound boundary decisions fail assessment |
| Fairness indicators | Score distribution across demographic groups (language, region, age, background) |
| Assessor drift | Change in assessor scoring patterns across a session or over time |
| Scenario sampling stability | Whether assessed capability varies significantly across scenario choices |
| Validity against independent expert judgement | Degree to which assessment outcomes correlate with judgements of independent subject-matter experts |
| Moderation frequency | Proportion of assessments referred for moderation review |
| Assessor confidence recording | Assessors self-record confidence level in each decision |
| Evidence sufficiency | Whether evidence presented in scenario is sufficient to support sound decisions |

**Note:** These metrics are identified as candidate validation measures. Appropriate minimum thresholds for each metric depend on the specific assessment purpose and should be determined empirically during pilot design, not adopted from generic psychometric conventions.

## PART 17 — Falsification Attempts

Each claim is tested against the identified evidence.

**1. Diagnostic judgement can be reliably assessed.**

Testing: Analogous professional systems (medical, aviation, engineering) demonstrate that diagnostic judgement assessment is feasible under controlled conditions with calibrated assessors. No construction-trade-specific reliability evidence was identified. The claim that diagnostic judgement can be reliably assessed in a construction-trade renovation context is **PLAUSIBLE — NOT_ESTABLISHED**. The claim that it cannot be assessed is equally not established. The falsification attempt is **INCONCLUSIVE** — neither confirmed nor refuted by available evidence.

**2. Boundary recognition can be reliably assessed.**

Testing: Boundary recognition is a recognised assessable dimension in professional competency frameworks (Engineers Australia, LBP NZ). Reliable assessment under construction-trade scenario conditions is plausible but has no identified validation evidence. **PLAUSIBLE — NOT_ESTABLISHED**. Falsification **INCONCLUSIVE**.

**3. Scenario assessment is valid.**

Testing: Scenario-based assessment is widely used and accepted as valid in professional and VET contexts. Validity in the specific ARC context depends on scenario design, realism and rubric alignment. The general method is not invalidated by available evidence, but **construct validity for this specific application is NOT_ESTABLISHED**. Falsification **INCONCLUSIVE**.

**4. Assessor calibration can control subjectivity.**

Testing: Evidence supports that assessor calibration and moderation reduce variability. No evidence supports that calibration eliminates subjectivity. The claim that calibration can control but not eliminate subjectivity is **SUPPORTED (analogous)**. The stronger claim that calibration is sufficient for reliable assessment without further validation is **NOT_ESTABLISHED**. The claim that subjectivity cannot be adequately controlled is equally **NOT_ESTABLISHED**. Falsification **PARTIALLY_INCONCLUSIVE** — calibration is supported as a necessary but not proven sufficient condition.

**5. Critical-fail logic is defensible.**

Testing: Critical-fail logic is used in safety-critical professional assessment (medical, aviation) and is structurally analogous to the AQF competent/NYC binary. No construction-trade validation of critical-fail thresholds was identified. Critical-fail logic is **PLAUSIBLE — NOT_VALIDATED** in this specific context. Falsification **INCONCLUSIVE**.

**6. Candidate reasoning can be assessed fairly.**

Testing: Fairness risks are real and identifiable (literacy, language, communication style, assessor bias). Mitigating design features are available and required under ASQA guidelines. The claim that reasoning can be assessed fairly is **PLAUSIBLE with deliberate design investment**. The claim that it cannot be assessed fairly is **NOT_ESTABLISHED** given available mitigation options. Falsification **INCONCLUSIVE — conditional**.

**7. Pilot evidence could validate reliability.**

Testing: Pilot programmes with appropriate design and sample size can generate inter-rater reliability data and begin the validation process. This is standard methodology in professional assessment development. The claim is **SUPPORTED** as a general proposition. Whether a specific pilot will achieve adequate reliability depends on execution, scenario design and assessor calibration. Falsification **NOT_SUCCESSFUL — claim is plausible and supported by general evidence**.

**8. ARC assessment architecture is capable of becoming reliable.**

Testing: The ARC framework contains design features consistent with recognised good practice. Whether those features will achieve adequate reliability in practice is **NOT_ESTABLISHED**. No design feature identified in the ARC framework is inherently contrary to good practice. Falsification **INCONCLUSIVE** — the architecture is not demonstrated to be incapable of reliability, nor is it validated.

## PART 18 — Final Classification

Having reviewed available evidence independently and without importing the third-party classification, this review determines:

### Classification

**B — ASSESSMENT_METHODS_ARE_PLAUSIBLE_BUT_RELIABILITY_IS_UNPROVEN**

### Reasoning

The residual function capabilities — diagnostic judgement, uncertainty recognition, boundary recognition, stop/refer/withhold decisions and sequencing under uncertainty — are each describable, and there is plausible support from analogous professional assessment systems for the proposition that they can be observed and scored. Assessment design features consistent with good practice exist within the project hypothesis (ARC framework).

However:

- No direct construction-trade-specific inter-rater reliability evidence was identified.
- No validated rubric, calibration protocol or scoring system for these capabilities in a construction-trade context was identified.
- Validity of scenario-based methods for the specific capability combination has not been independently established.
- Fairness risks are real and require deliberate design investment.
- Assessor calibration reduces but does not eliminate subjectivity, and its effect in this specific context is unquantified.

A legitimate intermediate finding is:

> Existing evidence supports development of a controlled assessability hypothesis for testing, but does not support a claim of validated reliability.

Classification D (ASSESSABILITY_IS_MATERIALLY_SUPPORTED_BUT_PILOT_VALIDATION_IS_REQUIRED) was considered. However, the absence of direct construction evidence and the significance of the reliability, validity and fairness gaps means the more conservative classification B is the appropriate governed finding. The step from plausibility to material support requires more than analogous professional evidence alone.

### Confidence

**ASSESSABILITY_CONFIDENCE:** **55%**

This reflects that the evidence reviewed provides meaningful analogous support but no direct construction-trade validation. The classification B is held with moderate confidence. A well-designed pilot could materially move this finding.

### Note on third-party comparison

The third party proposed C at 68%. This governed review independently reaches B at 55%. The difference reflects this review's more conservative treatment of analogous evidence, explicit non-inheritance of unverified thresholds, and the absence of direct construction-trade reliability evidence.
