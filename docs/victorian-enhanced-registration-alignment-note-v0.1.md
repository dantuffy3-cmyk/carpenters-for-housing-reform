# Victorian Enhanced Registration Alignment Note v0.1

**Status:** CURRENT EVIDENCE ALIGNMENT NOTE  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Policy status:** Initiative position. Not government policy.

---

## Purpose

This note maps the initiative’s current questions and pilot architecture against principles identified in the Victorian Government Building Statement reform direction.

Source basis is used as policy context only. This note does not claim that government supports this initiative, RRC, an advanced carpenter registration class, or any specific implementation vehicle.

---

| Victorian reform principle | Initiative response | Current evidence status | What remains unresolved | Prohibited conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expanded participant registration | Tests whether advanced residential renovation carpentry is a sufficiently distinct practitioner function to assess. | Proposed / unvalidated | Whether government considers the function sufficiently distinct for intervention. | Government has decided to expand registration to include this function. |
| Clear authorised scopes | Uses bounded scope and exclusion concepts, with mandatory escalation triggers and role boundaries. | Proposed / partially specified | Whether scope can be defined and enforced without duplication or confusion. | Government has accepted these scope settings. |
| Objective competence benchmarks | Uses seven capability domains as observable benchmarks for advanced renovation capability. | Proposed / unvalidated | Whether benchmark definitions are sufficiently objective in live use. | Government has accepted these benchmarks. |
| Practical experience requirements | Treats experience as eligibility evidence, not automatic competence conferral. | Proposed / unvalidated | What threshold and verification standard are proportionate. | Experience duration alone has been accepted as proof of competence. |
| Assessment for registration | Uses assessment framework and scenario-based methods to test reliability and transparency. | Proposed / unvalidated | Whether assessment can produce reliable, repeatable outcomes. | Government has accepted the assessment model for registration. |
| Consumer protection | Uses consumer-role disclosure, comprehension testing and evidence-record architecture. | Proposed / unvalidated | Whether these controls materially improve consumer outcomes in practice. | Pilot design has already proven consumer-harm reduction. |
| Practitioner skill and integrity | Uses independent assessment, calibration controls and governance checks on evidence quality. | Proposed / unvalidated | Whether assessor consistency and integrity controls are sufficient. | Government has accepted that practitioner integrity settings are satisfied. |
| Transparent and adaptive system | Uses staged stop/pause/redesign controls and preserves no-reform as a valid outcome. | Proposed / unvalidated | Whether adaptive controls are workable and proportionate in practice. | Government has committed to implementation or to a new class. |

---

## Plumbing precedent control

Where plumbing classes are referenced in policy discussion, they are used only as policy-design precedent showing governments may revisit practitioner classifications when capability and risk differ.

They are not used to imply that:

- plumbing legislation applies to carpentry;
- carpentry should copy plumbing;
- plumbing classes legally justify RRC; or
- government intends equivalent carpentry classes.
