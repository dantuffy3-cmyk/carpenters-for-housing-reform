# Practitioner Function Decision Hierarchy v0.1

**Status:** WORKING DRAFT — governance refinement  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Policy status:** Initiative position. Not government policy.

---

## Purpose

This document sets out the canonical decision hierarchy for government-facing evaluation of advanced residential renovation carpentry.

The hierarchy preserves capability-first governance:

**Practitioner function first → capability evidence → regulatory vehicle.**

Core government question:

> Does advanced residential renovation carpentry constitute a sufficiently distinct practitioner function that it warrants defined competence assessment, role boundaries and potentially formal recognition within Victoria’s enhanced practitioner registration framework?

This is a question for evaluation, not a conclusion.

For the integrated pilot operating sequence that applies this hierarchy across governance, calibration, assessment, evidence and public-interest review, see `docs/advanced-carpentry-pilot-master-operating-model-v0.1.md`.

---

## 1) Public-Interest Problem

Some residential renovation work can involve:

- concealed conditions;
- incomplete evidence;
- sequencing changes;
- scope expansion;
- temporary-support questions;
- structural-boundary recognition;
- coordination across practitioners;
- escalation to builder, engineer, surveyor or licensed trade; and
- consumer misunderstanding of responsibility.

This document does not claim that all renovations contain these risks.

## 2) Practitioner-Function Question

Does managing this category of work safely and professionally require a distinct advanced residential carpentry capability?

## 3) Capability-Definition Question

Can the function be described through observable capability domains?

The current domains are retained without change:

1. Technical Capability
2. Diagnostic Capability
3. Structural Understanding
4. Planning Capability
5. Coordination Capability
6. Professional Judgement
7. Boundary Recognition

## 4) Assessment Question

Can the capability be assessed reliably and transparently?

Reference: `docs/advanced-residential-carpentry-assessment-framework-v0.1.md`.

## 5) Calibration Question

Can different assessors apply the framework with sufficient consistency?

Reference: `docs/advanced-carpentry-assessor-calibration-pack-v0.1.md`.

## 6) Public-Interest / Regulatory Question

If capability is real and assessable, would formal recognition materially improve:

- consumer role clarity;
- accountability;
- professional boundary recognition;
- evidence quality;
- regulatory transparency; and
- practitioner capability assurance?

This question is evidence-dependent. A positive answer is not assumed.

## 7) Statutory Vehicle Question

Only after the preceding questions are satisfied should government compare vehicles, including:

- distinct RRC class/category;
- DB-L Carpentry reform, endorsement or assessment stream;
- advanced credential without additional statutory authority;
- another proportionate government-selected mechanism; and
- **no formal recognition / status quo**.

No-formal-recognition remains a legitimate evidence-based outcome.

---

## Public-Interest Test Questions (Post-Validity)

After capability validity is established, government may test:

- consumer benefit;
- role clarity;
- defect and non-compliance risk;
- regulatory burden;
- market complexity;
- insurance implications;
- enforcement practicality;
- interaction with builders;
- interaction with engineers, surveyors and licensed trades;
- workforce effects; and
- unintended consequences.

These are policy-evaluation questions and are not answered here.

---

## Governance Controls

- Government reform context is not government endorsement.
- Pilot success does not automatically justify registration.
- Recognition does not create automatic contracting authority.
- Existing builder, engineer, surveyor and licensed-trade responsibilities remain intact.
