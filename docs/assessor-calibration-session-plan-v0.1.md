---

## Important

This is a calibration session plan, not a training course design.

The purpose of this session is to test whether assessors can apply the Advanced Residential Carpentry assessment framework consistently. It is not designed to teach assessors how to score — it is designed to reveal where they score differently and why.

Assessors who require familiarisation training with the framework before calibration should undertake that preparation separately and in advance of this session. Preparation requirements are set out in Section 4 of the Calibration Pack (`docs/advanced-carpentry-assessor-calibration-pack-v0.1.md`).

This session does not confer assessor accreditation. Completion of this session is a governance requirement before live pilot assessment, not a qualification.

## Stage 1 — Orientation (approximately 30 minutes)

**Purpose:** Confirm shared understanding of the framework, boundaries and calibration principles before any benchmark exercises begin.

**Content:**

1.1 — Framework boundaries
- What the assessment framework does and does not do
- Statutory scope: assessment determines capability evidence only; it does not confer authority, registration or permit rights
- The seven capability domains and their relationship to each other
- The role of Professional Judgement as the integrating domain

1.2 — Capability vs authority
- Demonstrating structural understanding does not confer engineering authority
- Demonstrating renovation capability does not extend builder registration
- Boundary recognition is assessed as a substantive capability domain, not a formality

1.3 — Evidence governance
- What counts as evidence in the assessment
- Missing evidence: must not be filled by assessor assumption
- Uncertainty: must not be scored as competence

1.4 — Critical-fail rules
- The seven critical-fail rules (Assessment Framework v0.1 Section 6.4)
- What triggers a critical fail; what does not
- The second-assessor review requirement for critical-fail findings

1.5 — Calibration principles
- Assessors confirm they have read the twelve calibration principles in Section 5 of the Calibration Pack
- Assessors confirm they will score independently before discussion
- Assessors confirm that rationale is mandatory

**Facilitated discussion questions:**
- Is there any calibration principle that is unclear or requires clarification before we begin?
- Are there any preparation questions arising from the framework or scenario bank review?

**Output:** Calibration chair confirms all assessors are ready to proceed. Any unresolved preparation gaps are noted.

## Stage 2 — Independent Benchmark 1 (approximately 30 minutes)

**Benchmark profile:** Profile A (Advanced Response)
**Scenario:** Calibration Scenario A (Scenario Bank — Scenario 2: Subfloor Access)

**Assessor task:**
- Read the benchmark response independently
- Complete a score sheet independently
- Do not discuss results with other assessors

**Facilitation instruction:**
Score sheets must be completed and collected by the calibration chair before any discussion begins. Do not allow assessors to consult each other during scoring.

---

## Stage 3 — Compare and Discuss — Benchmark 1 (approximately 30 minutes)

**Purpose:** Compare assessor scores for Profile A. Identify agreement and disagreement. Review rationale.

**Process:**

3.1 — Calibration chair presents the aggregate score comparison (without identifying individual assessors if possible).
3.2 — Assessors identify domains where scores differ.
3.3 — Each assessor explains the evidence basis for their score on any domain where disagreement exists.
3.4 — The shared rubric is applied to resolve disagreement.
3.5 — Material disagreements are recorded in accordance with Section 11 of the Calibration Pack.
3.6 — Non-material disagreements are noted.
3.7 — Calibration chair records whether rubric or scenario ambiguity contributed to disagreement.

**Expected output:** Agreement on the domains and reasoning for Profile A. Any unresolved disagreements recorded for rubric review.

---

## Stage 4 — Independent Benchmark 2 (approximately 30 minutes)

**Benchmark profile:** Profile B (Competent but Not Advanced) or Profile C (Incomplete/Evidence-Poor) — calibration chair selects based on calibration objectives.
**Scenario:** Calibration Scenario A or B as determined by calibration chair.

**Assessor task:**
- Read the benchmark response independently
- Complete a score sheet independently
- Do not discuss results

---

## Stage 5 — Compare and Discuss — Benchmark 2 (approximately 30 minutes)

**Purpose:** Compare assessor scores for the selected profile. Key calibration question: do assessors distinguish consistently between Profile A (Advanced) and Profile B (Competent) or Profile C (Incomplete)?

**Process:** As per Stage 3.

**Expected output:** Assessors confirm they can distinguish the profile levels from each other. Any criteria on which profiles A and B or A and C are not consistently distinguished are recorded as rubric ambiguity.

## Stage 6 — Independent Benchmark 3 (approximately 30 minutes)

**Benchmark profile:** Profile D (Unsafe / Boundary-Fail)
**Scenario:** Calibration Scenario B (Scenario Bank — Scenario 1: Bathroom Renovation)

**Assessor task:**
- Read the benchmark response independently
- Complete a score sheet independently
- Pay particular attention to the critical-fail assessment section of the score sheet

---

## Stage 7 — Critical-Fail Calibration Exercise (approximately 30 minutes)

**Purpose:** Test whether assessors consistently identify critical-fail triggers across the compact fragment set.

**Process:**

7.1 — Calibration chair distributes the six critical-fail fragments (Section 14 of the Calibration Pack).
7.2 — Assessors complete the critical-fail determination for each fragment independently.
7.3 — Results are compared.
7.4 — Assessors discuss the threshold fragment (CF-2: Internal wall removal, competent but uncertain reasoning) — this is the deliberately ambiguous case.
7.5 — Calibration chair records whether critical-fail agreement is achieved for all unambiguous fragments (CF-1, CF-3, CF-4, CF-5, CF-6).
7.6 — Any fragment on which critical-fail status is genuinely disputed by multiple assessors is recorded as a rubric ambiguity requiring review.

**Expected output:** Agreement on critical-fail status for all unambiguous fragments. Documented discussion of CF-2 threshold.

---

## Stage 8 — Consumer Communication Calibration (approximately 20 minutes)

**Purpose:** Test whether assessors can consistently apply the consumer communication scoring criteria.

**Process:**

8.1 — Assessors review the two consumer communication benchmark responses in Section 13 of the Calibration Pack independently.
8.2 — For each response, assessors complete the consumer communication section of the score sheet independently.
8.3 — Results are compared.
8.4 — Particular attention is given to the intentionally ambiguous response (Section 13.3): does the characterisation of engineering sign-off as "a formality" trigger a critical fail or a significant development item?
8.5 — Calibration chair records the assessor determination and rationale for the ambiguous response.

**Expected output:** Consistent scoring of the advanced consumer communication response. Documented assessor judgement on the ambiguous response, including whether critical-fail rule 6 is triggered.

---

## Stage 9 — Record Assessor Variance (approximately 20 minutes)

**Purpose:** Compile the calibration record before forming a readiness recommendation.

**Process:**

9.1 — Calibration chair compiles aggregate statistics from all score sheets:
- exact-score agreement rate by domain
- score-band agreement rate by domain
- outcome agreement rate
- critical-fail agreement rate
- escalation-state agreement rate
- domains or criteria with highest variance

9.2 — Calibration chair identifies:
- rubric ambiguities identified during the session
- scenario ambiguities identified during the session
- evidence-governance issues identified
- consumer communication disagreements unresolved

9.3 — All material disagreements are confirmed as documented.
9.4 — Assessors confirm that all score sheets have been signed and are complete.

---

## Stage 10 — Determine Whether Rubric Refinement Is Required (approximately 20 minutes)

**Purpose:** Form a collective view on whether the rubric and scenarios are sufficiently clear for live pilot delivery.

**Process:**

10.1 — Calibration chair presents the rubric and scenario ambiguities identified during the session.
10.2 — Assessors discuss whether each ambiguity is:
- minor and unlikely to affect live assessment outcomes;
- significant and requiring rubric or scenario revision before pilot delivery; or
- a systemic pattern suggesting a fundamental gap in the framework.

10.3 — Calibration chair records the consensus assessment for each identified ambiguity.
10.4 — Calibration chair forms a readiness recommendation, using the readiness categories in the Calibration Results Template (`docs/assessor-calibration-results-template-v0.1.md`):

- `READY_FOR_LIMITED_PILOT`
- `READY_WITH_CONDITIONS`
- `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`
- `FRAMEWORK_REVISION_REQUIRED`
- `UNABLE_TO_FORM_RELIABLE_POSITION`

10.5 — Calibration chair records the readiness recommendation with supporting reasoning.

**Principle:** Readiness is determined by calibration evidence, not by the desire to proceed. If the calibration evidence does not support pilot readiness, the correct action is to record that finding, address the identified ambiguities and recalibrate.

---

## Post-Session Requirements

After the session, the calibration chair must:

1. Complete the Calibration Results Template (`docs/assessor-calibration-results-template-v0.1.md`).
2. Ensure all score sheets are retained as calibration records.
3. Ensure all material disagreements are documented with resolution reasoning.
4. Submit identified rubric and scenario defects for governance review.
5. Report the readiness recommendation to the pilot administrator.
