# Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment Framework — Pilot Version 0.1

**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
**Status:** DRAFT — pilot assessment architecture for consultation and validation
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Important — What This Framework Is Not

This document is a draft assessment framework for consultation and validation purposes only.

This framework:

- **is not a licence**;
- **is not registration**;
- **is not builder authority**;
- **is not engineering authority**;
- **is not permit authority**;
- **does not extend a participant's lawful scope of work in any way**;
- **has not yet been independently validated**.

Participants assessed under any pilot using this framework remain within their existing lawful authority throughout. Assessment determines capability evidence only. It does not confer statutory recognition.

---

## Purpose

This framework defines how Advanced Residential Carpentry capability could be assessed consistently during a controlled pilot.

The central question the framework is designed to answer is:

> Can advanced residential renovation carpentry capability be assessed reliably, consistently and transparently enough to inform government consideration of future recognition?

This assessment question follows a prior practitioner-function question: whether advanced residential renovation carpentry is sufficiently distinct to warrant defined competence assessment at all.

The framework converts the capability-first policy architecture into a testable assessment model. It does not predetermine whether any formal recognition mechanism will follow.

---

## Core Architecture

The reform object is **Advanced Residential Carpentry capability**.

The current capability level is: **Advanced Residential Carpenter — independently assessed**.

Implementation vehicles remain open. Registered Residential Carpenter is one possible vehicle only. Other vehicles under consideration include:

- DB-L Carpentry reform, endorsement or assessment stream;
- advanced trade credential without additional statutory authority;
- another government-selected mechanism.

## Section 1 — Assessment Principles

The following principles govern all assessment design, delivery, scoring and review conducted under this framework. They are not administrative preferences. They are binding constraints on any pilot using this architecture.

1. **Capability must be demonstrated, not inferred from years of experience alone.**
   Duration of practice establishes eligibility and evidence of exposure. It does not automatically establish competence.

2. **Experience establishes eligibility and exposure, not automatic competence.**
   A candidate with many years of experience may still fail to demonstrate capability. A candidate with less experience may demonstrate stronger capability. Assessment must evaluate demonstrated performance.

3. **Assessment must examine decision-making under uncertainty.**
   Renovation work involves incomplete information. Assessment scenarios must reflect this. Certainty-seeking responses to inherently uncertain conditions must not score positively.

4. **Renovation capability must be tested in existing-building contexts.**
   Existing buildings create uncertainty through concealed conditions, previous alterations, deterioration, incomplete documentation and variable construction methods. Assessment must reflect this environment.

5. **Hidden-condition recognition must be assessed.**
   The ability to recognise, record, respond to and communicate the significance of concealed conditions is core to advanced renovation capability.

6. **Boundary recognition is part of competence.**
   Knowing where one's own authority and capability end is as important as knowing what one can do. Boundary recognition must be assessed as a substantive capability domain.

7. **Choosing to stop, refer or escalate can be evidence of advanced competence.**
   In appropriate circumstances, a decision to pause work, seek specialist input or refer to a builder, engineer, building surveyor or licensed trade is evidence of good professional judgement — not weakness or failure.

8. **Unsupported confidence must not score positively.**
   A candidate who expresses certainty without adequate evidence, or who proceeds without recognising known unknowns, must not receive a higher score than a candidate who recognises uncertainty and responds appropriately.

9. **Missing evidence must not be treated as evidence of competence.**
   Absence of documented risk does not mean no risk exists. Assessors must not infer capability from gaps in the candidate's record.

10. **Assessment must distinguish knowledge from demonstrated judgement.**
    A candidate may know what the correct response is in theory. Assessment must test whether they can apply that knowledge in context, explain their reasoning and recognise when their reasoning may be insufficient.

11. **Assessor decisions must be traceable.**
    Every scoring decision must be accompanied by written assessor rationale. Scores without rationale are invalid.

12. **Consumer communication must form part of professional capability.**
    Advanced renovation capability includes the ability to explain scope, uncertainty, escalation and boundaries clearly to a non-specialist consumer. This is not a soft skill add-on. It is a substantive capability requirement.

13. **Structural understanding must remain bounded from engineering design authority.**
    A candidate may demonstrate structural awareness and understanding of how loads are transferred, temporary support is managed and structural boundaries are recognised. This does not authorise engineering design. Assessment must reflect this boundary explicitly.

14. **Assessment outcomes must be reviewable and auditable.**
    Candidates must have access to a review and appeal pathway. Assessment records must be retained and available for audit.

## Section 2 — Eligibility Gate

Eligibility is a precondition for assessment. It is not a competence pass.

Completing the eligibility stage does not mean a candidate has demonstrated advanced capability. It means the candidate has demonstrated sufficient background to proceed to assessment.

### 2.1 Required Eligibility Evidence

Candidates must provide evidence of all of the following:

| Requirement | Evidence Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Recognised carpentry qualification | Certificate or equivalent | Must be a recognised Australian qualification or equivalent |
| Completed apprenticeship or equivalent pathway | Completion record or equivalent recognised pathway documentation | Where applicable to the candidate's entry pathway |
| Verified residential renovation experience | Portfolio summary, employer or client references, or equivalent | See note on experience thresholds below |
| Project portfolio | Documented project records across multiple existing-building contexts | See Task 11 — Candidate Evidence Requirements |
| References or equivalent verification | Written references or verifiable contact details for verification | Minimum number to be determined during pilot design |
| Identity and practitioner history | Identity document and practitioner registration or licensing records | Must be current and verifiable |
| Evidence of work across multiple existing-building contexts | Portfolio documentation | Must include evidence of renovation work, not only new construction |

### 2.2 Experience Thresholds

**Current proposal:** Where an existing policy document contains a proposed experience threshold, that threshold is identified as a current proposal, not an independently validated requirement.

Do not treat any duration of experience as automatically sufficient. Experience thresholds set eligibility gates. They do not certify competence.

The pilot design phase must determine what constitutes sufficient verified experience for eligibility, with reference to available evidence and stakeholder input.

### 2.3 Eligibility Outcome Categories

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ELIGIBLE_FOR_ASSESSMENT` | All eligibility requirements satisfied. Candidate may proceed to assessment. |
| `ELIGIBLE_WITH_EVIDENCE_GAPS` | Most requirements satisfied. Identified gaps must be resolved before proceeding. |
| `NOT_YET_ELIGIBLE` | Material eligibility requirements not yet satisfied. Candidate should address gaps and re-apply. |
| `UNABLE_TO_VERIFY` | Eligibility cannot be determined from submitted evidence. Further verified documentation required. |

### 2.4 Eligibility Principles

- `ELIGIBLE_FOR_ASSESSMENT` is not a competence finding.
- `ELIGIBLE_WITH_EVIDENCE_GAPS` does not permit the candidate to proceed to assessment with unresolved mandatory evidence gaps. The eligibility gate is not satisfied until the eligibility reviewer confirms in writing that all identified gaps have been resolved. This confirmation must be recorded. Gaps must not be deferred to the assessment stage.
- Eligibility review must be conducted by a person independent of the primary assessor.
- Eligibility decisions must be documented and retained.
- Candidates must be informed of their eligibility outcome and the reasons for any finding other than `ELIGIBLE_FOR_ASSESSMENT`.

## Section 3 — Seven Capability Domains

The following seven domains form the canonical assessment structure. They reflect the capability model set out in `docs/advanced-residential-carpentry-capability-model-v1.md`.

Professional Judgement is the integrating domain. It does not merely add an eighth category. It tests whether the candidate can combine the other six domains in context.

---

### Domain 1 — Technical Capability

**What is being assessed:**
The candidate's ability to apply carpentry knowledge, construction methods and material understanding accurately in existing-building renovation contexts.

**Why this domain matters in renovation work:**
New construction occurs in controlled conditions with known materials. Renovation occurs in variable conditions, with aged or unknown materials, and requires adaptation of technical knowledge to circumstances that were not anticipated at design stage.

**Acceptable evidence:**
- Portfolio documentation showing varied construction methods across multiple projects
- Photographs showing sequenced construction stages, material conditions and joinery
- Scope documents describing technical decisions and their basis
- Interview responses that correctly identify material properties, construction methods and relevant standards

**Weak evidence:**
- Generic statements of qualifications without project-specific content
- Photographs without context, annotation or candidate narrative
- Responses that recite standards without connecting them to specific conditions

**Red-flag responses:**
- Misidentifying structural members, load paths or support requirements
- Proposing construction methods inconsistent with existing conditions
- Inability to describe how they adapted technique to conditions discovered during a project

**Assessor prompts:**
- Tell me about a project where you encountered a condition that required you to change your technical approach.
- How did you determine what fixing method or structural connection was appropriate in this situation?
- What technical standards apply here, and how did you verify the existing work met those standards?

**Observable decision behaviours:**
- Candidate describes specific technical decisions with clear reasoning
- Candidate acknowledges limitations in their technical assessment
- Candidate distinguishes between conditions they could assess and those requiring specialist input

---

### Domain 2 — Diagnostic Capability

**What is being assessed:**
The candidate's ability to identify existing conditions, recognise indicators of deterioration, defect or risk, and form a reliable working assessment of what is present and what may be concealed.

**Why this domain matters in renovation work:**
Renovation work routinely reveals conditions not visible at project commencement. Diagnostic capability is what allows a practitioner to work safely and responsibly in this environment. Weak diagnostic capability creates risk for the consumer, the building and other parties.

**Acceptable evidence:**
- Portfolio documentation recording conditions identified before, during and after work
- Evidence of concealed-condition discovery, recording and response
- Interview responses demonstrating systematic diagnostic reasoning
- Scenario responses that correctly identify risk indicators and apply appropriate escalation

**Weak evidence:**
- Claims that conditions were "as expected" without examination evidence
- No record of pre-work condition assessment
- Responses that identify conditions only in retrospect, without a described diagnostic process

**Red-flag responses:**
- Failing to identify visible risk indicators in assessment scenarios
- Treating absence of visible defects as confirmation of structural soundness
- Describing diagnostic processes that would be unsafe or inadequate in practice

**Assessor prompts:**
- Walk me through how you assessed the condition of this area before starting work.
- What did you look for? What could you not see? What assumptions did you make?
- When you encountered this condition, what were your first steps?

**Observable decision behaviours:**
- Candidate describes a systematic examination process, not a casual visual check
- Candidate explicitly records what they could not determine
- Candidate explains how uncertainty in diagnosis affected their subsequent decisions

---

### Domain 3 — Structural Understanding

**What is being assessed:**
The candidate's understanding of how loads are transferred through existing residential structures, how renovation work may affect structural elements, and where structural assessment or engineering input is required.

**Important boundary:** Structural understanding is not structural design authority. Assessment must reflect this boundary explicitly. A candidate demonstrating structural understanding is not being assessed for engineering competence or authorised to perform engineering work.

**Why this domain matters in renovation work:**
Renovation work frequently involves elements that affect load transfer — wall removal, underpinning, alterations to framing, deck or subfloor work. A practitioner who does not understand structural principles may unknowingly create risk. A practitioner who understands structural principles but does not recognise the boundary of engineering authority creates a different but equally serious risk.

**Acceptable evidence:**
- Documentation showing correct identification of structural elements, load paths and temporary support
- Evidence of referrals to engineers where structural assessment was required
- Interview responses demonstrating structural awareness bounded by appropriate referral behaviour
- Scenario responses correctly identifying when engineering input is mandatory

**Weak evidence:**
- Generic descriptions of "structural work" without demonstration of underlying understanding
- Claims of structural capability without examples or reasoning
- References to engineer involvement without evidence of genuine integration or understanding

**Red-flag responses:**
- Describing structural decisions that should have involved engineering input without any referral
- Treating visual inspection as sufficient to determine structural adequacy in contexts requiring engineering assessment
- Claiming to have "designed" structural elements without the authority to do so

**Assessor prompts:**
- In this scenario, can you identify which elements are carrying load?
- At what point would you decide that engineering input is required?
- Walk me through how you manage temporary support during a structural alteration.

**Observable decision behaviours:**
- Candidate identifies structural elements accurately and articulates their load-carrying function
- Candidate explicitly identifies the point at which engineering authority is required
- Candidate describes temporary support measures with specific reference to the project conditions

---

### Domain 4 — Planning Capability

**What is being assessed:**
The candidate's ability to plan renovation work in existing-building contexts, accounting for sequencing, scope uncertainty, risk of concealed conditions, integration with other trades and contractual implications of scope change.

**Why this domain matters in renovation work:**
Renovation planning differs from new construction planning in that conditions cannot be fully specified in advance. Good planning in this context means anticipating uncertainty, building in decision points and managing the consumer relationship through scope variation.

**Acceptable evidence:**
- Portfolio documentation showing initial scopes, variations and final outcomes with explanation
- Evidence of pre-work planning, staged access and condition-contingent decision points
- Interview responses demonstrating sequencing logic and scope-change management
- Scenario responses that account for incomplete information and build in investigation before proceeding

**Weak evidence:**
- Scopes that treat existing conditions as fully known before commencement
- No documented variation process
- Descriptions of planning that do not account for concealed-condition risk

**Red-flag responses:**
- Presenting a fixed plan that does not account for the possibility of concealed conditions
- Describing scope changes without a consumer communication or contractual variation process
- Planning sequences that would expose concealed conditions without a prior decision framework

**Assessor prompts:**
- How do you plan a project when you cannot fully see what you are working with?
- Walk me through how you handled a situation where you discovered conditions that changed the scope.
- How do you communicate scope uncertainty to a consumer at the planning stage?

**Observable decision behaviours:**
- Candidate articulates decision points, not just task sequences
- Candidate describes how the plan accommodates unknown conditions
- Candidate connects planning to consumer communication and contractual documentation

---

### Domain 5 — Coordination Capability

**What is being assessed:**
The candidate's ability to coordinate with other trades, professionals and regulatory participants involved in residential renovation work, including builders, engineers, building surveyors and licensed trades.

**Why this domain matters in renovation work:**
Renovation work rarely involves only one practitioner. Effective coordination means knowing who needs to be involved, when they need to be involved, and what information they need. Poor coordination can result in unsafe work sequences, missed escalation requirements and regulatory non-compliance.

**Acceptable evidence:**
- Portfolio documentation showing engagement with other parties (references, communications, handoff records)
- Evidence of engagement with building surveyors, engineers or other professionals in projects involving permits or structural work
- Interview responses demonstrating an understanding of when and how to engage other parties
- Scenario responses that correctly identify which party should be involved at which stage

**Weak evidence:**
- References to coordination without specific evidence of what was coordinated and with whom
- No documentation of engagement with other professionals in projects where such engagement was required
- Descriptions of coordination limited to subcontractor management without reference to professional boundaries

**Red-flag responses:**
- Describing coordination in ways that omit parties who should have been involved
- Treating coordination as an administrative function only, without understanding its regulatory or professional significance
- Failing to identify when building surveyor or engineer involvement was required

**Assessor prompts:**
- Tell me about a project where you needed to coordinate with a building surveyor or engineer. What triggered the involvement? What was your role?
- In this scenario, who else would need to be involved before you could proceed?

**Observable decision behaviours:**
- Candidate names specific parties and describes specific coordination actions
- Candidate explains the reason for involving each party, not just the fact of involvement
- Candidate distinguishes between coordination for information exchange and coordination required by regulation or safety

---

### Domain 6 — Professional Judgement

**What is being assessed:**
Professional Judgement is the integrating domain. It tests whether the candidate can combine Technical Capability, Diagnostic Capability, Structural Understanding, Planning Capability, Coordination Capability and Boundary Recognition into a coherent, proportionate, well-reasoned response to a complex renovation situation.

Professional Judgement is assessed through the decision cycle: **observe → diagnose → predict → plan → execute → verify**.

Assessment must test all stages of this cycle, not only the execution stage.

**Why this domain matters in renovation work:**
Renovation work requires ongoing professional judgement throughout a project, not only at commencement. Conditions change. Concealed conditions emerge. Risk profiles shift. A practitioner who can only apply static technical knowledge, without the ability to adapt their judgement to evolving circumstances, is not demonstrating advanced renovation capability.

**Acceptable evidence:**
- Complex case documentation showing multiple decision points with recorded reasoning
- Interview responses that demonstrate integrated reasoning across multiple domains
- Scenario responses that adapt to changing information without losing coherence
- Evidence of escalation decisions that reflect genuine assessment, not reflexive risk-transfer

**Weak evidence:**
- Responses that are technically correct but fail to account for the specific context
- Decision sequences that are rigid and do not adapt to new information
- Escalation decisions that appear reflexive ("I always refer to an engineer") without evidence of actual assessment

**Red-flag responses:**
- Inconsistent reasoning across different parts of an assessment
- Inability to explain the basis for a decision when challenged
- Treating certainty as attainable in inherently uncertain conditions
- Judgement that systematically underweights consumer communication

**Assessor prompts:**
- In this scenario, what is your overall assessment of what is happening and what should happen next?
- If you discovered mid-project that conditions had changed significantly, walk me through how your thinking would evolve.
- What do you know? What are you uncertain about? What would improve your certainty?

**Observable decision behaviours:**
- Candidate explicitly states what is known and what is uncertain
- Candidate's response evolves when new information is provided
- Candidate's judgement is proportionate to the level of risk presented
- Candidate communicates their reasoning coherently without being prompted

---

### Domain 7 — Boundary Recognition

**What is being assessed:**
The candidate's ability to identify the limits of their own authority and capability, and to recognise the conditions requiring involvement of:

- a registered builder;
- a structural or civil engineer;
- a building surveyor (for permit or certification matters);
- a licensed trade (plumber, electrician, gas fitter or other);
- a specialist investigator (asbestos assessor, geotechnical specialist, or other);
- any other party whose involvement is legally or professionally required.

**Why this domain matters in renovation work:**
Existing-building renovation frequently produces conditions that cross multiple professional and regulatory boundaries. A practitioner who cannot recognise these boundaries creates consumer risk, regulatory exposure and potential harm. Boundary recognition is not merely knowing where one's authority ends. It is actively applying that knowledge in dynamic conditions.

**Acceptable evidence:**
- Documentation of escalation decisions with clear reasoning
- Evidence of referrals to other professionals in appropriate circumstances
- Interview responses correctly identifying mandatory involvement requirements
- Scenario responses that correctly apply escalation triggers without being prompted

**Weak evidence:**
- General statements about knowing when to refer without specific examples
- Evidence of referrals that appear reactive rather than structured
- Responses that apply escalation inconsistently across similar scenarios

**Red-flag responses:**
- Failing to recognise a clear engineering boundary
- Describing work that should have required builder involvement without referral
- Proceeding with work that required a building permit without identifying the permit requirement
- Treating a licensed-trade boundary as irrelevant or negotiable

**Assessor prompts:**
- At what point in this scenario would you stop and involve someone else? Who? Why?
- Can you identify any element of this project that sits outside your authority to carry out independently?
- What would you do if you discovered asbestos during this project?

**Observable decision behaviours:**
- Candidate correctly identifies regulatory and professional boundaries without being prompted
- Candidate can articulate the reason a boundary exists, not merely state that a referral is required
- Candidate demonstrates that escalation decisions are integrated into their project planning, not treated as exceptions
- Candidate's boundary recognition is consistent across scenario variations

## Section 4 — Assessment Methods

The assessment draws on six complementary evidence types. No single method is sufficient alone.

**Note:** Live structural work or unsafe practical exercises must not be used in pilot v0.1.

---

### Method A — Portfolio Evidence Review

The assessor reviews real prior projects submitted by the candidate.

Portfolio evidence must be reviewed for:

| Portfolio Element | Assessment Focus |
|---|---|
| Initial project scope | Completeness, realism, identification of known unknowns |
| Discovered conditions | Whether conditions were recognised, recorded and responded to |
| Photographs | Before, during and after evidence; condition documentation |
| Scope changes | Whether variations were documented and communicated |
| Work sequencing | Evidence of sequenced decision-making, not just task completion |
| Escalation decisions | Whether escalation was triggered appropriately and documented |
| Professional referrals | Evidence of engagement with builders, engineers, surveyors or trades |
| Completion evidence | Whether work was completed to documented specification |

---

### Method B — Structured Technical Interview

A structured interview testing the candidate's reasoning, diagnosis, decision sequence, boundary awareness and response to uncertainty.

Interview questions must:

- test reasoning, not recall;
- use open questioning before closed questioning;
- present realistic renovation conditions;
- include questions about uncertainty and limitation;
- include questions about boundary recognition across all relevant parties;
- be consistent across candidates;
- be documented and scored against the rubric.

---

### Method C — Scenario-Based Assessment

Candidates are presented with realistic renovation cases involving incomplete information.

Each scenario must:

- present a realistic existing-building condition;
- include information that is missing, ambiguous or potentially significant;
- require the candidate to form and explain an assessment;
- require the candidate to identify what would change their assessment;
- include at least one mandatory escalation trigger;
- not encode a single correct answer where professional judgement legitimately allows more than one safe response.

See `docs/assessment-scenario-bank-v0.1.md` for the governed scenario set.

---

### Method D — Evidence Interpretation Exercise

Assessors provide photographs, sketches, project notes or defect evidence.

Candidates must identify:

| Question | Why It Is Asked |
|---|---|
| What is known? | Tests whether the candidate can separate established fact from assumption |
| What is uncertain? | Tests recognition of the limits of available evidence |
| What requires investigation before proceeding? | Tests diagnostic capability and caution |
| What can safely proceed? | Tests proportionate decision-making |
| What must stop? | Tests safety and boundary awareness |
| Who should be involved next? | Tests boundary recognition and coordination |

---

### Method E — Consumer Communication Exercise

The candidate must explain a scope, uncertainty or escalation decision in plain language to a simulated homeowner.

The exercise tests:

- whether the candidate can communicate technical content accessibly;
- whether the candidate is honest about limitations and uncertainty;
- whether the candidate correctly conveys what the consumer needs to do next;
- whether the candidate maintains professional boundaries in consumer communication.

Assessors must score this exercise against the rubric, not against personal communication preferences.

---

### Method F — Assessor Clarification and Oral Defence

Assessors may challenge the candidate's stated reasoning to test whether responses are genuinely understood rather than rehearsed.

Oral defence questions must:

- be consistent with the challenge intent in assessor guidelines;
- be documented;
- be restricted to material already raised by the candidate or in the assessment materials;
- not introduce new scenario elements without prior documentation.

---

## Section 5 — Decision States

The following states represent the range of assessment responses available to a candidate when responding to a scenario or evidence exercise. They are **assessment decision states, not legal determinations**.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PROCEED_WITHIN_SCOPE` | Available evidence supports proceeding with planned work within current authority |
| `PROCEED_WITH_CONTROLS` | Proceeding is appropriate, but with identified controls, monitoring or documentation requirements |
| `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` | Insufficient evidence to proceed without further investigation |
| `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` | A specialist (asbestos assessor, geotechnical, other) must be engaged before proceeding |
| `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` | An engineer must be engaged. Work affecting structural elements must not proceed independently |
| `BUILDER_INVOLVEMENT_REQUIRED` | Work requires builder registration, authority or oversight |
| `PERMIT_OR_SURVEYOR_CLARIFICATION_REQUIRED` | A building permit may be required. Clarification from a building surveyor must be obtained |
| `LICENSED_TRADE_REQUIRED` | Work involves a licensed trade boundary. The relevant licensed trade must be engaged |
| `STOP_AND_MAKE_SAFE` | Work must stop. The area must be made safe before any further action |
| `UNABLE_TO_FORM_RELIABLE_POSITION` | Available evidence is insufficient to form a reliable assessment. Further information is required before any decision can be made |

### Candidate Explanation Requirements

For each decision state applied, a candidate must explain:

1. **Evidence basis** — what specific evidence supports this decision;
2. **Uncertainty** — what is not known and how that affects the decision;
3. **Reasoning** — how the evidence and uncertainty led to this decision;
4. **Boundary** — what limits apply to the candidate's own authority at this point;
5. **Next action** — what should happen next, and who is responsible for it.

## Section 6 — Scoring Model

### 6.1 Performance Levels

| Score | Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | `DEMONSTRATED_ADVANCED` | Candidate consistently demonstrates advanced capability in this domain. Reasoning is clear, evidence-based, proportionate and integrating. Boundary recognition is explicit and accurate. |
| 3 | `DEMONSTRATED_COMPETENT` | Candidate demonstrates sound capability in this domain. Reasoning is sound. There may be minor gaps in integration or depth but these do not indicate risk. |
| 2 | `PARTIALLY_DEMONSTRATED` | Candidate demonstrates some relevant capability but with material gaps, inconsistencies or areas of weakness that would need to be addressed. |
| 1 | `NOT_DEMONSTRATED` | Candidate does not demonstrate the required capability in this domain. Evidence is absent, insufficient or contradicted by the candidate's responses. |
| 0 | `UNSAFE_OR_BOUNDARY_FAILURE` | Candidate demonstrates behaviour, reasoning or decision-making that is unsafe or that crosses a critical professional boundary. This triggers critical-fail review regardless of performance in other domains. |

### 6.2 Assessor Notes Requirement

Every scored criterion must include assessor notes specifying:

- the evidence or response on which the score is based;
- the reasoning for the assigned level;
- any specific concerns or strengths;
- any departure from expected reasoning that the assessor observed.

Scores without assessor notes are invalid and must be returned for completion before any outcome is determined.

### 6.3 Aggregate Assessment Approach

Assessment outcomes must not be determined by a simple percentage score. The overall outcome must reflect:

- performance across all seven capability domains;
- critical-fail check;
- assessor narrative and rationale;
- second-assessor review where required.

Strong technical performance must not compensate for a critical boundary failure.

### 6.4 Critical-Fail Rules

A candidate cannot achieve an `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED` or `CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED_WITH_DEVELOPMENT_ITEMS` outcome if they:

1. knowingly continue unsafe work in a scenario or exercise;
2. fail to recognise a clear engineering boundary when it is present in assessment materials;
3. fail to recognise a clear builder authority, permit requirement or licensed-trade boundary when it is present in assessment materials;
4. treat major uncertainty as confirmation of safety;
5. fabricate, misrepresent or substantially mischaracterise evidence;
6. recommend a course of action outside their lawful authority as if it were within their authority;
7. fail to communicate material risk to the consumer in two or more separate assessment components (portfolio review, technical interview, scenario assessment, consumer communication exercise or oral defence).

Critical failures must be documented by the assessor with specific reference to the response or behaviour that triggered the rule. Critical-fail findings must be reviewed by a second assessor before the outcome is finalised.

---

## Section 7 — Assessment Outcomes

### 7.1 Outcome Categories

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED` | The candidate has demonstrated advanced residential renovation carpentry capability across the seven domains. No critical failures. Assessment is suitable as evidence for government consideration of formal recognition. |
| `CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED_WITH_DEVELOPMENT_ITEMS` | The candidate demonstrates general competence with identified areas for development. No critical failures. Specific development items must be documented. |
| `FURTHER_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` | The assessment is incomplete or insufficient evidence was provided to reach a conclusion. The candidate should be advised what further evidence is required. |
| `REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` | The candidate has not demonstrated sufficient capability in one or more domains for a positive outcome, but has not triggered a critical failure. Reassessment in identified domains is recommended. |
| `CAPABILITY_NOT_DEMONSTRATED` | The candidate has not demonstrated the required capability. Documentation must specify the domains and components in which capability was not demonstrated. |
| `UNABLE_TO_VERIFY` | The assessment cannot be concluded because submitted evidence cannot be verified. |

### 7.2 Prohibited Outcome Labels

The following outcome labels must not be used under this framework:

- `LICENSED`
- `REGISTERED`
- `APPROVED_TO_CONTRACT`
- `BUILDER_READY`

The outcome of assessment under this framework is **capability evidence only**. It does not confer statutory authority, builder registration or contracting rights.

## Section 8 — Pilot Metrics

The following questions must be measurable during the pilot. Specific thresholds remain to be determined during pilot design. Success criteria have not yet been validated.

| # | Pilot Question | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Can assessors distinguish advanced capability consistently? | Assessor outcome comparison |
| 2 | What is inter-assessor agreement across domains and scenarios? | Scoring variance analysis |
| 3 | Which capability domains produce the greatest assessor disagreement? | Domain-level variance analysis |
| 4 | Can candidates identify concealed-condition escalation triggers reliably? | Scenario response analysis |
| 5 | Can candidates identify professional boundaries reliably? | Boundary-recognition domain scoring and scenario analysis |
| 6 | Do consumers understand practitioner role after disclosure? | Consumer comprehension pre/post test — see `docs/consumer-role-comprehension-test-v0.1.md` |
| 7 | Are evidence records complete enough for later review? | Portfolio audit |
| 8 | Are stop / refer / escalate decisions traceable across candidate records? | Record audit |
| 9 | Does assessment identify candidates who are technically strong but weak in boundary recognition? | Cross-domain scoring analysis |
| 10 | Are assessment tasks too easy, too difficult or poorly calibrated? | Post-pilot calibration review |

Pilot outcomes are evidence inputs only. They do not predetermine formal recognition and may support a no-additional-recognition conclusion.

---

## Section 9 — Pilot Stop and Redesign Conditions

Government must retain the ability to stop, pause, redesign, narrow or decline further implementation at any point. The following conditions should trigger mandatory pilot stop or redesign review:

1. Unacceptable inter-assessor inconsistency that cannot be resolved through calibration.
2. Repeated consumer misunderstanding of practitioner scope that is not corrected by the disclosure process.
3. Unsafe scenario responses that are not captured or addressed by the scoring rubric.
4. Evidence system unable to verify candidate claims with adequate confidence.
5. Systematic confusion among candidates between advanced trade competence and builder authority.
6. Inability to define a bounded, assessable scope reliably across diverse project types.
7. Legal or regulatory conflict identified by qualified legal review.
8. Pilot design creates incentives — anticipated or observed — for participants to operate outside their lawful authority.

Where these conditions cannot be resolved proportionately, government may conclude no additional formal recognition is justified.

## Section 10 — Pilot Assessment Flow

The following represents the end-to-end assessment process for pilot v0.1.

| Stage | Action | Party Responsible |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Application | Candidate submits application and initial documentation | Candidate |
| 2. Eligibility verification | Eligibility officer reviews submitted evidence against gate criteria | Eligibility reviewer (independent of lead assessor) |
| 3. Evidence portfolio submission | Candidate submits full project portfolio | Candidate |
| 4. Evidence review | Lead assessor reviews portfolio against domain rubric | Lead assessor |
| 5. Scenario assessment | Candidate completes scenario-based assessment tasks | Candidate, observed by assessor |
| 6. Technical interview | Structured interview conducted and recorded | Lead assessor |
| 7. Consumer communication exercise | Candidate completes consumer communication task | Candidate, observed by assessor |
| 8. Assessor scoring | Lead assessor completes rubric, including written rationale for every scored criterion | Lead assessor |
| 9. Critical-fail check | Assessor confirms whether any critical-fail rule has been triggered | Lead assessor |
| 10. Second review | Where outcome is borderline, or where a critical fail is identified, second assessor independently reviews the record | Second assessor |
| 11. Outcome | Outcome determined and documented using permitted outcome vocabulary | Lead assessor (endorsed by second assessor where applicable) |
| 12. Candidate feedback | Candidate receives written feedback specifying performance by domain, strengths, development areas and, where applicable, reasons for critical-fail finding | Assessment body |
| 13. Review and appeal pathway | Candidate may request review of the outcome within a defined period | Review panel (independent of lead assessor) |
| 14. Pilot evidence capture | All records retained for pilot evaluation and audit | Assessment body |
| 15. Aggregate evaluation | Pilot outcomes, metrics and assessor variance analysed for aggregate evaluation | Pilot evaluation team |

**Important:** Completion of this process does not confer statutory authority, registration or contracting rights of any kind.

## Section 11 — Unresolved Design Questions

The following questions are not resolved by this framework. They must be addressed during pilot design.

| ID | Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| AQ-001 | What assessor composition provides sufficient technical independence? | Assessor composition affects credibility, balance and conflict-of-interest management |
| AQ-002 | What level of inter-assessor agreement is acceptable before recalibration is required? | Without a defined threshold, it is not possible to evaluate whether assessors are applying the framework consistently |
| AQ-003 | Which capability domains should be designated as mandatory critical domains? | If all domains are treated equally, a weak boundary recogniser may pass on technical strength |
| AQ-004 | What evidence should be independently verified rather than candidate-declared? | Verification requirements affect the integrity of the eligibility gate |
| AQ-005 | What constitutes sufficient verified renovation experience for eligibility? | Experience thresholds affect who can enter the pilot and how the results can be interpreted |
| AQ-006 | What appeal mechanism is proportionate for a pilot assessment? | Candidates have a legitimate interest in a reviewable outcome |
| AQ-007 | What legal review is required before pilot delivery? | The framework must not inadvertently create obligations or authorities it is not designed to create |
