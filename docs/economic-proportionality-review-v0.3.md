# Economic Proportionality and Renovation Risk — v0.3
**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria** · 14 November 2026 · LOCAL DRAFT / NOT APPROVED

## Finding
There is a plausible question whether clearer competence assessment, lawful scope boundaries and evidence-based escalation in existing-home renovation can improve consumer outcomes at proportionate cost. Neither a carpentry-specific causal harm estimate nor a positive net economic benefit has yet been established. This document does **not** justify a new statutory class.

## Decision sequence
Define and seek evidence of the public-interest harm or market failure, alongside mapping the proposed baseline carpenter registration/licensing arrangements → determine whether a residual practitioner function exists → test capability and assessment reliability → evaluate incremental consumer outcomes and economic burden → only then compare statutory and non-statutory vehicles. No new builder, engineer or licensed-trade authority is implied.

## Options, all open
0. No additional intervention beyond the baseline actually adopted by government.
1. Non-statutory guidance and clearer voluntary handoff practices.
2. Independently assessed advanced credential without additional authority.
3. Possible assessment or endorsement within an existing pathway — **statutory feasibility gate:** do not cost or describe as available until the Building Act 1993, Building Regulations 2018 and applicable class determinations/orders are checked; legislative amendment may be necessary.
4. Distinct bounded registration category only if evidence supports necessity and proportionality.

## Economic assessment method
For each option compare with the same **dated and explicitly defined** baseline, including announced reforms that would happen without this initiative. Estimate net incremental social benefit = verified avoided harm and coordination costs minus implementation, assessment, training, administration, enforcement, transition, insurance, extra referral/delay, workforce exit and reduced supply, consumer price pass-through, potential unregistered-market displacement, registry/IT, complaint handling, regulator audit and enforcement opportunity costs, and insurance market costs. Verify underwriting responses rather than assuming premium reductions. Separate one-off and recurring costs, identify who bears them, and discount multi-year effects where appropriate. No monetary estimate is asserted here.

Avoided-harm estimates require: a defensible population of eligible renovation decisions; verified baseline incidents; reliable allocation of decisions to relevant practitioners; credible counterfactual and attribution; achievable adoption and effect size; event-level non-overlapping cost categories; uncertainty ranges. Do not multiply general building defect or tribunal statistics by assumed carpenter involvement.

## Evidence design
- Baseline: classify jobs, hidden-condition discoveries, decision authority, existing referral practices, outcomes and current regulatory obligations.
- Process measures: documentation time, completeness, scope comprehension, referral appropriateness, false-positive referrals and delays.
- Outcome measures: independently verified rework, defects, disputes, consumer comprehension, builder workload, costs and adverse effects over defined follow-up periods.
- Comparison: matched or staged comparison where feasible, with documented selection bias and confounding. Pre-specify exploratory versus confirmatory objectives, selection-bias and confounder adjustment, and any confirmatory sample-size/power protocol before recruitment. A proposed 6–12 month post-completion observation period can assess process and short-term rework, not long-term structural or weatherproofing durability; long-term follow-up requires separate design. A small pilot cannot establish long-run defect reductions.
- Equity and behaviour: separately measure sole-trader, experienced-worker, regional, builder, consumer and regulator burdens; assess practitioner retirement/exit, local supply, consumer pass-through and displacement toward unregistered work without assuming their size.
- Stop/revise: terminate or redesign if scope confusion, unsafe overconfidence, assessor inconsistency, unnecessary referral or net burden outweigh benefits.

## Illustrative practitioner situations
Concealed bathroom framing deterioration; unexpected bearer/joist deterioration; and window/weatherproofing interfaces may require decisions about stopping, documenting, referring and revising scope. These are **scenarios**, not verified incident rates, cost savings or permission to undertake restricted work.
