# ADR-004: Capability model above statutory vehicle

## Status

Accepted

## Context

The original initiative used Registered Residential Carpenter (RRC) as both the capability model and the proposed statutory mechanism.

Current Victorian building reform is reconsidering practitioner registration around:

- practitioner coverage;
- authorised scope;
- competence;
- qualifications and experience;
- practical assessment;
- accountability; and
- consumer protection.

A reform architecture tied only to a standalone RRC statutory class would make the initiative depend on one legislative solution before government has tested whether the underlying capability should be recognised in another way.

## Decision

Separate the capability architecture from the final legal vehicle.

The initiative will advocate for independently assessed **Advanced Residential Carpentry** capability while remaining open to implementation through:

- Registered Residential Carpenter registration;
- DB-L reform;
- endorsement within an existing pathway;
- an advanced credential; or
- another proportionate model selected through government reform processes.

## Rationale

This decision:

- preserves the occupational-progression objective;
- reduces dependence on a single legislative solution;
- enables capability testing before authority expansion;
- improves alignment with current Victorian reform architecture;
- allows government to choose the most proportionate legal mechanism; and
- reduces the “builder-lite licence” criticism.

## Non-negotiables

Regardless of vehicle, the initiative position remains that any mature model should preserve:

- qualified carpenter entry;
- independent assessment;
- bounded scope;
- consumer disclosure;
- escalation requirements;
- evidence records;
- professional boundaries;
- accountability; and
- CPD in a mature model.

## Consequences

### Positive

- keeps RRC available as a serious implementation option;
- makes the capability architecture easier to test through policy or pilot work;
- aligns better with registration reform language about scope, competence, qualifications and experience; and
- supports proportionate government decision-making.

### Trade-offs

- the initiative must now explain capability and statutory vehicle separately;
- some existing RRC-first wording will require controlled revision; and
- unresolved legal and implementation questions remain open.
