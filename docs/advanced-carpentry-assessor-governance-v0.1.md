# Advanced Residential Carpentry Assessor Governance — Pilot Version 0.1

**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
**Status:** DRAFT — assessor governance framework for pilot consultation and validation
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

This document defines the requirements, controls and accountability structures for assessors engaged in any pilot using the Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment Framework v0.1.

---

## Important

This document does not predetermine who the regulator or accrediting body would be.

Potential assessor backgrounds are identified for consultation purposes only. They do not predetermine the composition of any assessor panel.

---

## 1 — Assessor Independence

Assessors must be independent of the candidate.

For the purposes of this framework, assessor independence requires that the assessor:

- has no current or recent commercial relationship with the candidate;
- has no personal relationship that would reasonably affect impartial scoring;
- has no financial interest in the candidate's outcome;
- has not provided training, coaching or preparation to the candidate for this specific assessment.

Where an assessor identifies a conflict or potential conflict, they must declare it immediately and withdraw from the affected assessment.

---

## 2 — Conflict-of-Interest Declaration

Every assessor must complete a conflict-of-interest declaration before each assessment.

The declaration must record:

- assessor identity;
- candidate identity;
- any relationship between assessor and candidate;
- assessor's confirmation of independence or declaration of conflict;
- date of declaration.

Declarations must be retained as part of the assessment record.

A candidate must be allocated a different assessor if a conflict is identified. If no conflict-free assessor is available, the assessment must be deferred and the pilot administrator notified.

---

## 3 — Assessor Calibration

Before participation in any pilot assessment, assessors must complete a calibration process.

Calibration must include:

- familiarisation with the assessment framework and scoring rubric;
- review of the scenario bank and expected reasoning themes;
- practice scoring of at least one sample assessment record;
- discussion of scoring rationale and inter-assessor variance in the calibration session;
- sign-off confirming readiness to assess.

Calibration records must be retained.

Assessors who demonstrate significant scoring variance during calibration must not proceed to live assessment until variance is resolved.

---

## 4 — Shared Scoring Rubric

All assessors must use the shared scoring rubric as set out in Section 6 of `docs/advanced-residential-carpentry-assessment-framework-v0.1.md`.

Assessors must not apply personal criteria that are inconsistent with the shared rubric.

Assessors may record qualitative observations in assessor notes. These observations must be consistent with the rubric, not a substitute for it.

---

## 5 — Requirement for Written Rationale

Every scored criterion must be accompanied by written assessor rationale.

Rationale must:

- identify the specific evidence, response or behaviour that informed the score;
- explain why the assigned performance level was selected;
- note any specific strengths or concerns;
- note any departure from expected reasoning that the assessor observed.

Scores without rationale are invalid. Assessors must complete all rationale fields before submitting a scoring record.

---

## 6 — Second-Assessor Review for Borderline Outcomes

Where an assessor reaches a borderline outcome determination, a second assessor must independently review the record before the outcome is finalised.

A borderline outcome is defined as:

- any outcome at the boundary between `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED` and `CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED_WITH_DEVELOPMENT_ITEMS`; or
- any outcome at the boundary between `CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED_WITH_DEVELOPMENT_ITEMS` and `REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`; or
- any outcome where the lead assessor records material uncertainty about the correct performance level in any domain.

The second assessor's review must be conducted independently — without reference to the lead assessor's scoring until the second assessor has completed their own assessment.

The second assessor's findings must be documented and retained.

Where second assessors disagree, the discrepancy must be resolved through a structured discussion and documented consensus. If consensus cannot be reached, the assessment must be escalated to the pilot administrator for determination.

---

## 7 — Mandatory Second Review for Critical-Fail Cases

Where a lead assessor identifies a critical failure as defined in Section 6.4 of the assessment framework, a second assessor must review the record independently before the critical-fail finding is confirmed.

The second assessor must:

- review the specific response or behaviour alleged to constitute the critical failure;
- confirm or dispute the critical-fail finding with written rationale;
- not be the same person as the lead assessor.

A critical-fail outcome must not be finalised without second-assessor confirmation.

---

## 8 — Record Retention

The following records must be retained for every assessment:

| Record | Retention Period |
|---|---|
| Application and eligibility documentation | To be determined during pilot design |
| Portfolio submission | To be determined during pilot design |
| Scoring record with assessor rationale | To be determined during pilot design |
| Conflict-of-interest declaration | To be determined during pilot design |
| Calibration sign-off | To be determined during pilot design |
| Second-assessor review record (where applicable) | To be determined during pilot design |
| Outcome notification to candidate | To be determined during pilot design |
| Appeal documentation (where applicable) | To be determined during pilot design |

Specific retention periods must be determined during pilot design, with reference to applicable regulatory requirements and privacy obligations.

Records must be stored securely and accessible for audit.

---

## 9 — Candidate Review and Appeal Mechanism

Every candidate must have access to a review and appeal pathway.

The review pathway must:

- be available within a defined period following the outcome notification;
- allow the candidate to request review of the scoring record;
- allow the candidate to identify specific concerns about the assessment process or outcome;
- be conducted by a reviewer independent of the lead assessor and second assessor;
- result in a written determination with reasons.

The appeal pathway must:

- be available where the candidate believes the review determination is itself procedurally or substantively flawed;
- be conducted by a panel independent of the original assessment;
- result in a written determination with reasons.

The specific timeframes and procedures for review and appeal must be determined during pilot design.

Appeals must not be managed by the same person who conducted the original assessment.

---

## 10 — Assessor Variance Analysis

During the pilot, assessor variance must be monitored systematically.

Variance analysis must record:

- scoring distributions by assessor across comparable assessments;
- domains where variance is highest;
- whether variance is attributable to candidate differences or assessor differences;
- individual assessors who demonstrate persistent high-variance scoring.

Assessors demonstrating persistent high-variance scoring must be recalibrated before continuing.

Results of variance analysis must be reported as part of the aggregate pilot evaluation.

---

## 11 — Audit Sampling

A defined proportion of assessment records must be subject to independent audit sampling during the pilot.

Audit sampling must:

- include a random sample of completed assessments;
- include all critical-fail cases;
- include all second-assessor review cases;
- include cases selected specifically to test for assessor drift over time;
- be conducted by a party independent of the assessment delivery team.

The audit sampling proportion must be determined during pilot design.

Audit findings must be reported to the pilot administrator and must inform ongoing assessor governance.

---

## 12 — Potential Assessor Backgrounds

The following backgrounds are identified for consultation purposes only. They do not predetermine assessor composition.

Not all assessors need to hold all of the roles listed below. Different backgrounds may be appropriate for different assessment components.

| Background | Potential Role |
|---|---|
| Experienced qualified carpenter with renovation focus | Technical and domain capability assessment |
| Registered builder with residential renovation experience | Technical, structural-boundary and coordination assessment |
| Building surveyor | Structural-boundary, permit and regulatory-boundary assessment |
| Engineer (structural or civil) | Structural understanding domain assessment |
| Regulator or policy observer | Process and governance observation |
| Consumer representative | Consumer communication exercise assessment |

Panel composition must ensure that no single background dominates the assessment of domains requiring multiple perspectives.

---

## 13 — Governance Questions Not Yet Resolved

The following questions must be addressed during pilot design:

- What is the minimum number of assessors per assessment?
- What training and qualification requirements apply to assessors?
- Who governs the assessor governance framework itself?
- What recourse exists if the assessment body fails to comply with this framework?
- What privacy obligations apply to candidate and assessor records?
- How is assessor remuneration managed to avoid financial incentives affecting outcomes?
