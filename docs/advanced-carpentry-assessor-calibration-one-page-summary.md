## What This Is

This one-page summary explains the assessor calibration component of the Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment pilot.

It does not imply government endorsement of the assessment framework or any proposed recognition mechanism.

---

## Why Calibration Matters

A capability assessment framework is only useful if different assessors reviewing the same candidate evidence reach materially consistent conclusions.

If assessors score the same response differently — particularly on critical safety decisions, professional boundary recognition or escalation judgements — the assessment outcomes cannot be relied upon as defensible evidence of candidate capability.

Calibration tests this consistency before any live candidate is assessed.

---

## What Calibration Tests

Assessors independently review benchmark candidate responses and score them against the shared framework rubric. Results are compared. The process tests:

- **Domain scores** — whether assessors assign consistent scores to the same benchmark response across each of the seven capability domains
- **Escalation decisions** — whether assessors agree on when a candidate's stop/escalate decision was appropriate
- **Critical-fail recognition** — whether assessors consistently identify responses that trigger the critical-fail rules (e.g., unsupported structural conclusions, engineering-boundary breaches, failure to disclose material risk)
- **Assessment outcomes** — whether assessors reach the same overall candidate outcome for each benchmark profile
- **Reasoning** — whether assessors identify the same evidence basis for their conclusions

---

## What Happens If Assessors Disagree

Disagreement is not a failure of the process. It is the data the process is designed to collect.

Where assessors disagree:

1. The specific point of disagreement is precisely identified.
2. Each assessor records the evidence basis for their position.
3. The shared rubric is applied to resolve the dispute.
4. If the rubric does not resolve the dispute, a second reviewer is engaged.
5. If the dispute arose because the rubric or scenario was ambiguous, that ambiguity is recorded separately as a framework defect requiring revision.
6. The resolution reasoning is documented. Hierarchy or seniority does not determine the outcome.

Material disagreements — particularly on critical-fail recognition, engineering boundaries or candidate outcomes — receive enhanced review.

---

## Core Line

> "The pilot should not proceed to live candidate assessment until assessor calibration shows that material safety, boundary and outcome decisions can be applied with sufficient consistency."

Acceptable inter-assessor agreement thresholds remain to be validated through calibration data. No numeric threshold is presented as a validated standard in this document.

---

## What Calibration Does Not Do

- It does not determine statutory competence standards.
- It does not confer practitioner authority on candidates.
- It does not constitute government approval of the framework.
- It does not provide a validated agreement threshold without supporting evidence.
- It does not replace legal or regulatory review of the framework's boundary definitions.

## Readiness Categories

Following calibration, one of the following readiness recommendations is formed:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `READY_FOR_LIMITED_PILOT` | Adequate assessor agreement demonstrated |
| `READY_WITH_CONDITIONS` | Adequate for most domains; specific conditions apply |
| `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` | Material disagreements or rubric ambiguity must be resolved first |
| `FRAMEWORK_REVISION_REQUIRED` | Systemic framework issue identified; broader revision required |
| `UNABLE_TO_FORM_RELIABLE_POSITION` | Calibration could not produce a reliable basis for a recommendation |

These are calibration-readiness outcomes only. They are not government positions.
