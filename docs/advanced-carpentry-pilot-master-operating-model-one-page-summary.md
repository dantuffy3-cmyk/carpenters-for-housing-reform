## Purpose

This summary gives DTP, BPC and government reviewers a single-view operating map of the current pilot architecture.

**The master operating model integrates the pilot’s governance, assessment, calibration and evidence controls without predetermining any regulatory outcome.**

Where conflict exists, the detailed governed source document controls.

---

↓

↓  
**Assessor calibration**  
↓  
**Assessment**  
↓  
**Review**  
↓  
**Pilot evidence**  
↓  
**Public-interest evaluation**  
↓  
**Government decision**

---

## What the model does

- shows the pilot in operating order;
- identifies who leads each step;
- makes decision gates visible;
- identifies what records are created;
- shows where the pilot must pause;
- separates candidate outcomes from public-interest evaluation; and
- keeps regulatory vehicle selection to the end.

---

## Core control points

| Control point | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Governance readiness before recruitment | No live recruitment until readiness is recorded |
| Calibration before live assessment | No candidate assessment until assessor consistency is tested |
| Eligibility separate from competence | Entry to assessment is not a pass |
| Evidence sufficiency explicit | Missing evidence cannot improve an outcome |
| Critical-fail review | Critical fail cannot be averaged away |
| Second review | Borderline and critical-fail matters require independent review |
| Complaint separation | Complaints review process fairness; they do not automatically alter competence findings |
| Aggregate evaluation before policy evaluation | Pilot evidence is aggregated before any public-interest claim |
| Government decision last | Vehicle choice remains open, including no formal recognition |

---

## Evidence pathway

**Source evidence**  
→ verified / classified evidence  
→ assessment evidence  
→ assessor rationale  
→ candidate outcome  
→ pilot aggregate evidence  
→ public-interest analysis  
→ government decision support

Canonical evidence statuses remain those already defined in the evidence requirements document.

---

## Decision gates

1. Governance ready?
2. Assessors calibrated?
3. Candidate eligible?
4. Evidence sufficient to assess?
5. Critical failure present?
6. Second review required?
7. Candidate outcome
8. Material assessor divergence?
9. Pilot evidence sufficient for evaluation?
10. Public-interest case established?
11. Regulatory vehicle, if any?

---

## What the pilot does not do

- It does **not** confer statutory authority.
- It does **not** create automatic recognition.
- It does **not** predetermine RRC, DB-L reform or any other vehicle.
- It does **not** require government to choose formal recognition.

**Government may choose no formal recognition.**

---

## Operating conclusion

Successful pilot performance would justify further policy evaluation, **not automatic statutory recognition**.
