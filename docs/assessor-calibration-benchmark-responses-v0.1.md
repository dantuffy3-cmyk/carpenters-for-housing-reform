## Important

These benchmark candidate responses are calibration instruments.

They are designed to test whether assessors can apply the Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment Framework v0.1 consistently when reviewing realistic candidate responses.

These responses:

- **are not model answers that candidates must reproduce**;
- **are not definitions of the only acceptable professional response**;
- **do not confer authority or recognition on any practitioner**;
- **have not been independently validated as representative of real candidate populations**.

Assessors must apply the shared rubric to these responses. Expected outcomes are indicative, not binding. If an assessor's reasoned rubric application leads to a different outcome, that divergence must be recorded and reviewed.

---

## Scenario Used for Primary Calibration

All four benchmark profiles respond to **Calibration Scenario A: Subfloor Access Revealing Moisture, Ventilation and Timber Deterioration** (Assessment Scenario Bank v0.1 — Scenario 2), with the exception of Profile D, which is drawn from Calibration Scenario B: Bathroom Renovation Revealing Framing Deterioration (Assessment Scenario Bank v0.1 — Scenario 1) to allow cross-scenario critical-fail calibration.

Assessors may be asked to apply any of the three selected calibration scenarios to these profiles. Each profile's primary response addresses Scenario A. Scenario-specific cross-references are noted where relevant.

---

## Assessment Questions Being Responded To

The benchmark responses address the following questions from the scenario:

1. How many separate conditions have you identified?
2. What is the priority order for addressing them?
3. What decision state applies to each condition?
4. Who needs to be involved for each condition?
5. Can any remediation work begin now? If so, which?
6. What do you tell the consumer, and in what order?

## Profile A — Advanced Response

### Candidate Background Descriptor

Experienced residential renovation carpenter. Documented project portfolio. Structured written response. References to progressive discovery, escalation triggers and boundary recognition.

### Response

**Question 1 — Conditions identified:**

I've found three distinct conditions that need to be treated separately:

First, moisture is present on the underside of the floor boards across a wider area than the original complaint — this isn't confined to the reported room.

Second, there's significant timber deterioration in the floor joists in approximately a 15m² area at the rear. The timber is soft, discoloured and penetrates with a probe. I can describe what I've observed, but I can't conclusively determine whether this is fungal decay, insect damage or another process from visual inspection alone.

Third, there are tunnels and surface galleries on two stumps and the underside of a bearer that are consistent with termite activity. I'm noting these as possible indicators of termite damage, not confirmed infestation. Whether the activity is current or historical hasn't been established by a qualified person.

Additionally, the concrete pour has blocked part of the subfloor ventilation, which has contributed to the stagnant air conditions and moisture build-up.

**Question 2 — Priority order:**

1. Make the subfloor area safe and identify any immediate structural risk before proceeding.
2. Engage a licensed pest specialist before any structural timber decisions are made — I cannot determine whether the timber deterioration is connected to the termite indicators or whether any infestation is active.
3. Engage a structural engineer to assess the extent of bearer and joist damage once pest clearance has addressed the termite question.
4. Subfloor drainage and ventilation assessment, which may be separable from the structural repair depending on the pest and engineering advice.

I'm not beginning structural timber work before items 1 and 2 are completed.

**Question 3 — Decision states:**

- Termite indicators: `SPECIALIST_INPUT_REQUIRED` — licensed pest specialist required before any structural timber decisions.
- Structural deterioration: `ENGINEERING_INPUT_REQUIRED` — once pest assessment is complete, the extent and structural adequacy of affected bearers and joists requires engineering assessment.
- Full moisture extent: `INVESTIGATE_BEFORE_PROCEEDING` — full moisture mapping across the subfloor is needed before remediation scope is fixed.
- Ventilation blockage: this may fall within scope once the structural and pest questions are addressed, but I won't begin that work until the structural risk is assessed.

**Question 4 — Who needs to be involved:**

- Licensed pest specialist: to assess the termite indicators and confirm whether activity is current or historical, and whether any treatment is required before structural work proceeds.
- Structural engineer: to assess the extent of the bearer and joist deterioration and confirm what replacement is structurally appropriate.
- Possibly the building surveyor: if the extent of structural replacement triggers a permit requirement.
- The owner: immediately, because the scope and budget for this project have changed materially.

**Question 5 — Can any work begin now:**

Subfloor ventilation assessment can be noted and documented, but I won't begin remediation of the ventilation blockage until the structural risk is resolved. I won't commence any structural timber work. I am making the subfloor safe and secure and ensuring there's no immediate risk before I leave the site.

The only work that is appropriate now is documentation, photography and making the area safe.

**Question 6 — Consumer communication:**

I would communicate to the owner in person, then follow up in writing:

> "I need to explain what I've found, because it significantly affects what we're dealing with.
>
> I've identified three separate conditions in the subfloor: moisture across a larger area than you reported, significant timber deterioration in a 15m² zone at the rear, and indicators of what may be termite activity on two stumps and a bearer. I can't tell you from visual inspection whether the termites are current or historical, and I can't tell you the full extent of the structural damage until I have expert input.
>
> I'm stopping structural work on the framing immediately. Before any timber is replaced, I need a licensed pest specialist to assess the termite indicators — that's not my call to make, and getting it wrong would mean you might replace structurally sound timber in a compromised area.
>
> Once I have the pest assessment, I'll engage a structural engineer to determine what needs replacing and to what standard.
>
> The original scope and price cannot stand. I don't know by how much it will change, but I'll give you a realistic picture as soon as I have the specialist input.
>
> I'll give you a written summary of today's findings. You should consider notifying your insurer."

**Expected outcome:** Likely `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED` for the tested domain set, subject to full portfolio and oral assessment. Assessors should reach consistent agreement that this response demonstrates advanced capability across Diagnostic, Structural Understanding, Planning, Boundary Recognition, Professional Judgement and Consumer Communication domains.

## Profile B — Competent but Not Advanced Response

### Candidate Background Descriptor

Competent renovation carpenter. Reasonable instincts. Less structured response. Recognises the major issues but shows limited diagnostic depth in sequencing and documentation. Requires some assessor prompting to complete.

### Response

**Question 1 — Conditions identified:**

There's moisture damage through the floor boards in various spots, and some joists at the back are quite soft — clearly deteriorated. I also noticed what looked like possible termite damage or activity on a couple of stumps, but I'm not a pest inspector so I didn't want to draw too many conclusions. The concrete pour has blocked some of the ventilation which probably hasn't helped.

**Question 2 — Priority order:**

The structural timber is the main concern. We need to sort out the joist situation before anything else. The termite question probably needs a pest inspector to look at it. Ventilation is lower priority.

**Question 3 — Decision states:**

I'd say stop and investigate before we proceed on the structural side. The pest question needs a specialist.

**Question 4 — Who needs to be involved:**

A pest inspector definitely. I'd probably want an engineer involved somewhere along the way if the extent of the damage is significant. The owner needs to know the scope has changed.

**Question 5 — Can any work begin now:**

Not on the structure, no. I'd hold off until I had the pest inspection done. I might be able to start planning the ventilation work.

**Question 6 — Consumer communication:**

I'd tell the owner what I found — the moisture, the deteriorated joists and the possible termite issue. I'd explain that I need a pest inspection before I touch the structural timber, and that the scope has changed. I'd try to give them a sense of the likely cost increase, but I'd be honest that I don't know the full picture yet.

**Assessor note:** This response requires light prompting to produce the full picture. The candidate does not use the decision-state language but the reasoning is broadly correct. The priority order is approximately right but the candidate doesn't clearly articulate the sequencing logic. The consumer communication is adequate but lacks the precision and documentation commitment of Profile A. There is no mention of a written summary or insurer notification. The structural and pest sequencing is correct in substance.

**Expected outcome:** Likely `CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED_WITH_DEVELOPMENT_ITEMS` — candidate demonstrates sound general reasoning with identified development areas in structured diagnostic depth, written documentation practice and consumer communication precision. No critical fail.

## Profile C — Incomplete / Evidence-Poor Response

### Candidate Background Descriptor

Candidate with reasonable practical instincts but insufficient evidence basis in their response. Identifies some issues but leaves key unknowns unaddressed. Does not fully commit to an escalation position.

### Response

**Question 1 — Conditions identified:**

There's moisture damage and some joist deterioration in part of the subfloor. There were also some marks on the stumps that might be termite related, but it's hard to say without a closer look.

**Question 2 — Priority order:**

I'd want to sort the structural stuff first. The termite thing would depend on what the inspection found.

**Question 3 — Decision states:**

I'm not sure what to do exactly. There's clearly work needed but I'd need more information before I could say what the decision state is.

**Question 4 — Who needs to be involved:**

Probably a pest inspector at some point. Might need an engineer for the structural side if it's bad enough.

**Question 5 — Can any work begin now:**

I think we could probably make a start on some of the easier stuff while we wait for the reports.

**Question 6 — Consumer communication:**

I'd let them know there's a bit more work involved than originally thought and that I'm getting some people in to have a look.

**Assessor note:** This response does not demonstrate adequate diagnostic depth. Key unknowns are not explicitly identified. The sequencing is reversed — the candidate suggests starting structural work before pest clearance. The consumer communication does not disclose material risk and provides false reassurance through vagueness. The response does not demonstrate boundary recognition for the pest specialist or engineering referral. Evidence is absent across multiple criteria.

**Expected outcome:** `FURTHER_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` or `REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` — the candidate has not provided sufficient evidence to reach a conclusion on capability in the tested domains. The assessor must record what evidence is missing and what further evidence would be required to revisit.

*Note: This response does not trigger a critical fail, but may do so if the candidate confirmed in an oral component that they intended to proceed with structural work before pest clearance.*

## Profile D — Unsafe / Boundary-Fail Response

### Candidate Background Descriptor

Candidate who has worked in residential renovation for many years. Confident communicator. Response reveals at least one clear critical-failure pattern. Scenario is drawn from Calibration Scenario B (Bathroom Renovation) to allow cross-scenario testing of critical-fail recognition.

### Response

**Context:** The candidate is responding to questions after discovering the framing deterioration in the bathroom renovation scenario.

**Question 1 — What is your assessment of what you have found:**

There's definitely some rot in the wall framing — bottom plate and a couple of studs are soft. That's pretty standard in wet areas that have had leaks for a while. I've dealt with this type of thing many times. The nogging at 900mm is also soft but that's just a nogging, so it's less of a concern.

**Question 2 — What additional information do you need before proceeding:**

I've got a pretty good read on what's there. I might want to get a look under the floor at some point, but I think I have enough to proceed with replacing the affected members. If the engineer is needed I can get one in, but in my experience these jobs don't always need it.

**Question 3 — What decision state applies:**

I'd say we can proceed with the repair. I'm satisfied with my assessment.

**Question 4 — Who needs to be involved:**

It's within my capability to handle this. I'd consult an engineer if I find something I'm genuinely unsure about, but I don't think it's needed here.

**Question 5 — What do you tell the consumer:**

I'd tell them we've found some rot as expected, and we're going to replace the affected framing and then proceed with the bathroom. I'd keep it brief — consumers don't need to be alarmed by normal renovation findings.

**Question 6 — What records do you create:**

I'd take some photos and note it in the job file.

**Assessor note:** This response contains multiple critical-fail triggers:

1. The candidate concludes they can proceed with structural member replacement without establishing whether the wall is load-bearing. This is an unsupported structural conclusion.
2. The candidate dismisses the need for engineering input without investigation — treating uncertainty as confirmation that no engineering is needed.
3. The candidate decides not to fully disclose the discovered condition to the consumer, characterising it as "normal renovation findings" to avoid alarming them. This suppresses the consumer's ability to make an informed decision.
4. The candidate treats the nogging deterioration as less significant without establishing its structural role.

**Expected outcome:** `CAPABILITY_NOT_DEMONSTRATED` — critical-fail rules 2, 4 and 7 are triggered. Assessors should reach consistent agreement that this response fails on critical-fail grounds. The response is realistic enough that assessors must apply the rubric carefully — the candidate sounds confident and experienced, which tests whether assessors are influenced by presentation rather than reasoning quality.

## Calibration Notes for Assessors

**Distinguishing A from B:**
Profile A explicitly sequences the pest and structural questions, names the decision states, commits to written documentation and identifies the insurer notification. Profile B arrives at the right substantive decision but with less depth and precision. Both are safe. The difference is in integration and depth, not in fundamental competence.

**Distinguishing B from C:**
Profile B may require prompting but produces a broadly correct response. Profile C does not produce adequate evidence even with the questions as a prompt. Profile C has a sequencing error (structural before pest) that is a substantive weakness. Neither profile triggers a critical fail on the written response alone.

**Recognising D as a critical fail:**
The candidate in Profile D sounds experienced and calm. The response is not cartoonishly incompetent. Assessors must apply the rubric, not react to tone. The critical failures are: (1) unsupported structural conclusion; (2) dismissal of engineering referral without investigation; (3) consumer communication failure. All three are present in the written response.
