## Decision proposed

Retain the baseline-first, capability-first and vehicle-neutral architecture. Refine the submission to address both the justification for regulation and practical implementation. The new sources strengthen the need to address evidence, burden and transition; they do not establish a final regulatory model or endorse RRC.

The genuine v0.3 already contains minimum-intervention reasoning, attribution safeguards, transition controls and a separate advanced-capability evidence test. The task is targeted refinement, not replacement of those positions.

Proposed sequence: public-interest problem and evidence limitations → baseline competence → technical and contracting/accountability boundaries → competence-based transition → test any residual existing-building or specialist capability → assess evidence and proportionality → choose mechanism last.

## Source reconciliation before drafting

1. **Support and opposition:** S2 DTPM-005 reports an early survey support signal. S4 reports support and opposition across three sessions held between 24 September and 1 October. Retain both with their different populations, periods and limits. Neither establishes consensus or majority support.
2. **Authority:** S3 slide 3 supplies a proposed employee-permission table absent from the truncated S2 transcript. Cite the deck for its table; do not represent this as recovery of missing transcript words. S2 DTPM-018 separately records a DTP statement concerning work under $10,000. S3 does not resolve that below-threshold question. Preserve the distinction and request clarification before making legal assertions.
3. **Workforce:** S3 slide 6 directly records DTP's estimate of over 20,000 carpenters. This supports attribution of the estimate to DTP, without verifying its method or converting it into a renovation-cohort count. S2 DTPM-021 was structured-summary evidence only.
4. **Assessment:** S3 slide 4 describes trust and verify, risk assessment of class and applicant, reduced paperwork and exams for higher-risk classes. It does not establish the exact future carpenter exam or show that every portfolio, practical or interview method is prohibited. Any broader BPC process claim requires a separately cited current primary source.
5. **Timing/status:** S4 is a 6 October circulation of presentation slides and a stakeholder-feedback synthesis. It does not say the September consultation paper is superseded. Advice will go to the incoming Government for consideration; no final decision or commencement date is announced.
6. **Learning pathways:** S3 slide 6 recognises qualifications, apprenticeships, supervised experience and on-the-job learning for workforce transition. It does not settle future entrant eligibility. Avoid silently changing Dan's apprenticeship position or conflating future entry with existing-worker transition.

## Contrary evidence and unresolved questions to retain

- S4 reports both support and opposition; no proportions, unanimity or endorsement are supplied.
- Recruitment, retention and quality benefits are stakeholder expectations, not measured results.
- Whether individual worker licensing offers incremental benefit over contractor-focused controls remains open.
- Defect attribution and causal magnitude remain uncertain; practitioner experience is not a population study.
- S2's missing later transcript and ambiguous action-item identities remain unresolved. The email does not identify the participants previously described as “meeting participant.”
- A general licence, five-year pathway and advanced credentials remain discussion positions, not adopted settings.
- The qualification audit is not re-performed here. “Not clearly mandatory” must not become “not taught” or “not covered.”
- Proposed tiers in the deck do not themselves commence legal obligations. Detailed under-$10,000 contracting, permits, corporate roles, insurance and commencement need authoritative clarification.
- No source justifies a mandatory RRC stage before builder registration or dilution of builder/engineer/surveyor/licensed-trade responsibilities.
- The $16m program commitment does not validate benefit assumptions or allocate funds to our proposed instruments.
- Session-feedback survey is separate from substantive consultation responses. No feedback, submission or contact has been sent by this work.
