# Advanced Residential Carpentry Capability Assessment — Pilot: One-Page Government Summary

**Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**
**Status:** DRAFT — for government and regulator review
**Audience:** DTP / Building and Plumbing Commission / Government reviewers
**Version:** 0.1
**Date:** August 2026

---

## The Problem

Residential renovation in existing buildings creates assessment complexity that standard carpentry qualifications do not fully test. Concealed conditions, load-path uncertainty, progressive scope change and multi-party professional boundaries regularly arise in renovation work. There is no current mechanism in Victoria that assesses and evidences advanced renovation capability at the trade level in a way that is transparent, consistent and reviewable.

---

## What Is Being Tested

**The pilot tests whether Advanced Residential Carpentry capability can be assessed reliably before government considers whether any formal recognition is warranted.**

The pilot does not grant, imply or simulate additional statutory authority. Participants remain within their existing lawful authority throughout.

The pilot is designed to answer one question:

> Can advanced residential renovation carpentry capability be assessed reliably, consistently and transparently enough to inform government consideration of future recognition?

---

## Seven Capability Domains

Assessment tests capability across seven domains:

1. **Technical Capability** — application of carpentry knowledge in existing-building contexts
2. **Diagnostic Capability** — recognition of existing conditions, concealed conditions and risk indicators
3. **Structural Understanding** — recognition of load paths and structural boundaries, bounded from engineering design authority
4. **Planning Capability** — renovation planning under uncertainty, including scope-change management
5. **Coordination Capability** — coordination with builders, engineers, building surveyors and licensed trades
6. **Professional Judgement** — integration of all domains through the decision cycle: observe, diagnose, predict, plan, execute, verify
7. **Boundary Recognition** — recognition of the limits of the practitioner's own authority and capability

---

## Assessment Methods

Assessment uses six complementary components:

| Component | What It Tests |
|---|---|
| Portfolio Evidence Review | Real project evidence across multiple existing-building contexts |
| Structured Technical Interview | Reasoning, diagnosis, decision sequence and boundary awareness |
| Scenario-Based Assessment | Decision-making under uncertainty using realistic renovation cases |
| Evidence Interpretation Exercise | Candidate's ability to distinguish what is known, uncertain and requires action |
| Consumer Communication Exercise | Ability to explain scope, uncertainty and escalation clearly to a homeowner |
| Assessor Clarification / Oral Defence | Whether reasoning is understood, not merely rehearsed |

---

## Safety and Authority Boundary

- Assessment does not grant registration, licensing or builder authority.
- Participants remain within their existing lawful authority throughout.
- Structural understanding is bounded from engineering design authority.
- Boundary recognition is a scored assessment domain — candidates who cannot identify professional boundaries cannot achieve an advanced outcome.
- Critical-fail rules prevent strong technical scores from compensating for boundary failures.
- The framework itself has not yet been independently validated.

---

## Candidate Outcomes

Assessment produces capability evidence only. Outcome categories are:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ADVANCED_CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED` | Evidence suitable for government consideration of formal recognition |
| `CAPABILITY_DEMONSTRATED_WITH_DEVELOPMENT_ITEMS` | Competent with identified development areas |
| `FURTHER_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` | Assessment incomplete |
| `REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` | Insufficient capability demonstrated in identified domains |
| `CAPABILITY_NOT_DEMONSTRATED` | Required capability not demonstrated |
| `UNABLE_TO_VERIFY` | Assessment cannot be concluded |

Assessment does not produce outcomes labelled: Licensed, Registered, Approved to Contract, or Builder Ready.

---

## Pilot Metrics

The pilot measures ten questions:

1. Can assessors distinguish advanced capability consistently?
2. What is inter-assessor agreement?
3. Which domains produce the most assessor disagreement?
4. Can candidates identify concealed-condition escalation reliably?
5. Can candidates identify professional boundaries reliably?
6. Do consumers understand practitioner role after disclosure?
7. Are evidence records complete enough for later review?
8. Are stop / refer / escalate decisions traceable?
9. Does assessment identify technically strong but boundary-weak candidates?
10. Are assessment tasks appropriately calibrated?

---

## Government Decision After Pilot

Government retains full discretion following the pilot.

Government may:

- proceed with a formal recognition mechanism (RRC, DB-L reform, advanced credential or alternative);
- continue the pilot with modifications;
- commission further evaluation;
- decline to proceed.

The pilot produces capability evidence. The formal recognition decision is a separate government question.

Government must also retain the ability to stop, pause or redesign the pilot at any point.
