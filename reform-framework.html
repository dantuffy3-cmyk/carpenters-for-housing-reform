Reform architecture · consultation draft · 2026

A connected framework for professional recognition, quality assurance and practical reform

Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria has developed a connected group of reform proposals addressing practitioner recognition, renovation responsibility, quality assurance and evidence-based regulation.

The proposals are consultation concepts. They do not claim Victorian Government endorsement and do not seek unrestricted deregulation.

Core architecture

Four connected reform proposals

  • Registered Residential Carpenter — first pilot
  • Advanced Residential Trades Framework — staged architecture
  • Waterproofing Integrity Framework — risk-based assurance
  • Residential Building Reform Institute — evidence platform
Framework overview

Four proposals, one connected reform platform

Each component addresses a distinct dimension of residential building reform. Together they form a coherent, evidence-based approach to improving practitioner recognition, responsibility and quality outcomes.

First proposed professional-recognition pilot

Registered Residential Carpenter

A bounded pathway for experienced renovation carpenters, supported by assessment, insurance, documentation, audits and clear exclusions. The RRC is proposed as the first pilot — not an expansion of builder scope.

Registered builders remain responsible for broader, structural and multi-trade work.

View RRC Pathway
Scalable long-term regulatory architecture

Advanced Residential Trades Framework

A staged system for recognising advanced specialist competence while maintaining separate evidence, assessment and scope controls for every trade pathway. Future trade investigations are staged for later consideration only.

View Advanced Trades Framework
Risk-based quality-assurance model

Waterproofing Integrity Framework

A targeted approach using hold points, product-system records, digital evidence, practitioner accountability and proportionate regulatory oversight. Waterproofing is treated as a separate high-risk category requiring dedicated assurance settings.

View Waterproofing Work
Independent research and evidence platform

Residential Building Reform Institute

A platform for practical research, consultation, publication and reform development based on trade experience, consumer risk and regulatory evidence.

How the proposals connect

A logical reform sequence

Step 1

WHO IS COMPETENT

The RRC and Advanced Trades Framework define practitioner recognition and assessment standards.

Step 2

WHAT RESPONSIBILITY THEY MAY ACCEPT

Clear scope boundaries and builder handoff triggers define the limits of recognised practitioner responsibility.

Step 3

HOW THE WORK IS VERIFIED

The Waterproofing Integrity Framework demonstrates how risk-based hold points and documentation provide quality assurance.

HOW EVIDENCE IMPROVES REGULATION — The Reform Institute gathers, analyses and publishes the evidence needed to support or refine future reform decisions.

Reform rationale

Why these proposals belong together

  • The RRC tests an advanced practitioner pathway within a defined, bounded scope — it is the first and primary pilot.
  • The Advanced Trades Framework creates possible future regulatory architecture for other specialist trades, staged to allow evidence-led evaluation before any broader pathway discussions.
  • The Waterproofing Integrity Framework demonstrates differentiated risk controls appropriate to high-consequence concealed work, keeping waterproofing separate from general trade-pathway sequencing.
  • The Institute gathers and publishes the practical evidence needed to support, refine or challenge future reform proposals.
  • Registered builders remain responsible for broader, structural and multi-trade residential building work throughout.

Recognise competence. Define responsibility. Verify outcomes. Improve the system through evidence.

Consultation

Contribute Practical Evidence

This reform initiative relies on practical, on-the-ground evidence from working tradespeople, builders, consumers and industry professionals. If you have relevant experience, we want to hear from you.

When contacting us, please include information about:

  • Your trade or professional role
  • Your qualification and registration status
  • Your years of relevant experience
  • An example of hidden or changed project conditions you have encountered
  • The original expected scope or value of that project
  • The final scope or value after conditions changed
  • Current regulatory difficulties you face
  • What work you believe should remain builder-controlled
  • What evidence you believe should prove advanced practitioner competence
  • Whether you would be willing to provide a confidential interview or case study

Where information is sensitive, you may indicate that it is provided in confidence and we will treat it accordingly.
