# AGENTS.md

## Project Overview

This repository supports **Carpenters for Housing Reform Victoria**.

Its flagship policy project is:

# Residential Renovation Capability in Victoria

The project examines how advanced residential renovation capability should be defined, evidenced and recognised within Victoria’s existing building regulatory framework.

The project is evidence-led, consumer-focused and professionally neutral.

---

## Current Policy Position

Residential Renovation Capability is the primary policy subject.

The Registered Residential Carpenter pathway is one possible recognition mechanism. It must not be treated as the predetermined outcome.

Policy options may include:

1. Maintaining the current framework
2. Reforming or modernising DB-L Carpentry
3. Introducing an advanced renovation capability endorsement
4. Establishing an optional Registered Residential Carpenter pathway
5. Using non-statutory recognition or assessment mechanisms

Agents must evaluate these options objectively.

## Non-Negotiable Principles

All work must preserve the following positions:

- Consumer protection is the primary objective.
- Evidence takes precedence over advocacy.
- Existing builder pathways remain available and unchanged.
- Certificate V pathways remain directly available.
- An RRC pathway must never become mandatory before builder registration.
- Registered Builders retain their existing authority.
- Engineers retain engineering responsibilities.
- Building Surveyors retain surveying and certification responsibilities.
- Licensed trades retain their existing regulated responsibilities.
- Recognition must be based on demonstrated capability.
- Professional boundaries must be clear.
- Recommendations must strengthen, not destabilise, Victoria’s regulatory framework.

## Core Capability Framework

The project currently recognises seven capability domains:

1. Technical Capability
2. Diagnostic Capability
3. Structural Understanding
4. Planning Capability
5. Coordination Capability
6. Professional Judgement
7. Boundary Recognition

Professional Judgement integrates the other capability domains.

The professional decision cycle is:

Observe  
Diagnose  
Predict  
Plan  
Execute  
Verify

Agents must preserve these concepts unless specifically instructed to review or revise them.

## Key Renovation Principle

Residential renovation is distinguished not only by construction activity, but by the complexity of decisions required before, during and after construction.

Existing buildings create uncertainty through:

- concealed conditions;
- previous alterations;
- deterioration;
- incomplete documentation;
- variable construction methods;
- changing load paths;
- progressive discovery.

Agents must not describe renovation work as reliably separable into simple structural and non-structural categories without recognising these uncertainties.

---

## Progressive Discovery

Progressive discovery is a normal feature of residential renovation.

When concealed conditions change the risk, scope or regulatory requirements of work, the preferred response is:

1. Make the area safe.
2. Pause the affected work.
3. Record the discovered condition.
4. Notify the owner.
5. Obtain the appropriate builder, engineer, building surveyor or licensed-trade input.
6. Revise scope, responsibility and contractual arrangements before continuing.
7. Verify the completed response.

Agents must not invent legal immunity, liability transfer or insurance outcomes unless supported by verified law or authoritative evidence.

## Evidence Rules

Clearly distinguish between:

### Verified Evidence

Legislation, regulations, official guidance, government data, recognised standards, published research and authoritative industry sources.

### Practitioner Evidence

Documented observations and case studies from experienced residential renovation practitioners.

### Policy Analysis

Reasoned evaluation of options, risks, benefits and implementation requirements.

### Scenario Analysis

Hypothetical, future-facing or adversarial material used to test the proposal.

Scenario analysis must never be presented as verified evidence.

Do not invent:

- legislation;
- statistics;
- court findings;
- insurance rules;
- government positions;
- future policy outcomes;
- regulatory powers;
- stakeholder endorsements.

Flag unsupported claims for review.

## Change Management

For significant policy changes:

- state the existing position;
- explain the proposed change;
- identify the reason;
- list affected files;
- preserve a record in `DECISION_REGISTER.md`.

Do not perform broad repository rewrites without a scoped task.
